**Black History Month 2027 covers February 2027, which runs from Monday, Feb. 1 to Sunday, Feb. 28, a span of 28 days, and the federal portal blackhistorymonth.gov, run by the Library of Congress with partners, states that "February is Black History Month."** The Association for the Study of African American Life and History (ASALH) selects the annual theme, and we could not confirm a 2027 theme on ASALH's own website as of Nov. 4, 2026.

Below: the dates for the month, last year's theme, where the observance comes from and a free printable with a box for every day.

## What is the Black History Month 2027 theme?

It has not been confirmed. ASALH's theme pages were not readable to us, and we do not print wording from unofficial sites. We will add the theme once ASALH's own site shows it. ASALH's February 2027 events had not been posted where we could read them either.

For reference, the 2026 theme was "A Century of Black History Commemorations," according to GovInfo, the U.S. Government Publishing Office, in its "Black History Month 2026" feature published Feb. 11, 2026. The same page says ASALH selected that theme. That is last year's theme, not this year's.

## Black History Month 2027 dates by weekday

February 2027 has exactly four full weeks, so the grid below is complete with no partial week.

| Date | Weekday | What it is | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feb. 1, 2027 | Monday | First day of February, the month the federal portal names | blackhistorymonth.gov |

The NPS "Rosa Parks" page gives "Date of birth: February 4, 1913" and "Place of birth: Tuskegee, AL." OPM's 2027 schedule lists "Monday, February 15" for Washington's Birthday; see our [Presidents Day 2027 story](/news/presidents-day-2027-february-15-whats-open-closed/) for what is open and closed that day. We have not listed other February observances because we could not confirm them on an official page.

## Where did Black History Month come from?

GovInfo credits "Carter G. Woodson's creation of Negro History Week in 1926" and says that "in 1976, Negro History Week was extended to a month-long celebration to coincide with the nation's bicentennial."

On Feb. 10, 1976, President Gerald Ford issued a message on the observance, preserved by the American Presidency Project at the University of California, Santa Barbara. In it he wrote, "I urge my fellow citizens to join me in tribute to Black History Month," and noted that "Dr. Carter G. Woodson founded the Association for the Study of Afro-American Life and History." That organization is the one now called ASALH.
