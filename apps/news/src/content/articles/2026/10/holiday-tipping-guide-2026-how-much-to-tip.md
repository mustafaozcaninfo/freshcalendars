The Emily Post Institute's [Holiday Tipping Guide](https://emilypost.com/advice/holiday-tipping-guide) suggests $15 to $80 for a doorman and $20 to $80 for a building superintendent, while the U.S. Postal Service (USPS) lets a mail carrier accept only a non-cash gift worth $20 or less. This guide puts the Emily Post amounts in one table, explains the USPS limits, and offers a printable checklist with tick boxes to use before [Christmas Day](/when-is/christmas-day/).

Emily Post's own framing matters: "These are not rules." The amounts are given "as a suggestion only," and the guide says "averages and ranges can vary based on the type of establishment, regional customs, and your own budget." It adds that "Tipping averages tend to be higher in larger cities" and that you "shouldn't feel obligated to go beyond your personal budget." Every figure below is Emily Post's suggestion, not an average and not a requirement.

## How much does Emily Post suggest tipping building and outdoor staff?

| Provider | Emily Post suggestion |
|---|---|
| Superintendent | $20-$80 or a gift |
| Doorman | $15-$80; $15 or more each for multiple doormen, or a gift |
| Elevator operator | $15-$40 each |
| Garage attendants | $10-$30 or a small gift |
| Handyman | $15-$40 |
| Trash and recycling collectors | $10-$30 each |
| Yard or garden worker | $20-$50 each |
| Pool cleaner | Up to the cost of one cleaning, split among the crew |
| Newspaper delivery person | $10-$30 or a small gift |

## What does Emily Post suggest for home help, child care and personal services?

| Provider | Emily Post suggestion |
|---|---|
| Au pair or live-in nanny | Up to one week's pay and a gift from your child(ren) |
| Regular babysitter | Up to one evening's pay and a small gift from your child(ren) |
| Day care provider | A gift from you or $25-$70 for each staff member who works with your child(ren), and a small gift from your child(ren) |
| Housekeeper or cleaner | Up to one week's pay and/or a small gift |
| Live-in help | Up to one week to one month of pay plus a gift |
| Personal caregiver | Up to one week to one month's salary or a gift |
| Private nurse | A thoughtful gift |
| Home health employees | A thoughtful gift, if not against company policy |
| Nursing home employees | A gift the staff can share, such as flowers or food |
| Barber | Up to the cost of one haircut or a gift |
| Beauty salon staff | Up to the cost of one salon visit, divided among the staff who work with you |
| Personal trainer, massage therapist, pet groomer | Up to the cost of one session or a gift |
| Dog walker | Up to one week's pay or a gift |

Emily Post also says any gift or tip should come with "a short handwritten note of appreciation."

## What are the USPS rules for tipping a mail carrier?

Emily Post lists a mail carrier as "small gift only" and points to USPS rules. The [USPS ethics page for current employees](https://about.usps.com/who/legal/ethics/current-employees.htm) says an employee "may accept a non-cash gift with a market value of $20 or less per occasion from a customer" but "not more than $50 worth of gifts from that same customer in a calendar year."

Accepting "cash, including cash equivalents" is prohibited, and USPS gives Visa, Mastercard and American Express gift cards as examples. A [USPS Employee News reminder from Dec. 3, 2024](https://news.usps.com/2024/12/03/as-the-holidays-near-heres-a-reminder-about-gifts/) repeats the point: "Employees may not accept cash, checks, money orders or cash equivalents."

Emily Post's USPS section adds that snacks and beverages or perishable gifts that are not part of a meal are allowed, and small gifts should be "clearly no more than $20 in value." Perishable gifts worth more "must be shared with the entire branch." If you are also mailing presents, see the [USPS holiday shipping deadlines for 2026](/news/usps-holiday-shipping-deadlines-2026/).

## Can you tip a package deliverer?

Emily Post says to give a package deliverer a small gift only, no cash, and only if you receive regular deliveries: "Small gift in the $20 range. Most delivery companies discourage or prohibit cash gifts." We could not confirm the current gift rules for UPS or FedEx drivers, so check with the carrier before you give anything.

## What about teachers, tutors and coaches?

Emily Post's holiday tipping guide has no entry for teachers, tutors or coaches, so there is no suggested amount to quote here. School and district gift rules vary, so check with the school before giving. We did not confirm a specific district rule.
