Clocks went back one hour at 2 a.m. local time on **Sunday, Nov. 1, 2026**, in Ontario's Eastern Time zone, Quebec and Atlantic Canada, but British Columbia (BC), Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Yukon and the Northwest Territories (NWT) did not change, according to their governments. For BC, Alberta, Manitoba and the NWT it was the first fall without the change. Our table below lists every province and territory, and a second table shows what that does to the time differences across Canada this winter.

A one-page [printable list of the Nov. 1 changes](/news/downloads/canada-time-2026/canada-clock-changes-nov-1-2026.pdf) is on this page for the fridge or the office wall.

## Which provinces changed clocks on Nov. 1, 2026?

Times are given in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) offsets, so a reader can compare any two places. "UTC-7" means seven hours behind UTC.

| Province or territory | Clocks change Nov. 1? | Time from Nov. 1 | UTC offset | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| British Columbia | No | Pacific time, kept at daylight saving time (DST) all year | UTC-7 | BC Ministry of Attorney General, March 2, 2026 |
| Yukon | No | Same time all year since 2020 | UTC-7 | Manitoba release of Sept. 17, 2026 lists Yukon with permanent time |
| Alberta | No | Alberta Time, all year | UTC-6 | Government of Alberta |
| Saskatchewan | No | Central Standard Time (CST), all year | UTC-6 | Government of Saskatchewan |
| Northwest Territories | No | Northwest Territories Time, all year | UTC-6 | Government of the NWT, Sept. 23, 2026 |
| Manitoba | No | Stays on daylight time | UTC-5 | Province of Manitoba, Sept. 17, 2026 |
| Northwestern Ontario (for example Kenora) | Reported no | Stays on daylight time, reported | UTC-5 | CP24 / Canadian Press, Oct. 13, 2026 |
| Ontario (Eastern Time zone) | Yes, 2 a.m. | Eastern Standard Time (EST) | UTC-5 | No change announced |
| Quebec | Yes, 2 a.m. | Eastern Standard Time | UTC-5 | No change announced |
| New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island | Yes, 2 a.m. | Atlantic Standard Time | UTC-4 | No change announced |
| Newfoundland and Labrador | Yes, 2 a.m. | Newfoundland Standard Time (Labrador mostly Atlantic Standard Time, UTC-4) | UTC-3:30 | No change announced |
| Nunavut | Yes, in most of the territory | Three zones, including Eastern Standard Time and Central Standard Time | Varies | No change announced |

Where the last column says "No change announced", the provincial governments have said nothing about ending the clock change, so clocks went back as usual.

## What each government says

**British Columbia.** A March 2, 2026, release from the Ministry of Attorney General called the March 8 spring-forward "the final time change in British Columbia." It adds: "On Nov. 1, 2026, when previously clocks would have been turned back an hour, no change will be made, and the adjustment will not be required." Pacific time "will be set seven hours behind co-ordinated universal time (UTC-7), matching the current offset used during daylight saving time."

**Alberta.** The Government of Alberta says the province "officially moves to Alberta Time on November 1, 2026." Alberta Time, "previously called Mountain Daylight Time or MDT in Alberta," is equivalent to UTC-6. "Beginning in November 2026, clocks will no longer reset to Mountain Standard Time (MST), and Alberta will remain on Alberta Time year-round."

**Saskatchewan.** Under The Time Act, 2026, "All of Saskatchewan observes Central Standard Time (CST), or Universal Coordinated Time (UTC) -6, year-round," and there are "currently no time option areas." Until 2026, Lloydminster and the surrounding area followed Mountain Standard Time (UTC-7) from November to March to stay aligned with Alberta. With Alberta now at UTC-6, Saskatchewan, Alberta and the Northwest Territories share one time all year.

**Manitoba.** On Sept. 17, 2026, Premier Wab Kinew announced that Manitoba "has decided to remain on daylight saving time (DST) permanently," meaning "clocks will not revert to standard time on Nov. 1." The province said 92 per cent of Manitobans surveyed wanted an end to seasonal changes; 58 per cent preferred permanent DST and 34 per cent preferred staying on standard time. Manitoba stays on Central Daylight Time, UTC-5.

**Northwest Territories.** Regulations creating Northwest Territories Time (UTC-06:00) came into force on Aug. 21, 2026. The Sept. 23 release says that on Nov. 1, "when clocks would normally be turned back by one hour, no time change will occur in the NWT."

**Yukon.** Yukon has kept UTC-7 all year since 2020.

## What about eastern British Columbia and northwestern Ontario?

The BC release says mountain-time communities in eastern BC "will not be affected by these changes." Dawson Creek observes Mountain Standard Time (MST) year-round and will share the time of most of BC in winter and summer. Cranbrook, which switches between standard and daylight time, was described as aligned with the rest of the province in winter. Because Alberta itself is moving to UTC-6 all year, communities on mountain time in eastern BC should check with their municipality.

In northwestern Ontario, the Canadian Press reported on Oct. 13, 2026, that the Ontario government said it intends to keep communities that follow Central Time, such as Kenora, on daylight time to stay aligned with Manitoba, with changes in place by Nov. 1. We could not find Ontario's own announcement, so treat this as reported and check with your municipality. Ontario has announced no change for the Eastern Time zone, so Toronto still went back.

## How big is the time difference across Canada this winter?

The gaps changed on Nov. 1 because some places moved and others did not. This table compares each city with Toronto, which was on Eastern Daylight Time (UTC-4) until Oct. 31 and is on Eastern Standard Time (UTC-5) now. U.S. clocks also went back at 2 a.m. local time on Nov. 1, as our [report on the U.S. change](/news/daylight-saving-time-ends-november-1-2026/) explains.

| City | UTC offset from Nov. 1 | Behind or ahead of Toronto until Oct. 31 | Behind or ahead of Toronto from Nov. 1 | Same time as, this winter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vancouver | UTC-7 | 3 hours behind | 2 hours behind | Denver (Mountain Standard Time) |
| Calgary | UTC-6 | 2 hours behind | 1 hour behind | Chicago (CST) |
| Regina | UTC-6 | 2 hours behind | 1 hour behind | Chicago (CST) |
| Winnipeg | UTC-5 | 1 hour behind | Same time | New York (EST) |
| Toronto | UTC-5 | Not applicable | Not applicable | New York (EST) |
| Halifax | UTC-4 | 1 hour ahead | 1 hour ahead | One hour ahead of New York |
| St. John's | UTC-3:30 | 1.5 hours ahead | 1.5 hours ahead | One and a half hours ahead of New York |

Computed from the offsets above. Vancouver at UTC-7 is one hour ahead of Seattle on Pacific Standard Time (PST, UTC-8) and one hour behind Calgary.

## When do clocks go forward next?

For regions that still change clocks, the next change is on **Sunday, March 14, 2027**, the second Sunday in March.
