**To view your sample ballot for Election Day, Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, start with your state's official voter lookup page or the National Association of Secretaries of State (NASS) "Can I Vote" tool.** NASS says the tool "links directly to state election websites and trusted resources" and "does not capture any information." Below: the lookup page for 33 states and Washington, D.C. that loaded when we checked, what to do in the 17 states that did not, and a free printable checklist.

## Where do I vote on Nov. 3? Find your polling place

Most of the state voter portals linked in the table below also show your polling place on the same page as your registration. If your state is not one of the four below, open its link in that table, or use the [NASS Can I Vote tool](https://www.nass.org/can-i-vote). These four states have an official polling-place page, confirmed Oct. 28, 2026:

| State | Official polling-place lookup | What the page says |
|---|---|---|
| California | [My Voter Status](https://voterstatus.sos.ca.gov/), California Secretary of State | Lists "Find your polling place." |
| Pennsylvania | [Polling Place Information](https://www.pavoterservices.pa.gov/pages/pollingplaceinfo.aspx), Pennsylvania Department of State | "The Polling Place Search tool will help you locate a polling place for Election day." |
| North Carolina | [Election Day Polling Place Search](https://vt.ncsbe.gov/PPLkup/), North Carolina State Board of Elections | Search by address or precinct. |
| Virginia | [Virginia Voter Information Citizen Portal](https://vote.elections.virginia.gov/), Virginia Department of Elections | Lets you view your polling place. |

On Election Day you vote at the polling place assigned to your registered address. Early voting sites can differ from Election Day sites, so confirm which kind of site your lookup shows. If your name is not on the list when you arrive, ask for a provisional ballot. Check your state's election site for the details.

## Can I find a sample ballot by ZIP code?

In most states, no. A sample ballot depends on your exact address and precinct, so most state tools ask for your name and date of birth or address. A ZIP code alone does not tell an election office which ballot you get. Our [Texas sample ballot story](/news/sample-ballot-by-zip-code-2026-texas-ballot/) has a ZIP code finder and county links for that state.

North Carolina's State Board of Elections says you can find your sample ballot "by entering your first and last name in the Voter Search." It adds that "sample ballots for each election are available only after they have been finalized." If your ballot is not posted yet, check back later.

## How do I use the NASS Can I Vote tool?

Go to the [NASS Can I Vote page](https://www.nass.org/can-i-vote) (canivote.org redirects there). On its [polling place page](https://www.nass.org/can-i-vote/find-your-polling-place), NASS says to "select your place of residence in the drop down menu." It says many states offer online services that can help you look up your local voting site, and others list contact information for local election officials.

## What if my state is not in the table?

For these states, the lookup page blocked our automated check or has moved: Alaska, Arizona, Georgia, Iowa, Kentucky, Maine, Michigan, Missouri, New Hampshire, New Mexico, New York, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Texas and Wisconsin. Use the [NASS Can I Vote tool](https://www.nass.org/can-i-vote) to reach your state's election website, then your state election office.

Texas has its own story: see [sample ballot by ZIP code in Texas](/news/sample-ballot-by-zip-code-2026-texas-ballot/). We do not list a link we could not confirm.

## What should I do with my sample ballot?

Read each race and measure, and write your choices on the sample ballot. Then check polling hours and your state's ID rules on its election site, and check whether your state allows you to bring a marked sample ballot to vote.
