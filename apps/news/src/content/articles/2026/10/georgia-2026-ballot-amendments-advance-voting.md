**Georgia voters elect a U.S. senator, a governor and six other statewide officials on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, and decide three constitutional amendments.** Advance in-person voting ran Oct. 13 through Friday, Oct. 30 and has ended; remaining voters cast ballots at their assigned polling place on Election Day. Because Georgia requires candidates to win a majority, some races could continue to a runoff on Tuesday, Dec. 1.

## Offices on the ballot

The Georgia Secretary of State's list of federal and state offices up in 2026, published Jan. 29, includes:

| Office | Term |
|---|---|
| U.S. senator | Six years |
| Governor | Four years |
| Lieutenant governor | Four years |
| Secretary of state | Four years |
| Insurance and fire safety commissioner | Four years |
| Labor commissioner | Four years |
| Agriculture commissioner | Four years |
| State school superintendent | Four years |
| U.S. House | All 14 districts |
| State Senate and State House | All 56 and all 180 seats |

Gov. Brian Kemp is term-limited, so the governor's race is open. Georgia's Supreme Court and Court of Appeals seats are filled in nonpartisan elections held with the spring primary rather than in November. Your sample ballot, available on the state's My Voter Page, lists every certified candidate and your local races.

## The three amendments

All three were placed on the ballot by the General Assembly with a two-thirds vote in each chamber and need a simple majority of voters to pass. News outlets have numbered them differently, so match them by subject on your sample ballot.

**Farmland and timberland.** Raises the cap on how much land one owner can enroll in the conservation-use property tax program, from 2,000 acres to 4,000 acres. Yes: raise the limit. No: keep 2,000 acres.

**Probate judges.** Requires all probate court judges to be chosen in nonpartisan elections, so candidates would no longer run under a party label. Yes: make the races nonpartisan. No: counties keep their current method.

**Next Generation 9-1-1.** Lets the General Assembly create a dedicated fund for 9-1-1 systems, with the money restricted to expanding, maintaining and operating emergency response. Yes: allow the fund. No: no dedicated fund.

## Key dates

| Date | What |
|---|---|
| Oct. 5 | Registration deadline (passed) |
| Tue., Oct. 13-Fri., Oct. 30 | Advance in-person voting, including two Saturdays; some counties add a Sunday |
| Fri., Oct. 23 | Last day to request an absentee ballot (passed) |
| Tue., Nov. 3, 7 a.m.-7 p.m. | Election Day; absentee ballots must be received by 7 p.m. |
| Nov. 23-25 | Early voting for any runoff, shortened for Thanksgiving |
| Tue., Dec. 1 | General election runoff, if needed |

During advance voting you can vote at any site in your county. On Election Day you must vote at your assigned polling place. Absentee ballots must be received, not just postmarked, by 7 p.m. on Nov. 3; see [mail ballot deadlines by state](/news/mail-ballot-deadlines-2026-by-state/).

## The runoff

If no candidate in a race wins more than 50% of the vote, the top two advance to a runoff on Dec. 1, with early voting Nov. 23-25. Georgia's Senate races went to runoffs in 2020 and 2022. Mark both dates on our [November](/november-calendars.html) and [December](/december-calendars.html) printable calendars.

## ID at the polls

Georgia requires photo ID to vote in person: a Georgia driver's license, a free voter ID card from your county registrar or the Department of Driver Services, a U.S. passport, a government employee ID, a U.S. military ID or a tribal photo ID. Absentee voters provide a driver's license or state ID number on their application.
