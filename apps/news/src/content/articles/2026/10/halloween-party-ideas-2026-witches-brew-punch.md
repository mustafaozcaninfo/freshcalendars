## Witches' brew punch recipe

Makes about 13 cups, enough for 10 to 12 servings. Prep takes 10 minutes. Chill it for 1 to 2 hours for the best flavor.

**Ingredients**

- 4 cups (960 milliliters) cranberry or cranberry-raspberry juice, chilled
- 4 cups (960 milliliters) apple juice, chilled
- 2 cups (480 milliliters) pineapple juice, chilled
- 2 tablespoons fresh lemon or lime juice
- 3 cups (720 milliliters) chilled lemon-lime soda or sparkling water, added just before serving
- 1 orange, thinly sliced
- 1 cup frozen raspberries or blackberries
- Ice
- Optional garnish: gummy worms or frozen berry "eyeballs"

Check labels for allergens and dietary needs. For a less sweet punch, swap some or all of the soda for sparkling water. Keep non-food decorations out of the drink.

**Steps**

1. Chill the juices and soda in the refrigerator.
2. In a clean punch bowl or large food-safe container, combine the cranberry, apple, pineapple and lemon or lime juices.
3. Add the orange slices and frozen berries, then refrigerate until the party.
4. Just before serving, slowly stir in the soda or sparkling water to keep the fizz.
5. Add ice to each cup rather than the bowl, so the punch does not turn watery, then ladle and garnish.

You can mix the juices up to one day ahead. Keep them covered in the refrigerator and add the soda at the last minute. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) advises serving pasteurized juice or cider, because unpasteurized products can carry bacteria such as Salmonella. It also says perishable food should not sit out of the refrigerator for more than two hours, or one hour when the air is above 90°F.

## A bubbling cauldron without dry ice

The simplest safe option leaves the bubbles out of the drink entirely.

- Put the punch in a clean, food-safe black bowl.
- Set that bowl inside a larger decorative container, with a gap between them.
- Use green or purple battery-powered lights, paper flames or fabric "smoke" around the outside.
- If you add a small fog machine, keep its fluid and output away from the food and follow the maker's instructions.

Battery-powered lights are also the safer choice near costumes and paper decorations. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) has advised, in general Halloween guidance from 2022, using battery-operated lights or glow sticks rather than open-flame candles.

## Dry ice and punch: what the safety sources say

Dry ice makes dramatic fog, and that is why people search for it each October. It is also a real hazard. Never put dry ice in drinking cups, and never serve punch while pieces may remain in the liquid.

- The FDA's Food Code interpretation on liquid nitrogen and dry ice says dry ice should not be eaten or drunk or touch exposed skin, and that it should be fully sublimated (turned to gas) before a drink is served.
- The Illinois Poison Center says contact can cause frostbite-type injuries and burns. It also warns against using or carrying dry ice in a poorly ventilated space, because it releases carbon dioxide, and against placing it directly in drinking cups.
- America's Poison Centers says touching or swallowing dry ice can cause serious burns like frostbite, and advises using it only in well-ventilated areas.

For a family party we recommend the no-dry-ice setup above. If you have a question about an exposure, the poison help line is 1-800-222-1222.

## Three easy variations

**Green Monster Punch.** Replace 2 cups of cranberry juice with green fruit punch or another green drink. Add lime slices and green grapes.

**Vampire Berry Punch.** Use cranberry-raspberry juice as the main juice, add frozen raspberries and serve in clear cups with a red berry garnish.

**Midnight Grape Punch.** Combine chilled purple grape juice, apple juice and sparkling water. Orange slices or dark berries deepen the color without food coloring.

Check labels and use only food-safe garnishes in each version.

## Drink safety for hosts

- Use pasteurized juice or cider.
- Keep perishable food chilled and out of the fridge no more than two hours.
- Keep small decorations, skewers and non-food items out of the punch, where children could swallow them.
- Label common allergens and offer water as well as sweet drinks.
- Keep dry ice away from children, pets, bare skin and closed rooms, and out of cups.
- Use battery-powered lights instead of open flames near costumes and paper decorations.
