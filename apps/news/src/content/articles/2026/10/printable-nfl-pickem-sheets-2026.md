## Which pick'em sheet should you print?

All sheets are original designs with team names in plain text and no logos. Pick the format that matches how your group plays.

| Format | What players fill in | Scoring | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classic pick'em sheet | A tick box for the away or home team in each game, the final score blanks and a tiebreaker | One point per correct pick is the simplest | Family, friends and one-sheet-per-person pools |
| Confidence pool sheet | A pick per game plus a point value from 1 to the number of games that week, each number used once, and a "Won?" box | A correct pick earns the points you gave it | Players who want to rank how sure they are |
| Office pool grid | One column per player (8 on US Letter, 7 on A4), tiebreaker and correct-picks rows | Count each player's correct picks at the bottom | One shared sheet on a break room wall |
| Survivor pool sheet | One team per week for Weeks 1 to 18, won and lost boxes, and a 32-team checklist with bye weeks | You stay in while your pick wins; each team once | Season-long pools with a single pick a week |
| Season standings tracker | Each player's points for every week and a season total | Add the weekly scores; highest total wins | Keeping a pool's season standings on one page |

The number of games changes from week to week because of byes, so the confidence scale changes too: a 13-game week runs from 1 to 13, a 16-game week from 1 to 16. The sheets print the right range for each week.

## How do you run an NFL pick'em pool on paper?

1. Print one classic or confidence sheet per player, or one office pool grid for the group.
2. Hand the sheets out early in the week and set the deadline at the week's first kickoff, usually Thursday at 8:15 p.m. ET (Wednesday, Nov. 25 in Thanksgiving week). In weeks with an international game, the first Sunday kickoff can be as early as 9:30 a.m. ET, so check the table above.
3. Collect the sheets before the deadline. On confidence sheets, check that each number is used once.
4. After each game, tick the winner and write the final score. The table above fills in final scores as games end.
5. Add up correct picks, or confidence points, after the last game of the week.
6. Break a tie with the closest guess of total points in the tiebreaker game, and agree on that rule before play starts.
7. Copy each player's weekly score onto the season standings tracker.

### Scoring rules groups use

- **One point a win:** the simplest rule; most picks correct wins the week.
- **Confidence points:** a correct pick scores the number you assigned, so a week's maximum is the sum of 1 to the number of games (91 in a 13-game week, 136 in a 16-game week, by our count).
- **Survivor:** no points, only survival. Decide before Week 1 what happens if everyone is out in the same week; many pools let those players share the win.
- **Season total:** add weekly scores on the tracker. Some pools drop each player's lowest week; write your rule in the "Scoring" line.

## Letter or A4?

Print the US Letter version on US Letter paper and the A4 version on A4 paper. Set the print dialog to Actual size or 100%, not Fit, so the tick boxes and score blanks stay full size. The office pool grid has one column fewer on A4 (7 players instead of 8). The standings tracker prints in landscape. For a calendar to pin beside the sheets, see the [November 2026 printable calendar](/november-calendars.html), the [December 2026 calendar](/december-calendars.html) or the [printable weekly calendar](/printable-weekly-calendar.html). ## Where the times on the sheets come from

Games, kickoff times and networks come from the NFL schedule. Before each update we compare every game with the 32 club schedule pages and ESPN's schedule: a kickoff change goes on the sheets only when a club site confirms it, and the change is noted in our schedule records. Final scores in the table come from ESPN after each game. Times can still change, so check NFL.com on game day. Fresh Calendars is not affiliated with the NFL.
