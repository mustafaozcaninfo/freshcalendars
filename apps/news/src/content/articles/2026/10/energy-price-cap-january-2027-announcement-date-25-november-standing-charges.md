**The Office of Gas and Electricity Markets (Ofgem) announced on 25 November 2026 that the energy price cap for 1 January to 31 March 2027 is £2,166 a year**, up 26% on the previous period, according to Ofgem. Below are the new unit rates and standing charges next to the current ones, what the standing charges cost over a year (our calculation), the next announcement dates and a one-page printable.

## What is the energy price cap for January 2027?

Ofgem's unit rates page says: "From 1 January to 31 March 2027, the price cap will be £2,166 per year. This has gone up by 26% compared to the last price cap period." Ofgem's announcement says: "From 1 January to 31 March 2027 energy prices will go up by 26% for a typical household who use electricity and gas and pay by Direct Debit."

£2,166 minus the £1,723 cap for October to December 2026 is £443 a year more (our calculation).

## What are the new unit rates and standing charges?

The figures below are Direct Debit averages across England, Scotland and Wales (Great Britain).

| Direct Debit average | 1 Oct. to 31 Dec. 2026 | 1 Jan. to 31 Mar. 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Electricity unit rate | 26.32p per kilowatt hour (kWh) | 32.69p per kWh |
| Electricity standing charge | 54.83p per day | 54.77p per day |
| Gas unit rate | 7.97p per kWh | 10.96p per kWh |
| Gas standing charge | 29.68p per day | 29.65p per day |

Source: Ofgem, "Energy price cap unit rates and standing charges" and the 25 November 2026 announcement. Figures are rounded to two decimal places.

Both periods are on the same tax basis, so the columns can be compared line by line. Ofgem says the government has removed value-added tax (VAT) from electricity bills for the price cap period from 1 October 2026 to 30 June 2027, while gas includes VAT at 5%.

The UK government "has also announced that around 20 million households will receive £100 towards their energy bill from January", according to Ofgem.

## When is the next energy price cap announcement?

Ofgem sets the cap every 3 months.

| Announced by | Weekday | Cap period it sets |
|---|---|---|
| 25 November 2026 (published) | Wednesday | 1 January 2027 to 31 March 2027 |
| 23 February 2027 | Tuesday | 1 April 2027 to 30 June 2027 |
| 26 May 2027 | Wednesday | 1 July 2027 to 30 September 2027 |

Source: Ofgem, 25 November 2026 (it says the April to June levels will be published "by 23 February 2027"); the 26 May 2027 date is from Ofgem's 26 August 2026 page. Weekdays are computed.

## How much is the standing charge over a year?

A standing charge is a fixed daily amount you pay whether or not you use any energy. Our calculation over 365 days:

| Fuel | Oct. to Dec. 2026 (daily; yearly) | Jan. to Mar. 2027 (daily; yearly) |
|---|---|---|
| Electricity | 54.83p; £200.13 | 54.77p; £199.91 |
| Gas | 29.68p; £108.33 | 29.65p; £108.22 |
| Both | 84.51p; £308.46 | 84.42p; £308.13 |

Computed from Ofgem's Direct Debit averages; a real bill depends on your supplier, region and tariff, and rates change each period.

## Are gas standing charges being scrapped?

Yes, but not yet. On 28 October 2026, Budget day, HM Treasury announced that "Tens of millions of households will no longer pay standing charges on their gas bills." The Chancellor "will ask Ofgem to deliver removal of these charges – which are £108 a year for the typical household on average – in 2027 or as soon as possible after."

That £108 matches the 365-day figure above (£108.33 at 29.68p a day). The Treasury says the fixed costs will move onto the unit rate instead, "which could save 60% of account-holding households around an average of £45 off their annual bill." Households that use more gas could pay more per unit, and the Treasury says it will set out "targeted mitigation for a small number of households" later.

Ofgem's 25 November 2026 announcement says: "The UK government has asked to remove standing charges from gas bills in 2027 or as soon as possible afterwards. We are working on options to make this change and will share details at a later stage." The 29.65p gas standing charge applies from 1 January 2027. The electricity standing charge is not part of this change.

## Is there a lower standing charge tariff?

Ofgem is running a pilot. Its page on lower standing charge tariffs, updated 22 June 2026, says: "we are launching a one-year, lower standing charge tariff pilot starting from June 2026." Ofgem is "encouraging more suppliers to participate", so it is voluntary. It also warns: "The pilot tariffs may not mean that the energy bills of those who take part will be cheaper than other tariffs currently available."

The pilot replaced an earlier 2025 plan to require such tariffs by January 2026.
