<p class="callout"><b>Last updated 28 October 2026, 8:38 a.m. ET.</b> Ahead of today's Budget, HM Treasury said gas standing charges will be scrapped, with Ofgem asked to remove them "in 2027 or as soon as possible after". No start date is set. Details are in the new section below.</p>

**Ofgem will announce the energy price cap for 1 January to 31 March 2027 by Wednesday, 25 November 2026**, according to the Office of Gas and Electricity Markets (Ofgem). The level is not yet announced, and this story does not forecast it. Ofgem also says it may publish before that date "if we need to because of external reasons". Below are all three upcoming announcement dates in one table, what the standing charge costs over a year (our calculation) and a one-page printable.

## When is the next energy price cap announcement?

Ofgem sets the cap every 3 months. On its page about the 1 October to 31 December 2026 cap, it lists when the next levels will be announced.

| Announced by | Weekday | Cap period it sets |
|---|---|---|
| 25 November 2026 | Wednesday | 1 January 2027 to 31 March 2027 |
| 23 February 2027 | Tuesday | 1 April 2027 to 30 June 2027 |
| 26 May 2027 | Wednesday | 1 July 2027 to 30 September 2027 |

Source: Ofgem, "Changes to energy price cap between 1 October and 31 December 2026", published 26 August 2026. Weekdays are our calculation.

The UK Budget is today, Wednesday, 28 October 2026. Any energy measures in it are not covered here; see our [UK Budget 2026 date and time story](/news/uk-budget-2026-date-wednesday-28-october-what-time/).

## What is the cap now, from October to December 2026?

Ofgem says that from 1 October to 31 December 2026, "energy prices will go up by 4% for a typical household who use electricity and gas and pay by Direct Debit." The figures below are averages across England, Scotland and Wales (Great Britain) for Direct Debit customers, with last year's January to March 2026 figures for reference.

| Direct Debit average | 1 Oct. to 31 Dec. 2026 | 1 Jan. to 31 Mar. 2026 (last year) |
|---|---|---|
| Electricity unit rate | 26.32p per kilowatt hour (kWh) | 27.69p per kWh |
| Electricity standing charge | 54.83p per day | 54.75p per day |
| Gas unit rate | 7.97p per kWh | 5.93p per kWh |
| Gas standing charge | 29.68p per day | 35.09p per day |

Sources: Ofgem, 26 August 2026 (current period); Ofgem, 21 November 2025 (last year's period, when the typical dual-fuel Direct Debit bill was £1,758 a year, up 0.2%).

Do not compare the two columns line by line. The government has removed value-added tax (VAT) from electricity bills from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027, so the current electricity figures exclude VAT, while gas includes VAT at 5%. Ofgem says costs cannot be compared directly with previous periods because of this change. Ofgem's page does not give a typical annual bill for October to December 2026, so we do not give one.

## How much is the standing charge over a year?

A standing charge is a fixed daily amount you pay whether or not you use any energy. At the October to December 2026 rates, our calculation over 365 days is:

| Fuel | Daily standing charge | Over 365 days |
|---|---|---|
| Electricity | 54.83p | £200.13 |
| Gas | 29.68p | £108.33 |
| Both | 84.51p | £308.46 |

Our calculation from Ofgem's Direct Debit averages; a real bill depends on your supplier, region and tariff, and rates change each period.

## Are gas standing charges being scrapped?

Yes, but not yet. On 28 October 2026, Budget day, HM Treasury announced that "Tens of millions of households will no longer pay standing charges on their gas bills." The Chancellor "will ask Ofgem to deliver removal of these charges – which are £108 a year for the typical household on average – in 2027 or as soon as possible after."

That £108 matches our 365-day figure above (£108.33 at 29.68p a day). The Treasury says the fixed costs will move onto the unit rate instead, "which could save 60% of account-holding households around an average of £45 off their annual bill." Households that use more gas could pay more per unit, and the Treasury says it will set out "targeted mitigation for a small number of households" later.

No date has been announced. Ofgem has not yet said whether the 1 January 2027 cap or a later one will be the first without a gas standing charge, so check the 25 November announcement. The electricity standing charge is not part of this change.

## Is there a lower standing charge tariff?

Ofgem is running a pilot. Its page on lower standing charge tariffs, updated 22 June 2026, says: "we are launching a one-year, lower standing charge tariff pilot starting from June 2026." Ofgem is "encouraging more suppliers to participate", so it is voluntary. It also warns: "The pilot tariffs may not mean that the energy bills of those who take part will be cheaper than other tariffs currently available."

The pilot replaced an earlier 2025 plan to require such tariffs by January 2026.

The dates that matter are 25 November 2026 (announcement), 1 January 2027 (new cap starts) and 31 March 2027, when the VAT removal on electricity bills ends.
