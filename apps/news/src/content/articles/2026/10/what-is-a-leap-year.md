A leap year has 366 days instead of 365, and the extra day is Feb. 29, according to NASA's calendar reference. The U.S. Naval Observatory (USNO) gives the Gregorian rule: "years evenly divisible by 4 are leap years, with the exception of centurial years that are not evenly divisible by 400." The next one is 2028, and the table below lists every leap year to 2100 with the weekday of its Feb. 29.

## How does the leap year rule work?

The test takes three steps, in this order:

1. Divide the year by 4. If it does not divide evenly, it is a common year with 365 days.
2. If it divides by 4, divide it by 100. If it does not divide evenly by 100, it is a leap year.
3. If it divides by 100, divide it by 400. The National Research Council Canada (NRC) says a year divisible evenly by 400 is a leap year, so 2000 is one. A year divisible by 100 but not by 400 is a normal year.

Stopping after step 1 gives wrong answers for century years. The NRC works the example: 1900 divided by 100 is 19, so 1900 is a normal year of 365 days. The USNO lists 1700, 1800, 1900 and 2100 as years that are common, and 1600, 2000 and 2400 as leap years.

| Year | Divisible by 4? | By 100? | By 400? | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | No | n/a | n/a | Common year, 365 days |
| 2024 | Yes | No | n/a | Leap year, 366 days |
| 1900 | Yes | Yes | No | Common year, 365 days |
| 2000 | Yes | Yes | Yes | Leap year, 366 days |
| 2100 | Yes | Yes | No | Common year, 365 days |
| 2400 | Yes | Yes | Yes | Leap year, 366 days |

Royal Museums Greenwich makes the same point: the year 2000 was a leap year, although 1900 was not. Our [one-page leap year test](#leap-year-printable) puts the three steps and these examples on a sheet.

## Why does the calendar need leap years?

The USNO says the cycle of the seasons, the tropical year, is approximately 365.2422 days. A calendar year has to be a whole number of days, so a plain 365-day year falls about 0.2422 day short each year. Four such years leave a gap of almost a full day (4 x 0.2422 = 0.9688), which is what one leap day makes up.

The Julian calendar added a leap day every fourth year, an average year of 365.25 days, the USNO says. That is slightly longer than the tropical year, and the difference adds up. NASA says 10 days were deleted in 1582, so that October 4 was followed by October 15. The Royal Observatory of Belgium gives Oct. 15, 1582, as the official date the Gregorian calendar began, and the USNO credits its introduction to a pope named Gregory.

The Gregorian rule drops three leap days every 400 years, which sets the average year at 365.2425 days, the USNO says. Subtracting the two USNO figures leaves 0.0003 day a year, so the Gregorian calendar drifts by one day only after a little over 3,000 years. That subtraction is our arithmetic, not a figure the USNO publishes.

## Which years are leap years from 2024 to 2100?

NASA's reference describes the Gregorian calendar as a cycle of 400 years with 146,097 days. We counted 97 leap years in that cycle, so 97 x 366 plus 303 x 365 equals 146,097. Because 146,097 is evenly divisible by 7, NASA says the calendar repeats exactly after 400 years: 20,871 whole weeks. For the shorter repeats inside a century, see [how often the calendar repeats](/news/how-often-does-the-calendar-repeat/).

## Is 2028 the next leap year?

Yes. Today is Oct. 30, 2026, so the next Feb. 29 is in 2028, on a Tuesday. February has 28 days in 2026 and 2027, and 29 in 2028. The following leap years are 2032, 2036 and 2040. If you plan across a leap day, the [day-of-year calculation](/news/how-to-calculate-day-of-year/) shifts by one from March 1 onward. ## Why does February get the extra day?

The Rice University Galileo Project says the additional day that came every fourth year was given to February "as being the shortest month." The NASA reference describes the leap day as an intercalary day, designated Feb. 29, that comes before Mar. 1. The Royal Observatory of Belgium says the Gregorian calendar adds, as in the Julian calendar, a leap day in February.

The Encyclopaedia Romana at the University of Chicago, which summarizes ancient authors, says Censorinus wrote that Januarius and Februarius were added to the Roman calendar with 28 days each. Why February has 28 days in the first place has its own story: [why February has 28 days](/news/why-does-february-have-28-days/).

## Which leap year mistakes are common?

- **Dividing by 4 and stopping.** 1900 and 2100 divide by 4 and are still common years.
- **Treating every century as common.** 2000 and 2400 divide by 400 and are leap years.
- **Expecting a longer year to change other months.** The leap day is the Feb. 29 that NASA describes; the year then has 366 days.
- **Applying the Gregorian rule to older dates.** Royal Museums Greenwich says the Julian calendar averaged 365.25 days a year, and that Britain and its colonies changed calendars in 1752, when 2 September was followed by 14 September.
