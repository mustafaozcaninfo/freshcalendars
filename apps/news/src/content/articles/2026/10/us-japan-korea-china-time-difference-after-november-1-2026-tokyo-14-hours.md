U.S. clocks fall back one hour at 2 a.m. local time on **Sunday, Nov. 1, 2026**, and from then New York is 14 hours behind Tokyo and Seoul and 13 hours behind Beijing, up from 13 and 12 hours. The rule comes from the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), and none of the three countries moves its clocks, so the whole change lands on the U.S. side. A one-page [printable U.S. to Japan, Korea and China time chart](/news/downloads/time-change-2026/us-japan-korea-china-time-chart-november-2026.pdf) is on this page for your desk.

The figures below are our calculation from the Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) offsets in the time zone database and NIST's dates. Japan Standard Time (JST) and Korea Standard Time (KST) are both UTC+9. Beijing Time, the China standard time, is UTC+8.

## Do Japan, South Korea and China change their clocks on Nov. 1, 2026?

No. The time zone database that phones and computers use, kept by the Internet Assigned Numbers Authority (IANA), lists Asia/Tokyo at UTC+9, Asia/Seoul at UTC+9 and Asia/Shanghai at UTC+8, and none has a current daylight saving time (DST) rule. Its rule lines show Japan last used DST from 1948 to 1951, South Korea in 1987 and 1988, and China from 1986 to 1991.

China officially uses one time zone nationwide, Beijing Time. The IANA file also notes Xinjiang time, which many people in western China use informally; this story uses Beijing Time.

The U.S. side does change. NIST says that "during 2026, daylight saving time is in effect from March 8 at 2 a.m. (local time) to November 1 at 2 a.m. (local time)." Eastern Time (ET) goes from Eastern Daylight Time (EDT, UTC-4) to Eastern Standard Time (EST, UTC-5), and the other zones move the same way. Our [report on the U.S. change](/news/daylight-saving-time-ends-november-1-2026/) covers the full rule.

## How far ahead of each U.S. zone are Tokyo, Seoul and Beijing?

| U.S. zone | UTC offset until Oct. 31 / from Nov. 1 | Tokyo and Seoul ahead until Oct. 31 | Tokyo and Seoul ahead from Nov. 1 | Beijing ahead until Oct. 31 | Beijing ahead from Nov. 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eastern (New York) | UTC-4 / UTC-5 | 13 hours | 14 hours | 12 hours | 13 hours |
| Central (Chicago) | UTC-5 / UTC-6 | 14 hours | 15 hours | 13 hours | 14 hours |
| Mountain (Denver) | UTC-6 / UTC-7 | 15 hours | 16 hours | 14 hours | 15 hours |
| Pacific (Los Angeles) | UTC-7 / UTC-8 | 16 hours | 17 hours | 15 hours | 16 hours |
| Alaska (Anchorage) | UTC-8 / UTC-9 | 17 hours | 18 hours | 16 hours | 17 hours |
| Arizona (Phoenix, Mountain Standard Time all year) | UTC-7 / UTC-7 | 16 hours | 16 hours | 15 hours | 15 hours |
| Hawaii (Honolulu) | UTC-10 / UTC-10 | 19 hours | 19 hours | 18 hours | 18 hours |

NIST says that "DST is not observed in Hawaii, American Samoa, Guam, Puerto Rico, the Virgin Islands and the state of Arizona" with the exception of the Navajo Indian Reservation, which does observe it. So Phoenix and Honolulu keep their gaps, while the Navajo Nation in Arizona follows the Nov. 1 change.

## What time is it in Tokyo, Seoul and Beijing when each U.S. zone changes?

Each U.S. zone changes at 2 a.m. on its own clock, so the moment arrives at a different hour in Asia. All of these fall on the afternoon or evening of Sunday, Nov. 1 in Tokyo, Seoul and Beijing (checked against the UTC offsets).

| U.S. zone, 2 a.m. daylight time on Nov. 1 | UTC | Tokyo and Seoul | Beijing |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York | 06:00 | 3 p.m. | 2 p.m. |
| Chicago | 07:00 | 4 p.m. | 3 p.m. |
| Denver | 08:00 | 5 p.m. | 4 p.m. |
| Los Angeles | 09:00 | 6 p.m. | 5 p.m. |
| Anchorage | 10:00 | 7 p.m. | 6 p.m. |

## Which call times work between the U.S. and East Asia from Nov. 1?

The grid starts from the Tokyo or Seoul clock and shows the same moment in Beijing and in three U.S. zones, valid from Nov. 1 until March 2027. "Day before" means the previous calendar day in the U.S.

| Tokyo or Seoul | Beijing | New York | Chicago | Los Angeles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 a.m. | 6 a.m. | 5 p.m., day before | 4 p.m., day before | 2 p.m., day before |
| 8 a.m. | 7 a.m. | 6 p.m., day before | 5 p.m., day before | 3 p.m., day before |
| 9 a.m. | 8 a.m. | 7 p.m., day before | 6 p.m., day before | 4 p.m., day before |
| 10 a.m. | 9 a.m. | 8 p.m., day before | 7 p.m., day before | 5 p.m., day before |
| 9 p.m. | 8 p.m. | 7 a.m., same day | 6 a.m., same day | 4 a.m., same day |
| 10 p.m. | 9 p.m. | 8 a.m., same day | 7 a.m., same day | 5 a.m., same day |
| 11 p.m. | 10 p.m. | 9 a.m., same day | 8 a.m., same day | 6 a.m., same day |

So 8 a.m. in Tokyo is 6 p.m. the evening before in New York and 3 p.m. in Los Angeles. Going the other way, 9 a.m. in New York is 11 p.m. in Tokyo and Seoul and 10 p.m. in Beijing on the same day.

## Does a standing call with Japan, South Korea or China move on Nov. 1?

On U.S. clocks, yes; on Asian clocks, no. A call fixed at 8 a.m. Tokyo time was at 7 p.m. in New York the day before until Oct. 31 and is at 6 p.m. from Nov. 1. A call fixed in New York time lands one hour later on the Tokyo clock instead: 9 a.m. in New York was 10 p.m. in Tokyo until Oct. 31 and is 11 p.m. from Nov. 1. Phones update on their own when the automatic time zone setting is on, but an invite saved in a fixed zone needs a manual check. For a country that also keeps one offset all year, see the [U.S.-Philippines time-difference story](/news/us-philippines-time-difference-after-november-1-2026-manila-13-hours/) and the [U.S.-India story](/news/us-india-time-difference-after-november-1-2026-ist-10-5-hours/).

## When do the gaps with Tokyo, Seoul and Beijing shrink again?

Under 15 U.S.C. 260a and the NIST rule that DST begins at 2 a.m. on the second Sunday of March, daylight saving time returns on Sunday, March 14, 2027 (our calculation). New York will then be 13 hours behind Tokyo and Seoul and 12 hours behind Beijing again.
