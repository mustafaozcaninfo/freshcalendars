Under the week numbering in the ISO 8601 date standard, a year has 53 weeks when it contains 53 Thursdays: that happens when Jan. 1 is a Thursday, or when it is a leap year that starts on a Wednesday. Google Docs Editors Help describes the system as weeks that begin on Monday and end on Sunday, with Week 1 as "the week containing the first Thursday of the year." This page works out what that means, lists every week 53 from 2004 to 2060, and shows how to check any year in a few seconds. 2026 is one of them.

Week counts per year are covered in [how many weeks are in a year](/news/how-many-weeks-in-a-year/). Here the question is narrower: why the count sometimes reaches 53.

## What a 53-week year is

A Gregorian common year has 365 days and a leap year has 366, according to NASA's calendar notes. Fifty-two full weeks make 364 days, so every year carries one or two days beyond 52 weeks (365 = 52 x 7 + 1, and 366 = 52 x 7 + 2). Those spare days pile up. When enough of them land the right way, the numbering needs a 53rd week.

The 53 weeks belong to an ISO week-year, which is a labeling system and not a calendar year. A 53-week ISO year is 371 days long (53 x 7). The ISO year 2026, for example, runs from Monday, Dec. 29, 2025 to Sunday, Jan. 3, 2027, which is 371 days by our count. Jan. 1, 2027 is a Friday, and it sits in week 53 of 2026 rather than in 2027.

## The ISO rule: Week 1 holds the first Thursday

Markus Kuhn's summary of ISO 8601, hosted by NASA, puts it this way: "Week 01 of a year is per definition the first week that has the Thursday in this year," a week starts with Monday, and "possible ISO week numbers are in the range 01 to 53." That Thursday rule is the same as saying Week 1 contains Jan. 4, which is a computed equivalent we checked for every year from 1900 to 2100.

The consequence is simple. A week belongs to the year that holds its Thursday. A year with 52 Thursdays gets 52 weeks, and a year with 53 Thursdays gets 53. Week 1 can start as early as Dec. 29 of the year before, and the last week can end as late as Jan. 3 of the next year.

## When a year has 53 ISO weeks

There are two cases, and both come down to the weekday of Jan. 1. The table sets them next to the common alternatives.

| Year type | Jan. 1 falls on | ISO weeks |
|---|---|---|
| Common (365 days) | Thursday | 53 |
| Common (365 days) | any other day | 52 |
| Leap (366 days) | Wednesday or Thursday | 53 |
| Leap (366 days) | any other day | 52 |

A common year that starts on Thursday also ends on a Thursday (Dec. 31), so it holds 53 of them. A leap year has one more day, so it needs only to start on Wednesday (ending Thursday) or on Thursday (with Thursday again on Dec. 30). Put briefly: a year has 53 weeks when Jan. 1 or Dec. 31 is a Thursday. We confirmed this by two independent methods across 1900 to 2100: counting the ISO week of Dec. 28 with date arithmetic, and applying the weekday rule from a separate day-of-week formula. They agreed for every year.

Between 2000 and 2060 there are 11 years with a week 53. The gap between them is 5 or 6 years in this range. Week 53 starts on a Monday, Dec. 27 or Dec. 28, and ends the following Sunday. All dates below are computed from the rule above.

## Why a leap year does not always have 53 weeks

A leap year is a Gregorian rule about days: the U.S. Naval Observatory says years evenly divisible by 4 are leap years, except centurial years not divisible by 400. A 53-week ISO year is a rule about Thursdays. The two overlap only when a leap year starts on a Wednesday or Thursday.

From 2000 to 2060 there are 16 leap years, and 5 of them have a week 53 (2004, 2020, 2032, 2048 and 2060). The other 11 have 52 weeks, including 2024, which started on a Monday. Of the 11 week 53 years in the table, 6 are common years. A 366th day neither guarantees nor is needed for a 53rd week.

## How to check whether a year has week 53

Use Dec. 28. It is a computed fact that Dec. 28 always lies in the last ISO week of its year, so its week number is the number of weeks that year has. Spreadsheet functions that follow the ISO method return it: Microsoft Support lists ISOWEEKNUM(date), and Google Docs has the same function. Build the date with the date function (DATE) and enter `=ISOWEEKNUM(DATE(2026,12,28))` and you get 53; for 2027 you get 52.

Worked examples, all computed:

- 2026: Dec. 28, 2026 is a Monday and starts week 53.
- 2027: Jan. 1, 2027 is a Friday, so 2027 has no week 53. Dec. 28, 2027 is a Tuesday in week 52, which runs Dec. 27, 2027 to Jan. 2, 2028.
- 2020: a leap year starting on a Wednesday, so Dec. 28, 2020 is a Monday in week 53.

Be careful with the plain `WEEKNUM` function. Microsoft Support documents return types: type 1 or omitted starts weeks on Sunday and counts the week with Jan. 1 as week 1, while type 21 uses Monday and the ISO method. A calendar app that shows Jan. 1 as week 1 is using the first method. Also, the year function (YEAR) gives the calendar year: Jan. 1, 2027 is in calendar year 2027 and ISO week 53 of 2026.

## Does 53 ISO weeks mean 53 pay periods or a 53-week fiscal year?

No, these are three different counts that happen to use the same number.

- **ISO week 53** is a week label. It depends only on the date and needs no employer decision.
- **A 52-53-week tax year** is a company choice. The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) describes it as "a fiscal tax year that varies from 52 to 53 weeks" and notes it does not have to end on the last day of a month. Apple's Form 10-K says its fiscal year "is the 52- or 53-week period that ends on the last Saturday of September," and ended Sept. 27, 2025 for fiscal 2025.
- **Retail 4-5-4 years** add the extra week on purpose. The National Retail Federation (NRF) says it is "sometimes necessary to add a 53rd week to the end of the calendar for sales reporting purposes only." Our [NRF 4-5-4 calendar story](/news/nrf-4-5-4-calendar-2027-fiscal-year-dates-53-week-year-2028-printable/) covers fiscal 2027 and 2028.

Paydays follow a different arithmetic. Because 365 = 52 x 7 + 1, one weekday occurs 53 times in a common year, and two weekdays do in a leap year. A weekly payday on that weekday can fall 53 times, and that depends on the pay schedule, not on the ISO year. The [three-paycheck months story](/news/three-paycheck-months-2027-biweekly-payday-calendar/) shows a biweekly Friday schedule. This page is not payroll advice; your employer's schedule decides.
