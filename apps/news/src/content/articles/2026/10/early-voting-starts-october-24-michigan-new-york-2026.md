<p class="callout"><b>Election Day is tomorrow, Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026.</b> Early voting in Michigan and New York ended Sunday, Nov. 1. New York registration closed Oct. 24; Michigan voters can still register in person at their clerk's office through Election Day.</p>

**Early in-person voting for the Nov. 3, 2026 general election began Saturday, Oct. 24, in Michigan and New York, and ran nine days through Sunday, Nov. 1.** It worked like Election Day: voters marked a paper ballot at a site and fed it into a scanner themselves. In Michigan, sites were open at least eight hours every day of the period. In New York, each county set its own hours, and New York City posted its schedule. Below: the dates, ID and registration rules in each state, and a free printable plan.

## Early voting dates in Michigan and New York

| | Michigan | New York |

| Find your polling place | Michigan.gov/Vote | voterlookup.elections.ny.gov |

Polling hours are local time. For every state's closing time in Eastern Time, see [what time polls close on Election Day](/news/what-time-do-polls-close-election-day-2026/).

## Michigan: at least eight hours a day of early voting

Michigan voters added early voting to the state constitution in November 2022, and the first statewide early voting was the 2024 presidential primary. For federal and statewide elections, the required period starts on the second Saturday before Election Day and ends on the Sunday before it. Communities may add days, starting as early as the 29th day before the election, and beginning in 2026 they may also open on the Monday before Election Day. East Lansing and Grand Rapids were among the communities that opened sites early this year, the Michigan Department of State said on Oct. 20.

Look up your Election Day polling place at Michigan.gov/Vote. In line at closing time? Stay there: you can still vote. Ballots cast at early voting sites were sealed every night and are counted with absentee and Election Day ballots after polls close at 8 p.m. on Nov. 3.

## Michigan: ID, registration and mail ballots

- **ID:** Bring a photo ID on Election Day. If you do not have one, you can still vote after signing an affidavit.
- **Registration:** Online and mail registration closed on Oct. 19. You can still register in person at your local clerk's office, with proof of residency, through 8 p.m. on Election Day. Early voting sites did not register voters unless the site was at a clerk's office.
- **Mail ballots:** Clerks must receive your absentee ballot by 8 p.m. on Nov. 3; a postmark does not count. The state recommends hand-delivering it to your clerk's office or assigned drop box, or bringing it to your early voting site.

By 7 a.m. on Oct. 20, 789,572 Michigan voters had already cast a ballot, 10.7% of active registered voters, nearly all of them by mail. Voters had requested 2,121,458 mail ballots, 17% more than at the same point before the 2022 midterm. For what is on the ballot, see our [Michigan ballot guide](/news/michigan-ballot-2026-proposals-candidates/) and [New York ballot guide](/news/new-york-ballot-2026-sample-ballot-governor-nyc-proposals/).

## New York: early voting hours were set by each county

Hours differed by county. Every county board of elections set early voting hours for its own sites, so a site in Buffalo and one in Albany could open and close at different times. Outside New York City, voters could use any early voting site assigned to voters in their county. In New York City, each voter was assigned to one site. Find your Election Day poll site with the state's voter lookup at voterlookup.elections.ny.gov or through your county board of elections. Early votes are counted and included in the unofficial totals on election night, the State Board of Elections says.

## New York: registration and mail ballots

- **Registration:** Mail and in-person registration applications must be received by a board of elections by Saturday, Oct. 24, to vote on Nov. 3.
- **Mail ballots:** The online and by-mail application deadline was Oct. 24; in person at your county board of elections, the deadline is Nov. 2, today. Mail your ballot with a postmark no later than Nov. 3, or drop it off at a board of elections office or poll site by 9 p.m. on Nov. 3. Drop-off at early voting sites was available from Oct. 24 to Nov. 1. New York counts mailed ballots that arrive by Nov. 10, as explained in [when we will know the 2026 election results](/news/when-will-we-know-election-results-2026-midterms/).
