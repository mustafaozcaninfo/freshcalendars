**Scammers are already using the 2027 Social Security raise as bait.** The Social Security Administration (SSA) Office of the Inspector General (OIG) is warning beneficiaries about texts, letters and calls that demand a fee, often $100, or personal information to "activate" the 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) for 2027. None of that is real. The COLA is applied automatically, and nobody has to pay, sign up or confirm anything to receive it.

## What the inspector general is warning about

SSA announced the 3.6% increase on Oct. 14, 2026, the largest since 2023. Three days later, OIG senior adviser Mary Miller told Yahoo News that COLA scams have worked in past years and that fraudsters tend to reuse tactics that succeeded before. The watchdog described four patterns:

| Tactic | How it looks |
|---|---|
| Fake activation fees | A caller, text or letter says you must pay, or give your Social Security number, to get the 2027 raise |
| Fake messages and websites | Texts, emails, social media posts or letters with a link to a site that copies SSA's design |
| Government impersonation | A "fraud alert" from a store or bank, then a transfer to someone posing as SSA, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) or police |
| Threats and payment demands | Warnings of arrest or a suspended Social Security number, followed by a demand for payment |

Scammers ask to be paid in ways that are hard to trace: gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfers, prepaid debit cards, cash and even gold bars. The OIG also says spotting a scam by its bad spelling no longer works well, because fraudsters use artificial intelligence (AI) to write convincing messages and build realistic fake sites.

## Three alerts from the past three months

**Sept. 21, 2026: Fees to "boost" benefits.** The OIG warned about offers to "boost" or "maximize" a Social Security benefit for an upfront fee. SSA does not raise benefits in exchange for money. Benefit amounts are set by law from your earnings record and cannot be negotiated over the phone.

**Sept. 28, 2026: Barcodes and quick response (QR) codes.** A caller posing as police or a federal agent says you owe a fine, or that a relative needs bail, and texts you barcodes or QR codes to load with cash at a store. Some victims have lost thousands of dollars. The caller may even use a cloned voice of a family member.

**July 29, 2026: Fake call transfers.** A call starts with a "retailer" asking about a purchase you didn't make, then transfers you to "Social Security." According to the OIG, no company or other agency can transfer your call to SSA. When that happens, the agency says, "it is a scam, without exception, every time."

COLA scams are not new. In an earlier alert, the OIG described fake letters on copied SSA letterhead telling people to call a toll-free number to activate their COLA.

## Red flags at a glance

It is a scam if someone:

- asks for a fee or a gift card to activate or speed up your 2027 raise;
- asks you to confirm your Social Security number or bank account to receive the COLA;
- sends a link or a code to scan, out of the blue;
- transfers your call to "Social Security" from a store, bank or another agency;
- threatens arrest, legal action or a suspended Social Security number;
- tells you to keep the conversation secret, or to move money somewhere "safe."

## What to do if you are contacted

1. **Stop.** Hang up, or don't reply. Don't press any numbers, open links or scan codes.
2. **Check through SSA directly.** Type ssa.gov/myaccount into your browser, or call SSA at 1-800-772-1213. Caller ID can be faked, so use a number you look up yourself.
3. **Report it** to the inspector general at oig.ssa.gov/report or the Fraud Hotline, 1-800-269-0271, and to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
4. **Tell someone you trust.** Scammers depend on urgency and secrecy; a second opinion breaks both.

If you have already paid, contact your bank or card issuer immediately, and include the payment details in your report. The free COLA scam checklist below fits on one page and can go next to the phone of an older parent or neighbor.

## How big the problem is

People told the FTC they lost about $920 million to government impersonators in 2025, up from $789 million in 2024, according to data the agency released on June 15, 2026. Impostor scams of all kinds cost $3.5 billion, and nearly one in three fraud reports the FTC received was about an impostor.

## Social Security increase 2027 chart

Here is what the real 3.6% increase looks like. Multiply your current monthly benefit by 1.036 and round down to the dollar:

| 2026 monthly benefit | 2027 monthly benefit | Raise per month | Raise per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1,000 | $1,036 | +$36 | +$432 |
| $1,250 | $1,295 | +$45 | +$540 |
| $1,500 | $1,554 | +$54 | +$648 |
| $1,750 | $1,813 | +$63 | +$756 |
| $2,000 | $2,072 | +$72 | +$864 |
| $2,095 (average retiree) | $2,170 | +$75 | +$900 |
| $2,500 | $2,590 | +$90 | +$1,080 |
| $3,000 | $3,108 | +$108 | +$1,296 |
| $3,500 | $3,626 | +$126 | +$1,512 |
| $4,152 (maximum at full retirement age) | $4,188 | +$36 | +$432 |

These are estimates before Medicare premiums and tax withholding. SSA applies the COLA to your primary insurance amount rather than the payment after deductions, so your notice may differ by a dollar or two. The maximum benefit at full retirement age grows by less than 3.6% because it is also tied to the earnings cap for each year. The printable chart covers every $100 step from $600 to $5,100. Medicare Part B premiums, usually deducted from Social Security, will be announced by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, typically in November. ## Social Security payment schedule 2027

Social Security pays on a Wednesday set by your birth date. SSI pays on the 1st of the month, and people who began benefits before May 1997, or who also get SSI, are paid on the 3rd. When the 1st or 3rd falls on a weekend or federal holiday, the payment moves to the business day before.

| Month | SSI | The 3rd | Born 1st–10th | Born 11th–20th | Born 21st–31st |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| January | Dec. 31, 2026 | Dec. 31, 2026 | Jan. 13 | Jan. 20 | Jan. 27 |
| February | Feb. 1 | Feb. 3 | Feb. 10 | Feb. 17 | Feb. 24 |
| March | March 1 | March 3 | March 10 | March 17 | March 24 |
| April | April 1 | April 2 | April 14 | April 21 | April 28 |
| May | April 30 | May 3 | May 12 | May 19 | May 26 |
| June | June 1 | June 3 | June 9 | June 16 | June 23 |
| July | July 1 | July 2 | July 14 | July 21 | July 28 |
| August | July 30 | Aug. 3 | Aug. 11 | Aug. 18 | Aug. 25 |
| September | Sept. 1 | Sept. 3 | Sept. 8 | Sept. 15 | Sept. 22 |
| October | Oct. 1 | Oct. 1 | Oct. 13 | Oct. 20 | Oct. 27 |
| November | Nov. 1 | Nov. 3 | Nov. 10 | Nov. 17 | Nov. 24 |
| December | Dec. 1 | Dec. 3 | Dec. 8 | Dec. 15 | Dec. 22 |

Because of those moves, SSI recipients receive no payment dated in January, May or August 2027, and two in April (April 1 and 30), July (July 1 and 30) and December (Dec. 1 and Thursday, Dec. 30). The Dec. 30 payment is January 2028's benefit, paid early because New Year's Day 2028 falls on a Saturday and is observed on Friday, Dec. 31. Each second payment is the next month's benefit arriving early, not a bonus, and a message promising an "extra" payment is another common hook. These dates match SSA's official 2027 payment schedule, Publication No. 05-10031. ## Real dates for the 2027 COLA

| Date | What actually happens |
|---|---|
| Wednesday, Nov. 18, 2026 | Last day to opt in to online notices in my Social Security |
| Late November 2026 | COLA notices appear in my Social Security accounts |
| December 2026 | Paper COLA notices arrive by mail |
| Thursday, Dec. 31, 2026 | First SSI payment with the 3.6% increase |
| Jan. 13, 20 and 27, 2027 | First Social Security payments with the 3.6% increase |

The genuine notice comes from SSA by mail or inside your my Social Security account. It never asks for a payment. If a message about your COLA arrives any other way, start from the assumption that it is a scam, and keep a [December 2026 calendar](/december-calendars.html) handy to check the real dates.
