**When clocks fall back at 2 a.m. on Sunday, Nov. 1, 2026, sunset moves about an hour earlier overnight.** In New York, the sun sets at 5:53 p.m. on Saturday, Oct. 31 and at 4:52 p.m. on Sunday, according to U.S. Naval Observatory (USNO) data. The evenings keep shrinking after that, and the earliest sunset of the year arrives in early December, about two weeks before the shortest day.

## Why the drop is a little more than an hour

The clock change itself moves sunset by exactly one hour. The extra minute or two in most cities is the normal daily shrinkage: in late October and early November, sunset comes about a minute earlier each day at mid-northern latitudes. Anchorage loses more, about three minutes a day, which is why its sunset falls from 6:00 p.m. to 4:57 p.m.

Phoenix and Honolulu are the exceptions. Most of Arizona and all of Hawaii stay on standard time all year, so their sunsets move by only the usual one minute.

## The earliest sunset is not on the shortest day

The winter solstice is Monday, Dec. 21, 2026, the day with the least daylight. But in most of the country the earliest sunset comes one to three weeks earlier. That happens because solar noon, the moment the sun is highest, drifts later through December as Earth speeds up near the closest point of its orbit, which it reaches in early January. Sunset starts getting later in mid-December while sunrise keeps getting later until early January.

The pattern shifts with latitude. Southern cities such as Miami and Houston reach their earliest sunset in late November, while Anchorage, far to the north, does not reach it until mid-December, close to the solstice.

## What it means for your evenings

The first week of November brings the sharpest change in daily routines: school pickup, a dog walk or an evening run that happened in daylight on Saturday can be in twilight by Monday. Civil twilight, when there is still enough light for most outdoor activity, lasts about 25 to 30 minutes after sunset in most of the continental U.S. in November.
