The tournament published its full schedule on Oct. 29. The singles draw ceremony was set for Friday, Oct. 30 from 6:30 p.m. Paris time, which was 5:30 p.m. Greenwich Mean Time (GMT), 1:30 p.m. Eastern Daylight Time (EDT) and 10:30 a.m. Pacific Daylight Time (PDT). The singles final was set for 3 p.m. Paris time on Sunday, Nov. 8, after the doubles final at 12:30 p.m. This page does not cover the draw itself.

**The Rolex Paris Masters ran Saturday, Oct. 31 to Sunday, Nov. 8, 2026, and its provisional programme put the singles final at 3 p.m. Paris time on Sunday, which was 9 a.m. Eastern Standard Time (EST), the tournament says.** Carlos Alcaraz beat Ben Shelton 7-6 (5), 7-5 in that final, Tennis Majors and ABC News reported. Paris time here is Central European Time (CET), Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) plus 1 hour. It was the 40th anniversary edition, held at the Plenitude Arena in Nanterre. The U.S. columns below are our conversion, and the programme is labeled provisional ("programme prévisionnel").

## What time was the Paris Masters 2026 final?

The programme said the singles final "starts at 3pm" on Sunday, Nov. 8, with doors at 11:30 a.m. By our conversion that was 2 p.m. GMT, 9 a.m. EST, 8 a.m. CST and 6 a.m. PST. The doubles final came first at 12:30 p.m. Paris time, 6:30 a.m. EST.

On Saturday, Nov. 7, the doubles semi-final was at 12 p.m., the first singles semi-final "starts at 2.30pm" and the second was "not before 5pm". A "not before" time is the earliest start.

The wheelchair event ran Friday, Nov. 6 to Sunday, Nov. 8 on courts 2 and 3, and the wheelchair singles final was Sunday from 12:00 on court 2, according to the tournament's wheelchair page.

## Why is the gap to U.S. time different on Oct. 31 and Nov. 1?

France changed its clocks on Oct. 25, so Paris was on CET all tournament. U.S. clocks fell back at 2 a.m. on Sunday, Nov. 1, the day after the tournament opened. On Saturday, Oct. 31 Paris was 5 hours ahead of Eastern Time (EDT). From Sunday, Nov. 1 it was 6 hours ahead (EST). That is why the same 10:30 a.m. Paris doors time was 5:30 a.m. Eastern on Saturday and 4:30 a.m. on Sunday. Every match session from Nov. 2 uses the 6-hour gap. See our [daylight saving time ends Nov. 1, 2026](/news/daylight-saving-time-ends-november-1-2026/) story and the [daylight saving time page](/when-is/daylight-savings/) for the change itself.

## Where is the Paris Masters played, and who is in the field?

The tournament was at the Plenitude Arena, 99 jardins de l'Arche, 92000 Nanterre (Paris La Défense). The official ticketing page says that since the 2025 edition the move to "Europe's largest indoor venue" has marked a new milestone for the French Tennis Federation (FFT), and that La Plenitude Arena became a partner for the next 10 years in 2025. It can host four competition courts, including a centre court, and a practice court. The tournament gives no other reason for the move.

As of the Oct. 13 entry list, the field included Alexander Zverev, Carlos Alcaraz, Novak Djokovic, Ben Shelton, Frances Tiafoe, Arthur Fils, Daniil Medvedev, Alex de Minaur, Taylor Fritz, Tommy Paul, Jakub Mensik, Felix Auger-Aliassime, Rafael Jodar (making his debut), Holger Rune (2022 champion, entering on a protected ranking), Ugo Humbert and Arthur Rinderknech. The tournament said defending champion Jannik Sinner was absent.

## How much is the prize money, and where can you watch?

The 2026 prize money was not published on the official pages we could read. In 2025, the Association of Tennis Professionals (ATP) listed total prize money of €6,128,940, a singles champion's prize of €946,610 and 1,000 ranking points for the winner.

The tournament had not published a broadcaster list for the United States or United Kingdom on the pages we read; check your local listings. The singles draw ceremony was scheduled for Friday, Oct. 30 from 6:30 p.m. Paris time (1:30 p.m. EDT), according to the schedule the tournament published on Oct. 29; that date has passed.
