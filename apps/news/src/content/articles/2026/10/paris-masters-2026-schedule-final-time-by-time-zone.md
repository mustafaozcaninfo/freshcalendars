**The Rolex Paris Masters runs Saturday, Oct. 31 to Sunday, Nov. 8, 2026, and its provisional programme puts the singles final at 3 p.m. Paris time on Sunday, which is 9 a.m. Eastern Standard Time (EST), the tournament says.** Paris time here is Central European Time (CET), Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) plus 1 hour. This is the 40th anniversary edition, held at the Plenitude Arena in Nanterre. The U.S. columns below are our conversion, and the programme is labeled provisional ("programme prévisionnel").

## What is the Paris Masters 2026 schedule?

The tournament's ticketing site lists doors and start times for each session. Eastern Time (ET), Central Time (CT) and Pacific Time (PT) are computed from the Paris times; Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) is the London time.

From Nov. 1 onward the U.S. zones are on standard time: EST, Central Standard Time (CST) and Pacific Standard Time (PST). The Oct. 31 row is on Eastern Daylight Time (EDT), Central Daylight Time (CDT) and Pacific Daylight Time (PDT). For Monday to Thursday, the programme gives doors at 10 a.m. and arena access for the night session not before 6:30 p.m. The main draw starts Monday, Nov. 2. Qualifying start times are not listed.

## What time is the Paris Masters 2026 final?

The programme says the singles final "starts at 3pm" on Sunday, Nov. 8, with doors at 11:30 a.m. By our conversion that is 2 p.m. GMT, 9 a.m. EST, 8 a.m. CST and 6 a.m. PST. The doubles final comes first at 12:30 p.m. Paris time, 6:30 a.m. EST.

On Saturday, Nov. 7, the doubles semi-final is at 12 p.m., the first singles semi-final "starts at 2.30pm" and the second is "not before 5pm". A "not before" time is the earliest start. It moves later if the match before it runs long.

The wheelchair event runs Friday, Nov. 6 to Sunday, Nov. 8 on courts 2 and 3, and the wheelchair singles final is Sunday from 12:00 on court 2, according to the tournament's wheelchair page.

## Why is the gap to U.S. time different on Oct. 31 and Nov. 1?

France changed its clocks on Oct. 25, so Paris is on CET all tournament. U.S. clocks fall back at 2 a.m. on Sunday, Nov. 1, the day after the tournament opens. On Saturday, Oct. 31 Paris is 5 hours ahead of Eastern Time (EDT). From Sunday, Nov. 1 it is 6 hours ahead (EST). That is why the same 10:30 a.m. Paris doors time is 5:30 a.m. Eastern on Saturday and 4:30 a.m. on Sunday. Every match session from Nov. 2 uses the 6-hour gap. See our [daylight saving time ends Nov. 1, 2026](/news/daylight-saving-time-ends-november-1-2026/) story and the [daylight saving time page](/when-is/daylight-savings/) for the change itself.

## Where is the Paris Masters played, and who is in the field?

The tournament is at the Plenitude Arena, 99 jardins de l'Arche, 92000 Nanterre (Paris La Défense). The official ticketing page says that since the 2025 edition the move to "Europe's largest indoor venue" has marked a new milestone for the French Tennis Federation (FFT), and that La Plenitude Arena became a partner for the next 10 years in 2025. It can host four competition courts, including a centre court, and a practice court. The tournament gives no other reason for the move.

As of the Oct. 13 entry list, the field includes Alexander Zverev, Carlos Alcaraz, Novak Djokovic, Ben Shelton, Frances Tiafoe, Arthur Fils, Daniil Medvedev, Alex de Minaur, Taylor Fritz, Tommy Paul, Jakub Mensik, Felix Auger-Aliassime, Rafael Jodar (making his debut), Holger Rune (2022 champion, entering on a protected ranking), Ugo Humbert and Arthur Rinderknech. The tournament says defending champion Jannik Sinner is absent. Withdrawals can change the list.

## How much is the prize money, and where can you watch?

The 2026 prize money is not yet published on the official pages we could read. In 2025, the Association of Tennis Professionals (ATP) listed total prize money of €6,128,940, a singles champion's prize of €946,610 and 1,000 ranking points for the winner.

The tournament has not published a broadcaster list for the United States or United Kingdom, Check your local listings.
