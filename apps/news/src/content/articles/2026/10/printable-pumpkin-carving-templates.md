## What size to print for your pumpkin

Each stencil prints about 6 inches wide and 5 1/4 inches tall at 100% (Actual size), measured on the dashed outline. Measure the side of your pumpkin you plan to carve, from one side of the smooth area to the other, and set the print scale from this table. The black bar on each sheet is 2 inches at 100%, so you can check the printout with a ruler.

| Width of the area to carve | Print scale | Design size (width × height) | Narrowest bridge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 inches | 67% | 4 × 3 1/2 inches | 0.17 inch |
| 5 inches | 84% | 5 × 4 1/2 inches | 0.21 inch |
| 6 inches or more | 100% (Actual size) | 6 × 5 1/4 inches | 0.25 inch |

Scaling shrinks the bridges along with everything else. For a pumpkin smaller than about 5 inches across, choose an Easy design. On a bigger pumpkin, print at 100% and leave a wider border; scaling up past 100% pushes the design off the edge of a Letter sheet. On a printer, "Fit to page" changes the size, so turn it off when you want the table's numbers.

## How to use a printable pumpkin stencil

1. Print the PDF on US Letter paper at 100% or the scale from the table, and check the 2-inch bar.
2. Have an adult cut the lid. Children can scoop the seeds and pulp with a spoon.
3. Tape the stencil to the smoothest side of the pumpkin. Make a few small folds or cuts in the paper margin so it lies flat on the curve.
4. Poke holes about 1/8 inch apart along every line with a pushpin or poking tool, or trace hard with a ballpoint pen.
5. Take the paper off and join the dots with a washable marker.
6. An adult carves out the black areas, starting with the smallest shapes in the middle of the design.
7. Light it with a battery-operated light or glow stick.

## Pumpkin carving safety

Pumpkin carving is the largest single cause of Halloween injuries in estimates from the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC). In its October 2022 safety release, the agency estimated an annual average of 3,200 Halloween-related injuries treated in U.S. hospital emergency departments over the previous three years, and said 55% of them were related to pumpkin carving. Its advice is short: "Leave pumpkin carving to the adults." Child helpers "can grab a spoon and scoop out the inside or use a marker to trace the design."

The CPSC also recommends battery-operated lights or glow sticks instead of an open-flame candle. If you do use a candle, keep the pumpkin away from curtains, decorations and anything else that can catch fire, and never leave it burning unattended.
