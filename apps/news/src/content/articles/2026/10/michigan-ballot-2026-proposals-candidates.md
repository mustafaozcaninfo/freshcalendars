**Election Day 2026 in Michigan is Tuesday, Nov. 3, with polls open from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m.** The ballot has two statewide proposals and an unusually open top of the ticket. Gov. Gretchen Whitmer, Secretary of State Jocelyn Benson and Attorney General Dana Nessel are term-limited, and U.S. Sen. Gary Peters is not seeking reelection, so all four offices have new nominees. The candidates below come from the Michigan Bureau of Elections' certified general election listing, in the order the parties appear.

| Nov. 3 | Election Day; polls open 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. | - |

## Proposal 1: a constitutional convention

The Michigan Constitution requires voters to be asked every 16 years whether to hold a convention to rewrite it. The 2026 question reads, in part: "Shall a convention of elected delegates be convened in 2027 to draft a general revision of the State Constitution for presentation to the state's voters for their approval or rejection?"

- **Yes:** delegates would be elected to draft a revised constitution, which voters would then approve or reject.
- **No:** no convention; the question returns automatically in 2042.

Voters rejected the same question in 1978, 1994 and 2010.

## Proposal 2: utility and contractor contributions

Proposal 2 is a citizen-initiated law. It would bar regulated electric and gas utilities, companies with more than $250,000 a year in state government contracts, and people closely connected to them from contributing to officials and candidates whose decisions affect them. It would also extend disclosure rules to political communications that clearly identify a candidate or ballot question, including online ads.

- **Yes:** enact the contribution limits and disclosure rules.
- **No:** keep current campaign finance law.

The Michigan Supreme Court rejected a last-minute challenge to keep it off the ballot. A separate proposed amendment requiring proof of citizenship to register did not make the ballot.

Supreme Court candidates are nominated at party conventions but appear on the ballot without party labels. Your ballot also has two seats each on the State Board of Education and the governing boards of the University of Michigan, Michigan State University and Wayne State University, plus all 13 U.S. House seats and every seat in the state Senate and House.

## Key dates

| Date | What |
|---|---|
| Sat., Oct. 24-Sun., Nov. 1 | Early in-person voting, at least nine days statewide |
| Oct. 20-Nov. 3 | Register in person at your clerk's office with proof of residency |
| Fri., Oct. 30, 5 p.m. | Recommended last day to request an absentee ballot by mail or online |
| Mon., Nov. 2, 4 p.m. | Last day to request and vote an absentee ballot in person at your clerk's office |
| Tue., Nov. 3, 7 a.m.-8 p.m. | Election Day; absentee ballots must be received by 8 p.m. |

Michigan voters approved a nine-day minimum for early voting in 2022, and some communities start earlier. Absentee ballots come with prepaid return postage and can be returned to a drop box in every community. Ballots must be in the clerk's hands by 8 p.m. on Election Day; a postmark does not count. Check your registration, sample ballot and early voting sites at the Michigan Voter Information Center. Site hours and the rules for the nine days are in [early voting starts Oct. 24 in Michigan and New York](/news/early-voting-starts-october-24-michigan-new-york-2026/).

## ID at the polls

You will be asked to show photo ID, such as a Michigan driver's license or state ID, a passport, a military ID, a student ID or a tribal ID. If you do not have one, you can sign an affidavit and vote a regular ballot that counts like any other.
