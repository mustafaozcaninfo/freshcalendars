**Election Day 2026 in Michigan was Tuesday, Nov. 3, with polls open from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m.** The ballot had two statewide proposals and an unusually open top of the ticket. Gov. Gretchen Whitmer, Secretary of State Jocelyn Benson and Attorney General Dana Nessel are term-limited, and U.S. Sen. Gary Peters did not seek reelection, so all four offices had new nominees. Results are unofficial until the state canvass ends: county canvassers must certify by Tuesday, Nov. 17, and the Board of State Canvassers must vote to certify by Monday, Nov. 23, according to the Michigan Secretary of State. The candidates below come from the Michigan Bureau of Elections' certified general election listing, in the order the parties appear.

## Michigan election timeline: voting and certification

| Date | What happened or happens next |
|---|---|
| Oct. 24 | Early in-person voting began (at least nine days) |
| Nov. 1 | Early in-person voting ended |
| Nov. 3 | Election Day; polls open 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. |
| Nov. 17 | County boards of canvassers must certify the results |
| Nov. 23 | Board of State Canvassers must vote to certify; results are then final and official |

## Proposal 1: a constitutional convention

The Michigan Constitution requires voters to be asked every 16 years whether to hold a convention to rewrite it. The 2026 question read, in part: "Shall a convention of elected delegates be convened in 2027 to draft a general revision of the State Constitution for presentation to the state's voters for their approval or rejection?"

- **Yes:** delegates would be elected to draft a revised constitution, which voters would then approve or reject.
- **No:** no convention; the question returns automatically in 2042.

Voters rejected the same question in 1978, 1994 and 2010.

## Proposal 2: utility and contractor contributions

Proposal 2 is a citizen-initiated law. It would bar regulated electric and gas utilities, companies with more than $250,000 a year in state government contracts, and people closely connected to them from contributing to officials and candidates whose decisions affect them. It would also extend disclosure rules to political communications that clearly identify a candidate or ballot question, including online ads.

- **Yes:** enact the contribution limits and disclosure rules.
- **No:** keep current campaign finance law.

The Michigan Supreme Court rejected a last-minute challenge to keep it off the ballot. A separate proposed amendment requiring proof of citizenship to register did not make the ballot.

Supreme Court candidates are nominated at party conventions but appeared on the ballot without party labels. Ballots also had two seats each on the State Board of Education and the governing boards of the University of Michigan, Michigan State University and Wayne State University, plus all 13 U.S. House seats and every seat in the state Senate and House.

## Key dates

| Date | What |
|---|---|
| Sat., Oct. 24-Sun., Nov. 1 | Early in-person voting, at least nine days statewide |
| Oct. 20-Nov. 3 | Register in person at your clerk's office with proof of residency |
| Fri., Oct. 30, 5 p.m. | Recommended last day to request an absentee ballot by mail or online |
| Mon., Nov. 2, 4 p.m. | Last day to request and vote an absentee ballot in person at your clerk's office |
| Tue., Nov. 3, 7 a.m.-8 p.m. | Election Day; absentee ballots had to be received by 8 p.m. |
| Tue., Nov. 17 | Deadline for county boards of canvassers to certify |
| Mon., Nov. 23 | Deadline for the Board of State Canvassers to vote to certify |

Michigan voters approved a nine-day minimum for early voting in 2022, and some communities started earlier. Absentee ballots had to be in the clerk's hands by 8 p.m. on Election Day; a postmark did not count. Unofficial results are on county clerk websites, and the Secretary of State lists them at mi.gov/vote once all counties report. The rules for the nine days are in our [Michigan and New York early voting guide](/news/early-voting-2026-which-states-are-voting-now/).

## ID at the polls

Voters are asked to show photo ID, such as a Michigan driver's license or state ID, a passport, a military ID, a student ID or a tribal ID. Voters without one can sign an affidavit and vote a regular ballot that counts like any other.
