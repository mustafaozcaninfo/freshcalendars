A Julian date calendar numbers every day of the year from 001 to 365, so Jan. 1 is 001, Feb. 1 is 032 and Dec. 31 is 365. Both **2026 and 2027 have 365 days**, so the numbers line up the same way in each year. Below are four free printable templates for each year, followed by how to read the codes printed on egg cartons, warehouse labels and military paperwork, and how the astronomers' Julian Day differs.

## Two things called a Julian date

The same name is used for two different numbers, and mixing them up is the most common mistake.

| | Day-of-year "Julian date" | Astronomical Julian Day |
|---|---|---|
| What it counts | Days since Jan. 1 of the current year | Days since noon UT on Jan. 1, 4713 BC |
| Jan. 1, 2027 | 001 (code 27001) | 2461407 |
| Dec. 31, 2027 | 365 (code 27365) | 2461771 |
| Who uses it | Food packers, warehouses, the military, programmers | Astronomers and scientific software |
| Day starts at | Midnight | Noon Universal Time (UT) |

Strictly speaking, only the second is a Julian date. The day-of-year number is an ordinal date, which the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) writes in its ISO 8601 standard as YYYY-DDD (2027-032). Outside astronomy, though, "Julian date" almost always means the day of the year, and that is what most people searching for a Julian calendar need.

## How to read a day-of-year Julian date

Find the month, then the date. If you have no chart, add the date to the number of days before that month:

| Month | Days before it (2026 and 2027) | Days before it (leap year 2028) |
|---|---|---|
| January | 0 | 0 |
| February | 31 | 31 |
| March | 59 | 60 |
| April | 90 | 91 |
| May | 120 | 121 |
| June | 151 | 152 |
| July | 181 | 182 |
| August | 212 | 213 |
| September | 243 | 244 |
| October | 273 | 274 |
| November | 304 | 305 |
| December | 334 | 335 |

Example: Oct. 18 is 273 + 18 = 291. Running the other way, 185 falls in July, because 185 is more than 181 but not more than 212; 185 − 181 = 4, so it is July 4.

## Where you will see these codes

**Egg cartons.** Cartons carrying the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) grade shield show a three-digit pack date: the day of the year the eggs were washed, graded and packed. The USDA Agricultural Marketing Service gives 001 for Jan. 1, 049 for Feb. 18 and 198 for July 17. A sell-by date, when printed, cannot be more than 30 days after that pack date, according to USDA.

**Lot and batch codes.** Manufacturers often print YYDDD or YYYYDDD on packaging, so 27032 or 2027032 both mean Feb. 1, 2027. Our date code lookup lists every one for the year.

**Military paperwork.** U.S. military supply requisitions use a four-digit YDDD date inside the document number: one digit for the year and three for the day. In that format, described in the Defense Logistics Agency's standards, 7032 is Feb. 1, 2027. Because the year is a single digit, the same code repeats every 10 years.

**Software and data.** Spreadsheets and programming languages call it the day of year, often with the ISO 8601 form 2027-032.

## The astronomical Julian Day

In 1583 the scholar Joseph Scaliger proposed the Julian Period, a 7,980-year cycle that combines three older calendar cycles of 28, 19 and 15 years. Day 0 of the count starts at noon Universal Time on Jan. 1, 4713 BC in the proleptic Julian calendar. Astronomers adopted it because a single running number makes it easy to measure the time between observations, no matter which calendar or month length applies.

Each Julian Day begins at noon UT, not midnight. The U.S. Naval Observatory notes that 00:00 UT corresponds to a fraction of .5, so the night of Jan. 1, 2027 starts at JD 2461406.5 and noon that day is JD 2461407.0. Scientists often use the Modified Julian Date (MJD) instead, defined as MJD = JD − 2400000.5, which starts at midnight. Jan. 1, 2027 is MJD 61406.

Another useful check: noon on Jan. 1, 2000, a standard reference point known as J2000, is Julian Day 2451545.

## Leap years change the count after February

In a leap year, Feb. 29 becomes day 060 and every later date moves up by one, ending at 366 on Dec. 31. Neither 2026 nor 2027 is a leap year. The next is 2028, then 2032. If you keep one of these charts on the wall, replace it when the year changes, because Jan. 1 always restarts the count at 001.
