**Voters in five states, North Carolina, Ohio, Arizona, Nevada and Colorado, decide measures on voter identification on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, according to each state's official ballot language.** North Carolina, Ohio, Arizona and Nevada have constitutional amendments on the ballot, as does Colorado, where Amendment 84 is a citizen initiative. This guide puts them in one table and adds the federal rule that already applies to first-time voters who registered by mail.

## Voter ID measures on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot

| State | Measure | In-person rule in the measure | Mail rule in the measure | When it would apply |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North Carolina | Constitutional amendment (Session Law 2024-58) | Photo identification before voting | Covered: "all voters, not just those presenting to vote in person" | Not stated in the sources we used |
| Ohio | Issue 3, constitutional amendment | Photo identification; Ballot Board language refers to government-issued photo ID for in-person voting | Not stated in the sources we used | Not stated in the sources we used |
| Arizona | Proposition 144, constitutional amendment | Government-issued identification to cast a ballot | Not stated in the sources we used | Not stated in the sources we used |
| Nevada | Question 7 (Initiative Petition C-02-2023), constitutional amendment | Photo identification | Last four digits of Nevada driver's license number, or of Social Security number if none | Legislature could act in 2027; changes for the 2028 election cycle |
| Colorado | Amendment 84, constitutional amendment by citizen initiative; needs at least 55% of votes cast | Not stated in the sources we used | Last four digits of Social Security number, or Colorado driver's license or ID number, included with the mail ballot | Not stated in the sources we used |

Where a cell says "not stated," the source we reviewed does not say. Check your state election office before you rely on a detail the table leaves open.

## What does North Carolina's amendment change?

The North Carolina State Board of Elections says on its Voter ID page: "Voters will be asked to show photo ID when voting in North Carolina." The same page says: "All voters will be allowed to vote with or without a photo ID." Voters without ID can fill out an ID exception form, as covered in our [North Carolina sample ballot guide](/news/north-carolina-sample-ballot-2026-senate-amendments/).

The Nov. 3 question comes from Session Law 2024-58, published by the North Carolina General Assembly. It reads in part: "Constitutional amendment to require all voters, not just those presenting to vote in person, to present photo identification before voting." The wording names mail voters explicitly. Each amendment on the North Carolina ballot is a For or Against vote.

## What would Ohio's Issue 3 do?

Issue 3 is the only statewide issue on Ohio's ballot. Signal Cleveland reported the ballot title: it would require voters to present photo identification in order to vote. The Statehouse News Bureau, on Aug. 3, 2026, described the Ohio Ballot Board's approved language as asking voters whether they should have to show government-issued photo ID for any in-person voting.

Ohio already requires an unexpired photo ID to vote in person, per the state's guidance summarized in our [Ohio sample ballot guide](/news/ohio-sample-ballot-2026-governor-senate-issue-3/). The amendment would put the rule in the constitution. This story does not cover Ohio's mail-voting rules because the sources above do not address them.

## What is Arizona's Proposition 144?

Proposition 144 is one of eight measures the Legislature referred to Arizona voters. Per the Arizona Secretary of State's final ballot language, it would state that only citizens may register and vote, bar foreign nationals from contributing to influence Arizona elections, require voters to provide government-issued identification to cast a ballot, and give voters a right to have their vote tabulated at their voting location.

Today, Arizona voters show identification in person in one of three ways: one valid photo ID with name and address; two documents without a photo that show name and address; or a photo ID plus one such document. Our [Arizona propositions guide](/news/arizona-propositions-2026-ballot-explained/) lists all eight measures. Arizona counts an early ballot only if it arrives by 7 p.m. on Election Day, and the county checks the signature on the affidavit envelope against the voter record.

## What would Nevada's Question 7 require?

Question 7 would amend the Nevada Constitution. According to the Clark County Election Department's 2026 General Election Guide, in-person voters would present photo identification, and mail voters would provide the last four digits of their Nevada driver's license number, or of their Social Security number if they have no license.

This is a second vote: the measure was approved in 2024 and must pass again. If voters approve it, the Legislature could pass related laws in 2027, and the changes would take effect for the 2028 election cycle. Our guide to [Nevada's Question 6 and Question 7](/news/nevada-ballot-questions-2026-question-6-question-7-explained/) covers both measures.

## What would Colorado's Amendment 84 require?

Amendment 84 is a citizen initiative to amend the Colorado constitution, and it needs at least 55% of votes cast to pass. The Colorado Legislative Council's 2026 State Ballot Information Booklet, dated Sept. 10, 2026 (page 8), says it would require voters to include ID information with mail ballots: the last four digits of their Social Security number, or their Colorado driver's license or ID number. The sources we used do not describe an in-person rule or a start date. Colorado's other measures are in our [Colorado ballot measures guide](/news/colorado-ballot-measures-2026-amendments-propositions-explained/).

## What does federal law already require of first-time mail registrants?

Separate from any state measure, the Help America Vote Act (HAVA) has a rule for one group. Under 52 U.S.C. §21083(b), as published by the Legal Information Institute at Cornell Law School, a first-time voter who registered by mail and has not been verified must show a current and valid photo ID, or a copy of a current utility bill, bank statement, government check, paycheck or other government document showing name and address. A voter without one of these may cast a provisional ballot.

| If you are | Federal rule under 52 U.S.C. §21083(b) |
|---|---|
| A first-time voter who registered by mail and has not been verified | Show a current and valid photo ID, or a copy of a utility bill, bank statement, government check, paycheck or other government document with your name and address |
| Unable to show either | You may cast a provisional ballot |

The statute describes the federal minimum for this group. State rules for other voters are set by each state.

## How can I check my own state's rules?

The National Association of Secretaries of State (NASS) runs a "Can I Vote" page at nass.org/can-i-vote that links to each state's election office. This story does not give a count of states with voter ID laws, because we did not confirm one from an official source.
