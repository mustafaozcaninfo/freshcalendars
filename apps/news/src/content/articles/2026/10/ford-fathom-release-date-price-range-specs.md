<p class="callout"><b>Checked Oct. 29, 2026.</b> We will update this page when Ford publishes range, battery and delivery details.</p>

**The Ford Fathom starts at $28,350, and Ford says preorders begin in early 2027.** That is the manufacturer's suggested retail price (MSRP) for the midsize all-electric pickup with its standard-range battery, according to [Ford's Fathom page](https://www.ford.com/electric/fathom/). The same page lists a $1,595 destination and delivery charge, so the two together come to $29,945 before taxes, title, registration and dealer fees.

The newest official news is from Oct. 28, 2026: Ford said it has cast [more than 5,000 large aluminum unicastings](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/us/en/articles/2026/ford-fathom-electric-truck-5000-unicastings) for the truck and still refers to a 2027 launch. What Ford has not published is just as useful to know. There is no driving range, no battery size, no charging time and no towing figure yet.

This guide puts every date Ford has given in one timeline, splits the specs into confirmed and not published, and ends with a [printable preorder checklist](#downloads).

Ford has given seasons and quarters, not days. The table lists each step with the Ford source that states it.

| Thu., Aug. 6, 2026 | Ford names the truck Fathom and gives the $28,350 starting MSRP | Ford From the Road |

The gap between those last two rows is where the real release date sits. Ford has not named a delivery month, and a preorder is a place in line, not a delivery date. Some news reports have said "fall 2027"; we could not find that wording on any Ford page, so treat it as unconfirmed. ## How much will the Ford Fathom cost?

Ford's page says the $28,350 starting MSRP does "not include destination and delivery or dealer documentation fees." Ford's name-reveal article adds that it also excludes taxes, title and registration.

| Cost item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Starting MSRP, standard-range battery | $28,350 |
| Destination and delivery (Ford.com) | $1,595 |
| MSRP plus destination and delivery | **$29,945** |
| Taxes, title, registration | Depends on your state |
| Dealer documentation fee | Set by the dealer |
| Higher trims and options | Not yet announced |

The price came down during development. In August 2025 Ford talked about "about $30,000"; a year later it put the number at $28,350. The $29,945 total is our sum of Ford's two published figures. Ford does not state it, and it is not an out-the-door price.

## What is the Ford Fathom's range?

**Ford has not published it.** The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) rates the range of electric vehicles (EVs) sold in the U.S., and Ford's Fathom page says: "The projected EPA-estimated driving range figures for Ford Fathom are not yet available."

Ford has said a little about the battery. The launch truck uses a "standard-range battery," and in its 2025 platform announcement Ford said the truck would use prismatic lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cells built at BlueOval Battery Park Michigan. It has not given the capacity in kilowatt-hours (kWh), the charging speed or whether a longer-range battery will be offered.

Any range number you see before Ford publishes one is a guess. Weight, speed, cold weather and towing all change how far an electric pickup goes, so wait for the EPA figure and then for independent tests.

## Can the Ford Fathom power your home?

Yes, with Ford's equipment. In two articles on Oct. 27, 2026, Ford said the Fathom will supply [over a projected 10 kW of AC power](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/us/en/articles/2026/fathom-ev-bidirectional-home-power) to a home without a separate wall-mounted inverter, because the inverter is built into one of the truck's five main modules. Ford says it is the first EV from a U.S. automaker with an MSRP below $30,000 that can do that.

| Use | What it does | Hardware Ford lists | Cost Ford gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home Power Management | Charge when power is cheaper, run the house from the truck when it costs more | Ford Home Power Station, utility approval, active Ford plan or trial | About half the price of Ford's first-generation bidirectional charger |
| Home Backup Power | Run the house from the truck during an outage | Home Power Station plus Ford Home Backup Power Panel; utility notice or approval may apply | At least 60% less than Ford's first-generation backup hardware |

Ford has not published a dollar price for either kit. It also said it formed a working group in July 2026 with American Electric Power, BC Hydro, Consumers Energy, Exelon and Pacific Gas and Electric Co. to simplify how utilities connect bidirectional EVs before the truck arrives.

## How Ford is building it: 146 parts become two

The Oct. 28 update is about the body structure. Ford says its unicastings reduce "146 structural parts to just two." The castings are made at Ford's Shelbyville Technical Center from aluminum that melts at about 1,300 degrees Fahrenheit, then go to Louisville for process testing and prototype assembly.

Ford's new process splits a traditional assembly line into three main branches. In August, Ford said this lets Louisville assemble the Fathom 40% faster than the plant's current products, and that assembly "will net out at 15% faster" than the plant today. The plant was emptied for a $2 billion overhaul. These are Ford's targets; how fast the line runs, and at what cost, will show once production starts.

## Is the Ford Fathom delayed?

**Ford has not announced a delay.** In early October, Ford chief executive Jim Farley said, [as reported on Oct. 6, 2026](https://www.cbtnews.com/farley-fathom-launch/) by an auto industry news site: "It's going to be a heck of an effort to launch at this scale, at cost, on time." The same report noted that he did not confirm a delay or give a new timeline. Three weeks later, Ford's unicasting update still described "the affordable truck's 2027 launch."

The plain reading: 2027 is still Ford's public plan, and the hard part is the ramp-up. If that changes, Ford's newsroom is where it will be said first, and we will update this page.

## Should you wait for the Fathom?

The Fathom makes sense to watch if you can charge at home and want a pickup with a bed, a frunk and home backup power at a starting MSRP under $30,000. If you tow often, carry heavy loads or need a truck before late 2027, there is nothing to compare yet: the ratings that matter for that work are unpublished.
