Joe Biden and Kamala Harris won the 2020 presidential election with 306 electoral votes, and Donald Trump and Mike Pence received 232, according to the National Archives and Records Administration (NARA). A candidate needs 270 of the 538 electoral votes. The table below lists every state's vote, and the timeline shows each date from Election Day to Inauguration Day.

## What were the 2020 electoral vote totals?

NARA lists 24 states plus the District of Columbia for Biden and 24 states for Trump. Maine and Nebraska award electoral votes by congressional district, so both split their votes: Maine gave 3 to Biden and 1 to Trump, and Nebraska gave 4 to Trump and 1 to Biden.

| Candidate (ticket) | Electoral votes, 2020 |
|---|---|
| Joe Biden and Kamala Harris | 306 |
| Donald Trump and Mike Pence | 232 |
| Needed to win | 270 of 538 |

Source: NARA, "2020 Electoral College Results."

## What are the key dates from Election Day to Inauguration Day?

Each date below comes from federal law as it applied in 2020, and the weekday is computed from the calendar.

| Date | Event | Rule behind it |
|---|---|---|
| Tue., Nov. 3, 2020 | Election Day | The Tuesday after the first Monday in November (3 U.S.C. 1) |
| Mon., Dec. 14, 2020 | Electors met in each state | The first Monday after the second Wednesday in December, the rule then in force |
| Wed., Jan. 6, 2021 | Congress met in joint session to count the votes | 3 U.S.C. 15: "Congress shall be in session on the sixth day of January succeeding every meeting of the electors" |
| 3:44 a.m., Thu., Jan. 7, 2021 | Count completed and results certified | NARA: results were not certified until then because of violent unrest in and around the Capitol |
| Wed., Jan. 20, 2021, noon | Inauguration | Twentieth Amendment: terms end "at noon on the 20th day of January" |

Congress changed the electors' meeting day in 2022 with the Electoral Count Reform Act, which amended 3 U.S.C. 7. Electors now meet on the first Tuesday after the second Wednesday in December. The 2020 date above follows the earlier rule.

## What happened on Jan. 6, 2021?

The [Congressional Record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2021-01-06/html/CREC-2021-01-06-pt1-PgD22.htm) Daily Digest for Jan. 6, 2021, shows that the Senate voted on objections to two states' electoral votes. It rejected the objection to Arizona's votes by 6 yeas to 93 nays (Vote No. 1) and the objection to Pennsylvania's votes by 7 yeas to 92 nays (Vote No. 2). Neither objection was sustained. NARA records that the results were not certified until 3:44 a.m. on Jan. 7 because of violent unrest in and around the Capitol.

## Where is the 2020 popular vote?

The president is chosen by electoral votes, so this story reports those. The Federal Election Commission (FEC) publishes official vote totals in its [Federal Elections 2020](https://www.fec.gov/documents/4227/federalelections2020.pdf) report.

## How does the 2024 result compare, and what about 2026?

For context, NARA's "2024 Electoral College Results" page lists Donald Trump with 312 electoral votes and Kamala Harris with 226. The next presidential Election Day is Tuesday, Nov. 7, 2028, a date computed from the same federal rule that set Nov. 3, 2020.

Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, is a midterm election, so there is no presidential race and no electors to choose.
