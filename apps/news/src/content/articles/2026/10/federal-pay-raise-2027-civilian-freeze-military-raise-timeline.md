**The President's alternative pay plan, House Document 119-189 dated Aug. 26, 2026, says base pay and locality pay for civilian federal employees "will not change from the 2026 rates" in 2027.** The plan was transmitted to the Speaker of the House. It also sets military basic pay raises of 5.0 to 7.0 percent. None of it is final yet: Federal News Network reported on Aug. 28, 2026 that the amounts "will not be final until the president signs an executive order to officially set the rates."

## Who gets what in 2027?

The table puts the President's plan next to the two defense bills and the automatic formula. It uses only the figures in the plan letter, the Congressional Research Service (CRS) and the Military Officers Association of America (MOAA). A dash means the sources we used give no figure.

| Group | President's plan (Aug. 26, 2026) | House defense bill (H.R. 8800) | Senate committee bill (S. 4784) | Automatic formula |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Civilian General Schedule (GS) base and locality pay | Freeze at 2026 rates | - | - | Locality pay "would automatically increase by an average of 20.6 percent" (plan letter) |
| Law enforcement personnel | +3.8 percent | - | - | - |
| Military grades E1 to E5 | +7.0 percent | +7 percent | +3.6 percent | 3.6 percent |
| Military grades E6 to O3 | +6.0 percent | +6 percent | +3.6 percent | 3.6 percent |
| Military grades O4 and above | +5.0 percent | +5 percent | +3.6 percent | 3.6 percent |

The plan cites authority under Title 5 of the U.S. Code for the civilian adjustments. The 20.6 percent figure is the plan letter's own description of what locality pay would do without the plan.

## What is the civilian freeze proposal?

House Document 119-189 is titled "Alternative Plan for Pay Adjustments." It says base pay and locality pay for civilian federal employees will not change from the 2026 rates. Without the plan, the document says, locality pay would automatically increase by an average of 20.6 percent. For law enforcement, it directs the Office of Personnel Management (OPM) to "increase 2027 compensation for law enforcement personnel by 3.8 percent."

For comparison, in 2026 Executive Order 14368, signed Dec. 18, 2025, set a 1.0% across-the-board increase for statutory pay systems and kept locality rates at 2025 levels. That was last year's order, not a 2027 figure.

## How much would military pay rise?

The plan sets monthly basic pay raises of 7.0 percent for grades E1 to E5, 6.0 percent for grades E6 to O3 and 5.0 percent for grades O4 and above. CRS Insight IN12706, dated July 14, 2026, says the fiscal year (FY) 2027 budget includes "$5.8 billion for a 7-6-5 percent targeted military pay raise effective on January 1, 2027." CRS describes that as "larger than the automatic annual adjustment of 3.6% for calendar year 2027," which comes from 37 U.S.C. 1009 and is tied to the Employment Cost Index.

## Where does the National Defense Authorization Act stand?

MOAA reported on Oct. 7, 2026 that the House passed H.R. 8800, the National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA), by 216-212 on July 22, 2026, with the 7/6/5 tiers. The Senate Armed Services Committee's S. 4784, reported June 15, funds 3.6 percent across the board. MOAA wrote that "NDAA negotiations will begin in earnest after the election."
