**To find out whether you are registered for Election Day, Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, open your state's official lookup page or the National Association of Secretaries of State (NASS) "Can I Vote" tool, which USAGov points to in its guide updated Oct. 6, 2026.** The Federal Election Commission (FEC) lists Nov. 3, 2026 as the end of its 2026 federal election activity periods. The table below gives the official lookup page for 31 states and Washington, D.C.; every link was opened between Oct. 25 and Oct. 29, 2026.

The printable one-page checklist for this story walks through each check. Pair it with the [November 2026 printable calendar](/november-calendars.html) to mark the day, or put the steps on a [printable weekly calendar](/printable-weekly-calendar.html) for the week before.

## How do I check my voter registration status?

USAGov, the federal government's guide to services, gives these steps (page last updated Oct. 6, 2026):

1. Visit Can I Vote, run by NASS, and select "voter registration status."
2. Choose your state. The tool takes you to your state election website.
3. Check whether you are registered.

USAGov says to check before your state's registration deadline, which "could be up to 30 days before the election." Some deadlines have already passed; our [Oct. 19 registration deadline story](/news/voter-registration-deadline-october-19-2026-early-voting/) covers California, Pennsylvania and Michigan.

Enter personal details only on the official state page or on NASS. Do not type your name, birth date or driver's license number into an unofficial site.

## What should I confirm once I find my record?

USAGov lists these checks:

- **Name and address.** Make sure both match where and how you plan to vote.
- **Party affiliation.** Some states do not list a party because they do not require a declaration.
- **Active status.** Confirm the registration is not marked inactive.
- **Polling place.** Confirm where you vote. A [sample ballot lookup by state](/news/sample-ballot-2026-by-state-official-lookup-links/) shows what is on your ballot, and the [poll closing times story](/news/what-time-do-polls-close-election-day-2026/) covers when polls close.

## What does an inactive voter registration mean?

USAGov warns that "inactive registrations can require extra steps before voting or lead to a provisional ballot." Each state sets its own rules, so what an inactive voter must do depends on the state. Contact your state or local election office to find out.

Federal law sets the outer limits. Under the National Voter Registration Act (NVRA), 52 U.S.C. 20507(b)(2), a registrant may not be removed "by reason of the person's failure to vote." States may use notice procedures for people who did not respond to a notice and did not vote in two consecutive general federal elections. Where a notice was not returned, "affirmation or confirmation of the registrant's address may be required" before voting in a federal election.

## Can I still register if I am not on the list?

It depends on the state. In our earlier story, California and Michigan allow in-person registration through Election Day, and Connecticut offers same-day registration at early voting sites. Pennsylvania has no same-day registration. Texas set its registration deadline at Oct. 5, and on Oct. 16 the Texas Secretary of State reported a record 19,041,684 registered voters. Confirm your state's rule with its election office before you go.
