The U.S. Capitol Christmas Tree has five stops left. After Peoria, Illinois, on Friday, Nov. 13, it is in Richmond, Indiana, on Saturday, Nov. 14 (5 to 7 p.m. ET), Morgantown, West Virginia, on Monday, Nov. 16, Hagerstown, Maryland, and Littlestown, Pennsylvania, on Tuesday, Nov. 17, and Joint Base Andrews, Maryland, on Thursday, Nov. 19 (military ID access only). The Architect of the Capitol (AOC) lists its arrival at the Capitol for Friday, Nov. 20, at 10 a.m. Below are the remaining stops with times and venues, the 16 stops already made, and a one-page printable tracker.

## Where did the 2026 Capitol Christmas Tree come from?

The Architect of the Capitol (AOC) lists the 2026 tree as an Engelmann spruce, 76 feet tall, from the Nez Perce-Clearwater National Forests in Idaho. The project homepage says it was harvested from the Lochsa-Powell Ranger District and that companion trees and handmade ornaments are making the journey too. The number of ornaments is not announced. In an Aug. 13, 2026 release, the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) Forest Service said the tree would be harvested in late October, ahead of a lighting "in early December."

## When will the Capitol tree arrive and be lit?

The tree arrives at the Capitol on Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, at 10 a.m., according to the AOC page "Coming Soon: the 2026 Capitol Christmas Tree." The same page says the lighting ceremony on the West Front Lawn has a date "to be announced." The lighting date and time are not yet announced. The AOC says the tree will then be lit from dusk until 11 p.m. each evening through early January 2027. Visiting the tree is free, and no ticket is required.

When a date is set, mark it on the [December 2026 printable calendar](/december-calendars.html); the day it falls near is also on our [When is Christmas?](/when-is/christmas/) page.

## How is the Capitol tree different from the National Christmas Tree?

They are two separate trees and two separate events. The Capitol tree travels from a national forest to the West Lawn of the U.S. Capitol Building. The National Christmas Tree is lit on the Ellipse near the White House, and its free ticket lottery is run by the National Park Service. Our story on the [National Christmas Tree lighting, ticket lottery and Dec. 3 ceremony](/news/national-christmas-tree-lighting-2026-ticket-lottery-october-28/) has those dates. For other trees and lights, see the [New York holiday calendar with the Rockefeller tree](/news/nyc-holiday-calendar-2026-rockefeller-tree-macys-parade/) and [U.S. Christmas market opening dates](/news/us-christmas-markets-2026-opening-dates-philadelphia-dc-detroit-nyc/).
