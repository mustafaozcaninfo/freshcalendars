**Sovereignty won the 2026 Longines Breeders' Cup Classic at Keeneland Race Course in Lexington, Ky., on Saturday, Oct. 31, beating The Puma by 1 3/4 lengths under jockey Junior Alvarado for trainer Bill Mott, LEX18 and NBC Sports reported.** The $7 million race closed two days and 14 Championship races. All 14 winners are below, followed by the post times and TV windows the races ran to and the printable race tracker.

## When was the Breeders' Cup 2026?

The 43rd Breeders' Cup World Championships were Friday, Oct. 30 and Saturday, Oct. 31, 2026, at Keeneland. The Breeders' Cup says the two days carried 14 Grade 1 races and more than $34 million in purses and awards. Keeneland hosted for the fourth time, after 2015, 2020 and 2022.

Friday's 10-race card started at 11:55 a.m. ET, with five undercard races before the first Championship race. Saturday's 12-race card started at 11:15 a.m. ET, with three undercard races first.

## Was the Breeders' Cup Classic at 3:30 or 6:16 p.m.?

The race itself was scheduled for 6:16 p.m. ET. NBC Sports' Oct. 28 press release headline says the Classic is "this Saturday, Oct. 31, at 3:30 p.m. ET on NBC and Peacock," and that time was when NBC's broadcast window opened. The same release says Saturday's coverage "begins at 1 p.m. ET on USA Network and Peacock and continues on NBC and Peacock at 3:30 p.m. ET." The Breeders' Cup describes the Classic as anchoring "a three-hour NBC broadcast window on Saturday (3:30-6:30 p.m. ET) as Race 12 with a post time of 6:16 p.m. ET."

Some third-party guides list other times, such as 6:25 p.m. for the Classic. We use the Breeders' Cup's own race order.

## Who ran in the 2026 Classic?

The Breeders' Cup's Oct. 21 release named three headliners: Forever Young of Japan, the defending Classic winner; Sovereignty, last year's Kentucky Derby and Belmont Stakes winner and Horse of the Year; and Golden Tempo, this year's Kentucky Derby and Belmont Stakes winner. NBC Sports added that Golden Tempo is trained by Cherie DeVaux, "the first woman to train a Kentucky Derby winner," and that Forever Young was looking "to join Tiznow (2000-01) as the only back-to-back winners of the Classic." Sovereignty won instead, so Tiznow remains the only horse to win the Classic twice in a row.
