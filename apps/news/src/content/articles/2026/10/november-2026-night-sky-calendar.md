Mars and Jupiter pass 1.2° apart in the southeast sky before dawn on Sun., Nov. 15 and Mon., Nov. 16, 2026, by our calculation, and Uranus reaches opposition on Wed., Nov. 25, according to The Planetary Society and EarthSky. The month also brings the Full Beaver Moon on Tue., Nov. 24 at 9:53 a.m. Eastern Standard Time (EST), per the U.S. Naval Observatory (USNO), and three meteor showers. A free one-page [November 2026 night sky calendar](/news/downloads/sky-2026/night-sky-calendar-november-2026.pdf) lists every date below; for a general month grid with holidays, use our [November 2026 printable calendar](/november-calendars.html).

Clocks fall back at 2 a.m. on Sun., Nov. 1 ([when daylight saving time ends](/when-is/daylight-savings/)), so all times here are EST, and sky positions are for New York unless stated.

## When are the Moon phases in November 2026?

The USNO lists four phases for the month, in Universal Time (UT). We converted them to U.S. zones: Eastern Standard Time (EST) is UT minus 5 hours, Central Standard Time (CST) minus 6, Mountain Standard Time (MST) minus 7 and Pacific Standard Time (PST) minus 8.

| Phase | UT | EST | CST | MST | PST |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Last quarter, Sun., Nov. 1 | 20:28 | 3:28 p.m. | 2:28 p.m. | 1:28 p.m. | 12:28 p.m. |
| New Moon, Mon., Nov. 9 | 07:02 | 2:02 a.m. | 1:02 a.m. | 12:02 a.m. | 11:02 p.m., Sun., Nov. 8 |
| First quarter, Tue., Nov. 17 | 11:48 | 6:48 a.m. | 5:48 a.m. | 4:48 a.m. | 3:48 a.m. |

## Which planets can I see in November 2026?

Venus, Jupiter and Mars are in the morning sky, Saturn in the evening, and Uranus is faint. Magnitudes and heights below are our calculations with Astronomy Engine for New York; dates for Mercury and Venus come from EarthSky.

| Planet | Where and when | Brightness |
|---|---|---|
| Venus | Morning sky, east-southeast before dawn; climbs from 19° to 38° from the Sun through the month | Magnitude about -4.5 to -4.9 |
| Mars | Morning sky, in Leo | Magnitude about 0.8 to 0.6 |
| Jupiter | Morning sky, in Leo | Magnitude about -2.0 to -2.2 |
| Mercury | Morning sky near Nov. 20-21, low in the east-southeast | Greatest western elongation 20° from the Sun |
| Saturn | Evening sky | Magnitude about 0.5 |
| Uranus | All night around Nov. 25, in Taurus | Magnitude 5.6 |

Venus passed inferior conjunction, between the Sun and Earth, at 4 UT on Oct. 24, 2026, according to EarthSky, and became a morning object afterward. On Sat., Nov. 7 at 6 a.m. EST a crescent Moon sits 1.6° from Venus, about 10° up in the east-southeast, with sunrise at 6:33 a.m.

Mercury reaches greatest western elongation at 0 UT on Nov. 21, which is 6 p.m. Central Time on Nov. 20, EarthSky says, 20° from the Sun. Look low in the east-southeast about 45 minutes before sunrise around Nov. 20-21. In New York on Nov. 21, sunrise is about 6:49 a.m. EST and Mercury stands about 9° up 45 minutes earlier, with Venus higher at about 22°.

Saturn is the evening planet. The Moon passes about 6° from it on the evening of Fri., Nov. 20.

## When do Mars and Jupiter meet?

Mars and Jupiter are closest, 1.2° apart, on Nov. 15 at about 9 p.m. EST, when both are below the horizon in New York. The practical views are before dawn on Sun., Nov. 15 and Mon., Nov. 16, when the pair is still 1.2° apart and about 60° up in the southeast at 5 a.m. EST. Both planets are in Leo all month, Jupiter at magnitude about -2.0 to -2.2 and Mars at about 0.8 to 0.6, so Jupiter is the brighter of the two.

The Moon also visits the pair twice, on both ends of the month:

- Mon., Nov. 2, 5 a.m. EST: the Moon is 2.0° from Mars, with Jupiter about 7° away, high in the southeast.
- Mon., Nov. 30, 5 a.m. EST: the Moon is 1.9° from Jupiter, with Mars about 5° away, high in the south.

## When is Uranus at opposition?

Uranus is at opposition on Wed., Nov. 25, 2026, The Planetary Society lists in its 2026 calendar of space events. EarthSky puts the moment at 23 UT on Nov. 25, which is 6 p.m. EST, with Uranus in front of Taurus the Bull, the same constellation as the Pleiades.

Opposition means Earth sits between the Sun and the planet. Uranus then rises at sunset, stays up all night and is at its brightest for the year: EarthSky gives magnitude +5.6, the brightest for 2026. That is near the naked-eye limit under a very dark sky, so binoculars and a finder chart are the realistic way to find it.

## Which meteor showers peak in November 2026?

The American Meteor Society (AMS) lists three showers. Our [November 2026 meteor showers guide](/news/november-2026-meteor-showers-taurids-leonids/) covers each in depth; this table shows how they fit the sky calendar.

| Shower | Active | Peak | Moon at peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Southern Taurids | Sept. 20 to Nov. 20 | Night of Nov. 4-5 | 25% lit, waning crescent |
| Northern Taurids | Oct. 20 to Dec. 10 | Night of Nov. 11-12 | 4% lit |
| Leonids | Nov. 6-30 | Night of Nov. 16-17 | 39% lit |

The AMS calls the Southern Taurids "rich in fireballs", and its fireball-swarm component peaks on Nov. 5. For the Leonids, NASA lists fewer than 3 meteors per hour under dark skies, at 43.5 miles (69.7 kilometers) per second from comet 55P/Tempel-Tuttle, best viewed starting at about midnight local time. The Moon sets about 10:50 p.m. EST in New York on Nov. 16 (our calculation), so the sky is Moon-free by midnight that night.

## Does the Moon cover Pleiades stars again on Nov. 24?

For the western United States, yes. Before dawn on Tue., Nov. 24, the nearly full Moon covers stars of the Pleiades, in our calculation with Astronomy Engine. It is a repeat of the Oct. 27 event, when the Moon covered the same cluster for the eastern U.S. The Moon moves east against the stars by about its own width each hour, so a star vanishes at one edge and reappears at the other; binoculars help next to such a bright Moon.

| City | Star covered | Times |
|---|---|---|
| Denver | Taygeta | 5:03 to 5:56 a.m. MST |
| Denver | Maia | 5:22 to 6:04 a.m. MST |
| Salt Lake City | Maia | 5:21 to 6:01 a.m. MST |
| Seattle | Maia | 4:05 to 4:55 a.m. PST |
| Los Angeles | Taygeta only, the fainter star | 4:14 to 4:52 a.m. PST |

San Francisco sees only Taygeta as well. From the East the Moon is setting or it is daytime, so the covering is not visible there.

## How should I plan a night out?

- Check the time zone: from Nov. 1 every listed time is standard time.
- For Mars, Jupiter and Venus, go out before dawn. For Saturn, use the evening.
- For the Leonids, start at about midnight local time, per NASA, and note the Moon sets before then on Nov. 16.
- For Mercury, find a clear east-southeast horizon about 45 minutes before sunrise on Nov. 20-21.
- For Uranus, bring binoculars and a finder chart, and look in Taurus.
