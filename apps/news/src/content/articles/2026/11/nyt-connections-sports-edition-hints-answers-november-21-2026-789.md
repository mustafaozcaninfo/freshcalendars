**Connections: Sports Edition #789, the Saturday, Nov. 21, 2026 puzzle from The Athletic and New York Times (NYT) Games.** Each color has two tap-to-open hints, and the answers wait under the spoiler heading.

One tile on today's board is a famous name that points the wrong way, and a few others read like speed words. Our [Connections page](/news/topic/nyt-connections/#how-it-works) covers the rules; Saturday's question is which tiles are being straightforward and which are not.

## Saturday's hints for #789

Start with the tiles you can place by geography, and leave the odd ones for last.

- **Hint 1 (yellow):** Each tile is a place or a surface where a race can be run.
- **Hint 2 (yellow):** One tile is two words, and one is a single word you may have to look up.
- **Hint 1 (green):** All four tiles are nicknames of teams, shortened to one athlete.
- **Hint 2 (green):** The teams all come from one U.S. state, and one of the four tiles has eight letters, the longest in the group.
- **Hint 1 (blue):** Each tile is part of the name of a stadium that is no longer used by a big-league baseball team.
- **Hint 2 (blue):** Three of the four tiles are not the whole name, so something like "Field" or "Stadium" is missing.
- **Hint 1 (purple):** Every tile ends in a word that is also the second half of a women's pro soccer club's name.
- **Hint 2 (purple):** The tiles start with a number, a color, a kind of water movement and a group word.

## Why Saturday's tiles tempt the wrong group

Duke is the loudest trap. Most solvers hear a North Carolina university first, and a board with a college nickname or two invites that thought. The tile belongs with Virginia athletes, so a school in another state is the wrong lead.

Green Wave looks like a college nickname on its face, so it pulls toward the athletes group. It is the end of a phrase instead. The same goes for Team Spirit, which reads as a phrase about morale until you drop the city from a club name.

Speed words are the other bait. 100-Meter Dash and Speedway both suggest a race, and Track sounds like a race too, so a quick solver stacks them together. Rip Current and Swimming Pool both mean water, which makes a second false group. Check each guess against the whole board before you submit it. ## Saturday's answers, #789, Nov. 21, 2026 (spoilers)

Stop here if you still want to solve it. Beebom and Nerd's Chalk list the same four groups and sixteen entries. The group names below are those sites' descriptions, not official wording.

- **Yellow, race venues:** Speedway, Swimming Pool, Track, Velodrome
- **Green, Virginia college athletes:** Cavalier, Duke, Hokie, Monarch
- **Blue, former MLB ballparks:** Astrodome, Ebbets, Milwaukee County, Three Rivers
- **Purple, phrases ending in a National Women's Soccer League (NWSL) team's name:** 100-Meter Dash, Green Wave, Rip Current, Team Spirit

## Who each entry is

- **Cavalier:** a member of the Virginia Cavaliers, the University of Virginia's teams, per virginiasports.com.
- **Hokie:** a member of the Virginia Tech Hokies, per hokiesports.com.
- **Monarch:** a member of the Old Dominion Monarchs of Norfolk, Virginia, per odusports.com.
- **Duke:** it is not Duke University in North Carolina. Here it belongs to the Virginia group; the school is not confirmed on an official page in our check, so none is named.
- **Astrodome, Ebbets, Milwaukee County, Three Rivers:** the former Major League Baseball (MLB) ballparks Astrodome, Ebbets Field, Milwaukee County Stadium and Three Rivers Stadium.
- **100-Meter Dash:** ends in Dash, for the Houston Dash.
- **Green Wave:** ends in Wave, for San Diego Wave FC.
- **Rip Current:** ends in Current, for the Kansas City Current.
- **Team Spirit:** ends in Spirit, for the Washington Spirit.
- **Speedway, Swimming Pool, Track, Velodrome:** each is a place to race; a velodrome is a banked track for cycling.

The four clubs are on the NWSL list of 16 teams at nwslsoccer.com.

## The city is dropped in the purple group

| Entry | Club it ends with | Word kept |
|---|---|---|
| 100-Meter Dash | Houston Dash | Dash |

| Team Spirit | Washington Spirit | Spirit |

Each phrase keeps only the club's last word, and each club name is on the NWSL page. Green Wave is the one most likely to read as a college team.
