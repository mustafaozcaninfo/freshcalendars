**Strands #993 from The New York Times (NYT) is the puzzle for Saturday, Nov. 21, 2026, and its clue is "Fix this, please."** The puzzle data credits Christina Iverson as constructor and Tracy Bennett as editor. The board hides an 11-letter spangram and six theme words; the rules are on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). Hints come first, then start cells and a path map, and the answers wait under a spoiler warning at the bottom.

The clue is an instruction, and the words it points to belong to the work of getting a piece of writing ready to print.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: A short note at the head of a published piece that flags a fix or clarifies something.
- Hint 2: It has 11 letters, and both its first and last letter are E.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is the top-left corner of the board, row 1, column 1, and it begins by moving down the left edge.

## Theme word hints

Six words fit the clue, listed from shortest to longest:

- **5 letters, starts with E, has three R's:** a slip in a draft that a reader would circle.
- **5 letters, starts with W, ends with Y:** using more words than the point needs.
- **6 letters, starts with C, ends with E:** a phrase worn smooth from overuse.
- **6 letters, starts with S, ends with E:** the person or document a reporter's fact comes from.
- **7 letters, starts with A, has two W's:** clumsy, like a sentence that trips over itself.
- **8 letters, starts with S, ends with G:** getting the letters of each word in the right order.

Two of the six theme words end in the same letter and have the same length.

Rows count from the top and columns from the left; the first step is the direction from a word's first letter to its second. No word is named here.

- **5 letters, start cell row 8, column 3:** the first step goes straight right.
- **5 letters, start cell row 7, column 5:** the first step goes straight up.
- **6 letters, start cell row 5, column 2:** the first step goes straight left.
- **6 letters, start cell row 8, column 2:** the first step goes straight up.
- **7 letters, start cell row 3, column 2:** the first step goes straight right.
- **8 letters, start cell row 2, column 2:** the first step goes straight up.

## Where the spangram crosses the Nov. 21 board

Read in row and column cells, the spangram has 10 steps and none is diagonal. It drops from row 1, column 1 to row 4, column 1, runs right across all of row 4 to column 6, then goes down two more tiles to row 6, column 6. That makes a shape like a hockey stick lying on its side. Row 4 is entirely spangram. Rows 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 hold one spangram tile each, and rows 7 and 8 hold none. Column 1 holds 4 spangram tiles, column 6 holds 3, and columns 2 to 5 hold one apiece, all in row 4.

Because the spangram runs the full width of row 4, it cuts the grid in two. The top half, rows 1 to 4, has 24 tiles: 9 for the spangram, 7 for the 7-letter word and 8 for the 8-letter word. The bottom half has the other 24: 2 for the spangram and 22 split among the four shorter words, so every tile in rows 7 and 8 belongs to a theme word.

Counting only theme-word letters in four blocks of 12 tiles, the lower left (rows 5 to 8, columns 1 to 3) is the densest. All 12 tiles are theme letters: one 6-letter word is there whole, five letters of the other 6-letter word, and the first tile of one 5-letter word. The lower right has 10, the upper right 9 and the upper left 6, because the spangram claims 6 of its 12 tiles.

The six theme words take 31 steps between them. Counted by direction, 14 run across the board (13 right, 1 left), 15 run vertically (9 up, 6 down) and 2 are diagonal, one each in a 6-letter and a 5-letter word. With the spangram, 39 of the board's 41 steps are straight.
