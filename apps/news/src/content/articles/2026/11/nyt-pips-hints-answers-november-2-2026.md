## How Pips works

You place every domino so that each colored region meets its condition. A number means the pips in the region add up to it; = means every half shows the same value; ≠ means all are different; > and < set a total above or below a number; a region with no condition takes anything. Rows are counted from the top and columns from the left, so R2C5 is the second row, fifth column.

## Monday's Pips hints, Nov. 2, 2026

**Easy (five dominoes: 0-5, 3-4, 1-3, 1-4, 1-0)**

- Hint 1: R1C2 and R1C5 must each add up to 1, so each holds a 1. Only the 1-3, 1-4 and 1-0 dominoes have a 1.
- Hint 2: R3C6 must be more than 4, so it holds a 5. The 0-5 domino is the only one with a 5, so it fills R3C5 and R3C6.

**Medium (seven dominoes: 4-3, 5-1, 3-1, 2-5, 5-4, 4-4, 2-3)**

- Hint 1: R3C1 must be more than 3, so it holds a 4 or a 5.
- Hint 2: R1C6, R2C6 and R3C6 are equal, so you need three matching halves. The 4-4 domino supplies two of them, and a 4 appears on four halves in all.

**Hard (sixteen dominoes on an eight-row board)**

- Hint 1: R1C5, R2C6 and R3C5 must each add up to 1, so each holds a 1. Six halves show a 1, spread over the 3-1, 1-1 (two halves), 1-4, 1-6 and 1-2 dominoes.
- Hint 2: R2C7 must be more than 5, so it holds a 6. Only the 2-6 and 1-6 dominoes have a 6.

## Solving tips

Look for single cells with a limit: a cell that must be more than 3, as on Monday's Medium board, can only hold a 4 or a 5 there. An "equal" region shrinks your options fast once one value is known; a double such as the 4-4 can supply two matching halves at once. On the Hard board, count the single cells with an exact sum: three need a 1, two need a 2 and two need a 3, so seven of the 32 cells are fixed from the start. R4C1 must be less than 1, which makes it a 0.
