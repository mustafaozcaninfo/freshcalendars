**The New York Times (NYT) Midi Crossword for Monday, Nov. 23, 2026, is titled "The Descent of Man," a 9-by-9 grid with 31 clues by Natan Last, edited by Ian Livengood.** Every clue has a hint behind a tap, and the answers sit in the marked section at the bottom.

## What does "The Descent of Man" tell you about the grid?

The title is an instruction as much as a name. Read "descent" in its plain sense, as a direction of travel, and the last word as a short string of letters you can look for. No clue carries a question mark or a bracket to point at the theme, so the grid itself is where the trick shows. Four of the 14 Down entries take part, and they all fall in the odd-numbered columns. The full map is in the spoiler section.

## Which clue is the least literal today?

20-Across, *Palindromic name hidden in "headache"*. The answer is not found by knowledge, and it is not a word you would guess from the meaning of "headache". The clue asks you to look inside the printed word itself, where a three-letter name that reads the same in both directions is buried between its other letters. It begins and ends with A, and it is a girl's name.

## Where should you start?

The top-left corner is the friendliest. 1-Across and 1-Down share a square, and 7-Across and 9-Across each give you a letter of a long Down entry. 3-Down is crossed by 1-Across, 7-Across, 9-Across, 13-Across, 23-Across and 25-Across, so every answer you fill in on the left side hands you a letter of it.
