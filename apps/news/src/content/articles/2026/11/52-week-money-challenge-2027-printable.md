**The 52-week money challenge saves $1 in week 1, $2 in week 2 and $52 in week 52, which totals $1,378; in 2027 week 1 is the week of Monday, Jan. 4 and week 52 is the week of Monday, Dec. 27.** Fresh Calendars computed the dates from the 2027 calendar and the total as the sum of 1 to 52. The free printable tracker lists every date, and the reverse and half versions are below.

2027 starts on a Friday, so a Monday start puts week 1 on Jan. 4, and 2027 has 52 Mondays. Pair the tracker with our [2027 printable yearly calendar](/printable-yearly-calendar.html) or the [printable weekly calendar](/printable-weekly-calendar.html).

## How much do you save each quarter?

The classic order saves the most in the last 13 weeks. The reverse order saves the most in the first 13. The half version saves half of each classic amount, from $0.50 in week 1 to $26 in week 52, for $689 in all.

| Weeks | Dates | Classic | Reverse | Half |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-13 | Jan. 4 to March 29 | $91 | $598 | $45.50 |
| 14-26 | April 5 to June 28 | $260 | $429 | $130 |
| 27-39 | July 5 to Sept. 27 | $429 | $260 | $214.50 |
| 40-52 | Oct. 4 to Dec. 27 | $598 | $91 | $299 |
| Year | Jan. 4 to Dec. 27 | $1,378 | $1,378 | $689 |

By the week of June 28 (week 26), the classic order has saved $351 and the reverse order $1,027.

## How does the reverse 52-week challenge work?

The reverse challenge puts $52 in the week of Jan. 4, $51 in the week of Jan. 11 and ends with $1 in the week of Dec. 27. In January alone, weeks 1 to 4 add up to $202, against $10 for the classic order. If you expect December to cost more than January, the reverse order saves the large amounts first. The classic order suits a smaller start.

## How much interest would the money earn?

The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) publishes national average deposit rates. Its October 2026 table, last updated Oct. 19, 2026, lists these:

| Account type | National rate, October 2026 |
|---|---|
| Savings | 0.38% |
| Interest checking | 0.07% |
| Money market | 0.63% |
| 12-month certificate of deposit (CD) | 1.76% |

The FDIC says the money market rate represents an average of the $10,000 and $100,000 product tiers. These are national averages, and no bank is shown to pay them.

As a simple-interest illustration at 0.38%, with each deposit made on its Monday and held to Dec. 31, 2027, the classic order earns about $1.78 and the reverse order about $3.49. The reverse order earns more because its large deposits are held longer. Real accounts differ in rate, compounding and fees, so treat these as estimates.

## Is the money insured?

The FDIC says deposit insurance "covers $250,000 per depositor, per FDIC-insured bank, for each account ownership category." The National Credit Union Administration (NCUA) says "the $250,000 in coverage applies to each share owner, per insured credit union, for each account ownership category." A challenge total of $1,378 sits well inside both limits.

TreasuryDirect lists a composite rate of 5.51% and a fixed rate of 1.40% for I bonds issued Nov. 1, 2026 to April 30, 2027. We left out the rules for holding I bonds; read them on TreasuryDirect before you buy.
