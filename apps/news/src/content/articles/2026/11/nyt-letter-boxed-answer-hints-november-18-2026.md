## Which side is the trap today?

The counts come from the day's word list, one row per side. Each word is counted once at its first letter and once at its last.

| Side | Letters | Words that start on it | Words that end on it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A, C, P | 175 | 173 |
| 2 | H, O, R | 104 | 158 |
| 3 | D, U, G | 98 | 96 |
| 4 | W, T, M | 215 | 165 |

Side 3 is the trap. It has the fewest openers (98) and the fewest closers (96), so any route has to pass through it in the middle or squeeze one word out of it at an edge. Side 4 is the opposite, with 215 openers, and the M alone starts 101 words. Inside side 3, the U is rare: 10 words begin with it, against 49 for the D and 39 for the G.

## How thin are the routes and letter pairs?

Letters on one side cannot touch, which leaves 108 ordered pairs that can. In the word list, 14 of them never occur: C then D, G, W or M; P then G or M; H then G or M; R then W; D then P; G then C or P; W then C; and M then H. The C appears in 6 of the 14, so it is the awkward letter, with the G close behind in 5. Another 11 pairs occur in exactly one word.

The longest word has 10 letters, and only one word reaches that length. No word covers the box: five words tie for the widest at 8 of the 12 letters. Chaining every pair of words that share a join letter and use all twelve letters gives 2 two-word routes.

| Join letter | Two-word routes |
|---|---|
| G | 2 |

Both routes use the same first word and join on the G. Their totals are 13 and 15 letters.

## Five hints, light to strong

1. The Times' own route is two words, three under the par of 5.
2. The first word has 8 letters, one of 22 that long. The second has 5 letters, one of 111.
3. The first word opens on the W/T/M side and closes on the D/U/G side. The second opens on that D/U/G side and closes on the A/C/P side.
4. The U and the P sit in the second word. The H and the D sit in the first.
5. The first word starts with W and ends in G. The second starts with G and ends in P.
