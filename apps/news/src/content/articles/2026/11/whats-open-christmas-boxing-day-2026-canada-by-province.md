**Stores must close on Christmas Day, Friday, Dec. 25, 2026, and on Boxing Day, Saturday, Dec. 26, in Nova Scotia, New Brunswick and Newfoundland and Labrador**, according to each province's own holiday chart or statute. Quebec closes large food stores on Dec. 25 but lets them open on Dec. 26, the Government of Quebec says.

Each rule sits next to Canada Post, Bank of Canada and Toronto Transit Commission (TTC) dates, with a link to the page each row came from. A one-page printable follows the same rows. For whether Dec. 26 is a stat holiday, see our story on [Boxing Day 2026 in Canada](/news/boxing-day-2026-canada-saturday-december-26-day-off-monday-december-28/). The date itself is on [when is Christmas Day](/when-is/christmas-day/).

## What do the Nova Scotia, New Brunswick and Newfoundland laws say?

Nova Scotia's Labour, Skills and Immigration department lists Christmas Day (Dec. 25, 2026) and Boxing Day (Dec. 26, 2026) in its table of designated retail closing days. The page's exemptions were not confirmed, so none are listed here.

New Brunswick's Days of Rest Act names "Christmas Day" and "Boxing Day" among its prescribed days of rest. The act exempts a drug store with under 850 square metres of public area and a retail business that normally employs no more than three people and sells mainly food, newspapers, tobacco or confectionery. The text is as consolidated on the province's laws site, which is marked current to Jan. 1, 2024.

Newfoundland and Labrador's Shops' Closing Act lists "Christmas Day" and "Boxing Day" as holidays "in all areas of the province". On a holiday a shop owner "shall not admit members of the public to that shop." The exemptions in that act were not confirmed here.

## What are Quebec's rules for food stores?

Quebec sorts food stores by size. On Dec. 25 large stores must close, while small stores keep their normal hours. On Dec. 26 large stores may open between 6 a.m. and 9 p.m. under a pilot project, and small stores keep normal hours, according to a Quebec page last updated Nov. 9, 2026.

On Dec. 24 and Dec. 31 stores may open without restriction before 5 p.m. After 5 p.m. they may stay open until midnight with at most four people working. Large stores close again on Jan. 1; small stores keep normal hours, and all food stores return to normal hours on Jan. 2. Quebec's page adds that pharmacies may open all day, 24 hours a day, including on statutory holidays.

## What about Ontario, British Columbia and the other provinces?

Ontario's Retail Business Holidays Act decides which days most stores close there. Its official page could not be read on Nov. 19, 2026, so check ontario.ca before you go.

British Columbia lists Christmas Day, Friday, Dec. 25, as a statutory holiday and says Boxing Day is not one. Its page says nothing about store closing. Manitoba, Prince Edward Island, Alberta and Saskatchewan are not confirmed here.

## When do Canada Post, banks and transit take Boxing Day?

Canada Post lists Christmas Day on Dec. 25, Boxing Day on Monday, Dec. 28, and New Year's Day on Jan. 1, all as national holidays. The Bank of Canada lists Dec. 25, 2026, as Christmas Day and Dec. 28, 2026, as Boxing Day, both national holidays. That is the central bank's schedule, not a rule for every bank branch.

The Canada Revenue Agency's public holiday list shows Boxing Day on Saturday, Dec. 26, 2026, with no Dec. 28 row. For mailing dates, see [Canada Post Christmas shipping deadlines](/news/canada-post-holiday-shipping-deadlines-2026-christmas/).

In Toronto the TTC runs Sunday service on Friday, Dec. 25, with the subway starting around 8 a.m., and holiday service on Saturday, Dec. 26. TransLink in Metro Vancouver says the next statutory holiday is Christmas Day on Friday, Dec. 25; it has not posted Boxing Day service yet. Liquor store, mall and other transit hours are not confirmed. The [December 2026 printable calendar](/december-calendars.html) and the [holidays calendar](/holidays/) help with planning the week.
