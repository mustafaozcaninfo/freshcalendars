**Connections: Sports Edition #788, the Friday, Nov. 20, 2026 puzzle from The Athletic and New York Times (NYT) Games, mixes ordinary words with the names of Hall of Fame players.** Each color has two tap-to-open hints, and the answers wait under the spoiler heading.

Today's board asks you to read some tiles as plain words and others as pieces of a name. Our [Connections page](/news/topic/nyt-connections/#how-it-works) explains the rules; the question on Friday is which reading each tile wants.

## Friday's hints, #788, Nov. 20, 2026

Scan the board for first names that could precede more than one tile.

- **Hint 1 (yellow):** Each tile is something you put on your head or face.
- **Hint 2 (yellow):** Two of the four tiles have three letters.
- **Hint 1 (green):** All four tiles are physical parts of one sport's scoring target.
- **Hint 2 (green):** One of these tiles has nine letters, the longest on the board.
- **Hint 1 (blue):** One short first name leads into all four tiles, which are the second halves.
- **Hint 2 (blue):** One of the four tiles is a three-letter word you could say in everyday talk.
- **Hint 1 (purple):** A single word that is both a city and a first name leads into all four tiles.
- **Hint 2 (purple):** Two of the four tiles are everyday words, one for how fast you move and one for a large town.

## Where Friday's board sets traps

Mask and Net each bring a goalkeeper to mind, so pairing them feels natural. They belong to different colors, and that pair is the quickest way to burn a mistake. Post can read as a mail or newspaper word, which pulls it toward a group of its own that the board does not have.

The word tiles are the other problem. Pace, City, Magic and Pride all pass as ordinary vocabulary, yet one of them needs a person's name in front of it, and the puzzle spreads the word-like tiles over more than one color. Guy is another plain word that hides a name. The longest tile on the board is the most unfamiliar word, so solvers who know it can start there. ## Friday's answers, #788, Nov. 20, 2026 (spoilers)

Stop here if you still want to solve it. Beebom and Nerd's Chalk list the same four groups and sixteen entries.

- **Yellow, "Headgear":** Cap, Hat, Mask, Visor
- **Green, "Parts of a Soccer Goal":** Crossbar, Net, Post, Stanchion
- **Blue, "Ray ___":** Allen, Bourque, Guy, Kinsella
- **Purple, "Orlando ___":** City, Magic, Pace, Pride

## Who each entry is

- **Allen:** Ray Allen, an NBA shooting guard who played for Milwaukee, Seattle, Boston and Miami and went into the Naismith Hall of Fame in 2018, per NBA.com.
- **Bourque:** Ray Bourque, a defenseman drafted by the Boston Bruins in 1979 and inducted into the Hockey Hall of Fame in 2004, per the Hockey Hall of Fame.
- **Guy:** Ray Guy, a punter who spent all 14 of his seasons with the Raiders, was the first punter taken in the first round and entered the Pro Football Hall of Fame in the Class of 2014, per the Hall.
- **Kinsella:** the name that completes "Ray ___"; nothing more is confirmed here.
- **Pace:** Orlando Pace, an offensive tackle taken first overall in the 1997 NFL Draft by the St. Louis Rams and a member of the Pro Football Hall of Fame Class of 2016, per the Hall.
- **City, Magic, Pride:** Orlando City, the Orlando Magic and the Orlando Pride are the Orlando clubs that complete the group.
- **Cap, Hat, Mask, Visor:** each is worn on the head or face, per the group.
- **Crossbar, Net, Post, Stanchion:** each is a part of a soccer goal, per the group.

## The Hall of Fame thread in Friday's names

Four of the names are enshrined, and no two share the same year. Bourque went in with the Hockey Hall of Fame in 2004, Guy with the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 2014, Pace with the same football hall in 2016 and Allen with the Naismith Hall of Fame in 2018.

| Name | Sport | Hall of Fame | Year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ray Bourque | Hockey | Hockey Hall of Fame | 2004 |
| Ray Guy | Football | Pro Football Hall of Fame | 2014 |
| Orlando Pace | Football | Pro Football Hall of Fame | 2016 |
| Ray Allen | Basketball | Naismith Hall of Fame | 2018 |

Guy and Pace share a hall but sit in different colors, because one is filed under Ray and the other under Orlando.
