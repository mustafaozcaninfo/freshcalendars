## Test your own pangram, letter by letter

A pangram holds the center and every petal at least once. Write the seven letters in a row and tick each one found in your word:

| Tick | Letter | Role | Must appear |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | D | Center | at least once |
| 2 | A | Petal | at least once |
| 3 | E | Petal | at least once |
| 4 | M | Petal | at least once |
| 5 | P | Petal | at least once |
| 6 | R | Petal | at least once |
| 7 | V | Petal | at least once |

Seven ticks and you have a pangram. Six ticks is a good long word, not a pangram. To see how this fails, take a find built from D, A, E, M, P and R only: the V is missing, so it scores 6 of 7. A find with all six petals but no D fails for a different reason, because the center letter is missing.

Length alone proves nothing. A nine-letter find can miss one tick, and a pangram can be short, since seven distinct letters is the minimum. Counting slots helps: if your word has seven letters, each of D, A, E, M, P, R and V must appear exactly once. If it has more, the extra slots are repeats of letters you already ticked.

## Pangram hints, light to heavy

- Hint 1: The solver pages share one pangram, and it is longer than the flower.
- Hint 2: It has eight letters, one more than the seven in play.
- Hint 3: Run the tick check on it and exactly one letter is ticked twice.
- Hint 4: The repeated letter is a vowel petal, not the center.
- Hint 5: It begins with R and ends with the center letter D.

## Why one repeated vowel hides the pangram

Eight slots and seven letters leave one slot over, and it is filled by a petal you have already used. A search that places each letter once stops at seven and finds nothing. The slot over is the trap. If a seven-letter skeleton of D, A, E, M, P, R and V leaves you stuck, add one repeat from the two vowel petals and try the word again.
