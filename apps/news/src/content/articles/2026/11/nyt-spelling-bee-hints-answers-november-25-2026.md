**Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026, puts I in the middle of a Spelling Bee from The New York Times (NYT), with B, K, M, N, P and U on the outside.** This is a short list: 16 words worth 77 points, with Genius at 54. Four-letter words barely move the score.

Every answer has to hold I, and only one other vowel letter, U, is on the board. The [Spelling Bee page](/news/topic/nyt-spelling-bee/#how-it-works) explains scoring; the sections below stay with this small board and the 54-point bar.

## The pangram clues

- **Length:** the pangram has 7 letters, the longest on the list, and no letter repeats.
- **The ends:** it starts with B and finishes with N, and it is worth 14 points.
- **The company:** two other 7-letter answers exist, and neither is a pangram.

## What makes the 54-point bar hard to reach?

The list is short, so each miss costs more. Counting different letters per answer: 1 answer holds all seven, 1 holds six, 9 hold four and 5 hold three. The answer with six different letters misses only B, so the nearest miss is a single letter away.

Two answers repeat a letter back to back, one with BB and one with PP.

Four-letter words score 1 point, longer words 1 point per letter, and the pangram adds 7. The running total adds the rows from the top.

- **Six letters lead:** 4 words hold 24 points, nearly a third of the 77.
- **The 7-letter block:** two words are worth 7 points each and the pangram is worth 14.

- **Longest answers:** three of 7 letters, four of 6, four of 5 and five of 4.
- **Rare starts:** BI, BU, KI, NI, NU and PU begin one answer each, while MI and PI begin 4 apiece.

## The gap from four-letter words to Genius

The 5 four-letter answers give 5 points, about 9% of the 54 needed. That leaves 49 points to find among the 11 answers of five letters or more, which hold 72 in all, 14 of them from the pangram. Without the pangram the list is worth 63 points, 9 above the bar.
