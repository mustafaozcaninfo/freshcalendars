**Strands #1000 from The New York Times (NYT) is the Saturday, Nov. 28, 2026 puzzle, and the theme clue is "Feeling nostalgic."** Christina Iverson is listed as constructor and Tracy Bennett as editor. Counting from #1 on March 4, 2024, this is the 1,000th game in the numbering. Five theme words and a 12-letter spangram fill the board, and the rules sit on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works) if you need a refresher before a round this long.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: Every theme word is the title of a TV series. The spangram says when those series aired and what kind of entertainment they were.
- Hint 2: It has 12 letters, begins with N and ends with W, and it is two words joined. Four different letters each appear twice.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 1, column 1, in the top left corner, and the first step goes straight down.

## Theme word hints

Five series fit the clue, listed by length, shortest first:

- **7 letters, starts with C, ends with D:** how you feel when something pleases you greatly, or when a spell has been cast on you. All seven letters differ.
- **7 letters, starts with F, ends with S:** the people you choose to spend time with and trust. All seven letters differ.
- **7 letters, starts with R, ends with S:** a playful name for small children who crawl across the floor. One letter appears twice, and it is the first letter.
- **7 letters, starts with B, ends with M:** what a fruit tree does in spring when its buds open. Two different letters each appear twice.
- **8 letters, starts with F, ends with Y:** a word for deep, lasting happiness. One letter appears twice.

## Start-cell hints

Rows count from the top and columns from the left. The first step is the direction from a word's first tile to its second. No word is named here.

- **7 letters, start cell row 6, column 3:** the first step goes straight left.
- **7 letters, start cell row 3, column 2:** the first step goes straight up.
- **7 letters, start cell row 7, column 4:** the first step goes straight down.
- **7 letters, start cell row 5, column 6:** the first step goes diagonally down and to the left.
- **8 letters, start cell row 2, column 4:** the first step goes straight right.

## The Nov. 28 spangram path and the board map

Start in the top left corner and walk down column 1 for three steps to row 4. The path then turns and runs straight along row 4, five steps to the right edge at column 6. From there it falls away diagonally, down and to the left, for three more steps: row 5, column 5, then row 6, column 4, and finally row 7, column 3.

That makes 11 steps in all: 3 straight down, 5 straight right and 3 diagonal. No step goes up, and the only leftward moves come in the diagonal tail. All 6 columns hold at least one spangram tile (column 1 has 4, the rest 2 or 1), and rows 1 to 7 each hold one or more. Only row 8 is free of it.

Row 4 is the spangram from edge to edge, so the theme words cannot cross the board's middle. Two words live in rows 1 to 3 and three in rows 5 to 8:

| Row | Spangram tiles | Theme tiles and where they come from |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 5 from the 7-letter word that starts at row 3, column 2 |
| 2 | 1 | 4 from the 8-letter word, 1 from the 7-letter word that starts at row 3, column 2 |
| 3 | 1 | 4 from the 8-letter word, 1 from the 7-letter word that starts at row 3, column 2 |
| 4 | 6 | none |
| 5 | 1 | 4 from the 7-letter word that starts at row 6, column 3, 1 from the word that starts at row 5, column 6 |
| 6 | 1 | 3 from the word that starts at row 6, column 3, 2 from the word that starts at row 5, column 6 |
| 7 | 1 | 3 from the word that starts at row 7, column 4, 2 from the word that starts at row 5, column 6 |
| 8 | 0 | 4 from the word that starts at row 7, column 4, 2 from the word that starts at row 5, column 6 |

Several words here fill neat rectangles. The 8-letter word takes every tile of the 2-by-4 block in rows 2 and 3, columns 3 to 6, looping right along row 2, back along row 3 and up one tile. Below the middle row, three words each fill a block of eight tiles with one tile left over, and in all three the left-over tile belongs to the spangram:

- The word from row 6, column 3 fills rows 5 and 6, columns 1 to 4, except row 6, column 4.
- The word from row 7, column 4 fills rows 7 and 8, columns 1 to 4, except row 7, column 3, where the spangram ends.
- The word from row 5, column 6 fills rows 5 to 8, columns 5 and 6, except row 5, column 5.

So the diagonal tail of the spangram plugs one corner of each of the three lower blocks.

## Solving tips

Only two words sit above row 4: one runs along row 1 and the other loops through rows 2 and 3. Once those two are marked off, the left column and row 4 stand out as the spangram.
