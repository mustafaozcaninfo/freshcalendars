**Joel Fagliano built the New York Times (NYT) Mini Crossword for Friday, Nov. 13, 2026, a 5-by-5 grid with 10 clues and two black squares side by side in the bottom left corner.** Each clue has a hint that opens on a tap; the filled grid sits in the marked spoiler section at the end.

## Hints for every clue

**Across**

- **1-Across** (5 letters), *England's neighbor to the west*: a country, not a county; it starts with W and ends with S, the letter that opens 5-Down.
- **6-Across** (5 letters), *Something a selfie taker tries to get right*: the tilt of the phone relative to your face; it starts with A and ends with E.
- **7-Across** (5 letters), *Where emails pile up*: the folder where new messages land; it starts with I, and its last letter is the X in the middle of 5-Down.
- **8-Across** (5 letters), *"I love you a ___!" (coffee drinker's pun)*: read the quoted line aloud and the blank sounds like a phrase for "very much"; the spelling in the grid names a milky espresso drink. It starts with L and ends with E.
- **9-Across** (3 letters), *End of a mathematical proof*: three capital letters that stand for a phrase; it starts with Q, a letter that appears nowhere else in the grid, and ends with D.

**Down**

- **1-Down** (4 letters), *Cry loudly*: what a siren does; it starts with W, the same letter that opens 1-Across.
- **2-Down** (4 letters), *Actress Kendrick of "Pitch Perfect"*: her first name, which reads the same forward and backward; it starts with A.
- **3-Down** (5 letters), *Pride initials*: five capital letters with no vowel among them; it starts with L and ends with the grid's lone Q.
- **4-Down** (5 letters), *Mexican street corn*: a Spanish word for corn on the cob; it starts and ends with the same vowel.
- **5-Down** (5 letters), *High school health class topic, for short*: a two-word school subject joined into one entry; it starts with S, ends with D and holds two of the same vowel.

## Friday's grid, clue by clue against Thursday's

Start with 8-Across if you want the day's one trick. The clue is a spoken pun, so the answer is a word you say out loud and then respell: the coffee in the parenthesis points at the drink, while the quoted sentence points at the sound. Every other clue is a plain definition, a name or an abbreviation.

Sorted by what they ask for, the 10 clues fall into these groups:

| Clue type | Count | Clues |
|---|---|---|
| Plain definition | 2 | 7-Across, 1-Down |
| Name or trivia | 3 | 1-Across, 2-Down, 4-Down |
| Abbreviation answer | 3 | 3-Down, 5-Down, 9-Across |
| Wordplay on the printed words | 1 | 6-Across |
| Fill-in-the-blank pun | 1 | 8-Across |
| Question mark | 0 | none |

Only 3-Down ("initials") and 5-Down ("for short") flag the abbreviation in the clue. 9-Across gives no such signal, so its three letters have to come from the crossings or from knowing the phrase. Thursday's Mini had no abbreviation answer, and its one fill-in-the-blank asked for an author's surname.

Both grids keep 23 white squares, but Friday's four top rows are open from edge to edge. That makes 1-Across and 8-Across the long anchors: each one crosses all five Down entries, while 9-Across touches only the three on the right. The two Down entries on the left stop one row early, at four letters.
