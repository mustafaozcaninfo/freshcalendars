## Hints for each group

**Yellow**

- Hint 1: Picture a packed stadium; each tile is a word for a huge number of things.
- Hint 2: The entries have three, four, four and four letters.

**Green**

- Hint 1: Each is a short sound you blurt out right before you say what you think.
- Hint 2: The entries have three, three, three and four letters.

**Blue**

- Hint 1: Soap suds, a chewy sweet and a tapioca drink all start with the same word, which rhymes with "trouble."
- Hint 2: The entries have three, three, four and four letters.

**Purple**

- Hint 1: Say each one aloud and ignore the spelling; it sounds like a small word people use to point at themselves or others.
- Hint 2: All four entries have three letters, and every one of them contains the letter E.

## Solving tips

Read the tiles aloud before you group anything. Half this board only makes sense by ear, and the spellings are a distraction. Start with the group whose first word you can name outright, then use what is left to test the sound-based ideas.

## A red-herring map: tiles that pass for two groups

Four overlaps make this board hard, and each has a test that settles it.

| Tiles | Why they tempt | Test that sorts them | Where they sit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sea, Tea, Gee, Why, Eye, Ewe | Each sounds like one letter (C, T, G, Y, I, U), so a spelled-out-letters group seems possible | Six candidates for a four-tile group means the idea is wrong; sort by the other three groups first | Yellow, Blue, Green, Green, Purple, Purple |
| Hee, Gee, Wee | All three end in the same "ee" sound, and Hee is a laugh that could pass for an interjection | Swap in a pronoun: Hee sounds like "he" and Wee like "we", but Gee sounds only like a letter | Purple, Green, Purple |
| Sea, Well, Bath, Tea | Each is tied to water or liquid, so a water group looks likely | Put "bubble" in front: Bath and Tea take it, Sea and Well need other homes | Yellow, Green, Blue, Blue |
| Wee, Slew, Mass, Host | Wee is the size word on a board where several tiles mean a large amount | Wee means small, so it cannot be a word for many; it fits only as the sound of "we" | Purple, Yellow, Yellow, Yellow |

Say, Why and Well are the clean case: each opens a sentence and none rides on its sound. Gum and Wrap hide no second meaning here, which makes them safe starting points for blue.
