**Strands #989 from The New York Times (NYT) is the puzzle for Tuesday, Nov. 17, 2026, and its clue is "It's going down."** Tracy Bennett is credited as both constructor and editor in the puzzle data. This board hides a 10-letter spangram and six theme words, and the spangram stays out of the top three rows. The rules for a 10-letter spangram or any other are on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). Hints come first; answers wait in a marked section at the end.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: A three-word old saying, the sort of phrase a storyteller uses when an event arrives.
- Hint 2: It has 10 letters, starting with C and ending with S.
- Hint 3: Its first letter is in the bottom-left corner of the board.

## Theme word hints

Take the clue's "going down" as slang for something occurring. The six theme words are listed from shortest to longest:

- **5 letters, starts with O:** the verb in "when did the accident ___?"
- **5 letters, starts with E:** to follow as a result, the way chaos may after a power cut.
- **6 letters, starts with H:** finish the saying "accidents will ___."
- **6 letters, starts with B:** fate's verb for the misfortune that comes upon a traveler.
- **7 letters, starts with D:** what a photographer did to film in a darkroom, and what a plot does over time.
- **9 letters, starts with T:** a formal verb for events, and a plant's way of releasing water vapor through its leaves.

## How the clue frames the theme

The clue is "It's going down." Read it literally and you would hunt for words about falling, sinking or dropping. The puzzle uses the casual sense of the phrase, where something is going down when it is taking place. That makes the six theme words close synonyms, so the clue gives you meaning and the letters on the board decide which synonym sits where.

## Where the spangram runs on Tuesday's board

Rows count from the top and columns from the left. The spangram starts in the bottom-left corner (row 8, column 1) and runs along the bottom row for three tiles. It then climbs column 3 from row 8 up to row 4, and turns right along row 4 to the last column, ending on the right edge. It touches the left, bottom and right edges and never the top.

Because it stops at row 4, the top three rows hold 18 tiles of theme words only. The two-column block at the lower left, rows 5 to 7, is a single six-letter word. The densest corner is the lower right, rows 5 to 8 and columns 4 to 6: its 12 tiles are shared by just two theme words, one of 7 letters and one of 5. The six theme words use 38 letters, and with the 10-letter spangram they fill all 48 tiles.

Start from the lower-left block, since a six-letter word fills it completely and the spangram hugs its edge. A hint, earned by spelling three ordinary words, lights up the letters of one theme word.
