**Connections: Sports Edition #794 is the Thursday, Nov. 26, 2026 puzzle from The Athletic and New York Times (NYT) Games, and it lands on Thanksgiving Day.** Two hints wait behind a tap for each color, and the answers sit under the spoiler heading.

Thanksgiving is the fourth Thursday of November, and this board has a tile to match. The rules are on our [Connections page](/news/topic/nyt-connections/#how-it-works). Wednesday's board is on [Sports Edition #793](/news/nyt-connections-sports-edition-hints-answers-november-25-2026-793/), and the main game has its own page, [Connections for Nov. 26](/news/nyt-connections-hints-answers-november-26-2026-1264/).

## Thursday's hints for #794

Think about shapes, surnames, lanes and old scoreboards.

- **Hint 1 (yellow):** Each tile is an object used in a sport, and none of them is shaped like a ball.
- **Hint 2 (yellow):** Two tiles have four letters, and the longest has eleven.
- **Hint 1 (green):** Each tile is a surname, and each person shares the same distinction on draft night in the Women's National Basketball Association (WNBA).
- **Hint 2 (green):** Two tiles have four letters, and the other two are longer than seven.
- **Hint 1 (blue):** Each tile describes a run of the same result in a row on a lane.
- **Hint 2 (blue):** Two tiles have six letters, and one has a hyphen.
- **Hint 1 (purple):** Each tile is the singular form of a nickname that a Major League Baseball club no longer uses.
- **Hint 2 (purple):** The shortest tile on the whole board, at three letters, belongs here.

## Thanksgiving traps on the Sports Edition board

Start with the tile that matches the holiday, Turkey. It invites you to look for a food or an animal group, but the board has no such group. Bird is the other creature on the grid, and the two together make a tempting pair that you should not trust.

Baseball vocabulary is the second trap. Double and American both read as baseball words, yet they do not share a group. Football and American make a third pair, since together they name a sport, but they sit in different groups too. Test each tile against all four hints before you commit. ## Thursday's answers, #794, Nov. 26, 2026 (spoilers)

Stop here if you still want to solve it. Beebom and Nerdschalk print the same four groups and sixteen entries, and the group names below are as those sites print them.

- **Yellow, Non-sphere sports objects:** Disc, Football, Puck, Shuttlecock
- **Green, WNBA No. 1 picks:** Augustus, Bird, Holdsclaw, Plum
- **Blue, Consecutive strikes in bowling:** Double, Hambone, Six-Pack, Turkey
- **Purple, Former MLB team names, in singular form:** American, Highlander, Nap, Senator

## Who or what each entry is

- **Disc, Football, Puck, Shuttlecock:** each is an object used in a sport, and none is a sphere, per the published group title. We add nothing more about them.
- **Augustus, Bird, Holdsclaw, Plum:** each is a surname of a WNBA No. 1 overall draft pick, per the league's draft history. Chamique Holdsclaw went first in 1999 to the Washington Mystics, Sue Bird in 2002 to the Seattle Storm, Seimone Augustus in 2006 to the Minnesota Lynx and Kelsey Plum in 2017 to the San Antonio Stars.
- **Double, Hambone, Six-Pack, Turkey:** per Bowling.com, a double is two strikes in a row, a turkey is three in a row and a hambone is four in a row. The puzzle groups Six-Pack with these strike runs, and we state no count for it.
- **American, Highlander, Nap, Senator:** each is the singular of a former Major League Baseball (MLB) team nickname, per the puzzle's own group name. We give no team details.
