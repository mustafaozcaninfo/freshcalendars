**A midterm election is a congressional election held halfway between presidential elections, according to USAGov, and the next one is Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026.** Voters decide all 435 seats in the U.S. House of Representatives and 35 of the 100 seats in the Senate. The president is not on the ballot. Most states also elect governors, state legislators and local officials the same day.

## Why is it called a midterm election?

It falls in the middle of a president's four-year term. The Constitution gives the president a four-year term (Article II), House members two-year terms (Article I, Section 2) and senators six-year terms, divided into three classes so that "one third may be chosen every second Year" (Article I, Section 3), according to the National Archives' transcription. Because the House stands for election every two years, one House election lands in the middle of each presidential term.

## What does the Nov. 3, 2026 election decide?

| Office | On the ballot Nov. 3 | Term | Next regular election |
|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. House | All 435 voting seats | 2 years | November 2028 |
| U.S. Senate, Class II | 33 regularly scheduled seats | 6 years | November 2032 |
| U.S. Senate, special elections | Class III seats in Ohio and Florida | Rest of a term that ends in 2029 | November 2028 |
| President | Not on the ballot | 4 years | November 2028 |

The House has had 435 voting members since Congress capped its size, according to the House's History, Art & Archives office. The Senate's own lists show 33 Class II seats with terms expiring in 2027 and Class III seats held by senators from Ohio and Florida. Our [midterm tracker](/news/2026-midterm-elections-whats-on-the-ballot-results-tracker/) explains why those two states hold special elections and lists every Senate and governor's race.

Beyond Congress, ballots differ by state. Many include governors, state legislators, judges, ballot measures and city or county offices. To see yours, use the [official sample ballot lookup for your state](/news/sample-ballot-2026-by-state-official-lookup-links/). California's My Voter Status, Pennsylvania's Voter Registration Status and North Carolina's Voter Search are three examples.

## How many races are on the ballot on Nov. 3?

Ballotpedia, the online encyclopedia of American politics, says in its Nov. 3, 2026 Daily Brew newsletter that it is "following a total of 44,443 races today featuring a total of 80,984 candidates." That count covers congressional, state and local races in all 50 states. It is Ballotpedia's count of the races it tracks, so no single voter sees more than a small share of them.

| Level | On the ballot Nov. 3, per Ballotpedia |
|---|---|
| U.S. Senate | 35 seats, including special elections in Florida and Ohio |
| U.S. House | All 435 seats |
| Governors | 36 governorships |
| Other state executive offices | 296 offices in 43 states, including 31 lieutenant governors, 30 attorneys general and 26 secretaries of state |
| State legislatures | 6,139 of 7,386 seats, in 88 of 99 chambers across 46 states |
| State supreme courts | Seats in 25 states |
| Statewide ballot measures | 145 measures in 39 states |
| School boards | 475 districts in Ballotpedia's coverage |
| Recall elections | 15 recalls of 24 local officials in five states |

Source: Ballotpedia, Daily Brew, Nov. 3, 2026. Ballotpedia says it follows every race on the ballot in 32 states and selected local races elsewhere, so its count does not include every local race in the other 18 states.

## How is a midterm different from a presidential election?

In a presidential election year, voters also choose the president and vice president, according to USAGov, along with the House and the Senate class up that year. In a midterm, the president is not on the ballot, so the contests are for Congress and for state and local offices. The same federal rules apply to both: Election Day is "the Tuesday next after the 1st Monday in November, in every even numbered year," under 2 U.S.C. 7, which gives Nov. 3 this year.

## Who can vote on Nov. 3?

USAGov lists three requirements: be a U.S. citizen, be 18 years old on or before Election Day, and be registered by your state's deadline. North Dakota does not require voter registration. Many state deadlines have passed; our guide to the [voter registration deadline](/news/voter-registration-deadline-october-19-2026-early-voting/) and the page on how to [check whether you are registered](/news/am-i-registered-to-vote-check-status-2026/) cover what is still open.

## What happens after Election Day?

Counts reported on election night are unofficial. States canvass and certify results in the weeks after, and Georgia holds a runoff on Tue., Dec. 1 for races where no candidate wins a majority. See [when 2026 election winners take office](/news/when-do-2026-election-winners-take-office-congress-governors/) and [when the country will know the results](/news/when-will-we-know-election-results-2026-midterms/).

The 20th Amendment starts the terms of senators and representatives at noon on Jan. 3, unless Congress sets a different day by law. In 2027 that day is a Sunday. The Senate Historical Office notes that a "new" Congress begins in odd-numbered years, after the elections.
