**The March equinox falls on Saturday, Mar. 20, 2027, at 4:25 p.m. Eastern Daylight Time (EDT), and the Full Moon follows on Monday, Mar. 22, at 6:44 a.m. EDT**, according to the U.S. Naval Observatory (USNO). Mars and Jupiter passed opposition in February, per the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan (NAOJ), so both are evening planets in March. Print the Full Moon night on one page with the [March 2027 night sky sheet](#downloads).

## March 2027 night sky calendar by date

| Date | Event | Time | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tue., Mar. 2 | Mercury stationary | 2:56 p.m. EST | NAOJ |
| Mon., Mar. 8 | New Moon | 4:29 a.m. EST | USNO |
| Mon., Mar. 15 | First quarter Moon | 12:25 p.m. EDT | USNO |
| Wed., Mar. 17 | Mercury at greatest elongation west | About 3 a.m. EDT | NAOJ |
| Sat., Mar. 20 | March equinox | 4:25 p.m. EDT | USNO |
| Mon., Mar. 22 | Full Moon | 6:44 a.m. EDT | USNO |
| Wed., Mar. 24 | Neptune at conjunction with the Sun | 7:13 p.m. EDT | NAOJ |
| Mon., Mar. 29 | Last quarter Moon | 8:54 p.m. EDT | USNO |

NAOJ publishes in Japan Standard Time (JST), nine hours ahead of Universal Time (UT), and lists the Mercury elongation by the hour only ("16h" on Mar. 17 JST), so it is "about 3 a.m." in EDT. We converted every NAOJ time, so Neptune's "08:13" on Mar. 25 JST is 7:13 p.m. EDT on Mar. 24. The USNO times are in UT, also converted. U.S. daylight saving time begins Sunday, Mar. 14, 2027, at 2 a.m. local time, so the early rows are in Eastern Standard Time (EST) and the later ones in EDT.

## Which planets are visible in March 2027?

The NAOJ table has no March rows for Venus or Mars, and none for an opposition or a planet-Moon conjunction. What it does list puts the planets in these places.

| Planet | NAOJ row | What it means for March |
|---|---|---|
| Jupiter | Opposition Feb. 10, 7:29 p.m. EST | Follows its opposition, so it rises in the east around sunset in the weeks after |
| Mars | Opposition Feb. 19, 10:51 a.m. EST | Follows its opposition, so it is in the evening sky |
| Saturn | Conjunction with the Sun Apr. 7, 1:18 p.m. EDT | Heads toward the Sun, so it is lost in the twilight |
| Neptune | Conjunction with the Sun Mar. 24, 7:13 p.m. EDT | Too close to the Sun to see |
| Mercury | Stationary Mar. 2; greatest elongation west Mar. 17 | A morning-sky position; the table does not say how easy it is to see |
| Venus | Greatest elongation west Jan. 4, 2027 (46° 57') | A morning planet since that date; no March event is listed |

An opposition puts a planet opposite the Sun in the sky. Jupiter and Mars are weeks past theirs, and Mars reaches a stationary point on Apr. 2 in EDT. NAOJ gives dates, not rise and set times, and the USNO service used here covers only the Sun and Moon, so we do not print planet times. For your city, use a planetarium app. The February dates are in the [February 2027 night sky calendar](/news/planets-visible-february-2027-night-sky-calendar/), and the whole year is in our [planets in 2027 calendar](/news/planets-2027-calendar-jupiter-mars-opposition-saturn-venus-elongation-dates/).

## What are the Moon phases in March 2027?

The USNO lists new Moon on Monday, Mar. 8, first quarter on Monday, Mar. 15, full Moon on Monday, Mar. 22 and last quarter on Monday, Mar. 29. The equinox comes two days before the Full Moon. NASA's supermoon page lists no supermoon in 2027, so Mar. 22 is not one. For every phase of the year, see the [Moon phases 2027 guide](/news/moon-phases-2027-full-moon-calendar/).

## When is sunset in March 2027 in four U.S. cities?

The USNO gives these sunsets in local clock time. Mar. 1 is before the Mar. 14 clock change, and the other dates are after it.

| Date | New York | Chicago | Denver | Los Angeles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon., Mar. 1 | 5:47 p.m. | 5:41 p.m. | 5:52 p.m. | 5:49 p.m. |
| Sun., Mar. 14 | 7:02 p.m. | 6:56 p.m. | 7:06 p.m. | 7:00 p.m. |
| Sat., Mar. 20 | 7:08 p.m. | 7:03 p.m. | 7:12 p.m. | 7:05 p.m. |
| Mon., Mar. 22 | 7:10 p.m. | 7:05 p.m. | 7:14 p.m. | 7:06 p.m. |
| Wed., Mar. 31 | 7:20 p.m. | 7:15 p.m. | 7:23 p.m. | 7:13 p.m. |

In New York sunset moves from 5:47 p.m. on Mar. 1 to 7:20 p.m. on Mar. 31, 1 hour and 33 minutes later on the clock. Most of that jump is the clock change. From Mar. 14 to Mar. 31, both after the change, New York's sunset is 18 minutes later.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| New York | 7:10 p.m. | 7:49 p.m. | 6:44 a.m. | 6:56 a.m. |

In all four cities the Moon rises after sunset on Mar. 22, between 39 minutes later in New York and 44 minutes later in Denver, and sets before sunrise that morning. The printable lists the same Moon times.

## Is there an eclipse or meteor shower in March 2027?

No eclipse. NASA's tables list the annular solar eclipse of Feb. 6, the penumbral lunar eclipse of Feb. 20, the total solar eclipse of Aug. 2 and penumbral lunar eclipses on Jul. 18 and Aug. 17, none in March.

No shower either. The American Meteor Society (AMS) calendar lists no shower active in March 2027. The next is the Lyrids, active Apr. 14-30 with a peak on Apr. 21-22.

## Where do these dates come from?

The Moon phases are from the [USNO moon phases data](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/api/moon/phases/year?year=2027) and the equinox from the [USNO seasons data](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/api/seasons?year=2027), both labeled in UT. The planet events are from the [NAOJ Koyomi table](https://eco.mtk.nao.ac.jp/cgi-bin/koyomi/cande/phenomena_en.cgi?year=2027). The Sun and Moon times are from the USNO rise, set and transit service. The daylight saving rule is in [15 U.S. Code 260a](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/260a). The eclipses come from NASA's [solar](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/SEdecade/SEdecade2021.html) and [lunar](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/LEdecade/LEdecade2021.html) tables.
