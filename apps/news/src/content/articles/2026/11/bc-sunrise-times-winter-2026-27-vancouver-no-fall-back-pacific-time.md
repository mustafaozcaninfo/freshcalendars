On **Sunday, Nov. 1, 2026**, British Columbia (B.C.) did not turn clocks back, so sunrise in Vancouver today is 8:01 a.m. Pacific time (PCT) and sunset is 5:51 p.m., according to the U.S. Naval Observatory (USNO). Most of the province is on PCT, which is seven hours behind Coordinated Universal Time (UTC-7); the exceptions are northeastern B.C. and southeastern B.C., covered below. The tables give sunrise and sunset for six cities through March 1, 2027.

A one-page [printable B.C. winter sunrise and sunset chart](/news/downloads/bc-sunrise-2026/bc-winter-sunrise-sunset-chart-2026-27.pdf) is on this page.

## When is the latest sunrise in Vancouver?

Vancouver's latest sunrise is 9:08 a.m., from **Tuesday, Dec. 29, 2026, through Sunday, Jan. 3, 2027**, according to USNO. The earliest sunset of the winter in the table is 5:16 p.m., on both Dec. 1 and Dec. 21. Sunrise first goes back before 8 a.m. on **Saturday, Feb. 27, 2027**, at 7:59 a.m.; on Friday, Feb. 26, it is 8:01 a.m. By Mar. 1, 2027, Vancouver sunrise is 7:55 a.m.

Farther north and inland the mornings run later: on Jan. 1, 2027, sunrise is 9:08 a.m. in Vancouver, 9:29 a.m. in Prince George, 9:40 a.m. in Calgary and 9:27 a.m. in Winnipeg.

## How would sunrise look if B.C. had fallen back?

Under the old fall back, B.C. would have moved to UTC-8 for the winter, so every clock time above would be one hour earlier. This is our calculation from the USNO times, not a USNO figure.

| Vancouver | Nov. 1 | Dec. 1 | Dec. 21 | Jan. 1, 2027 | Feb. 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sunrise, PCT (UTC-7) | 8:01 a.m. | 8:47 a.m. | 9:05 a.m. | 9:08 a.m. | 8:44 a.m. |
| Sunrise if clocks had fallen back (UTC-8) | 7:01 a.m. | 7:47 a.m. | 8:05 a.m. | 8:08 a.m. | 7:44 a.m. |
| Sunset, PCT (UTC-7) | 5:51 p.m. | 5:16 p.m. | 5:16 p.m. | 5:25 p.m. | 6:09 p.m. |
| Sunset if clocks had fallen back (UTC-8) | 4:51 p.m. | 4:16 p.m. | 4:16 p.m. | 4:25 p.m. | 5:09 p.m. |

The trade is later light in the morning for later light in the evening. The B.C. government's page lists the benefits of the new time as "more consistency and fewer disruptions to sleep patterns, school schedules, and daily routines" and "more usable light in the evenings in winter." It does not address morning darkness.

## Which parts of B.C. use a different time?

The B.C. government's page "B.C.'s new time zone (Pacific time)," published Oct. 30, 2026, says: "On November 1, 2026, when previously clocks would have been turned back an hour, no change will be made." The March 2, 2026, release from the Ministry of Attorney General says B.C. adopts permanent daylight saving time after clocks moved forward on Sunday, March 8, 2026. Two areas differ:

- **Northeastern B.C.** (Chetwynd, Dawson Creek, Fort St. John, Fort Nelson, Hudson's Hope, Taylor and Tumbler Ridge) stays on year-round Mountain Standard Time (MST), UTC-7. Fort Ware uses PCT.
- **Southeastern B.C.** (Cranbrook, Fernie, Golden, Invermere, Kimberley, Sparwood, Elkford, Radium Hot Springs and the Columbia Shuswap Regional District) "will remain aligned with Alberta and use mountain daylight time (UTC-6) year round."

Alberta says its time is equivalent to UTC-6 and that it "officially moves to Alberta Time on November 1, 2026," so Calgary's column above uses UTC-6. For how every province handles the change, see our story on [which Canadian provinces do not change time](/news/canada-clocks-fall-back-november-1-2026-which-provinces-no-time-change/).
