**Watch Night 2026 falls on Thursday, Dec. 31, the date of the night the National Park Service (NPS) says enslaved and free Black Americans stayed up until midnight in 1862.** The NPS says that tradition "became known as Watch Night, or 'Freedom's Eve,'" and that Black church communities still celebrate it today. The timeline below runs from 1755 to 2021, and a printable marks the 2027 dates that follow.

## What is Watch Night, and why is it called Freedom's Eve?

On Dec. 31, 1862, "enslaved and free Black Americans across the country stayed up until midnight," the NPS says, awaiting the passage of the Emancipation Proclamation, which Lincoln was expected to issue on Jan. 1, 1863. They gathered to pray and sing as they waited for midnight. The tradition continued in following years under the names Watch Night and Freedom's Eve.

The name Watch Night is older than 1862. The United Methodist Church says John Wesley, Methodism's founder, first celebrated a covenant renewal as a "Watch Night" service in 1755, and that the term first meant a prayer gathering that could last well into the night. It was not originally tied to New Year's Eve.

## Watch Night timeline, 1755 to 2021

| Date | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1755 | Wesley celebrates his first covenant renewal as a "Watch Night" service | United Methodist Church |
| Sept. 22, 1862 | Lincoln first announces his plan for the Emancipation Proclamation, issuing a preliminary version | NPS |
| Dec. 31, 1862 | Enslaved and free Black Americans stay up until midnight | NPS |
| Jan. 1, 1863 | Lincoln issues the Emancipation Proclamation | National Archives |
| June 17, 2021 | Juneteenth National Independence Day Act approved (Public Law 117-17) | U.S. Congress |

The NPS adds that the outcome of the war "was far from decided on January 1, 1863." The National Archives says the proclamation applied only to states that had seceded and expressly exempted parts of the Confederacy that had come under Northern control. It issued it on Jan. 1, 1863, so Friday, Jan. 1, 2027 is the 164th anniversary.

## Where is the Emancipation Proclamation now?

The National Archives said in a June 2024 release that the Emancipation Proclamation would be put on permanent display in the Rotunda of the National Archives Building in Washington, DC, in 2026. Its June 17, 2026 release lists the Rotunda as featuring the Declaration of Independence, the Constitution, the Bill of Rights, the Emancipation Proclamation and the 19th Amendment. By those two releases, this is the first New Year's with the original in the Rotunda. Visiting hours are not covered here; check archives.gov before a trip.

## Are there Watch Night events in 2026?

No 2026 Watch Night event had been posted on the National Archives site as of Nov. 23, 2026. The Smithsonian's National Museum of African American History and Culture site could not be read for this story.

## Which freedom dates come after Watch Night in 2027?

The printable puts these on a 2027 calendar. Juneteenth became a law with the act approved June 17, 2021. In 2027 June 19 is a Saturday, and the U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM) federal holiday list shows Friday, June 18 as the observed day.
