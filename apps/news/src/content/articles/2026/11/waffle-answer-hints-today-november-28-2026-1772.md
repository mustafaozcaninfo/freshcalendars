## The nine crossings: where today's words meet

Nine squares belong to two words at once: the ones where rows 1, 3 and 5 cross columns 1, 3 and 5. Their starting colors:

| | Column 1 | Column 3 | Column 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Row 1 | Green | Yellow | Green |

Five of the nine crossings start green, two yellow and two gray. The four corners and the center are locked, so the open crossings are the middle of the top and bottom edges (yellow) and the middle of the left and right edges (gray).

The sixth green tile is not at a crossing at all; it sits in the middle of row 4. That leaves 12 squares outside the crossings, holding 1 green, 4 yellow and 7 gray tiles. In other words, most of the swapping happens in the squares that only one word uses.

### Why 10 swaps is the floor

Fifteen tiles start out of place, and a 10-swap route means they fall into 5 loops (15 minus 5). A computer search over every sequence of swaps between misplaced tiles found nothing at 9 swaps or fewer, so 10 is the minimum.
