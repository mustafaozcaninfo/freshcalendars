**Waffle (wafflegame.net) puzzle #1767, for Monday, Nov. 23, 2026, starts with 6 green tiles, 8 yellow and 7 gray.** One clue per word comes first, then the color counts, and the answers sit in the marked last section.

Ten of the 21 tiles belong to letters that occur more than once in the finished grid, so some yellows could point to two squares; the colors and swaps are explained on our [Waffle page](/news/topic/waffle/#how-it-works).

## One clue for each word

**Across**

- **Row 1:** starts with S and ends with M. A tempest with thunder, gusting wind and driving rain.
- **Row 3:** starts with R and ends with Y. Thin and piercing, as a voice or a high note can be; also grassy with tall water plants.
- **Row 5:** starts with G and ends with E. A spirit that grants wishes from a lamp.

**Down**

- **Column 1:** starts with S and ends with G. A small twig with a few leaves, such as a bit of parsley on a plate.
- **Column 3:** starts with O and ends with N. A vast body of salt water between continents.
- **Column 5:** starts with M and ends with E. Neither yes nor no; perhaps.

## Repeated letters make the yellows ambiguous

The solved grid repeats four letters: one occurs four times, and three occur twice each. That is 10 tiles, and they start as 3 green, 5 yellow and 2 gray. A yellow tile only says the letter belongs somewhere in one of its words, and with a repeat the game does not say which copy it is.

A gray repeated tile is the opposite trap. It looks useless, but its letter still has a home somewhere else on the board, so do not park it on a square where that letter already counts. The two gray tiles in the repeat group are named in the last section.

### Where the six green tiles sit

S at row 1, column 1; R at row 1, column 4; M at row 1, column 5; E at row 3, column 3; G at row 5, column 1; and E at row 5, column 5. Five crossings start green and four start yellow, so none of the nine crossings starts gray.

### The repeated letters on the board

E is the four-time letter: it sits in the solved grid at row 3, column 2, row 3, column 3, row 5, column 2 and row 5, column 5. The two E tiles at row 3, column 3 and row 5, column 5 start green. The E at row 5, column 4 is yellow, because row 5 still needs an E at column 2. The E at row 4, column 5 is gray: column 5 has its E already, and row 4 has no word.

R, A and I occur twice each. R starts green at row 1, column 4 and yellow at row 2, column 1. Both A tiles start yellow, at row 3, column 5 and row 5, column 3. The I at row 3, column 1 is yellow, and the I at row 3, column 2 is gray, since row 3 holds no I.

### Why 10 is the minimum

Fifteen tiles start out of place. A loop of n tiles costs n minus 1 swaps, so swaps equal misplaced tiles minus loops. Here the 15 tiles form 5 loops, which leaves 10 swaps, and no route can use fewer.
