**Dreidel starts with an equal share of currency for every player and one unit from each into the pot, according to Chabad.org's rules, and each spin of the top (nun, gimel, hey or shin) says what happens next.** Hanukkah 2026 begins Friday evening, Dec. 4, and continues through Saturday, Dec. 12, Chabad.org says. A free US Letter printable puts the rules card and a candle chart for the eight nights on one page.

## How to play dreidel step by step

Chabad.org's rules, "How to Play Dreidel," run in this order.

1. Divide the currency equally among all players. Chabad.org gives no number of tokens, so players choose an equal share.
2. Everyone puts one unit of the currency (a penny, a nut, etc.) into the pot.
3. Players take turns spinning. The letter that faces up decides what the spinner does (table below).
4. After a gimel, everyone, including the spinner, puts another unit into the pot, and the person to the spinner's left spins next. When the pot is emptied, every player adds again.
5. The game ends when there is one player left.

## What do the dreidel letters mean?

Each of the four letters on the top has a Yiddish word and a rule, according to Chabad.org.

| Letter | Yiddish word | Meaning | What you do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nun (נ) | nul | zero | Nothing happens; play passes left |
| Gimel (ג) | gantz | whole | Take the whole pot |
| Hey (ה) | halb | half | Take half the pot |
| Shin (ש) | shenk | give | Put one unit into the pot |

The four letters stand for "nes gadol hayah sham," "a great miracle happened there," says Chabad.org. In Israel, pey replaces shin and stands for "po," "here," so the phrase reads "a great miracle happened here."

## Why do people play dreidel on Hanukkah?

Chabad.org tells this story, and it is a tradition, not a dated record. Under King Antiochus, Greek-Syrian rule passed a series of laws outlawing the study of Torah. Children studied in hiding, and "it is said that if a Greek patrol passed by they would quickly pull out and play with small tops," Chabad.org says.

## Hanukkah 2026 candle chart for the eight nights

Hebcal lists the candle count for each night of Hanukkah 2026, and every night also has the shamash, the helper candle. Chabad.org's Printable Chanukah Guide says the first light goes on the right side of the menorah, and each following night adds a light to the left of the first.

| Night | Date | Candles | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fri., Dec. 4 | 1 + shamash | Before sundown |
| 2 | Sat., Dec. 5 | 2 + shamash | At nightfall, after Shabbat |
| 3 | Sun., Dec. 6 | 3 + shamash | |
| 4 | Mon., Dec. 7 | 4 + shamash | |
| 5 | Tue., Dec. 8 | 5 + shamash | |
| 6 | Wed., Dec. 9 | 6 + shamash | |
| 7 | Thu., Dec. 10 | 7 + shamash | |
| 8 | Fri., Dec. 11 | 8 + shamash | Before sundown |

On Friday, Chabad.org's guide says the menorah is lit before the Shabbat candles, 18 minutes before sunset. On Saturday, it is lit after Shabbat ends and the Havdalah is recited. The holiday ends at nightfall on Saturday, Dec. 12, Hebcal says.
