**Strands #1001 from The New York Times (NYT) is the Sunday, Nov. 29, 2026 puzzle, and the theme clue is "Breakfast in a bowl."** Tracy Bennett is listed as both constructor and editor. Counting from #1 on March 4, 2024 gives 1001. Seven theme words and a 6-letter spangram fill the 48 tiles, and the rules are on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works).

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: It names the dish the clue describes, and the theme words are what goes into it.
- Hint 2: It has 6 letters, begins with M and ends with I. All six letters differ.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 4, column 1, on the left edge, and the first step goes diagonally down and to the right.

## Theme word hints

Seven ingredients fit the clue, listed by length, shortest first:

- **4 letters, starts with B, ends with N:** the outer layer of a grain, kept in wholegrain flour. All four letters differ.
- **4 letters, starts with O, ends with S:** the grain rolled flat for porridge. All four letters differ.
- **6 letters, starts with B, ends with Y:** a grain used for malt and soup. All six letters differ.
- **7 letters, starts with A, ends with S:** tree nuts sold whole, sliced or slivered. All seven letters differ.
- **7 letters, starts with C, ends with T:** the white flesh of a large tropical fruit with a hairy shell. Two different letters each appear twice.
- **7 letters, starts with P, ends with S:** green pumpkin seeds. One letter appears twice, and it is the first letter.
- **7 letters, starts with R, ends with S:** dried grapes. Two different letters each appear twice.

## Row and column locator hints

Rows count from the top and columns from the left. Each rung narrows the search and none names a word.

- **Rows 1 and 2:** together they hold 12 tiles, 11 of them from just two words, one 7 letters long and one 4 letters long.
- **Row 3:** one 7-letter word covers all six tiles of the row.
- **Row 4:** the two end tiles are the spangram, and the four tiles between them are one 6-letter word.
- **Rows 5 to 8, column 1:** all four tiles are one 7-letter word.
- **Rows 7 and 8, columns 3 to 6:** seven of the eight tiles are one 7-letter word.
- **Column 6, rows 5 to 7:** these three tiles are part of a 4-letter word, and its fourth tile sits one column to the left.

## Start-cell hints

The first step is the direction from a word's first tile to its second.

- **4 letters, start cell row 1, column 2:** the first step goes straight left.
- **4 letters, start cell row 6, column 6:** the first step goes straight down.
- **6 letters, start cell row 5, column 4:** the first step goes straight left.
- **7 letters, start cell row 2, column 3:** the first step goes straight right.
- **7 letters, start cell row 3, column 1:** the first step goes straight right.
- **7 letters, start cell row 5, column 1:** the first step goes straight down.
- **7 letters, start cell row 7, column 3:** the first step goes straight right.

## The Nov. 29 spangram path and the board map

The spangram starts on the left edge at row 4, column 1, and drops diagonally twice to row 6, column 3. One step right lands on row 6, column 4. Two diagonal steps up and to the right then finish on the right edge at row 4, column 6. That is five steps: two down-right, one across, two up-right.

Read from the middle, the path is symmetric. Columns 1 and 6 both sit in row 4, columns 2 and 5 in row 5, and columns 3 and 4 in row 6. Each column holds exactly one spangram tile, and each of rows 4, 5 and 6 holds two.

The V works like a cup. The six tiles inside it, row 4 columns 2 to 5 and row 5 columns 3 and 4, are a complete 6-letter theme word, so that word cannot reach outside the cup. The rest of the board settles into blocks:

| Region | Tiles | What fills it |
|---|---|---|
| Rows 1 and 2, columns 1 and 2 | 4 | all of the 4-letter word that starts at row 1, column 2 |
| Rows 1 and 2, columns 3 to 6 | 8 | the 7-letter word that starts at row 2, column 3, plus row 2, column 6, the last tile of the word below |
| Row 3 | 6 | the 7-letter word that starts at row 3, column 1 |
| Inside the V | 6 | the 6-letter word that starts at row 5, column 4 |
| The V itself | 6 | the spangram |
| Rows 5 to 8, columns 1 and 2 | 7 | the 7-letter word that starts at row 5, column 1, a U that runs down column 1 and back up column 2 |
| Rows 5 to 7, columns 5 and 6 | 4 | the 4-letter word that starts at row 6, column 6 |
| Rows 7 and 8, columns 3 to 6 | 7 | the 7-letter word that starts at row 7, column 3 |

The 48 tiles split into 6 for the spangram and 42 for the seven theme words. Rows 1 to 3 hold no spangram tile at all, and neither do rows 7 and 8. The spangram also plugs a gap in the lower left: row 5, column 2 is the one tile of that block it takes from the U-shaped word.

## Solving tips

Row 3 is the easiest foothold, since one word runs straight across it before its last tile hops up. Clear that, then the 4-letter word in the top left corner, and the spangram's V is easy to see.
