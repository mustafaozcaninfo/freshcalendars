**Texas polls closed at 7 p.m. local time on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, which was 8 p.m. Eastern Time (ET) for most of the state, and a voter inside the polling place or waiting to enter at 7 p.m. was still entitled to vote.** The numbers reported that night are unofficial. Counties canvass the votes by Tue., Nov. 17, and the state canvass can begin no earlier than Sat., Nov. 21, under the Texas Election Code and the Texas Secretary of State's election calendar.

## When did Texas polls close?

The Texas Tribune's voter guide says Election Day hours were "7 a.m. to 7 p.m. local time," and El Paso County's Elections Department listed its vote centers as open from 7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m.

| Where | Polls close | In Eastern Time |
|---|---|---|
| Most of Texas (Central Time) | 7 p.m. | 8 p.m. |
| El Paso and Hudspeth counties (Mountain Time) | 7 p.m. | 9 p.m. |

Section 41.032 entitles a voter "inside or waiting to enter the polling place at 7 p.m." to vote. If people were still waiting, the presiding judge sent them in and closed the door to others, or handed out numbered cards to the voters in line. Our table of [poll closing times in every state](/news/what-time-do-polls-close-election-day-2026/) shows where Texas fell in the national schedule.

## Where to follow Texas results

Unofficial results come in two layers: statewide returns and each county's own totals. All of them stay unofficial until the canvass.

- **Statewide races.** The Texas Secretary of State's results pages and election calendar would not open for us on Wed., Nov. 4 or on Sat., Nov. 14 (the site returned an access error), so we do not link a confirmed statewide results page.
- **County results pages.** Counties post on their own. The Tarrant County Elections Administration's [results page](https://www.tarrantcountytx.gov/en/elections/past-election-information/2026-archives/november-3--2026--joint-general-and-special-election-results.html) listed timed reports in Central Time (CT), local time: early voting results no later than 7:15 p.m. on Nov. 3, then reports no later than 9 p.m., 10 p.m. and 11 p.m., then hourly reports on Nov. 4 from 12 a.m. to 4 a.m. The page, last modified Nov. 13, does not say whether its reports are official or give a canvass date, and lists some files as expected in December. The Harris County Clerk's Office Elections Department's [site](https://www.harrisvotes.com) is headed "November 3, 2026 - General and Special Elections" and has a "Live Results" link; it states no posting schedule. The Bexar County Elections Department's [home page](https://elections.bexar.gov/) links its "November 3, 2026 Election Returns" ([results page](https://results.enr.clarityelections.com/TX/Bexar/127291/)) and an unofficial reconciliation report; it also states no posting schedule.
- **County races and your own county.** Each county elections office posts its own unofficial totals. Our [sample ballot guide](/news/sample-ballot-by-zip-code-2026-texas-ballot/) links the official elections offices of the 12 most populous counties.
- **Release times.** We could not confirm one statewide release time. Each county posts on its own schedule, a point covered in [when we will know the 2026 results](/news/when-will-we-know-election-results-2026-midterms/).

| Provisional ballot with a photo ID problem | Voter shows ID within six days, by Mon., Nov. 9 | Texas Tribune voter guide |

## Texas canvass and certification dates

A canvass is the official count. Counties do it first, then the state.

| Step | Date | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Earliest local canvass | Fri., Nov. 6 | Not before the third day after Election Day (section 67.003) |
| Provisional ballots verified and counted | By Mon., Nov. 16 | 13th day, for an election on the general election date (section 65.051) |
| Latest local canvass | Tue., Nov. 17 | Up to the 14th day for this election (section 67.003) |
| State canvass | Sat., Nov. 21 to Sun., Dec. 6 | Between the 18th and 33rd day after Election Day (section 67.012) |
| Runoffs for local races | Sat., Dec. 12 | Texas Secretary of State's designation |

Other states certify on different dates; see [when 2026 election results are certified, by state](/news/when-are-2026-election-results-certified-deadlines-by-state/).

## Recounts in Texas

A candidate can petition for an initial recount in the cases listed in section 212.022. One is when "the difference in the number of votes received by the candidate and any candidate for the office who is shown by the election returns to be nominated, elected, or entitled to a place on a runoff ballot" is "less than 10 percent of that candidate's number of votes." Another is when all candidates together received fewer than 1,000 votes. The petition is due by 5 p.m. on the third business day after the canvass is completed (section 212.028); a deadline that falls on a weekend moves to 10 a.m. on the next business day.

## What Texans are voting on

The statewide ballot lists a U.S. senator, governor, lieutenant governor, attorney general, comptroller of public accounts, land commissioner, agriculture commissioner and one railroad commissioner seat, plus four Supreme Court, three Court of Criminal Appeals and three 15th Court of Appeals seats, according to the Texas Tribune. There are no statewide propositions this year. Every certified name is in our [Texas ballot guide](/news/texas-ballot-2026-candidates-senate-governor-statewide-races/).
