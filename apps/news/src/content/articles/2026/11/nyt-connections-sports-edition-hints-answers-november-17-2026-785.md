**Connections: Sports Edition #785, the Tuesday, Nov. 17, 2026 puzzle from The Athletic and New York Times (NYT) Games, hides one color inside a single sentence.** Two hints per color open one tap at a time, and answers wait below the spoiler heading.

Two colors on this board are word lists from sport, and one is a line someone once shouted. Rules live on our [Connections page](/news/topic/nyt-connections/#how-it-works).

## Tuesday's hints, #785, Nov. 17, 2026

Say each tile out loud before you group anything. One color only works when read as speech.

- Hint 1 (yellow): Each tile is a label for a rung on a ladder of organized play, from school gyms to local clubs.
- Hint 2 (yellow): One tile is a school-year label, and one is a word for teams that play away from home.
- Hint 1 (green): Each tile describes how a body is held in midair above water.
- Hint 2 (green): Swim England labels four of these with letters A through D.
- Hint 1 (blue): Add an s to each tile and it reads like a team name in a pro hockey league.
- Hint 2 (blue): Just one of these four is an animal.
- Hint 1 (purple): Put these tiles in a certain order and they form one complete sentence.
- Hint 2 (purple): A broadcaster shouted that sentence during a 1965 playoff game.

## What makes Tuesday's board slippery

Purple is the trap. Its tiles do not share a theme; they share a sentence, so two of them look like leftover filler. A solver hunting for four related nouns will keep rejecting them. Solve the three vocabulary groups first and the sentence assembles itself from what remains.

A few tiles also borrow meaning from sports they do not belong to. Check each of those against the group it seems to fit before you submit. ## Tuesday's answers, #785, Nov. 17, 2026 (spoilers)

Stop here if you still want to solve it. Beebom and Nerdschalk list the same four groups and sixteen entries.

- **Yellow, "Youth Sports Levels":** Freshman, Peewee, Travel, Varsity
- **Green, "Diving Positions":** Free, Pike, Straight, Tuck
- **Blue, "Former NHL Team Names, in Singular Form":** Coyote, Nordique, North Star, Whaler
- **Purple, "Havlicek Stole the Ball":** Havlicek, Stole, The, Ball

## Who each entry is

- **Havlicek, Stole, The, Ball:** NBA.com says John Havlicek of the Boston Celtics stole an inbounds pass in Game 7 of the 1965 Eastern Conference Finals against Philadelphia. Broadcaster Johnny Most's call was "Havlicek stole the ball!"
- **Straight, Pike, Tuck, Free:** Swim England lists the four diving positions as Straight (A), Pike (B), Tuck (C) and Free (D).
- **Coyote, Nordique, North Star, Whaler:** the group name calls them former National Hockey League (NHL) team names written in the singular.
- **Freshman, Peewee, Travel, Varsity:** common terms for levels of play, from school teams to community leagues.

## Where the traps sit on Tuesday's board

| Tile | Tempting spot | Where it goes | Why it tempts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | Youth Sports Levels | Diving Positions | It reads like a label for a division or a kind of kick, but Swim England lists Free as position D |
| Travel | Havlicek Stole the Ball | Youth Sports Levels | Travel is also a basketball violation, and purple is a basketball moment |
| Stole | a sports statistic | Havlicek Stole the Ball | It looks like a mangled steal; here it is the verb in the 1965 call |
| Straight | Youth Sports Levels | Diving Positions | A plain adjective with no sport attached, until you see it is position A |

The basketball pull on Travel is the sharpest trap, because the real basketball group is the one made of a sentence. Free comes next: it has three plausible homes and only the swimming list uses it as a formal position name.
