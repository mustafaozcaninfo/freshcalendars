**The New York Times (NYT) Crossword for Thursday, Nov. 12, 2026, was constructed by Jared Cappel and edited by Will Shortz: 78 clues in a grid the puzzle data lists as 15 columns by 17 rows.** Thursday brings a revealer and a missing-letters trick, six clues carry a question mark, and one entry is 15 squares wide. A hint for every clue comes first, each behind a tap, and the answers close the page.

## Six question-mark clues and how each one misleads

A question mark flags a clue that means something other than its plain reading. Two of today's six sit in the Across list and four in the Down list. None of the notes below names an answer.

- **4-Across, "What's never heard in indictments?"** (3 letters). The word is long and legal, and one of its letters is never pronounced. The entry is that letter written out the way you would say its name.
- **52-Across, "In which students might seek the root of their problems?"** (4 letters). "Problems" are homework questions here, and "root" is a number operation, not something under a plant. The entry is the class where both belong.
- **9-Down, "People who are told what to say?"** (6 letters). Someone hands these people a script. The entry is their job title, in the plural.
- **29-Down, "Bed rock item?"** (6 letters). Read "bed" as a piece of furniture for a baby and "rock" as a verb, and the geology reading drops away.
- **55-Down, "Move quickly?"** (4 letters). The question mark asks you to make a word quick: a longer word for relocating has been clipped. Nothing here involves speed.
- **60-Down, "Diesel in a muscle car?"** (3 letters). "Diesel" is not a fuel. It is a surname, and the entry is the first name that goes with it.

34-Down is a double-duty clue without a question mark: it names a Greek letter and an airline's service in one line, and a single three-letter entry satisfies both.

## How Thursday's theme is laid out

Four Down slots carry the theme: 3-Down (11 letters, column 3), 5-Down (8, column 6), 8-Down (8, column 10) and 10-Down (11, column 13). All four start in row 2, the first row with letters. Their clues describe whole phrases, but the grid holds each phrase without its opening word or syllable, three or four letters short in every case. The 55-Across revealer says those starts have "grown" in the plot it describes, and 60-Across, the 15-letter entry in row 15, is clued as a dish made from what grew there. The answer section names each missing start. Solve 55-Across and 60-Across first and the four short entries fall into line.

## What the 15-by-17 shape means

The data holds 255 squares: 15 columns and 17 rows. The first row is black from end to end, which leaves a solving area of 15 by 16, or 240 squares. Of those, 40 are black and 200 hold a letter, a black share of 16.7 percent. Wednesday's grid was 15 by 15 with 36 black squares and 189 letters, a black share of 16.0 percent. Counting the empty top row, Thursday has 55 black squares, 21.6 percent of 255, but only 40 of them shape the puzzle.

Both days have 78 entries. Today's split is 40 Across and 38 Down, where Wednesday's was 39 and 39. Every letter square is crossed by one Across and one Down entry, and every square holds a single letter, so there is no rebus. The black pattern does not match itself when the grid is turned upside down.
