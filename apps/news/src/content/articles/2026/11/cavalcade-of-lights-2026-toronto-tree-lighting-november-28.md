**Toronto's Cavalcade of Lights opening night is Saturday, Nov. 28, 2026, from 6 to 11 p.m. at Nathan Phillips Square, and the Christmas tree is lit at 6:30 p.m.**, according to the City of Toronto's [Cavalcade of Lights page](https://www.toronto.ca/cavalcade), dated Nov. 13, 2026. The city lists the event as running from Nov. 28 to Jan. 8, presented by Desjardins Financial Group, and calls it a free event that "welcomes winter with a celebration of light and local artists." Below is the city's full timed schedule for opening night and a printable version.

## How should you plan the opening night?

The tree lighting is at 6:30 p.m., half an hour after the 6 p.m. start of opening night, so arriving before 6:30 p.m. puts you at the square for the countdown. The city lists no fireworks in its 2026 opening-night program.

Public skating on the rink is listed in three windows: 6 to 6:45 p.m., 7:35 to 8:30 p.m. and 9:15 to 11 p.m. The rink hosts the Star Doll and Carmen Sutra skating performances at 7:25 p.m. and 9:05 p.m. The lantern parade runs from 8 to 8:30 p.m., and the lantern workshops that feed it are open from 6 to 10 p.m. The city says to bring your own skates or rent them on site; it does not list rental prices.

## What else is on at Nathan Phillips Square during the run?

Two art installations are "illuminated daily from 6 to 11 p.m.": Echo, an interactive voice installation produced by Desjardins Financial Group, and Ripples in Time, a human-scale spatial clock by Gathering. From Dec. 4 to 12 the city's official Menorah is illuminated "at approximately 4 p.m." every evening during Hanukkah, and one more candle is lit each night. The city has not published the rink's 2026-27 season opening date, regular hours or the nightly hours of the tree lights on this page.

## How do you get to Nathan Phillips Square?

The square is at 100 Queen St. W. The city says Queen Street West is closed between York Street and Bay Street from 3 to 10 p.m., and Queen Street remains closed between Bay Street and Church Street. Parking is available nearby and under the square. The city's page says only to visit the Toronto Transit Commission (TTC) website, TTC.ca, to plan a trip, and it mentions a GO Transit One-Day Weekend Pass and a Weekday Group Pass. It also says the square offers accessible ramps, underground parking and washroom facilities for people who use wheelchairs and mobility aids.
