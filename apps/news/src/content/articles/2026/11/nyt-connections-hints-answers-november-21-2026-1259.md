## Solving tips

Start with the tile that has the fewest meanings to juggle. A short word like Own is hard to place by meaning, but it snaps into a phrase quickly, so try a few opening words in front of it. For the longer tiles, read the hints' letter counts first; a count of eleven or twelve shrinks the choices fast.

## A red-herring map: tiles that pass for two groups

Four overlaps decide this board. Each has a test that settles it.

| Tiles | Why they tempt | Test that sorts them | Where they sit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Butter, Sugar | Pantry staples, so a baking-ingredients group seems likely | Add "cookie" after each; a solver who needs a fifth ingredient has the wrong idea | Yellow, Yellow |
| Girl Scout, Empress, Hierophant | Each names a kind of person or title | "The" fits Empress and Hierophant; "cookie" fits Girl Scout | Yellow, Blue, Blue |
| Breath, Tongue | Both belong to the mouth and throat | Put "Hold your" in front; Horses and Own work the same way, and no other body words appear | Purple, Purple |
| Courtesy, Helpfulness, Satisfaction | Manners and good qualities | Ask whether a form could give it a score from one to five; Wait Time passes the same test, in minutes | Green, Green, Green |

Multi-word tiles are a trap of their own. Black and White, Girl Scout and Wait Time all contain spaces, so a solver hunting for "phrases" may group them. Black and White and Girl Scout go to yellow and Wait Time goes to green, and the three never form a set.

Fool and Devil are everyday words that look like insults or character types. Neither one carries the group's idea until "The" is put in front. Hierophant is the tile that gives the whole group away.
