**Victor Schmitt is the constructor of the New York Times (NYT) Midi Crossword for Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026, titled "Out of Thin Air," a 9-by-10 grid with 32 clues.** Every clue has a hint behind a tap, and the full answers sit in the marked section at the bottom.

## How does "Out of Thin Air" tie the theme together?

The title is an idiom for something that appears from nowhere, and three clues talk about the puzzle itself, which is where the theme lives. 5-Down asks for an animal that is "pulled" in "this puzzle's magic act." 16-Across offers its answer as "an alternative title for this puzzle?" 19-Across, joined by 20-Across, supplies "appropriate shout upon solving this puzzle."

Put together, the clues describe a stage trick: something is produced, a title is renamed and an audience-style shout closes it. No answer is named here. The 16-Across entry is the longest in the grid, with 8 letters, and the 5-Down entry has 6.

## Which clue is the least literal today?

18-Down: *What comes around?* The question mark marks it as wordplay. The clue is half of a saying, so read it as the second half of a familiar line about what returns to you, not as a question about orbits or deliveries. The answer has 5 letters, and its first square is also the last square of 16-Across.

The other clue to read twice is 23-Across, whose own text contains the misspelling "corsswrod." That spelling is printed in the official data, so it is part of the puzzle.

## What does the grid shape show?

The grid has 90 squares: 23 black and 67 with a letter. It has 32 entries, 17 Across and 15 Down, and every white square belongs to both an Across and a Down entry.

| Entry length | Entries | Across | Down |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 letters | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| 3 letters | 14 | 9 | 5 |
| 4 letters | 3 | 0 | 3 |
| 5 letters | 5 | 3 | 2 |
| 6 letters | 6 | 1 | 5 |
| 7 letters | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| 8 letters | 1 | 1 | 0 |

The two longest entries both run Across: 16-Across with 8 letters in row 6, and 13-Across with 7 in row 4. Down holds five of the six 6-letter entries and all three 4-letter ones. Four of those 6-letter Downs, 3-Down, 4-Down, 5-Down and 6-Down, start in the top row and end in row 6.

Eight of the 15 Down entries cross 16-Across, one for each of its squares, and 7 cross 13-Across. The bottom of the grid is the opposite: from row 8 down, only 3-letter entries remain, in two 3-by-3 blocks at the bottom left and right. The black squares do not match when the grid is turned upside down.
