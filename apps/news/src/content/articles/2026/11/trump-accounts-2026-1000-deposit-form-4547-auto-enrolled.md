**The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) says that starting Oct. 1, 2026, the Secretary of the Treasury automatically enrolled eligible children in Trump Accounts, but the one-time $1,000 federal contribution still has to be elected with Form 4547.** The IRS says you must elect it "even if Treasury automatically opened the account." Below: a dated timeline, which money counts toward the $5,000 yearly limit and a two-step checklist. A one-page printable holds the dates.

## Who gets the $1,000 Trump Account contribution?

The IRS says the federal government provides a one-time $1,000 contribution to eligible children who meet all three conditions:

- They are U.S. citizens.
- They were born from Jan. 1, 2025, through Dec. 31, 2028.
- They have valid Social Security numbers (SSNs).

A separate rule decides who can have an account at all. The IRS frequently asked questions (FAQ) page says the child "will not turn 18 before the end of the calendar year" in which the account is established. Proposed regulations (IR-2026-33, Mar. 6, 2026) say the election to open an initial account "must be made on or before Dec. 31 of the calendar year" in which the individual turns 17. Those rules are proposed, not final.

## How do you claim the account and elect the $1,000?

Claiming the account and electing the money are two steps, and the IRS treats them separately. Per the IRS Trump Accounts page:

1. **Claim the account.** Even if an account was opened automatically, you must claim it before you can manage it. The IRS says you can claim it through the Trump Accounts app, and that Form 4547 is not required for this step.
2. **Elect the $1,000.** Submit Form 4547 and select the option for the $1,000 pilot program contribution. The IRS FAQ says you can file it through the Trump Accounts app, the Trump Accounts website or your IRS Individual Online Account.

The Form 4547 instructions (updated Apr. 30, 2026) add two more routes: file it with your current-year e-filed tax return, or on paper to the address at IRS.gov/PaperReturns. They also say Form 4547 "can be filed at any time." To fix a mistake on a Form 4547, the IRS FAQ says not to file Form 1040-X.

On Mar. 31, 2026, the IRS said 4 million children had been signed up for Trump Accounts and 1 million were claiming the $1,000 pilot contribution (IR-2026-42). Those counts are from that release; later figures are not in the sources used here.

## When will the $1,000 arrive?

No deposit date is published. The Form 4547 instructions say Treasury makes the pilot program contribution after the election is made, when practicable, and that no pilot contribution will be deposited in a child's account earlier than July 4, 2026. The IRS has not published a deadline to elect the $1,000, so the last-birth-date row above is the only end date in the sources.

## Which money counts toward the $5,000 limit?

IR-2025-117 describes an aggregate limit of $5,000 per year, indexed to inflation and adjusting starting after 2027. Notice 2025-68 sorts contributions this way:

| Type of money | Counts toward the $5,000? |
|---|---|
| Parents, the child or any other person | Yes |
| Employer, up to $2,500 per year (IR-2025-117) | Yes |
| The $1,000 pilot contribution | No |
| Qualified general contributions from states, the United States, tribal governments and 501(c)(3) charities | No |
| Rollovers | No |

Treasury's July 4, 2026 release adds that employers, charitable organizations and governments can contribute, and that children can receive those contributions only if they have an account. The sources do not say whether the $2,500 employer figure is indexed.

## How are the funds invested, and when can they be used?

Notice 2025-68 allows investment in a mutual fund or exchange-traded fund (ETF) that tracks an index of primarily U.S. companies, such as the S&P 500, and excludes funds with fees over 0.1% a year. The notice is an intent to issue regulations, not final rules. The growth period ends Jan. 1 of the year the child turns 18. After that, individual retirement account rules apply, including a 10% additional tax on early distributions unless an exception applies.

Michael and Susan Dell pledged $6.25 billion in December 2025, with $250 per child for children 10 and under, according to NBC News (Dec. 2, 2025) and the Associated Press via ABC7 (Dec. 3, 2025). The IRS pages used here do not describe that pledge.
