**Waffle (wafflegame.net) puzzle #1766, for Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026, deals 5 green tiles, 7 yellow and 9 gray.** A clue for each word comes first, then a look at the colors, and the answers wait in the marked last section.

Today the four corners and the centre are the only tiles in place, and one letter shows up four times in the finished grid; how the colors and swaps work is on our [Waffle page](/news/topic/waffle/#how-it-works).

## One clue for each word

**Across**

- **Row 1:** starts with M and ends with L. A small copy of a ship, plane or building, or a person who poses for artists.
- **Row 3:** starts with N and ends with Y. Loud and full of racket; a crowded street at rush hour is this.
- **Row 5:** starts with Y and ends with L. A way of singing that flips quickly between chest voice and a high falsetto, common in the Alps.

**Down**

- **Column 1:** starts with M and ends with Y. Describes a stray dog with patchy fur and a skin condition.
- **Column 3:** starts with D and ends with D. What fruit looks like after it has been left in the sun to shrivel.
- **Column 5:** starts with L and ends with L. Faithful to a friend, a team or a country.

## What the starting colors say about each word

Greens sit only on crossings today, and 16 of the 21 tiles are away from home: 7 yellow, 9 gray.

Row 1 has no gray tile, so every letter in it belongs somewhere in that word; only the order is wrong. Column 3 is the other busy one, with three yellows around its green centre.

Four of the six words repeat no letter. The other two each carry the same letter at both ends, column 3 and column 5, so every one of those four tiles sits on a crossing, and the two in column 5 already start green.

## The O shuffle

Four O tiles sit on the board at the start, at row 1, column 3, row 3, column 1, row 3, column 5 and row 5, column 3. None is on an O square. The finished grid has four O's too, in row 1, column 2, row 2, column 5, row 3, column 2 and row 5, column 2, so each O has to jump to a different square, and the three E tiles are all misplaced as well.

Watch for the O that is the most awkward of the four: one tile cannot reach its square directly and has to rest on the way. If you find yourself moving O twice, you are on the right track.

### How the O tiles travel

- **Swap 1:** the O from row 1, column 3 lands in row 1, column 2, its home. The E it replaces waits at row 1, column 3.
- **Swap 6:** the O from row 3, column 5 goes up to row 2, column 5, home.
- **Swap 8:** the O from row 5, column 3 moves to row 3, column 2, home, and D drops into place.
- **Swaps 7 and 10:** the O from row 3, column 1 is the long traveler. Swap 7 parks it at row 4, column 5 while N takes its place; swap 10 then sends it to row 5, column 2.

### Why 10 is the minimum

Sixteen tiles start out of place. A loop of n tiles costs n minus 1 swaps, so swaps equal misplaced tiles minus loops. Here the 16 tiles form 6 loops, which leaves 10 swaps, and no route can use fewer.
