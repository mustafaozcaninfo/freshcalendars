## How Pips works

You place every domino so that each colored region meets its condition. A number means the pips in the region add up to it; = means every half shows the same value; ≠ means all are different; > and < set a total above or below a number; a region with no condition takes anything. Rows are counted from the top and columns from the left, so R2C5 is the second row, fifth column.

## Solving tips

Look for single cells with a limit: a cell that must be less than 2, as R1C3 is on Thursday's Easy board, can only hold a 0 or a 1. An "equal" region shrinks your options fast once one value is known; a double such as the 3-3 on the Medium board can supply two matching halves at once. On the Easy board, the two regions that add up to 11 each need a 6 and a 5, so the 5-2 and 5-1 dominoes go in different regions. On the Hard board, R2C1, R3C1 and R3C2 must total more than 17. Three halves can total 18 at most, so all three hold a 6. The board has six 6s, and the 6-6 double can supply two of them at once. R7C8 must be more than 3, so it holds a 4, 5 or 6.
