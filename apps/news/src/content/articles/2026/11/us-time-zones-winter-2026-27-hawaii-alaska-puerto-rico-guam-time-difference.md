From Nov. 1, 2026 to March 14, 2027, when it is noon Eastern Time (ET) it is 11 a.m. Central Time (CT), 10 a.m. Mountain Time (MT) and in Arizona, 9 a.m. Pacific Time (PT), 8 a.m. in Alaska, 7 a.m. in Hawaii and 1 p.m. in Puerto Rico, under the nine zones set by federal law, 15 U.S.C. 263, as published by Cornell Law School's Legal Information Institute. The conversions are computed with the Internet Assigned Numbers Authority (IANA) Time Zone Database. Below are the winter and summer rows side by side, an hour-by-hour chart and what changes on March 14, 2027.

## Noon ET in every U.S. time zone, winter and summer

The table converts one fixed moment, noon ET. Winter is Nov. 1, 2026 to March 14, 2027. Summer is March 14 to Nov. 7, 2027, the stretch in which 15 U.S.C. 260a runs daylight saving time, from 2 a.m. on the second Sunday of March to the first Sunday of November.

| Place | When it is noon ET, winter | When it is noon ET, summer |
|---|---|---|
| Puerto Rico and U.S. Virgin Islands | 1 p.m. | noon |
| Central | 11 a.m. | 11 a.m. |
| Mountain | 10 a.m. | 10 a.m. |
| Arizona | 10 a.m. | 9 a.m. |
| Pacific | 9 a.m. | 9 a.m. |
| Alaska | 8 a.m. | 8 a.m. |
| Adak, Aleutian Islands | 7 a.m. | 7 a.m. |
| Hawaii | 7 a.m. | 6 a.m. |
| American Samoa | 6 a.m. | 5 a.m. |
| Guam and Northern Mariana Islands | 3 a.m. the next day | 2 a.m. the next day |

All values are computed with the IANA database. Adak, in the Aleutians, uses daylight time, so it keeps the same gap to ET in both seasons. Hawaii, Arizona, Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands and American Samoa keep the same clock time all year in the IANA database.

## Which nine zones does federal law name?

Section 263 lists the standard time zones of the United States. The names below are the statute's; the places are from the IANA database.

| Zone in 15 U.S.C. 263 | Places in this story |
|---|---|
| Atlantic standard time | Puerto Rico, U.S. Virgin Islands |
| Eastern standard time | The ET reference for every row here |
| Central standard time | CT rows |
| Mountain standard time | MT rows; Arizona uses the same winter clock |
| Pacific standard time | PT rows |
| Alaska standard time | Alaska rows |
| Hawaii-Aleutian standard time | Hawaii, Adak |
| Samoa standard time | American Samoa, UTC minus 11 |
| Chamorro standard time | Guam, Northern Mariana Islands, UTC plus 10 |

Atlantic Standard Time (AST) appears first in the statute's list. Samoa Standard Time (SST) is Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) minus 11 hours, and Chamorro Standard Time (ChST) is UTC plus 10 hours, according to the IANA database. Chamorro was added by Public Law 106-564 on Dec. 23, 2000. A 1983 law renamed "Yukon" to Alaska, "Alaska-Hawaii" to Hawaii-Aleutian and "Bering" to Samoa.

## What changes on March 14, 2027?

At 2 a.m. on Sunday, March 14, 2027, the second Sunday of March, daylight saving time starts under 15 U.S.C. 260a. Computed with the IANA database, the gap to ET shifts for the zones that do not move.

| Place | Moves on March 14, 2027? | Winter gap to ET | Summer gap to ET |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eastern, Central, Mountain, Pacific, Alaska, Adak | Yes, forward 1 hour | Unchanged within the group | Unchanged within the group |
| Puerto Rico, U.S. Virgin Islands | No | 1 hour ahead | Same time |
| Arizona | No | 2 hours behind | 3 hours behind |
| Hawaii | No | 5 hours behind | 6 hours behind |
| American Samoa | No | 6 hours behind | 7 hours behind |
| Guam, Northern Mariana Islands | No | 15 hours ahead | 14 hours ahead |

Arizona's entry covers the state except the Navajo Nation, which the IANA database lists separately. ## Which states can skip daylight saving time?

Under section 260a a State "may by law exempt itself" if the entire State observes standard time, and it has a separate rule for states in more than one zone. The statute describes the mechanism only; the clocks that stay fixed in the tables above come from the IANA database.
