**Festivus 2026 is Wednesday, Dec. 23, the date the Seinfeld episode "The Strike" gave the holiday, according to the Library of Congress.** The Library's American Folklife Center says the show aired in December and "the holiday in the show was set on December 23". Festivus is not a public holiday, so nothing closes. The weekdays of Dec. 23 through 2031 and a printable grievances card follow.

## When is Festivus in 2026?

Dec. 23, 2026 is a Wednesday. That is two days before Christmas Day on Friday, Dec. 25 (see [when is Christmas](/when-is/christmas/)) and two days after the winter solstice on Monday, Dec. 21, covered in our [winter solstice story](/news/winter-solstice-2026-december-21-shortest-day-sunset-times/). The full week of observances is in [observances from Dec. 21 to Dec. 27](/news/national-days-this-week-december-21-27-2026/).

The Library of Congress writes that Festivus "is most commonly celebrated on December 23 or another date in December", so a different December date is also fair game.

## What is Festivus?

The Library of Congress describes Festivus as a holiday from the Seinfeld episode "The Strike," "which aired on December 18, 1997." The show's traditions have become part of how people celebrate:

- **The pole.** An aluminum Festivus pole, which the Library describes in some homes as "a bare upright pole about the size of a Christmas tree". It adds that "Some people feel the aluminum Festivus pole should be decorated with lights".
- **The airing of grievances.** In the Library's words, it "is gleefully made part of new celebrations".
- **The feats of strength.** "The 'Feats of Strength' is a wrestling match on Seinfeld."
- **Festivus miracles.** Kramer declares ordinary events "Festivus miracles".
- **Dinner.** "For many, dinner is central to Festivus." The O'Keefe family dinner was "usually turkey, but also sometimes ham, lamb chops, or stew, followed by a pecan pie."

## Where did Festivus come from?

The Library says "In February 1966, author Daniel O'Keefe, Sr. and his wife Deborah celebrated the first Festivus". Their eldest son, Dan O'Keefe, became a writer on Seinfeld.

Two newsrooms agree the family holiday had no fixed date. FOX 5 New York reported on Dec. 23, 2021 that the celebration "could be sprung upon them at any time between May and September" and that "the holiday didn't technically fall specifically on Dec. 23 as it does on the show". The Philadelphia Inquirer wrote on Dec. 23, 2022: "It had no official date." In the show, the Inquirer reported, Festivus was set on Dec. 23 "to get a leg up on Christmas".

## Has a Festivus pole ever stood in a state capitol?

In December 2013, Deerfield Beach software developer Chaz Stevens put a 6-foot pole made of beer cans in the Florida State Capitol rotunda, the Tallahassee public radio newsroom of Florida State University (WFSU) and the University of Missouri-Kansas City public radio station (KCUR), which carried the story for National Public Radio (NPR), both reported on Dec. 11, 2013. It was a counter-display to a nativity scene.
