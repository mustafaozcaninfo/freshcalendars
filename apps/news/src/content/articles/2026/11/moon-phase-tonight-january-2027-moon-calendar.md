**The full moon of January 2027 is at 7:17 a.m. Eastern Standard Time (EST) on Friday, Jan. 22**, which is 12:17 Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), according to the U.S. Naval Observatory (USNO). In New York the Moon is 100% lit that Friday, rises at 5:25 p.m. and sets at 7:27 a.m., ten minutes after the exact full moon.

## Does January's full moon have a name?

The Old Farmer's Almanac calls January's full moon the Wolf Moon; USNO lists phases and times, not names. The sources we used give no distance from Earth for January's full moon, so none is given. For the whole year of full moons, see our [2027 moon phases story](/news/moon-phases-2027-full-moon-calendar/).

## Moon phase on New Year's Day and Martin Luther King Jr. Day

On Friday, Jan. 1 the Moon is a waning crescent, 31% lit, and rises at 1:46 a.m. in New York, setting at 12:07 p.m. On Monday, Jan. 18, Martin Luther King Jr. Day, it is a waxing gibbous, 80% lit, rising at 12:35 p.m. and setting at 3:27 a.m. the next morning in the table above, per USNO.

## How bright is the Moon on the Quadrantid peak night?

The American Meteor Society (AMS) lists the Quadrantid peak night as Jan. 3-4 and says predictions for 2027 show a peak near 3:30 UTC on Jan. 4, which is 10:30 p.m. EST on Sunday, Jan. 3. The AMS gives the Moon as 18% illuminated, a waning crescent, and calls conditions generally favorable. USNO puts the Moon at 15% lit on Jan. 3 and 9% on Jan. 4 in New York, with moonrise at 3:55 a.m. and 4:57 a.m., so the late-evening hours before moonrise are dark. We use USNO's percentages throughout.
