**Nguhi Ross and Erik Agard built the New York Times (NYT) Midi Crossword for Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, titled "Now in Color," a 10-by-10 grid with 33 clues.** Each clue has a hint behind a tap, and the full answers wait in the marked section at the bottom.

## How does "Now in Color" show up in the grid?

The six longest entries carry the theme, and where they sit is the joke. Rows 1, 2 and 3 each hold one 10-letter Across entry, and the clues for 1-Across, 11-Across and 12-Across all start with "Black-and-white." Rows 8, 9 and 10 hold the other three: 25-Across and 29-Across with 10 letters each, and 30-Across with 8, and their clues all start with "Colorful."

Read from top to bottom, the puzzle goes from black-and-white to color, as the title says. Those six entries use 58 of the 83 white squares. The four rows between them, 4 to 7, hold only entries of 3, 4 and 5 letters.

## Which clue is the least literal today?

2-Down: *Singer Rita referred to in The Onion headline "Agent Scores Singer Another Prime Crossword Puzzle Placement."* The clue does not ask for a word in the usual way. It points to a satirical headline that jokes about a name turning up in crosswords again and again, and the answer is that singer's surname. It has 3 letters, begins with O and ends with A. Its three squares are the second letters of 1-Across, 11-Across and 12-Across, so it runs straight through the top three theme rows.

The other clue to read slowly is 23-Down, which asks for an "Olympic code" rather than a country. The answer is a 3-letter abbreviation, not a full name.

## What does the grid shape show?

The grid has 100 squares: 17 black and 83 with a letter. It has 33 entries, 13 Across and 20 Down.

All five 10-letter entries run Across, and so does the only 8-letter one. Down tops out at 6 letters, with four of those. Ten Down entries start in row 1, one in every column, so each of the three top theme entries is crossed ten times. Row 8 is crossed ten times as well, by the Down entries 15, 16, 17, 19, 20, 23, 24, 26, 27 and 28.

The black squares sit in the middle of the grid, 4 in row 4, 5 in row 5, 3 in row 6 and 3 in row 7, plus the last two squares of row 10.
