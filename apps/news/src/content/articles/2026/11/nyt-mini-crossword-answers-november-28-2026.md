## The one clue that plays tricks

Several of the 16 clues are plain definitions, such as the crab limb, the quiz taker and the circuit of a track. The 9-Across clue, "Holders of private information?", is the least literal. Read in the usual way, "private information" suggests a password, a diary or a secret. The question mark is the setter's flag that this is not the usual reading.

Here "private" is a rank, and the information is the kind printed on a small piece of metal. The "holders" are the objects, and the clue asks for them in the plural. Once you stop thinking about secrets and picture someone in uniform, the seven letters come quickly, and the crossing Down answers (the 3-Down entry above all) confirm which letters sit in the middle.

## How the Saturday grid is built

Five black squares shape this puzzle: one at row 4, column 4, and four across the bottom row. Without a black square in rows 1, 2 or 3, those rows run edge to edge, so three 7-letter Across answers are stacked directly on top of each other. That leaves 44 of 49 squares open.

Three Across entries are only 3 letters: 10-Across and 11-Across on either side of the row-4 black square, and 15-Across alone in the bottom row. Three Down entries reach that last row, 3-Down, 13-Down and 5-Down, and each one drops a single letter into the bottom answer.
