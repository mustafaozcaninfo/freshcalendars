**The New York Times (NYT) Mini Crossword for Tuesday, Nov. 24, 2026, is a 5-by-5 grid by Joel Fagliano, according to the official data, with 10 clues.** Each hint below opens on a tap. The grid's two black squares sit one above the other at the left edge, which shortens one Down entry to three letters.

## Hints for every clue

**Across**

- **1-Across** (4 letters), *Spiders spin it*: the fine thread a spider produces, which is also a smooth fabric; its four letters start 1-Down, 2-Down, 3-Down and 4-Down in order.
- **5-Across** (4 letters), *Pad ___*: the adjective for a Southeast Asian country, and the word that follows Pad in a stir-fried noodle dish; its first letter is the second letter of 1-Down and its last is the second letter of 4-Down.
- **6-Across** (5 letters), *They make wakes on a lake*: a plural noun for vessels that leave a trail in the water; it starts 6-Down and ends with the third letter of 4-Down.
- **7-Across** (5 letters), *Occupant of Graceland*: the first name of the singer who lived at the Memphis estate; it starts with the second letter of 6-Down and ends with the fourth letter of 4-Down.
- **8-Across** (5 letters), *Itsy-bitsy*: a playful way to say very small; it starts with the last letter of 6-Down and ends with the last letter of 4-Down.

**Down**

- **1-Down** (5 letters), *Robbed*: the past tense of a verb for taking what is not yours; it starts with the first letter of 1-Across and its third letter is the second letter of 6-Across.
- **2-Down** (5 letters), *Response to "Who's seen this movie?"*: a short reply of subject and verb, run together in the grid; it starts with the second letter of 1-Across and its second letter is the second letter of 5-Across.
- **3-Down** (5 letters), *What you're mostly speaking when you say "He's a bona fide persona non grata"*: the name of a language, the one the foreign words in the quote come from; it starts with the third letter of 1-Across and ends with the fourth letter of 8-Across.
- **4-Down** (5 letters), *Kind of face with puckered lips*: an adjective that describes a face made for a smooch; it starts with the last letter of 1-Across and ends with the last letter of 8-Across.
- **6-Down** (3 letters), *Vegas wager*: the short word for what you place at a casino table; it starts with the first letter of 6-Across and ends with the first letter of 8-Across.

## How the longest clues steer you in the Nov. 24 Mini

The clues here are short almost everywhere. Nine of the ten run six words or fewer, and the 3-Down clue runs 14. That length is itself a clue: the setter needed a whole sentence because the answer is about the sentence, not about any one word in it.

Read 3-Down by its wording. The quotation marks hold a phrase with a foreign borrowing, and the question asks what you are "mostly speaking." That adverb admits the sentence is a blend, so the answer names the tongue that supplies the borrowed share of the words, and you only need to sort the words of the quote by origin. It is the least literal clue today because nothing in the grid is named; the quote is evidence you analyze.

The 2-Down clue works the other way around. "Response" tells you the entry answers a question, and the contraction in "Who's seen" hides a helping verb that a reply would reuse. Since a crossword grid has no gaps between words, the reply collapses into a single five-letter run. Fill it last: its second letter comes from 5-Across in row two, and its first from the fabric word at the top.

For 4-Down, "Kind of face" leaves a slot for an adjective before the word face, and the puckered lips supply the meaning of that adjective. Nothing in the clue is quoted or punned, so the difficulty is only finding the informal word.

The best first move is 1-Across. A spider's product is unambiguous, and its four letters start four of the five Down entries, so four clues have a first letter before you read them. After that, 6-Down gives the first letters of the bottom three Across entries.
