**The 2027 Formula 1 (F1) season has 24 Grands Prix in 22 countries, opening in Bahrain on Sun., March 14, 2027 and ending in Abu Dhabi on Sun., Dec. 12, 2027, with 10 Sprint weekends, according to Formula 1's official calendar.** The calendar came out on Sept. 16, 2026, after approval by the World Motor Sport Council, and the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA) said the number of Sprint events rose from six to ten for 2027. Session and race start times are not yet announced.

The table below lists every Grand Prix (GP) weekend with its race-day weekday, computed from the calendar, the circuit and a Sprint flag. A one-page copy is in the downloads box with this story. To place the races against other plans, use the [2027 printable yearly calendar](/printable-yearly-calendar.html).

## Which 2027 F1 weekends have a Sprint?

Ten of the 24 weekends include a Sprint: Bahrain, Australia, Japan, Canada, Monaco, Great Britain, Italy, São Paulo, Qatar and Abu Dhabi. Formula 1 says Bahrain, Australia, Japan, Monaco and Abu Dhabi will host Sprint events for the first time, and that Sprint events also return to Montreal, Silverstone, Monza, Sao Paulo and Lusail. The FIA put the change at six Sprint events to ten. Sprint session dates and format details are not yet announced.

## What is new on the 2027 F1 calendar?

Formula 1 lists a return to Portugal in June, after Monaco, and Türkiye rejoining in October between Azerbaijan and Singapore. Formula 1 announced on April 24, 2026 that Istanbul Park returns from 2027 on a five-year agreement, so it is on the calendar through the 2031 season. In Spain, Madring said on Sept. 18, 2026 that it will host the Formula 1 Lenovo Gran Premio de España from Sept. 10 to 12, 2027, in Madrid. The official 2027 calendar has no Dutch Grand Prix and no race in Barcelona.

## How many back-to-back race weekends are there in 2027?

These counts are computed from the race days above, not stated by Formula 1. Eleven of the 23 gaps between races are exactly seven days, so eleven pairs of races fall on consecutive weekends.

| Run of consecutive weekends | Race days |
|---|---|
| Three in a row | Australia, Japan and China (Apr. 4, 11 and 18) |
| Three in a row | Azerbaijan, Türkiye and Singapore (Sept. 26, Oct. 3 and 10) |
| Three in a row | United States, Mexico City and São Paulo (Oct. 24, 31 and Nov. 7) |
| Two in a row | Bahrain and Saudi Arabia (Mar. 14 and 21) |
| Two in a row | Great Britain and Austria (July 4 and 11) |
| Two in a row | Belgium and Hungary (July 25 and Aug. 1) |
| Two in a row | Italy and Spain (Sept. 5 and 12) |
| Two in a row | Qatar and Abu Dhabi (Dec. 5 and 12) |

The longest gap is 35 days, from Hungary on Aug. 1 to Italy on Sept. 5. The next longest is 21 days, from Miami on May 2 to Canada on May 23. Eight gaps are 14 days, one is 13 days (Brazil on Nov. 7 to Las Vegas on Nov. 20) and one is 15 days (Las Vegas to Qatar on Dec. 5).
