**Sam Ezersky built the New York Times (NYT) Mini Crossword for Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026, according to the official data: a 5-by-5 grid with 10 clues.** Hints open on a tap. Four black squares, one in the top right corner, one at the left edge of row 4 and two in row 5, cut the grid down to 21 open squares.

## Hints for every clue

**Across**

- **1-Across** (4 letters), *Apples that are typically 14 to 16 inches wide?*: a plural nickname for Apple's laptops, whose screens come in those widths; its four squares are the first squares of 1-Down, 2-Down, 3-Down and 4-Down.
- **5-Across** (5 letters), *Casino game with dice*: the table game where a shooter throws a pair and bets ride on the roll; its five squares cross all five Down entries.
- **7-Across** (5 letters), *Ditch, as plans*: a verb meaning to throw out an idea you no longer want, here in its base form; it sits directly under the casino word.
- **8-Across** (4 letters), *School such as Howard or Tuskegee, for short*: four initials for a kind of college with deep roots in American history; read the clue as asking for the category, not a campus name.
- **9-Across** (3 letters), *"___ the ramparts we watched ..."*: the missing word is a poetic clipped form of a preposition, taken from a line of the national anthem.

**Down**

- **1-Down** (3 letters), *Hosts ... or rappers*: the shortened plural of a word for a master of ceremonies, written as initials with a plural ending.
- **2-Down** (4 letters), *Towering attraction in St. Louis*: a curved structure by the Mississippi River; the shape is the same word that names a bend in a foot.
- **3-Down** (5 letters), *___-loading (pre-marathon activity)*: a clipped prefix for the nutrient runners pile onto their plates before a race; its last square is the first square of 9-Across.
- **4-Down** (5 letters), *Low bar?*: a key on a computer keyboard, found along the bottom edge; the answer is not about a pub or a limbo stick.
- **6-Down** (4 letters), *Spiky part of a cowboy boot*: the small wheel-tipped piece fixed at the heel that a rider uses to nudge a horse.

## Where to start in the Nov. 25 Mini

Counting shared squares in the grid gives a clear first move. Two Across entries (5-Across and 7-Across) and two Down entries (3-Down and 4-Down) each cross five other entries. The 4-letter 1-Across and 8-Across cross four each, and the 3-letter 9-Across and 1-Down cross three.

The casino clue is the best opener because it is plain trivia and its five squares touch every Down entry at once. Its letters land in the second square of 1-Down, 2-Down, 3-Down and 4-Down, and in the first square of 6-Down. With those five letters in, 1-Down needs only its first and third squares, and the casino word and 7-Across together give you the middle of every long Down entry.

The least literal clue is 4-Down. The question mark tells you "bar" is not a place that serves drinks and not a pole to clear. Think about a long, thin strip near the bottom of something you use every day; "Low" points to its position, and its five squares cross all five Across entries, so every row you fill checks it.

The other question mark, at 1-Across, plays on a brand name rather than a fruit, and the numbers in inches are the proof. At 1-Down, the three dots joining "Hosts" and "rappers" say one answer covers both jobs, which only works when the answer is an abbreviation.
