**The 2026 Rockefeller Center Christmas Tree Lighting Ceremony is Wednesday, Dec. 2, from 7 to 10 p.m. Eastern Time (ET), according to Rockefeller Center, and the tree reached Rockefeller Plaza on Saturday, Nov. 7.** The ceremony is not open to the public; the tree is lit near the end of NBC's Christmas in Rockefeller Center broadcast, which NBC New York says airs on NBC and Peacock.

Rockefeller Center has posted the lighting date and the tree's size and origin. The broadcast's airtime, host and performers are not yet announced. The dates, size and origin come from Rockefeller Center's pages unless noted; NBC New York and CBS New York supply the arrival details. Where a detail has not been announced, we say so.

## When the 2026 Rockefeller tree lighting starts in each time zone

Rockefeller Center lists the ceremony as "Wednesday, December 2, from 7–10 PM ET." Because it runs at one moment, the window falls an hour earlier in each zone to the west:

| Time zone | Lighting ceremony window |
|---|---|
| Eastern Time (ET) | 7 to 10 p.m. |
| Central Time (CT) | 6 to 9 p.m. |
| Mountain Time (MT) | 5 to 8 p.m. |
| Pacific Time (PT) | 4 to 7 p.m. |

The tree is lit near the end of the NBC broadcast, so the exact minute of the lighting depends on the airtime, which is not yet announced.

## Where the 2026 Rockefeller Christmas tree is from

The 2026 tree is a 76-foot-tall Norway spruce from West Edmeston, New York, in Otsego County. Rockefeller Center says it is about 75 years old, measures 50 feet in diameter and weighs about 11 tons. amNewYork reported that it was donated by Rebecca Decker.

Crews cut the tree down on Thursday, Nov. 5, and it traveled about 200 miles to Midtown on a 100-foot trailer, NBC New York reported. The truck turned into the plaza at about 7:30 a.m. on Saturday, Nov. 7, and a crane raised the tree into its stand. The donor family helped drive a spike into the trunk with a sledgehammer, an annual tradition. Next, crews put up scaffolding to wrap the tree in 50,000 lights and set the Swarovski star on top.

The tradition dates back to 1931, and the first formal Tree Lighting Ceremony followed in 1933, according to Rockefeller Center. For more than three decades, Rockefeller Center's head gardener, Erik Pauzé, has played a central role in selecting the annual tree.

## Rockefeller tree 2026 dates to mark

- **Saturday, Nov. 7:** The tree arrived in New York City on the second annual Meet the Tree Day. Rockefeller Center scheduled the free Holiday Workshop from 10 a.m. to 1 p.m., first come, first served.
- **Wednesday, Dec. 2:** The Tree Lighting Ceremony runs from 7 to 10 p.m. ET and airs on NBC and Peacock.
- **Thursday, Dec. 3:** A Holiday Top of the Rock ticket begins to include up-close access to the tree.
- **Jan. 9, 2027:** NBC New York reports the tree is on display until this date. Afterward it is milled into lumber and donated to Habitat for Humanity.

## How to watch the Rockefeller tree lighting

The tree lighting is free, and there are no public tickets for the ceremony. The area closest to the tree is reserved for invited guests and the broadcast, so spectators watch from the surrounding streets on a first-come, first-served basis. Rockefeller Center tells visitors to expect security checkpoints, street closures and a crowd that begins to build in the early afternoon.

From home, viewers can watch NBC's Christmas in Rockefeller Center broadcast on NBC and Peacock, according to NBC New York; the tree is lit near the end. NBC New York and Telemundo 47 said the winner of their Star Choir contest performs on the Dec. 2 broadcast. The host and the other performers are not yet announced.

Once the tree is up, viewing it from the Plaza is free, with the best view from the Channel Gardens along Fifth Avenue. Daily public viewing hours have not been posted. ## The tree's lights and the Swarovski star

Rockefeller Center says the tree carries more than 50,000 multicolored light-emitting diode (LED) lights strung along approximately five miles of wire. The Swarovski star, designed by architect Daniel Libeskind in 2018, weighs about 900 pounds; it features 70 spikes covered in three million crystals.
