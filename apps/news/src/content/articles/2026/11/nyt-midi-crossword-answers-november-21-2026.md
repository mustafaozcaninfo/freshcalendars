**The New York Times (NYT) Midi Crossword for Saturday, Nov. 21, 2026, is titled "Race Day," a 9-by-9 grid with 24 clues by Ian Livengood.** Every clue has a hint behind a tap, and the answers sit in the marked section near the bottom.

## How does "Race Day" show up in the grid?

Only one clue speaks to the title: the 9-letter entry across row 7 is clued as a flag that is "hinted at in middle of this puzzle." The hint is the shape of the black squares. Row 4 has black squares in columns 4 and 6, row 5 has one in column 5, and row 6 repeats columns 4 and 6, which draws an X around the center square.

The theme entry has no black square in its row, so it touches the left and right edges. The same pattern shows up twice, once in the answer and once in the black squares.

| Across 19 | Across | Row 8, column 1 | 9 | Directly below it, same length, same span |
| Down 17 | Down | Row 7, column 4 | 3 | Its first square is shared with the theme entry |
| Down 18 | Down | Row 7, column 6 | 3 | Its first square is shared with the theme entry |

Nine Down entries cross row 7, one in each column: 8-Down, 6-Down, 4-Down, 17-Down, 14-Down, 18-Down, 5-Down, 7-Down and 10-Down. No square of the theme entry stands alone, so a Down answer can confirm each of its letters.

## Which clue is the least literal today?

17-Down: *Beginning of "civilization"?* The quotation marks and the question mark are the signal. The clue is not after a word for beginnings. It wants the first letter of the quoted word, written out the way you would say the letter aloud, in 3 letters. It begins with C and ends with E.

The next clue down the list, 18-Down, also plays with the puzzle itself: it asks for a fitting reply to the last clue in a crossword, and it is the last clue in this one.

## What does the grid shape show?

The grid has 81 squares: 21 black and 60 with a letter. It has 24 entries, 12 Across and 12 Down.

The two 9-letter entries run Across and the two 8-letter ones run Down, so each direction owns one of the long lengths. The black squares sit at the top and bottom, 6 in row 1, 4 in row 2 and 4 in row 9, with none at all in rows 7 and 8.
