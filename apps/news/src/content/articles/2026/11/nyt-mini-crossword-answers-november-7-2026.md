**Joel Fagliano wrote the 20 clues in the New York Times (NYT) Mini Crossword for Saturday, Nov. 7, 2026, and the grid grows to seven squares on each side.** Two of the clues are puns, and one pairs a shark movie with a French film. Each hint below opens with a tap, and the answers stay at the bottom in a marked spoiler section.

## Hints for every clue

**Across**

- **1-Across** (3 letters), *Pupper*: internet slang for a young household animal that barks; it starts with D and ends with G.
- **4-Across** (3 letters), *Sight in a shark movie … or at the end of a French film*: the part of a shark that cuts through the water in English and a word for "the end" in French; it starts with F and ends with N.
- **7-Across** (3 letters), *"___ you serious?"*: the form of the verb "to be" that follows "you"; it starts with A and ends with E.
- **8-Across** (3 letters), *Genetic molecule*: the single-stranded cousin of the molecule that carries your genes, usually known by a three-letter short form; it starts with R and ends with A.
- **9-Across** (7 letters), *Canine expert*: the "canine" here is a kind of tooth, so think of the professional who checks your teeth; it starts with D and ends with T.
- **11-Across** (5 letters), *Common word in a hotel Wi-Fi network name*: the person staying in the room, who gets the network's login; it starts with G and ends with T.
- **12-Across** (7 letters), *What Dorothy from "The Wizard of Oz" wants to get over?*: the colorful arc from a famous song she sings; it starts with R and ends with W.
- **14-Across** (3 letters), *___ Taylor (fashion brand)*: a woman's first name; it starts with A and ends with N.
- **15-Across** (3 letters), *Prefix with friendly*: a clipped word meaning kind to the environment; it starts with E and ends with O.
- **16-Across** (3 letters), *"The Tell-Tale Heart" writer*: a 19th-century American author of gothic tales who also wrote about a raven; it starts with P and ends with E.
- **17-Across** (3 letters), *Barely manage, with "out"*: a verb for scraping together a living with difficulty; it starts and ends with E.

**Down**

- **2-Down** (7 letters), *Herb for spaghetti sauce*: a leafy herb that also tops a pizza; it starts and ends with O.
- **3-Down** (7 letters), *The real deal*: a word for authentic, the opposite of a fake; it starts with G and ends with E.

- **5-Down** (7 letters), *On the shelves*: a shop's way of saying an item is available to buy right now; it starts with I and ends with K.
- **6-Down** (3 letters), *Singer ___ King Cole*: a short first name for a mid-century crooner; it starts with N and ends with T.
- **10-Down** (3 letters), *Highest point value for a Scrabble tile*: the value printed on a Q tile in standard English Scrabble; it starts with T and ends with N.

- **13-Down** (3 letters), *Misfortune*: an old-fashioned word for sorrow or trouble; it starts with W and ends with E.

## Two shelves and a trip over the Oz question

Two clues in the Down list talk about shelves, and they sit side by side in the grid as 4-Down and 5-Down. One uses "flying off the shelves" as a pun, with a question mark to warn you that the phrase is about to be taken literally. The other states the plain opposite situation, with nothing tricky in it. If you solve the plain one first, its letters help you test the pun.

The least literal clue is the Dorothy one in 12-Across. "Get over" does not mean climbing a wall. It points to a song lyric about a place beyond something in the sky, so the answer is a thing in the sky, not a hurdle.

The "…" clue at 4-Across works differently. The three dots join two scenes with "or", and the one answer has to suit both: it is the part of a shark you spot above the waves and the closing word on a French film's last frame.
