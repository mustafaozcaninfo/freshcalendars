**The New York Times (NYT) Crossword for Tuesday, Nov. 10, 2026, was constructed by Sam Koperwas and Jeff Chen and edited by Will Shortz: a 15-by-15 grid with 76 clues.** Five of the clues are wordplay, and all five sit on the grid's longest Across entries. Below are the tricks, a map of the grid, one hint per clue behind a tap, and the answers at the bottom.

## Five question-mark clues and the tricks behind them

A question mark at the end of a clue signals that the surface reading is a decoy. Today every one of them is an Across clue, and no explanation below names an answer.

- **17-Across, "Kleenex?"** (9 squares). A brand name stands in for what the product is: a thing you pull one piece from and rip off. The answer is a two-word compound for a page torn from a publication.
- **24-Across, "Important female deer?"** (14 squares). Read "female deer" as the plural noun, then let that word turn into a verb at the start of a question. The rest of the entry asks whether something carries weight.
- **39-Across, "Soft touch on a golf course green?"** (15 squares). The golf stroke played on the green and the gentle manner in which something is said are the same verb form. The entry is a phrase that cushions bad news.
- **51-Across, "Prepares for a dog walk?"** (14 squares). The surface suggests clipping on a leash. The entry is a standard phrase for hurrying, in which the leash-like word means a heavy metal instead.
- **63-Across, "Prepare to shoot like Cupid?"** (9 squares). Cupid's weapon points one way, and an old stage custom points another. The answer is what a performer does at the end of a show.

## How the grid is built

The grid is 15 by 15, which is 225 squares: 40 are black and 185 hold a letter. The black pattern is the same when the grid turns upside down, a rotational symmetry (the clue data confirms every square has a matching partner). There are 76 entries: 35 Across and 41 Down.

Entry lengths are unusual. Only 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 14 and 15 letters appear; nothing runs 6 or 7 letters, and nothing 10 to 13. The tally is 19 entries of 3 letters (6 Across, 13 Down), 22 of 4 (14 and 8), 26 of 5 (10 and 16), two of 8 (both Down), four of 9 (two and two), two of 14 (both Across) and one of 15.

The five longest entries run in order down the grid. The two 9-letter Across entries, 17-Across and 63-Across, start at the left edge of row 3 and in column 7 of row 13. The two 14-letter entries, 24-Across and 51-Across, mirror each other through the center: 24-Across spans row 5 from column 2 to column 15, and 51-Across spans row 11 from column 1 to column 14. The 15-letter 39-Across takes the whole of row 8, so all 15 of its squares cross a different Down entry, among them 8-Down, 10-Down and 41-Down. The 8-letter Down entries are 8-Down and 41-Down; the 9-letter Down entries are 10-Down and 32-Down.

Four clues are fill-in-the-blank lines with a blank: 7-Down, 26-Down, 64-Down and 65-Down. 15-Across mentions the Global Positioning System (GPS) in its clue. With a stack of long phrases to fit, the daily rules are on our [NYT Crossword page](/news/topic/nyt-crossword/#how-it-works).
