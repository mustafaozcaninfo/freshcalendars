## Why is par 4 easy to beat on this box?

Two words are enough, but only barely. A script chained every pair of words in the list, where the second starts on the last letter of the first, and kept the pairs that use all twelve letters. Just 3 pairs pass.

| Check on the saved list | Result |
|---|---|
| Two-word routes covering all 12 letters | 3 |
| Letters those routes can join on | G (2 routes), E (1 route) |
| Total letters across the three routes | 15, 16 and 19 |
| One-word solutions | 0 |
| Three-word routes covering all 12 letters | 4,743 |
| Distinct words that can open a three-word route | 480 |
| Shortest total for a three-word route | 15 letters (10 routes) |

The three-word number counts only chains where the first two words leave a letter uncovered, so none of them is a two-word route with a spare word added. Every one of those 4,743 finishes beats par by a word. Joins matter there too: the G is one of the two joining letters in 3,034 of them, the E in 1,440.

The closest thing to a one-word answer reaches 9 letters, and three words do it. Each leaves out three letters, and the P is always among them. The longest words in the list run 11 letters, and five words reach that length.

## Which letters are hardest to place?

The P is the loner. It appears in just 150 of the 977 words, fewer than any other letter, and it starts 105 words but ends only 18. It is part of 9 of the 17 ordered pairs that never occur, such as the P followed by a D or a B.

| Letter | Words containing it |
|---|---|
| P | 150 |
| F | 162 |
| M | 276 |
| B | 286 |
| A | 659 |

The A sits at the other extreme, in 659 words. Counting words by the side of their first and last letter shows where routes can start and finish.

| Side | Letters | Words that start on it | Words that end on it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I, L, M | 192 | 163 |
| 2 | D, E, R | 188 | 365 |
| 3 | B, F, G | 347 | 229 |
| 4 | P, N, A | 250 | 220 |

Side 2 closes the most words, 365, and the E alone ends 242 of them. Side 3 opens the most, 347.

## Five hints, light to strong

1. The Times' own solution is two words, two fewer than par.
2. The longer word is 11 letters, one of five words that long in the list.
3. The first word starts and ends on the B/F/G side, and the second word begins on that same side.
4. The first word holds the F and the D; the second holds the L and the M.
5. The first word starts with B and ends in G. The second starts with G and ends in P.
