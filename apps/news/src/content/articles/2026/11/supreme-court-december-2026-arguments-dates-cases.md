The Supreme Court of the United States has eight arguments on its calendar for the session beginning **Monday, Nov. 30, 2026**, spread over six days through **Wednesday, Dec. 9**. The court's Monthly Argument Calendar for December 2026, amended Oct. 1, says the court convenes at 10 a.m. That is 10 a.m. Eastern Time (ET), or 7 a.m. Pacific by our conversion. Below are each day's cases, the question each one asks in plain terms, and how to listen.

## Supreme Court argument days and cases, Nov. 30 to Dec. 9

The calendar's header reads "October Term 2026, for the session beginning November 30, 2026." The court's own index of calendars also lists a session beginning Jan. 11, 2027. The table gives the docket number, the court that ruled first and a one-sentence summary of the question, paraphrased from the court's docket pages.

## Why Viramontes and Grant share one hour

The December calendar lists Viramontes v. Cook County (25-238) and Grant v. Higgins (25-566) together on Wednesday, Dec. 2, marked "Consolidated - 1 hr. for argument." The court granted the petitions as to the question in No. 25-238, which came from the 7th Circuit and was granted June 30, 2026. Because the two cases share a single hour, the day counts as one argument, which is why eight arguments cover nine docket numbers.

## What time do arguments start in each time zone?

The court convenes at 10 a.m. ET. The court's Oral Arguments page says that "typically, the Court holds two arguments each day beginning at 10:00 a.m." The calendar lists two arguments on Nov. 30 and Dec. 9 and one on each other day. We could not confirm start times for a second argument.

| Zone | Court convenes |
|---|---|
| Eastern Time | 10 a.m. |
| Central Time | 9 a.m. |
| Mountain Time | 8 a.m. |
| Pacific Time | 7 a.m. |

## How to listen live or read the transcript

The court's Oral Arguments page says an audio feed of each argument is live-streamed on the court's website, on its [live audio page](https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/live.aspx). The court posts the audio later in the day and, on the afternoon of each argument, posts the transcripts of that day's arguments.

## How to attend an argument in person

According to the court's Visitor's Guide to Oral Argument, all oral arguments are open to the public, but seating is limited. Seating for the first argument begins at 9:30 a.m. The guide describes a pilot online lottery for Courtroom seating at ticket.supremecourt.gov and a first-come, first-seated line. Visitors go through magnetometer and x-ray screening. We could not confirm lottery dates.

## When will the court decide these cases?

The court has not said when it will rule on any of the eight arguments. Its calendar covers argument days only; conference days and non-argument session dates are not on it. The court's index lists a separate session beginning Jan. 11, 2027; we have no argument days for it.
