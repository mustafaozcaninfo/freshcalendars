**The Monday, Nov. 30, 2026 puzzle, Strands #1002 from The New York Times (NYT), carries the clue "Flat and round."** Christina Iverson is the constructor and Tracy Bennett the editor. Counting from #1 on March 4, 2024 lands on 1002. Today's 11-letter spangram is a mid-length one, and the rules sit on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works).

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: It does not describe a shape. It is a catalog, the full list of what one musician or band has put out on albums.
- Hint 2: It has 11 letters, begins with D and ends with Y. No letter repeats.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 6, column 2, and the first step goes diagonally up and to the left.

## Theme word hints

Six words fit the clue, listed by length, shortest first:

- **4 letters, starts with C, ends with N:** a small round piece of metal money. All four letters differ.
- **4 letters, starts with P, ends with K:** the black rubber piece hockey players chase on ice. All four letters differ.
- **7 letters, starts with C, ends with R:** a mat that keeps a cold glass from marking the table. All seven letters differ.
- **7 letters, starts with F, ends with E:** a plastic toy tossed and caught at the park. One letter appears twice, and it is the last letter.
- **7 letters, starts with P, ends with E:** a round batter breakfast cooked on a griddle and stacked with syrup. One letter appears twice, and it is the second letter.
- **8 letters, starts with T, ends with A:** a thin flatbread of corn or wheat that wraps a taco. Two different letters each appear twice.

## Row and column locator hints

Rows count from the top and columns from the left. Each rung narrows the search and none names a word.

- **Rows 1 and 2:** all 12 tiles come from two 7-letter words, six tiles each. Their seventh tiles drop into row 3, one at each end.
- **Row 3:** the four middle tiles, columns 2 to 5, belong to the spangram.
- **Rows 4 and 5, columns 2 to 5:** all eight tiles are one 8-letter word.
- **Rows 4 and 5, columns 1 and 6:** these four edge tiles are the spangram.
- **Row 6:** three tiles are the spangram and three belong to three different words, one each in columns 1, 3 and 6.
- **Rows 7 and 8:** three words fill all 12 tiles, one 7-letter word with six tiles here and two 4-letter words with three each.

## Start-cell hints

The first step is the direction from a word's first tile to its second.

- **4 letters, start cell row 7, column 2:** the first step goes diagonally up and to the left.
- **4 letters, start cell row 7, column 5:** the first step goes diagonally down and to the right.
- **7 letters, start cell row 2, column 2:** the first step goes diagonally down and to the left.
- **7 letters, start cell row 2, column 4:** the first step goes straight up.
- **7 letters, start cell row 7, column 3:** the first step goes straight up.
- **8 letters, start cell row 5, column 5:** the first step goes straight up.

## The Nov. 30 spangram path and the board map

The path begins one tile in from the left edge, at row 6, column 2, and steps diagonally up to the left edge at row 5, column 1. It climbs straight to row 4, cuts diagonally up to row 3, column 2, and runs three tiles right along row 3 to column 5. A diagonal down puts it on the right edge at row 4, column 6. It drops to row 5, steps diagonally down and left to row 6, column 5 and finishes one tile further left at row 6, column 4. That is ten steps for 11 tiles, and every column from 1 to 6 is touched.

The shape is a cup that opens toward the bottom, with its rim in row 3 and a gap at row 6, column 3. Rows 3 to 6 are the only rows with a spangram tile, so rows 1, 2, 7 and 8 hold theme words alone:

| Rows | Tiles | Spangram tiles | What fills the rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 and 2 | 12 | 0 | two 7-letter words, six tiles each |
| 3 | 6 | 4 | the end tiles of those two words |
| 4 and 5 | 12 | 4 | one 8-letter word, all eight tiles |
| 6 | 6 | 3 | one tile each from three words that finish in rows 7 and 8 |
| 7 and 8 | 12 | 0 | one 7-letter word and two 4-letter words |

The bottom two rows are the densest corner: three of the six theme words, 15 letters in all, sit in rows 6 to 8, with 12 of them in rows 7 and 8 and one tile each poking up into row 6. The top is looser, with two words sharing 12 tiles and spilling one tile each into row 3.
