**The New York Times (NYT) Pips for Thursday, Nov. 26, 2026, Thanksgiving Day in the U.S., pairs Ian Livengood's five-domino Easy and eight-domino Medium with Rodolfo Kurchan's 16-domino Hard, a 32-cell board that spans 9 rows and 9 columns.** The placements near the end come from the Times' published puzzle data and satisfy every region. Easy leans on sums (three of its six regions) and has two "less than" cells. Medium is the only level with two "equal" pairs, and each of Medium and Hard has one cell that takes any value. Hard is the only level with an "all different" pair.

## Pip-sum check for Nov. 26

Add every half of every domino, then subtract what the sum regions demand. Whatever is left must fall into the other regions.

| Level | Pips on the dominoes | Sum regions | Pips they require | Left for other cells | Cells outside sum regions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Easy | 31 | 3 | 24 | 7 | 3 |
| Medium | 43 | 2 | 10 | 33 | 10 |
| Hard | 106 | 13 | 64 | 42 | 12 |

Easy is tight: its sum regions take 24 of 31 pips, and the three cells left over must hold the other 7. One of them must be more than 4 and the other two less than 2, so the more-than-4 cell holds a 5 or a 6. Medium has the opposite shape, with 33 pips spread over 10 free cells, an average of 3.3. Hard puts 64 of its 106 pips into 20 sum cells and leaves 42 pips for 12 cells, an average of 3.5.

## The forced first move on each board

Cells are named by row, then column, from the top left. A search of every legal placement against the saved data finds one arrangement on Easy, six on Medium and 64 on Hard.

**Easy.** The two nine-sum pairs can only be a 3 and a 6 or a 4 and a 5, and the set holds a single 3 and a single 4. One pair therefore takes the 3, the other takes the 4, and neither region can do without. Start with the dominoes that carry the 3 and the 4.

**Medium.** R1C4 adds up to 5 on its own and touches only R1C3. The 1-5 domino sits across R1C3 and R1C4 in all six arrangements, with the 5 in R1C4. R3C3 is more than 5 and stands alone, so it takes a 6. Begin with R1C4.

**Hard.** R1C7 adds up to 1 and R2C7 adds up to 5. R2C7 has only two neighbors that belong to the board, R1C7 and R3C7, and R3C7 adds up to 6. No domino pairs a 5 with a 6, so R2C7 pairs with R1C7, and the 1-5 domino goes there. Fixing R1C7, R2C7 and R3C7 takes three cells off the board before any pair is touched.

- Hint 2: R1C4 adds up to 5 and has a single neighbor, R1C3.

**Hard (16 dominoes on a frame 9 rows by 9 columns)**

- Hint 1: R1C7 adds up to 1, and the cell below it, R2C7, adds up to 5.
- Hint 2: Only one domino in the set carries both a 1 and a 5.

## Which Pips dominoes can be turned around on Nov. 26

Hard has six dominoes that swap ends without breaking a rule: the 4-5 at R1C3 and R2C3, the 6-0 at R6C3 and R7C3, the 5-3 at R1C1 and R1C2, the 0-5 at R8C7 and R9C7, the 3-1 at R6C7 and R7C7, and the 6-4 at R7C4 and R8C4. That is why the search counts 64 arrangements there and one on Easy. Medium has no single domino that turns, yet six arrangements exist because several dominoes can trade cells.
