**Indonesia's official 2027 holiday calendar lists Isra Mikraj twice: Tuesday, Jan. 5, 2027 (1448 AH) and Sunday, Dec. 26, 2027 (1449 AH).** The Ministry of State Secretariat (Setneg) published the decree on Sept. 15, 2026. The night itself, Laylat al-Mi'raj, is observed on the eve in many communities, and the exact day can differ by a day by country and by mosque calendar, depending on moon sighting or calculation. A U.S. date is not given here; check your mosque's calendar.

## Isra and Miraj dates in 2027

| | Date | Hijri year | Source line |
|---|---|---|---|
| First Isra Mikraj | Tuesday, Jan. 5, 2027 | 1448 AH | "Selasa, 5 Januari 2027: Isra Mikraj Nabi Muhammad saw." |
| Second Isra Mikraj | Sunday, Dec. 26, 2027 | 1449 AH | "Minggu, 26 Desember 2027: Isra Mikraj Nabi Muhammad saw." |

Both lines come from the Surat Keputusan Bersama (SKB) 3 Menteri, the joint decree of three ministers, on national holidays and collective leave for 2027, as printed by Setneg. Anno Hegirae (AH) counts years from the Prophet Muhammad's migration to Medina. Our [Ramadan 2027 story](/news/ramadan-2027-calendar-dates-timetable/) also lists Tuesday, Jan. 5 as 27 Rajab 1448.

## Why does 27 Rajab fall twice in 2027?

The Hijri year follows the moon and has 354 or 355 days, about 11 days fewer than the Gregorian year. A Hijri date therefore lands earlier on the Gregorian calendar each year. When it falls in the first days of January, the next one can still arrive before Dec. 31.

That is what the Indonesian decree shows. Counting from Jan. 5 to Dec. 26, 2027 gives 355 days, the length of a Hijri year of that size. The calendar year 2027 holds both days, one from 1448 AH and one from 1449 AH.

## What is Isra and Miraj?

Many Muslims remember 27 Rajab as the date of the Night Journey (Isra) from Mecca to Jerusalem and the Ascension (Mi'raj) that followed, according to tradition. Quran 17:1, on Quran.com, opens: "Glory be to the One Who took His servant ˹Muḥammad˺ by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque." The Farthest Mosque, al-Masjid al-Aqsa, is in Jerusalem. How the night is marked varies by community.

## Is the day or the night observed, and why can the date differ?

An Islamic day begins at sunset, so the night of 27 Rajab comes before its day. For the Jan. 5 date, the night begins at sunset on Monday, Jan. 4, in communities that follow the Indonesian date. For the Dec. 26 date, it begins at sunset on Saturday, Dec. 25.

The calendar a mosque follows decides the day. Moon-sighting communities wait for the crescent, and calculated calendars fix the month in advance, so neighboring countries or mosques can differ by one day. The Setneg date is Indonesia's own decree and is not a ruling for other countries.

## What about Malaysia, Turkey and the United States?

Each country sets its own date, so Malaysia and Turkey can land a day away from Indonesia; their own authorities publish their 2027 lists. In the United States there is no national date: each mosque follows a calculated calendar or local moon sighting, so the mosque you attend sets the night.
