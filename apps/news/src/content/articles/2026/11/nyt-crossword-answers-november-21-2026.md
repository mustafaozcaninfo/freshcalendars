**The New York Times (NYT) Crossword for Saturday, Nov. 21, 2026, was constructed by Zhou Zhang and edited by Will Shortz: 70 clues, 35 Across and 35 Down, in a 15-by-15 grid.** The black pattern is sparse, with 28 black squares, and six clues end in a question mark. Every clue has a hint behind a tap, and the answers close the page.

## Question-mark clues in the Nov. 21 Crossword

Six of the 70 clues end in a question mark, which flags a pun or a stretched meaning. Each trick is explained here without its answer.

- **16-Across, "Rock piece that might contain heavy metal samples?" (3 letters).** "Heavy metal" is the element, not the music. The "samples" are traces of metal inside the rock, and the answer names the kind of rock that is mined for them.
- **55-Across, "Turned a chat into a chart, perhaps?" (11 letters).** The clue is a worked example: "chat" becomes "chart" by a single extra letter. The answer says how a typist makes that slip, and it runs two words together.
- **58-Across, "Strapless?" (11 letters).** It is not about a dress. Read "-less" as "un-" and the straps become restraints, so the answer is a past participle meaning freed from them.
- **1-Down, "Set in France?" (4 letters).** "Set" is an adjective here, and the question mark asks for its French form. Think of a menu with one price for several courses.
- **7-Down, "Character-building activity, for short?" (3 letters).** The "character" is a hero you design and level up, not a trait. Both marks, the question mark and "for short," point to a three-letter initialism.
- **26-Down, "Fliers typically making round trips?" (10 letters).** The fliers are not passengers or birds. They are thrown objects that curve back to the hand, and the answer is plural.

Sorting the 70 clues by their wording gives a count for each kind. Question-mark clues and blanks are the easiest to spot; the rest is a judgment call, made here on the printed text alone.

Definitions make up two thirds of the grid, so the wordplay load is lighter than the black-square count suggests. The four quoted clues are all spoken phrases, and 14-Across and 30-Across are the two longest of them at 11 and 10 letters. One quoted clue, 37-Across ("Listen …"), is short enough to fill from the crossing letters.

## How the Nov. 21 grid is built

The grid is 15 columns by 15 rows, 225 squares in all: 28 black and 197 holding a letter, a black share of 12.4 percent. Rotating it a half turn leaves the pattern unchanged. Friday's grid, on our [Nov. 20 Crossword page](/news/nyt-crossword-answers-november-20-2026/), by Gia Bosko, had 39 black squares and 66 clues, so Saturday has 11 fewer black squares and four more clues. The average entry length is 5.6 letters on both days.

Three-letter entries and four-letter entries tie at 16 each, but they split by direction: 12 of the 16 three-letter answers run Across and 14 of the 16 four-letter answers run Down. The four 11-letter entries are all Across and come in two pairs. 14-Across and 17-Across sit in rows 2 and 3 from column 1 to column 11, and 55-Across and 58-Across sit in rows 13 and 14 from column 5 to the right edge. The two ten-letter Across entries are 30-Across, which fills row 7 from column 1, and 40-Across, which fills row 9 to the right edge.

The six ten-letter Down entries come in three groups. 11-Down and 12-Down run side by side in columns 13 and 14, rows 1 to 10, and 8-Down runs through column 9 over the same rows. 25-Down, 26-Down and 27-Down start in row 6 and reach row 15, in columns 2, 3 and 7. Rows 5 and 11 hold five black squares each, and every other row holds one or two, so those two rows are where the open middle is cut. The daily format is on our [NYT Crossword page](/news/topic/nyt-crossword/#how-it-works); on this Saturday grid, the stacked 11-letter pairs are where the long crossings meet.
