A Net 30 invoice dated Friday, Jan. 1, 2027 is due Sunday, Jan. 31, 30 calendar days later, and the Treasury Bureau of the Fiscal Service says a payment that falls due on a weekend or holiday "is due on the following business day," here Monday, Feb. 1. This guide counts calendar days the way Treasury's Prompt Payment rules do and gives the net 15, 30, 60 and 90 due dates for an invoice dated on the 1st and the 15th of every month of 2027. Those dates are our own computation. Private net terms follow your contract, so check it for the start day and the weekend rule.

## How do you calculate a net 30 due date?

Add the number of calendar days to the invoice date. The U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) uses the term "Net 30 account" in a 2016 newsroom article and lists net 45, net 60 and net 90 as longer terms. Net 60 and net 90 in this guide use the same count with 60 or 90 days.

Treasury's frequently asked questions page, last updated March 6, 2026, gives the federal version of the rule in four lines:

- A payment is due "30 days after the agency has received a proper invoice."
- "'Day' means a calendar day including weekends and federal holidays."
- When there is no receipt date, "the invoice is deemed 'received' on the invoice date."
- A payment due on a weekend or holiday "is due on the following business day."

The regulation says the same. Under Title 5 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), section 1315.4, the payment period begins on the date of receipt of a proper invoice, the due date is 30 days after the start of the payment period when the contract does not specify one, and payments falling due on a weekend or federal holiday may be made on the following business day without late payment interest penalties.

The Prompt Payment rule is written for federal agencies paying their vendors. A private invoice can start the clock on a different day or move weekend due dates differently, so the tables below are a worked example of calendar-day counting, not a rule for your contract. To count business days instead of calendar days, see [how many business days are in a year](/news/how-many-business-days-in-a-year/).

## How many 2027 due dates land on a weekend or holiday?

Of the 96 due dates in the tables (24 invoice dates times 4 terms), 32 fall on a Saturday, a Sunday or a federal non-business day. Twelve land on a Saturday and 16 on a Sunday. Four land on a holiday: Memorial Day, Mon., May 31, twice, and the Dec. 31, 2027 observed day, twice.

| Term | Due dates on a non-business day (of 24) |
|---|---|
| Net 15 | 6 |
| Net 30 | 10 |
| Net 60 | 8 |
| Net 90 | 8 |

Net 30 has the most in this sample, 10 of 24. That comes from counting the 1st and the 15th, not from a general rule: 30 days is 4 weeks and 2 days, so each due date falls two weekdays later in the week than its invoice date.

Three due dates move by more than a weekend:

- Net 15 on an invoice dated Fri., Jan. 1: Sat., Jan. 16, then Mon., Jan. 18 is Martin Luther King Jr. Day, so it moves to Tue., Jan. 19.
- Net 30 on an invoice dated Wed., Dec. 1: Fri., Dec. 31 is the observed New Year's Day 2028, so it moves to Mon., Jan. 3, 2028.
- Net 60 on an invoice dated Mon., Nov. 1: the same Fri., Dec. 31 moves to Mon., Jan. 3, 2028.

A leap day shows up once: Net 90 on an invoice dated Wed., Dec. 1, 2027 is due Tue., Feb. 29, 2028.

## What does the federal rule say about late payment interest?

The regulation says late payments get interest automatically. On the Treasury Prompt Payment page, updated June 30, 2026, the Prompt Payment interest rate for July 1 to Dec. 31, 2026 is 4.75%. Treasury has not announced the rate for Jan. 1 to June 30, 2027, so 4.75% is the 2026 half-year rate and should not be read as a 2027 figure. The interest applies to federal agency payments; late fees on a private invoice are whatever the contract says.

## When do longer terms like net 60 and net 90 come up?

The SBA article, titled "How Net 30 Accounts Help Conserve Business Cash Flow" and dated Sept. 13, 2016, says: "Negotiate the longest payback terms possible such as net 45, net 60 or even net 90 days." That is a 2016 statement from the agency, not a rule. In the tables, net 60 and net 90 each land on a non-business day 8 times in 24 invoice dates, against 10 for net 30.
