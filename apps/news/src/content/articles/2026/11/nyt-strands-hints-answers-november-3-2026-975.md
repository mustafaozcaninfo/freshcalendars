**Today's Strands from The New York Times (NYT) is puzzle #975, for Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, and its theme clue is "I've got a song in my heart."** Every answer is a word for a person who performs songs, so run through the labels we hang on such performers. It was constructed and edited by Tracy Bennett. Hints for the spangram and each theme word come first; the answers wait in a marked spoiler section at the end.

Five theme words and a nine-letter spangram fill all 48 tiles today, and the usual rules sit on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works).

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: it is what you do when you let a song ring out at full volume.
- Hint 2: three short words run together, the first being a strip of leather worn at the waist.
- Hint 3: nine letters, and it opens with B.

## Theme word hints

All five theme words name a kind of person who performs songs:

- **9 letters, starts with B:** a specialist in slow, story-telling numbers.
- **9 letters, starts with C:** one voice among many in a choir.
- **7 letters, starts with C:** a smooth, low-key performer of romantic standards.
- **6 letters, starts with S:** the plainest word for anyone who carries a tune.
- **8 letters, starts with V:** a performer defined by the instrument in the throat.

## Where today's spangram and theme words sit

The spangram begins on the left edge at row 3, column 1 and ends on the right edge at row 6, column 6. It never reaches the top or bottom row, holding to rows 3 through 6 while crossing all six columns, with two bends and a longest straight run of four tiles.

The thickest cluster is the bottom-left quarter (rows 5 to 8, columns 1 to 3): every one of its twelve circles is a theme tile, with no spangram letter mixed in, and it is shared by the words that start with S and V. The 9-letter B word and the 9-letter C word tie as the longest theme answers at nine letters each, level with the spangram, while the 6-letter S word is the shortest. Theme tiles by quarter come to 9 in the top-left, 10 in the top-right, 12 in the bottom-left and 8 in the bottom-right.
