**Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026 brings Quordle #1763 from Merriam-Webster.** You get a short ladder of clues for each of the four words, a table of five starting guesses scored on every grid, and the solutions at the bottom.

Saturday's solutions are on the [Quordle #1762 page](/news/quordle-hints-answers-november-21-2026-1762/). Today the last column of the four grids is where the puzzle gives itself away; the scoring rules are on the [Quordle guide](/news/topic/quordle/#how-it-works).

## Three clues for each Quordle word

The words are numbered alphabetically here, not by where they sit on the board.

**Word 1**

- Hint 1: Starts with the 6th letter of the alphabet and ends with the 20th.
- Hint 2: Two vowels sit next to each other, right after the first letter.
- Hint 3: Dim and hard to make out, or the feeling just before you pass out.

**Word 2**

- Hint 1: Starts with the 7th letter and ends with the 25th.
- Hint 2: Just one vowel, in second place, and one letter appears twice in a row.
- Hint 3: A mild cry of surprise that stands in for stronger language.

**Word 3**

- Hint 1: Starts with the 10th letter and ends with the 20th.
- Hint 2: Two vowels sit side by side, in second and third place.
- Hint 3: A short outing taken for the fun of it.

**Word 4**

- Hint 1: Starts with the 11th letter and ends with the 20th.
- Hint 2: A single vowel, right in the center.
- Hint 3: The past tense of a verb for resting on bent legs, as in a proposal or a prayer.

## Five opening guesses scored on all four boards

Twenty slots hold 13 distinct letters, and only six of the 20 are vowels. G means a green tile, Y a yellow one, scored the way the game colors repeats.

| Opener | Word 1 | Word 2 | Word 3 | Word 4 | Colored tiles in all |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Slate | 0G 2Y | 0G 1Y | 0G 2Y | 0G 3Y | 8 |
| Stone | 1G 1Y | 0G 1Y | 1G 1Y | 0G 3Y | 8 |
| Crane | 1G 1Y | 0G 0Y | 1G 1Y | 0G 2Y | 6 |
| Adieu | 1G 1Y | 0G 0Y | 0G 2Y | 0G 1Y | 5 |
| Raise | 2G 0Y | 0G 0Y | 1G 0Y | 0G 1Y | 4 |

Slate and Stone tie at eight, but Slate turns no tile green anywhere. Stone gets a green on two boards. Raise colors only four tiles, though two of them are green on the first board.

Word 2 is the hard grid for every opener: the five guesses color just two tiles on it, one from Slate and one from Stone. Word 4 takes the most, with ten colored tiles in all.

## Working from the back: how Faint, Golly, Jaunt and Knelt end

Three of the four words end in T: Faint, Jaunt and Knelt. Golly is the only one that ends in Y. A guess ending in T therefore turns up a green tile on three boards at once.

| Last two letters | Words | Count |
|---|---|---|
| NT | Faint, Jaunt | 2 |
| LT | Knelt | 1 |
| LY | Golly | 1 |

Faint and Jaunt differ only in the first and third letters. Both have A second, N fourth and T last, so once A, N and T are known the two words are told apart by the opening letter, F or J.

The fourth slot is split in the same way: N in Faint and Jaunt, L in Golly and Knelt. L is also the letter that repeats most inside one word, since Golly has it twice, in the third and fourth places, and Knelt has it fourth.

Counted as slots, T, N and L fill three of the 20 each. That leaves seven repeats in all: two of T, two of N, two of L and one of A. The other nine distinct letters, F, I, G, O, Y, J, U, K and E, turn up once each.

Vowels are scarce: Faint and Jaunt have two each, Golly and Knelt one each, so six vowels in 20 slots. Golly's closing Y does the work of a vowel, and a vowel-hunting opener such as Adieu cannot find it.
