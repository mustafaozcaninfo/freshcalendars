New York City follows Code Blue procedures when the temperature is 32 degrees or lower, including wind chill values, according to the NYC311 "Homeless Outreach Assistance" page, while Philadelphia, Washington, DC and Boston each use a different trigger. This story sets the four official rules side by side for the 2026-27 winter and adds a one-page wallet card with the numbers. The pages we read on Nov. 4, 2026 are cited in each row.

## Code Blue and cold alert rules in four cities

Code Blue is not one national rule. Each city decides what counts, and the differences matter near freezing.

| City | What it is called | Trigger | Season | Who to call | Where to go | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New York City | Code Blue; Enhanced Code Blue | 32°F or lower, including wind chill; Enhanced below -5°F (temperature or wind chill, any amount of time) | No dates on the page | 311; 911 for an emergency | Department of Homeless Services (DHS) drop-in centers and shelters; over 50 NYC hospitals | NYC311 |
| Philadelphia | Code Blue; Enhanced Code Blue | Precipitation and 32°F or lower, or a wind chill near or below 20°F | No dates on the page | (215) 232-1984 to report a person outdoors | A City-funded homeless intake center; warming centers in an Enhanced Code Blue | Office of Homeless Services |
| Washington, DC | Cold Alert; Extreme Cold Alert | Cold Alert: 16° to 32°F, or 21° to 40°F if precipitation is expected. Extreme: below 15°F, or below 20°F if precipitation is expected | Hypothermia Season, Nov. 1 to March 31 | 202-399-7093 or 311, 24/7 in season | Shelter, with free rides | DC Department of Human Services; Office of the Tenant Advocate (OTA) |
| Boston | No Code Blue name on the page | Below freezing (32 degrees): amnesty for nonviolent restrictions | No dates on the page | 311 or 911 | Boston Public Health Commission (BPHC) shelters, open 24 hours a day, seven days a week | Boston.gov |

## Why do the triggers differ?

Philadelphia ties its 32°F line to precipitation: the Office of Homeless Services says Code Blue applies "when there's precipitation and the temperature is 32 degrees Fahrenheit or lower," or when the wind chill is near or below 20 degrees. New York City's page has no precipitation condition and counts wind chill in the 32-degree figure.

Washington, DC writes its alerts as ranges. The DC Department of Human Services lists a Cold Alert when the temperature or wind chill is "between: 16° and 32°F, OR 21° to 40°F if precipitation (snow, rain, or hail) is expected," and an Extreme Cold Alert when it "falls below: 15°F, OR 20°F if precipitation (snow, rain, or hail) is expected." The page was last updated Monday, Nov. 2, 2026. DC's older name was "Hypothermia Alert"; the department now says Cold Alert.

Boston's page does not use the name Code Blue. It says that when temperatures are below freezing (32 degrees), amnesty is in place for those with nonviolent restrictions, and that BPHC emergency shelters are open 24 hours a day, seven days a week. The Mayor can declare a cold weather emergency, which opens Boston Centers for Youth & Families (BCYF) community centers as warming centers. Boston's page was last updated May 18, 2026.

## Who do you call in New York City, Philadelphia, DC and Boston?

- **New York City:** ask for outreach for someone outdoors through 311, and call 911 for an emergency. During Code Blue, the city says over 50 NYC hospitals plus DHS drop-in centers and shelters "operate with a full open-door policy."
- **Philadelphia:** (215) 232-1984 is the line to report a person who appears homeless and is outdoors during a Code Blue. It is a report line, not shelter intake. The page says to go to a City-funded homeless intake center, or in an Enhanced Code Blue to the warming centers on the city's map.
- **Washington, DC:** the shelter hotline is 202-399-7093, or 311, 24/7 during the season, with free rides to shelter.
- **Boston:** "Call 3-1-1 or 9-1-1 if you need help getting to a shelter."

In other cities, call 311 or check the city's own site. We could not confirm rules for Chicago, Minneapolis, Baltimore, Denver, Pittsburgh, Seattle, St. Louis or Cleveland from their official pages on Nov. 4, 2026, so none are listed here.

## When does DC's Hypothermia Season end?

The DC Office of the Tenant Advocate lists Hypothermia Season as Nov. 1 to March 31, and the Department of Human Services says it runs November through March. For the 2026-27 winter, that ends March 31, 2027.
