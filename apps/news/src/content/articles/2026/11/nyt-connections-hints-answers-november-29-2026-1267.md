**Connections #1267 from The New York Times (NYT) is the Sunday, Nov. 29, 2026 puzzle, edited by Wyna Liu.** The board mixes a 3-letter tile with one that reaches 15 letters once the space is dropped. Our [Connections page](/news/topic/nyt-connections/#how-it-works) covers the rules; two hints per color follow, and the solution waits under a spoiler line.

## Today's 16 words

Manticore, Burpee, Zoologist, Griffin, Farthing, Hill, Belcher, Crunch, Mountain Climber, Disappoint, Cent, Bear Crawl, Sou, Demeaning, Simpson, Pittance

## Hints for each group

**Yellow**

- Hint 1: Picture a gym-class warm-up where the only equipment is the floor under you.
- Hint 2: The entries have nine, six, six and fifteen letters, counting a two-word entry without its space.

**Green**

- Hint 1: Think of the word in "not a ___ of doubt", a sliver so small it hardly counts.
- Hint 2: The entries have four, eight, eight and three letters.

**Blue**

- Hint 1: Each tile is a last name that comes up on the evening schedule of animated sitcoms.
- Hint 2: The entries have seven, seven, four and seven letters.

**Purple**

- Hint 1: Ignore what each tile means and read how it finishes; the last few letters spell something else.
- Hint 2: The entries have nine, ten, nine and nine letters.

## Solving tips

Several tiles are made of shorter words, so reading a tile for what sits inside it can mislead you. Take the group with the least overlap first and let the leftovers show you the odd one out.

## Near-miss tiles in Connections #1267 and where they land

Two of the 16 tiles are two-word phrases, Bear Crawl and Mountain Climber, and both are yellow. The remaining 14 are single words. The bigger lesson sits in the table: most lures here come from what a tile contains or evokes, not from a second meaning.

| Tile | What lures you | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|
| Burpee | It starts with Burp, like Belcher and Farthing start with Belch and Fart | Bodyweight Exercises |
| Belcher | The same bodily-noise opening | Cartoon Family Surnames, with Griffin, Hill and Simpson |
| Farthing | The same bodily-noise opening | Whit, a tiny amount |
| Manticore | A mythical beast, like Griffin | Ending in Words for the Main Idea, through Core |
| Griffin | A mythical beast, like Manticore | Cartoon Family Surnames |
| Hill | A slope to climb, next to Mountain Climber | Cartoon Family Surnames |
| Zoologist | Zoo points to animals, as Bear Crawl and Manticore do | Ending in Words for the Main Idea, through Gist |

Only three tiles open with a bodily noise, so that idea never fills a fourth slot. The beast idea has Manticore and Griffin and nothing else that is truly mythical, which leaves it two tiles short.

The lock order follows which group has the fewest rivals:

1. **Bodyweight Exercises.** Bear Crawl, Burpee, Crunch and Mountain Climber are the only moves you could do on a mat.
2. **Cartoon Family Surnames.** Belcher, Griffin, Hill and Simpson are all household names from animated sitcoms; locking them removes the beast and slope lures.
3. **Whit.** Cent, Farthing, Pittance and Sou all name a tiny amount of money, and Farthing leaves the noise trap behind.
4. **Ending in Words for the Main Idea.** The four long tiles left over finish with Meaning, Point, Core and Gist.

Starting with purple is the hard route, because nothing in the tiles points to it until the other twelve are gone.
