**Connections #1261 from The New York Times (NYT) is the Monday, Nov. 23, 2026 puzzle, edited by Wyna Liu.** Sixteen words run from a two-letter tile to an eight-letter one, and four of them share a family that is not one of the answers. One tile is the short form of "estimated time of arrival (ETA)." Our [Connections page](/news/topic/nyt-connections/#how-it-works) explains the rules. Two hints per color come first, then the answers behind a spoiler line.

## Today's 16 words

Chickpea, Distance, Lambda, Doom, Iota, Pupil, Black, ETA, Nu, Shred, Thrash, Traffic, Particle, Cubism, Route, Modicum

## Hints for each group

**Yellow**

- Hint 1: Think of the smallest bit left of something, such as a speck you could barely see.
- Hint 2: The entries have four, five, seven and eight letters.

**Green**

- Hint 1: Open a phone before a drive and look at what the screen tells you about the trip.
- Hint 2: The entries have three, five, seven and eight letters.

**Blue**

- Hint 1: Loud guitars, heavy drums and a word that could follow each tile to name a sound.
- Hint 2: The entries have two, four, five and six letters.

**Purple**

- Hint 1: Ignore what each word means and read only its first few letters; a young animal is hiding there.
- Hint 2: The entries have five, six, six and eight letters.

## Solving tips

Four of these tiles could pass for one family, and that is the bait. Set them aside as soon as you notice it, and build from the groups that rest on meaning. The hidden-start group is the quickest to confirm, because you only need to read the first letters.

## Decoys in puzzle #1261: which tiles could join another group

Each overlap below can be checked letter by letter.

| Tiles | Why they tempt | Test that sorts them | Where they sit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iota, ETA, Nu, Lambda | All four are letters of the Greek alphabet, so a group of Greek letters looks ready made | Four tiles fit, yet each one has a better home: Iota is a speck, ETA is trip info, Nu is a metal style and Lambda begins with Lamb | Yellow, Green, Blue, Purple |
| Chickpea, Cubism, Lambda, Pupil, Particle | Particle opens with Part, but Pupil opens with Pup and the others with Chick, Cub and Lamb | Only a real young animal at the start counts, and Part is none | Purple, Purple, Purple, Purple, Yellow |
| Pupil, Black, Shred, Route | Each has five letters, so a length group is possible | Four tiles of equal length across four colors prove length is no clue | Purple, Blue, Yellow, Green |
| Particle, Distance, Chickpea | Each has eight letters and sits in a different group | Same test: equal length does not link them | Yellow, Green, Purple |

Doom, Black and Thrash share no letters in the same place, so only meaning joins them. Modicum is the one tile with a single plain job: it is an amount, and it fits no other idea on the board.
