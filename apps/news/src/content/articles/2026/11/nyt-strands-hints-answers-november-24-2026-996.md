**Strands #996 from The New York Times (NYT) is the Tuesday, Nov. 24, 2026 puzzle, and its clue is "Are we there yet?"** Tracy Bennett is listed as constructor and editor. This grid hides an 11-letter spangram and six theme words, and the rules are on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). Hints come first, then a map of where the words sit, then the answers behind a spoiler warning.

The clue is a question about distance, and every theme word names a stretch of effort that drags on.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: A phrase for a stretch of effort that drags on far past the point where you hoped to be done.
- Hint 2: It has 11 letters, begins with T and ends with L, and it is three words run together. Two different letters each appear twice.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 1, column 1, the top-left corner, and the first step goes diagonally down and to the right.

## Theme word hints

Six words fit the clue, listed by length, shortest first:

- **4 letters, starts with T, ends with K:** a hike through rough country that takes several days.
- **4 letters, starts with S, ends with G:** heavy-footed progress through mud, or a dull task that never seems to end.
- **5 letters, starts with G, ends with D:** daily work that wears you down, and what a mill does to grain.
- **8 letters, starts with C, ends with N:** months of organized effort toward a goal, with the second letter repeated as the fifth.
- **8 letters, starts with M, ends with N:** a famous footrace, and anything that feels as tiring as one; the second letter repeats as the fourth.
- **8 letters, starts with S, ends with E:** to fight for every inch against resistance, with a double letter in the middle.

## Start-cell hints

Rows count from the top and columns from the left; the first step is the direction from a word's first letter to its second. No word is named here.

- **4 letters, start cell row 7, column 4:** the first step goes straight left.
- **4 letters, start cell row 1, column 2:** the first step goes diagonally down and to the right.
- **5 letters, start cell row 6, column 3:** the first step goes diagonally down and to the left.
- **8 letters, start cell row 1, column 3:** the first step goes straight right.
- **8 letters, start cell row 3, column 5:** the first step goes diagonally down and to the right.
- **8 letters, start cell row 5, column 2:** the first step goes straight left.

## How the Nov. 24 spangram links the board's edges

A spangram has to join two opposite edges, and this one manages more. It starts in row 1, column 1, so it meets the top and left edges in one tile. It then steps diagonally down and to the right through row 2, column 2, row 3, column 3 and row 4, column 4. The fifth tile bends back to row 5, column 3, and three more diagonal steps lead to row 6, column 4, row 7, column 5 and row 8, column 6. That eighth tile sits in the bottom-right corner, so the right edge is touched as well. The last three steps go straight left along row 8, ending in column 3.

The path crosses every row from 1 to 8 and every column from 1 to 6. Row 8 holds four spangram tiles and no other row holds more than one. Columns 3 and 4 each hold three. Seven of the ten steps are diagonal. Across the six theme words, 11 of 31 steps are diagonal, and the 8-letter word that starts in row 1 has none.

One shape is easy to miss. The 4-letter word that starts in row 1, column 2 runs down the diagonal directly beside the spangram's first four tiles, one column to the right. Two parallel diagonals sit side by side in the upper left, which makes it tempting to swap tiles between them.

The top right is the densest corner for theme words: 11 of its 12 tiles belong to three of them. The bottom right is the opposite, with the spangram taking 5 of 12 tiles and only two theme words reaching in. Row 3 is the busiest row, with six tiles from five different answers.
