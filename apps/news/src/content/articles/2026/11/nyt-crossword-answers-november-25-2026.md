**The New York Times (NYT) Crossword for Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026, is by Peter Gordon and edited by Will Shortz: 79 clues, 37 Across and 42 Down, in a grid 16 squares wide and 15 tall.** Three clues end in a question mark, and four long entries hold the theme. Every clue has a hint behind a tap; the answers close the page.

## Three question marks, two real tricks

Only 3 of the 79 clues end in "?", the usual flag for wordplay, and one of those three is a false alarm. Each trick is explained without its answer, with the length in letters.

- **45-Across, "Locale for picture-taking?" (7 letters).** The hyphenated word is the joke. Read "taking" as acquiring, not photographing, and the place becomes a venue where pictures change hands. Two words run together.
- **65-Across, "What bulldogs and poodles compete in ... and where 18-, 29- and 51-Across each belong?" (16 letters).** The question mark flags a revealer. The answer is a category with a dog-show meaning, and it also names the box that the three other long entries share. Three words run together.
- **49-Across, "Possible reply to "Don't you want to have something?"" (4 letters).** The question mark here belongs to the quoted question, so it is a plain fill, not a pun. The answer is a full sentence squeezed into four letters.
- **48-Down, "Wow, can you believe him?!" (7 letters)** ends in an interrobang-style pair, not a lone question mark. It is spoken aloud: a pointer word and a noun, run together.

## Reading the 16-by-15 grid: lengths, crossings and clue types

The grid is 16 columns by 15 rows, 240 squares in all: 34 black and 206 holding a letter, a black share of 14.2 percent. The black pattern is unchanged by a half-turn, and no white square is left unchecked. Our [Nov. 24 Crossword page](/news/nyt-crossword-answers-november-24-2026/) described a 15-by-15 grid, so Wednesday adds one column.

Four-letter entries lead at 29, and 18 of them run Down. The two 16-letter entries are 18-Across in row 3 and 65-Across in row 13, the only two rows with no black square, so each runs from the left edge to the right. The two 12-letter entries are 29-Across in row 6, columns 1 to 12, and 51-Across in row 10, columns 5 to 16. The four long entries hold 56 of the 206 letter squares, 27.2 percent. The longest Down entries are two 10-letter answers (12-Down and 31-Down) and two 9-letter answers (4-Down and 39-Down).

Because every white square is checked, an entry's number of crossings equals its length. That makes 18-Across and 65-Across the keys, with 16 crossings each, followed by 29-Across and 51-Across at 12 and the 10-letter Down entries at 10. Fourteen Down entries cross two of the four long entries: 12-Down reaches 18- and 51-Across, and 31-Down reaches 29- and 65-Across. Letters from a Down entry that spans two of them are the quickest way to confirm a guess.
