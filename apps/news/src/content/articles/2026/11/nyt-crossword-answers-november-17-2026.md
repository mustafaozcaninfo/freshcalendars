**The New York Times (NYT) Crossword for Tuesday, Nov. 17, 2026, was constructed by Jeffrey Martinovic and Tarun Krishnamurthy and edited by Will Shortz: 76 clues, 35 Across and 41 Down, in a 15-by-15 grid.** Two clues end in a question mark and four long entries share a trick at their ends. Every clue has a hint behind a tap, and the answers close the page.

## The question-mark clues and the trick behind the long entries

Two clues end with "?", and they work differently. 14-Across, "What time ___?", is plain speech: the question mark belongs to the quoted sentence and the blank holds the last two words. Nothing in it is a pun.

57-Across is the real wordplay clue. It reads "Post-championship periods ... or what 17-, 22-, 36- and 50-Across end with?" and gives two readings of one answer. The first is a literal phrase from sports. The second is the theme: each of the four long Across entries finishes with a word that is one letter off from a season of the year, so the phrase fits as "off" seasons. Check the last word of each of the four against that rule before you write the revealer in; the exact swaps are in the spoiler section.

Other clues lean on signals in their wording. Counted from the puzzle data, 17 of the 76 carry at least one:

The counts overlap: 14-Across is a blank, a quotation and a question mark at once, so the rows add to more than 25.

## How the Nov. 17 grid is built

The grid is 15 columns by 15 rows, 225 squares in all: 42 black and 183 holding a letter, a black share of 18.7 percent. Turning it half a turn leaves the black pattern unchanged. Across has 35 entries and Down has 41, and there are no rebus squares.

Four-letter entries are the most common length at 22, with three-letter entries next at 19. The long entries all run Across: 17-Across (10 letters) fills row 3 from column 6 to the right edge, 22-Across (11) fills row 5 from the left edge to column 11, 36-Across (11) fills row 8 from column 3 to column 13, 50-Across (11) fills row 11 from column 5 to the right edge, and 57-Across (10) fills row 13 from the left edge to column 10. The three 11-letter entries start two columns further right each time, at columns 1, 3 and 5.

The only Down entries over six letters are two of eight: 10-Down runs from the top of column 13 to row 8 and 36-Down runs from row 8 of column 3 to the bottom row, so they cross 36-Across at its last and first squares. Rows 7 and 9 hold five black squares each, more than any other row; rows 3, 5, 11 and 13, the rows that carry the long entries, hold one each.
