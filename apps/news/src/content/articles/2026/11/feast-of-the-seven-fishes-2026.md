The Feast of the Seven Fishes falls on **Thursday, Dec. 24, 2026**, Christmas Eve. National Geographic calls it "a dear tradition to many Italian Americans who enjoy (at least) seven different seafood dishes on Christmas Eve." The page adds a printable seven-course planner with a cook-ahead plan for Dec. 21-24 and the Christmas Eve weekday through 2031.

## When is the Feast of the Seven Fishes in 2026?

It belongs to Christmas Eve, so the date is Thursday, Dec. 24, 2026. CalendarDate.com says "Christmas Eve for the year 2026 is celebrated/ observed on Thursday, December 24th." Mark it on the [December 2026 printable calendar](/december-calendars.html); our [Christmas Eve date page](/when-is/christmas-eve/) has the holiday itself. For stores that day, see the [Christmas Eve and Christmas Day 2026 store hours](/news/christmas-eve-christmas-day-2026-store-hours/).

## Where does the Feast of the Seven Fishes come from?

Sources differ on when it began. National Geographic says the Christmas Eve seafood feast "is an Italian American tradition that dates back to an immigration wave in the 1900s." The Philadelphia Inquirer says it "has its roots in post-World War II immigration to America," when Southern Italians brought La Vigilia, a Christmas Eve feast with no meat.

Mental Floss adds a point about the name. The earliest newspaper mention it found is a 1983 advertisement for a restaurant in Philadelphia, and immigrants "probably didn't call it the 'Feast of the Seven Fishes.'" It says they more likely said "La Cena della vigilia, Il Cenone, La vigilia di Natale, or simply La Vigilia." The Inquirer also gives the Italian name festa dei sette pesci.

## What does seven mean?

The sources do not agree. The Inquirer says the number "may refer to the seven sacraments." National Geographic describes "(at least) seven" dishes and gives no meaning for the number, so none is given.

## Do Catholics have to avoid meat on Dec. 24?

The Code of Canon Law (1983) does not list Dec. 24 as a day of abstinence. Canon 1250 says the penitential days are "every Friday of the whole year and the season of Lent." Canon 1251 says abstinence from meat is to be observed on all Fridays "unless a solemnity should fall on a Friday," and that "Abstinence and fasting are to be observed on Ash Wednesday and Good Friday." Canon 1252 binds abstinence from the completed fourteenth year.

Dec. 24, 2026, is a Thursday. In 2027 it is a Friday, so the Friday rule in canon 1251 applies to that date; the rules for U.S. Catholics are set by the bishops' conference, and they are not quoted here. Check your diocese or parish.
