**Strands #985 falls on Friday, Nov. 13, 2026, and The New York Times (NYT) prints the fitting clue "Not a good sign."** Superstition is the theme, and the answers do not stand alone. Hope Fourie constructed the grid, with Tracy Bennett editing. A ten-letter spangram names the mood; what a spangram has to reach is on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). Hints come first, and the answers wait in the spoiler section at the end.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: the two-word phrase for the bad fortune all these omens warn of.
- Hint 2: the first word means spoiled or gone off; the second is fortune or chance.
- Hint 3: ten letters across two words, starting with R and ending with K.

## Theme word hints

The six answers snap together into three two-word omens. Listed shortest to longest:

- **4 letters, starts with S:** a white seasoning you toss over a shoulder for luck.
- **5 letters, starts with F:** a story of a building; superstition sometimes skips the one numbered by the next answer.
- **6 letters, starts with B:** cracked into pieces.
- **6 letters, starts with M:** the glass that shows your reflection.
- **7 letters, starts with S:** tipped over and scattered, as a shaker might be.
- **10 letters, starts with T:** the ordinal right after twelfth.

## Three omens, and the spangram's L-shape

The answers are not loners; they join into three two-word omens, so finding one half points you to its partner close by.

The spangram makes a plain L. It begins in the fourth row on the left edge, drops one row, runs straight across the fifth row to the right edge, then falls down the last column into the bottom-right corner, touching the left, bottom and right edges. The ten-letter T word, the other long answer, fills the entire top-left quarter, and the six-letter B word sits packed in the top-right. Along the bottom, the five-letter F word takes the lower-left corner while the six-letter M word and four-letter S word share the lower-right, and the seven-letter S word spans the top-right rows just under the B word. The top-right quarter is fullest, all twelve of its tiles themed.

## Solving tips

Pair the words as you go: three answers are the first half of an omen and three are the second, so each find narrows the next. The two ten-letter answers sit in opposite corners, which helps you frame the grid.

Past this point everything is revealed.
