**Tracy Bennett is the constructor of the New York Times (NYT) Mini Crossword for Thursday, Nov. 26, 2026, according to the official data: a 5-by-5 grid with 10 clues.** Hints open on a tap. Black squares sit only at the top left and bottom right, which leaves 23 open squares.

## Hints for every clue

**Across**

- **1-Across** (4 letters), *Seasoned by time*: an adjective for something that has been left to mature, like cheese or wine; its first square is also the first square of 1-Down.
- **5-Across** (5 letters), *Word before "bird" or "voting"*: a word for ahead of schedule; it fits both phrases because each describes someone who comes ahead of the crowd.
- **6-Across** (5 letters), *Really want to eat ... or, when the second and third letters are switched, another apt verb for Turkey Day*: a verb for a strong appetite; after the swap it becomes something done to the main course with a knife.
- **7-Across** (5 letters), *Crossed one's fingers*: a past-tense verb for wishing for luck; it ends in the same letter as 4-Down.
- **8-Across** (4 letters), *Leftmost bills in tills*: the lowest paper denomination in a cash drawer, written as a plural; the bills sit at the far left of the tray.

**Down**

- **1-Down** (5 letters), *"The West Wing" creator Sorkin*: the writer's given name; its first square is shared with 1-Across.
- **2-Down** (5 letters), *Flavor of a purple popsicle*: a fruit that grows in bunches on a vine and also makes wine.
- **3-Down** (5 letters), *North Pole toymakers*: a plural for the small workshop helpers of a winter legend.
- **4-Down** (4 letters), *Tinted*: a past-tense verb for what happens to fabric in a vat of color.
- **5-Down** (4 letters), *Reverberation in a canyon*: the sound you hear when you shout and the walls answer back.

## Where the Nov. 26 Mini asks for a second look

Counting shared squares gives a flat picture. The six 5-letter entries (5-Across, 6-Across, 7-Across, 1-Down, 2-Down and 3-Down) each cross five others, and the four 4-letter entries (1-Across, 8-Across, 4-Down and 5-Down) each cross four. No entry stands out as the hub, so any fill-in you are sure of helps.

The least literal clue is 6-Across. It is the only clue in the grid that names Turkey Day, and the holiday word is a pointer, not the answer. Read the first half as plain trivia about appetite, fill it in, and the second half is a check: switching the second and third letters has to produce a real word, so the crossing Down entries must agree with both versions. That gives you a built-in test before you commit.

Tracy Bennett's grid has three full-width rows in the middle. The top row starts one square in and the bottom row stops one square short, so the black squares sit at opposite corners and the two short Down entries, 4-Down and 5-Down, start in the right and left columns instead of the top.
