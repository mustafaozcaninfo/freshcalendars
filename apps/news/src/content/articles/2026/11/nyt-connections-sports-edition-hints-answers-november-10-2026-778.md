**Connections: Sports Edition from The Athletic and New York Times (NYT) Games is puzzle #778 on Tuesday, Nov. 10, 2026.** Two hints per color come first. The answers, a note on each school nickname and the decoys follow in a spoiler section.

Today's board hides its trouble in plain sight, and the rules are on our [Connections page](/news/topic/nyt-connections/#how-it-works).

## Tuesday's hints, #778, Nov. 10, 2026

Some tiles look like they belong in two places at once. Sort the sixteen in your head before tapping any of them.

- **Yellow hint 1:** Four ways to say the same score, and none of them is the digit.
- **Yellow hint 2:** One tile is the word used for zero in soccer scores.
- **Green hint 1:** Four people who each sit on one side of a table when money is being settled.
- **Green hint 2:** Each tile is a role, not a team or a place.
- **Blue hint 1:** Four nicknames that all belong to colleges in one athletic conference.
- **Blue hint 2:** None of the four is a pro team, and one is a short word that sounds like a quick motion.
- **Purple hint 1:** Look inside each tile, not at the whole word.
- **Purple hint 2:** The hidden piece is a pro football team's nickname, and it is singular.

## Why Tuesday's board is tricky

Two breakfast foods sit on the board, and a sharp eye may go hunting for more snacks. Two animal words show up as well, and they do not land in the same color. A pair of job titles sounds like it could start a team-names group, yet they work in a different way here.

Hold your first guess until one color is certain. Our [printable weekly calendar](/printable-weekly-calendar.html) has room for a streak.

## Tuesday's answers, #778, Nov. 10, 2026 (spoilers)

Stop here if you are still sorting. Beebom and Nerd's Chalk both print these four groups, and the blue one is built on the Mid-American Conference (MAC).

- **Yellow, "Slang for zero":** Bagel, Donut, Goose Egg, Nil
- **Green, "People involved in a contract negotiation":** Agent, General Manager, Owner, Player
- **Blue, "MAC team nicknames":** Bobcats, Chippewas, Minutemen, Zips
- **Purple, "Ends in an NFL team, in singular form":** Beagle, Handkerchief, Million, Program

## Which schools use the four blue nicknames?

The blue group is a set of MAC nicknames. Each one is named on its school's official athletics site.

| Tile | Who it is | Official page |
|---|---|---|
| Bobcats | Ohio University athletics | ohiobobcats.com |
| Chippewas | Central Michigan University athletics | cmuchippewas.com |
| Minutemen | University of Massachusetts athletics | umassathletics.com |
| Zips | University of Akron athletics | gozips.com |

## What do the yellow and green tiles mean?

Yellow is slang for a score of nothing. Nil is the usual word in soccer scores, and a goose egg is a zero on a scoreboard. Bagel and Donut borrow the round shape of the numeral.

Green is the cast of a contract negotiation: the Player who signs, the Agent who speaks for them, the General Manager who builds the roster and the Owner who approves the money.

## Where does each pro team hide in the purple words?

The team name sits at the end of each word, spelled the same way.

| Tile | Hidden team name | Team |
|---|---|---|
| Beagle | Eagle | Philadelphia Eagles |
| Handkerchief | Chief | Kansas City Chiefs |
| Million | Lion | Detroit Lions |
| Program | Ram | Los Angeles Rams |

## Why Tuesday's decoys work

Bagel, Donut and Goose Egg all sound like zero, but the food angle tempts you to look for a fourth snack. Nil has no food meaning at all, which makes it the tile that confirms the group.

Beagle and Bobcats are both animals, and the lion inside Million is a big cat as well. Only the hidden-ending rule separates the purple tiles from the blue ones. Player and Owner could pass for team words, and Agent could be a spy, so the green group can take longer to see than its color suggests.
