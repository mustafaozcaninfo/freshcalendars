**The New York Times (NYT) Crossword for Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, was constructed by Gia Bosko and edited by Will Shortz: 66 clues, 30 Across and 36 Down, in a 15-by-15 grid.** Friday is a low-word-count grid, with six entries of 12 to 14 letters and six clues that end in a question mark. Every clue has a hint behind a tap, and the answers close the page.

## Question-mark clues in the Nov. 20 Crossword

Six of the 66 clues end in a question mark, the usual sign of a pun or a stretched meaning. Each is explained below without its answer, with the length in letters.

- **13-Across, "Passing remark?" (13 letters).** "Passing" is a legislative word here, and the "remark" is something said aloud at the end of a vote. The answer runs five words together.
- **19-Across, "Something kept under cover?" (5 letters).** The "cover" is a bed cover, so the answer is a piece of bedding, and the secrecy in the clue is a decoy.
- **29-Across, "Underground band?" (4 letters).** The "band" is a streak in the earth, not a group that plays in a club.
- **5-Down, "The ties that bind?" (7 letters).** Here the "ties" are literal cords, and "bind" is meant physically. The answer is plural and begins with a T.
- **29-Down, "Works with bricks?" (7 letters).** The bricks belong to a toy. The answer is two words run together: a brand name and a word for creative work.
- **42-Down, "Love of mythology?" (4 letters).** The question mark flags a translation: "love" in the language behind the Roman myths. The answer begins with a vowel and ends with a consonant.

One more clue looks like wordplay but is not: 28-Down is the quoted line "And?", a spoken reply, and it carries a question mark inside the quotation marks. It is dialogue to fill in as a two-word retort, not a pun, so it is not one of the six.

## How the Nov. 20 grid is built

The grid is 15 columns by 15 rows, 225 squares in all: 39 black and 186 holding a letter, a black share of 17.3 percent. A half-turn leaves the black pattern unchanged. Thursday's grid, on our [Nov. 19 Crossword page](/news/nyt-crossword-answers-november-19-2026/), by James Mattina, had 33 black squares and 74 clues, so Friday adds six black squares and drops eight clues.

Five-letter entries are the most common length at 22, 14 of them in Down. The six long entries are all in Across. 1-Across (12 letters) runs from column 4 to the right edge, 13-Across (13) from column 3 and 14-Across (14) from column 2, so the top three rows form a staircase against the right side. At the bottom, 43-Across (14 letters) fills columns 1 to 14, 46-Across (13) columns 1 to 13 and 47-Across (12) columns 1 to 12, a mirror of the same shape.

Row 8, the middle row, has seven black squares, more than any other row, and only three entries start in it, two Across and one Down. Down entries stop at eight letters, so the long stacks are crossed mostly by short and mid-length answers. Rows 1 to 3 and 13 to 15 each hold fewer than four black squares. The daily format is on our [NYT Crossword page](/news/topic/nyt-crossword/#how-it-works); on this Friday, the three-row stacks at the corners are the place to start.
