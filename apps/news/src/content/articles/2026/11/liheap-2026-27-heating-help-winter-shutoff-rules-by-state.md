Pennsylvania's Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) season opened Nov. 2, 2026 and runs through April 23, 2027, according to the state's LIHEAP page, and New York City's Regular Home Energy Assistance Program (HEAP) benefit is scheduled to open the same day, per the city's Energy Assistance page. Other states opened earlier or close later. This story puts each state's own application window and its winter shutoff protection side by side, with a one-page printable of the dates.

## When can you apply for heating help in 2026-27?

Each row comes from the state or city page named in it. A blank means the page does not say.

| State | Program | Opens | Last day to apply |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pennsylvania | LIHEAP | Nov. 2, 2026 (opened) | April 23, 2027 |
| New York City | Regular HEAP benefit | Scheduled for Nov. 2, 2026 | Not listed |
| New Jersey | LIHEAP and Universal Service Fund | Oct. 1, 2026 | June 30, 2027 |
| Ohio | HEAP | July 1, 2026 | May 31, 2027 |
| Minnesota | Energy Assistance Program | Not on the page | May 31, 2027 |
| Wisconsin | Wisconsin Home Energy Assistance Program | Taking applications per an Oct. 1, 2026 bulletin | Not stated |
| Connecticut | Connecticut Energy Assistance Program | Sept. 1, 2026 | May 31, 2027 |
| Virginia | Fuel assistance (rule 22VAC40-680-40) | Not earlier than Sept. 1 | Not later than March 31 |

Some details sit on the pages themselves. Pennsylvania says households can receive from $200 to $1,000 based on household size, income and fuel type. New Jersey says its application period runs on a first come, first served basis, and lists a hotline at 1-800-510-3102. Wisconsin's Department of Administration says the state is now taking applications for the upcoming heating season and points to [energybenefit.wi.gov](https://energybenefit.wi.gov/). Connecticut says online and in-person applications began Tuesday, Sept. 1, 2026, and that the last day a household can apply to establish eligibility is May 31, 2027.

Virginia's row is the rule, not this year's dates. The Board of Social Services sets the specific 2026-27 dates, and we could not confirm them. The Massachusetts, Michigan and Illinois pages did not load for our check, so use your state's own page.

## Which states have winter shutoff protections, and when?

These are different programs with different rules. Only the first three are blanket date ranges, and two of those carry conditions.

| State | Protection | Dates | Condition or limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minnesota | "Cold weather period" in state law | Oct. 1 through April 30 of the following year | Defined in Minnesota Statutes 2026, section 216B.096 |
| Connecticut | Electric winter moratorium | Nov. 1 through May 1 | In hardship cases where the customer lacks the financial resources to pay the entire account |
| Indiana | Winter disconnection moratorium | Dec. 1 to March 15 | Under conditions, including receiving help from the federally funded Energy Assistance Program (EAP) |
| Ohio | Special reconnect order, not a moratorium | Contact your utility between Oct. 12, 2026 and April 16, 2027 | Pay no more than $175 plus a reconnection charge that cannot exceed $36 |
| New Jersey | Winter Termination Program | Last season: Nov. 15, 2025 through March 15, 2026 | 2026-27 dates not yet announced |

Connecticut's dates come from the Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (PURA) "Winter Moratorium Details" page, which is a standing rule page. Indiana's come from the Office of Utility Consumer Counselor (OUCC) frequently asked questions page. The Ohio entry is a Sept. 30, 2026 bulletin from the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio (PUCO): customers who were disconnected can use the window to restore service. The New Jersey Board of Public Utilities announced last season's program on Nov. 14, 2025; treat those dates as last season's until the board posts this year's.

## Who qualifies for LIHEAP under federal law?

Under 42 U.S.C. 8624(b)(2)(B), as published in the [U.S. Code text at Cornell Law School's Legal Information Institute](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/8624), eligible households have incomes not above the greater of 150 percent of the state poverty level or 60 percent of state median income. That is the federal ceiling. States may set lower limits, so your state's application page decides. We do not list a federal funding figure for fiscal year 2027 here.
