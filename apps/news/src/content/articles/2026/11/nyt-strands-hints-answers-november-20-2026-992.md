**Strands #992 from The New York Times (NYT) is the puzzle for Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, and its clue is "There's just one small problem ..."** Tracy Bennett is listed as constructor and editor in the puzzle data. Today's board hides a 17-letter spangram and five theme words, and the spangram is long enough to snake down the whole grid; the rules are on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). Graded hints come first, then start cells and a map of the paths, and the answers sit under a spoiler warning at the end.

The clue is the setup for a saying, and each theme word names a different kind of small trouble.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: An idiom of five words for the one small flaw that spoils something otherwise good.
- Hint 2: It has 17 letters, starting with A and ending with T.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is the top-left corner of the board, row 1, column 1, and it begins by moving right along the top row.

## Theme word hints

Five words fit the clue, listed here from shortest to longest:

- **4 letters, starts with S:** a rough spot that catches thread, or a small obstacle in a plan.
- **5 letters, starts with H, ends with H:** a knot that ties a rope to a post, or a brief delay.
- **6 letters, starts with H, has a double C:** a short involuntary gulp of air, or a tiny glitch.
- **7 letters, starts with S, ends with K:** a reversal that costs you ground after progress.
- **9 letters, starts with H, ends with E:** something that stands in the way. It is the longest theme word.

Three of the five theme words begin with the same letter.

## Start-cell hints

Rows count from the top and columns from the left; the first step is the direction from a word's first letter to its second. No word is named here.

- **4 letters, start cell row 2, column 5:** the first step goes straight up.
- **5 letters, start cell row 8, column 5:** the first step goes straight right.
- **6 letters, start cell row 6, column 2:** the first step goes straight down.
- **7 letters, start cell row 4, column 5:** the first step goes straight down.
- **9 letters, start cell row 2, column 3:** the first step goes straight down.

## Path geometry of the Nov. 20 board

The spangram takes 16 steps and none is diagonal: 6 go right, 7 go down and 3 go left. Drawn on the grid it is an S. It runs right along row 1 from column 1 to column 4, drops down column 4 to row 5, turns left along row 5 back to column 1, goes down column 1 to row 8 and finishes by running right along row 8 to column 4. Rows 1, 5 and 8 hold four spangram tiles each, and the other five rows hold one. Column 1 holds 5 of its tiles, column 4 holds 6, and columns 2 and 3 hold 3 each.

The five theme words take 26 steps between them. Counted by direction, 8 steps run across the board (6 right, 2 left), 15 run vertically (8 down, 7 up) and only 3 are diagonal. Two of the diagonals belong to the 7-letter word and one to the 5-letter word; the other three words move in straight lines only. Of all 42 steps on the board, 39 are straight.

The spangram leaves two regions to fill by hand. Rows 2 to 4 of columns 1 to 3 form a closed pocket of 9 tiles, and the 9-letter theme word covers it exactly. The other 22 tiles sit on the right and along the bottom, and they belong to the 4-, 5-, 6- and 7-letter words. Columns 5 and 6 never meet the spangram, so their 16 tiles hold the 4-, 5- and 7-letter words and nothing else.

Split the grid into quarters of 12 tiles each. The lower right (rows 5 to 8, columns 4 to 6) has the most theme-word letters: 10, with the spangram taking the other 2. It carries three words, the 5-letter word whole plus 3 letters of the 7-letter word and 2 of the 6-letter word. The upper left holds 9 theme letters, all from the 9-letter word, the upper right holds 8 and the lower left only 4, because the spangram takes 8 of its 12 tiles.

## Solving tips

A good first target is the pocket in the upper left, where the longest word has nowhere else to go once the spangram's top row is found. After that, work down the two right-hand columns, which only theme words use.
