**Strands #998 from The New York Times (NYT) is the Thursday, Nov. 26, 2026 puzzle, and its clue is "A crowded table."** Tracy Bennett is listed as constructor and editor. Nov. 26 is Thanksgiving Day in the U.S., and this clue suits it. The rules for the game sit on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works), and today's board has five theme words besides the spangram. Hints come first, then a map of the board, then the answers behind a spoiler warning.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: Picture who sits at the table when the seats go to people you picked yourself, with no blood tie needed. The spangram names that bond.
- Hint 2: It has 12 letters, begins with C and ends with Y, and it is two words joined. No letter appears twice.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 3, column 1, on the left edge, and the first step goes straight down.

## Theme word hints

Five words fit the clue, listed by length, shortest first:

- **5 letters, starts with N, ends with D:** a wanderer with no fixed address, always on the move.
- **6 letters, starts with F, ends with D:** someone you like and trust, close to you without any family tie.
- **8 letters, starts with N, ends with R:** the person on the other side of your fence or apartment wall. All eight letters differ, and the third is I.
- **8 letters, starts with N, ends with R:** a guest who only just moved to town and may know few people yet. One letter appears twice.
- **9 letters, starts with C, ends with E:** a person you work alongside, from the same office. Two different letters each appear twice.

## Start-cell hints

Rows count from the top and columns from the left. The first step is the direction from a word's first tile to its second. No word is named here.

- **5 letters, start cell row 8, column 4:** the first step goes straight left.
- **6 letters, start cell row 3, column 2:** the first step goes diagonally up and to the left.
- **8 letters, start cell row 5, column 5:** the first step goes diagonally down and to the right.
- **8 letters, start cell row 5, column 1:** the first step goes straight right.
- **9 letters, start cell row 3, column 3:** the first step goes straight up.

## Where the Nov. 26 spangram runs, by row and column

The spangram opens on the left edge at row 3, column 1 and drops one tile to row 4, column 1. From there it runs right along row 4 to column 3, steps down to row 5, column 3 and moves right to row 5, column 4. Then it climbs two tiles, through row 4, column 4 to row 3, column 4, and turns right to row 3, column 5. Two more moves finish it: down to row 4, column 5, right to row 4, column 6, and a last drop to row 5, column 6 on the right edge.

Every one of the 11 steps is straight: 4 go down, 5 go right and 2 go up. None is diagonal and none goes left. The word never reaches row 1, row 2 or rows 6 to 8, so it touches neither the top nor the bottom edge. Row 4 holds six of its tiles, one in each column, and rows 3 and 5 hold three each.

That full row 4 acts as a wall. Rows 1 to 3 hold 15 theme tiles, the 9-letter word and the 6-letter word, and rows 5 to 8 hold the other 21, from the 5-letter word and both 8-letter words. No theme word crosses the line, so you can solve the top and the bottom as two separate puzzles.

Cutting the board into four quarters of 12 tiles (rows 1 to 4 or 5 to 8, columns 1 to 3 or 4 to 6) shows where the theme words gather:

| Quarter | Spangram tiles | Theme-word tiles | Which theme words reach it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top left | 4 | 8 | the 6-letter word (6 tiles), the 9-letter word (2) |
| Top right | 5 | 7 | the 9-letter word (7) |
| Bottom left | 1 | 11 | the 8-letter word from row 5, column 1 (8), the 5-letter word (3) |
| Bottom right | 2 | 10 | the 8-letter word from row 5, column 5 (8), the 5-letter word (2) |

The bottom-left corner is the densest: 11 of its 12 tiles belong to theme words, and the 8-letter word that starts at row 5, column 1 fills a solid block, 2 columns wide and 4 rows tall, in columns 1 and 2. The 5-letter word straddles the line between columns 3 and 4, with 3 tiles on the left and 2 on the right. The 9-letter word is the reverse case, with 7 of its 9 tiles in the top-right quarter.

## Solving tips

Start at the bottom-left, where one word takes a solid block of eight tiles along the left side. After it, the tiles beside the block in column 3 give you the short word's first steps.
