**Strands #999 from The New York Times (NYT) is the Friday, Nov. 27, 2026 puzzle, and the theme clue is "Take your leave."** Tracy Bennett is listed as constructor and editor. This board has seven theme words besides the spangram, so the hints below are split by word length and start cell. Rules for the game are on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works), and the next puzzle after this one is the milestone #1000.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: Think of what you ask a boss for when you want to be away from work for a few days. The spangram is that request, and every theme word is a kind of it.
- Hint 2: It has 7 letters, begins with T and ends with F, and it is two words joined. One letter appears twice, and it is the last letter.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 4, column 1, on the left edge, and the first step goes diagonally up and to the right.

## Theme word hints

Seven words fit the clue, listed by length, shortest first:

- **4 letters, starts with R, ends with T:** what a tired person needs in a chair or a bed. All four letters differ.
- **5 letters, starts with B, ends with K:** a short stop in the middle of work, and also what you can do to a bone or a promise. All five letters differ.
- **5 letters, starts with P, ends with E:** what you press on a remote to freeze a video in place. Three of its five letters are vowels.
- **6 letters, starts with H, ends with S:** a gap in a run of activity, such as a band stepping away for a few years. All six letters differ.
- **6 letters, starts with R, ends with S:** the stretch between classes when children go outside. Two different letters each appear twice.
- **7 letters, starts with H, ends with Y:** a day the calendar sets aside, when many people do not go to work. All seven letters differ.
- **8 letters, starts with V, ends with N:** a trip away from home, with a suitcase and a hotel booking. One letter appears twice.

## Start-cell hints

Rows count from the top and columns from the left. The first step is the direction from a word's first tile to its second. No word is named here.

- **4 letters, start cell row 2, column 2:** the first step goes diagonally down and to the left.
- **5 letters, start cell row 2, column 4:** the first step goes straight down.
- **5 letters, start cell row 4, column 5:** the first step goes straight down.
- **6 letters, start cell row 5, column 1:** the first step goes straight down.
- **6 letters, start cell row 2, column 5:** the first step goes diagonally down and to the right.
- **7 letters, start cell row 8, column 4:** the first step goes straight right.
- **8 letters, start cell row 8, column 3:** the first step goes straight up.

## The Nov. 27 spangram path and where the theme words crowd

The spangram opens on the left edge at row 4, column 1 and climbs diagonally to row 3, column 2. It then moves right to row 3, column 3, slips down and right to row 4, column 4, and climbs again to row 3, column 5. The last two moves are a diagonal drop to row 4, column 6, on the right edge, and one tile straight down to row 5, column 6.

Of the 6 steps, 4 are diagonal (2 up and to the right, 2 down and to the right), 1 goes straight right and 1 goes straight down. No step goes left or straight up. The spangram has one tile in each of columns 1 to 5 and two in column 6, and it holds 3 tiles in row 3, 3 in row 4 and 1 in row 5. Rows 1 and 2 and rows 6 to 8 have no spangram tile, so it touches neither the top nor the bottom edge.

Because the spangram stays in rows 3 to 5, the theme words fall into two camps. Three words lie wholly in rows 1 to 3, with 15 tiles between them, and four lie wholly in rows 4 to 8, with 26 tiles. No theme word has tiles on both sides. Row 3 is the only row shared between the spangram and the top group, where 3 spangram tiles sit beside 3 theme tiles.

Cutting the board into four quarters of 12 tiles (rows 1 to 4 or 5 to 8, columns 1 to 3 or 4 to 6) shows the mix:

| Quarter | Spangram tiles | Theme-word tiles | Which theme words reach it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top left | 3 | 9 | the 4-letter word (4 tiles), the 5-letter word from row 2, column 4 (3), the 8-letter word (2) |
| Top right | 3 | 9 | the 6-letter word from row 2, column 5 (6), the 5-letter word from row 2, column 4 (2), the 5-letter word from row 4, column 5 (1) |
| Bottom left | 0 | 12 | the 6-letter word from row 5, column 1 (6), the 8-letter word (6) |
| Bottom right | 1 | 11 | the 7-letter word (7), the 5-letter word from row 4, column 5 (4) |

The bottom-left quarter is the only one with no spangram tile, and the 6-letter word that starts at row 5, column 1 fills all of it together with six tiles of the 8-letter word. The 7-letter theme word is the tightest: it sits in a 3-by-3 block in rows 6 to 8 and columns 4 to 6, using 7 of the 9 tiles, and the other 2 belong to the 5-letter word that starts at row 4, column 5. The top-right quarter holds all six tiles of the other 6-letter word.

## Solving tips

Start at the bottom left, where two words take up an entire quarter, then work out the 7-letter word in the corner beside it. The three top words are the smaller job, and once they are marked off the middle rows give up the spangram.
