**Today's Connections from The New York Times (NYT) is puzzle #1248, for Tuesday, Nov. 10, 2026, edited by Wyna Liu.** One group is words for a commotion, one is old-fashioned jobs, one is literary devices, and one is words that use a single vowel five times and no other. All 16 entries are long single words, so the length of a word tells you little. Below are two levels of hints for each color, then the full solution and an explanation of the tricky words in a marked spoiler section.

## Today's 16 words

Abracadabra, Onomatopoeia, Apothecary, Foofaraw, Oxymoron, Protozoology, Hullabaloo, Haberdasher, Beekeeper, Scrivener, Hyperbole, Brouhaha, Wainwright, Kerfuffle, Invisibility, Metonymy

## How the game works

Sort the 16 entries into four groups of four. You can make four mistakes. The groups run from yellow to green, blue and purple, with purple the group that depends most on wordplay.

## Hints for each group

**Yellow**

- Hint 1: Think of a noisy fuss over something.
- Hint 2: One doubles its "ha," one has a double F in the middle, one ends in a double O, and one starts with a "foo" sound.

**Green**

- Hint 1: Each word names a job you would find in an old novel more than in a job listing.
- Hint 2: One mixed medicines, one sold men's hats and small sewing goods, one copied documents by hand, and one built wagons and carts.

**Blue**

- Hint 1: Each word is a term from an English class.
- Hint 2: One is deliberate exaggeration, one names a thing by something tied to it, one is a word that imitates a sound, and one puts two opposite words side by side.

**Purple**

- Hint 1: Look only at the vowels in each word.
- Hint 2: Each word uses one vowel five times and no other vowel: one for A, one for E, one for I and one for O.

## Solving tips

Start with the old jobs. Apothecary, Haberdasher, Scrivener and Wainwright are the only trades on the board. Then count vowels. Four words use just one vowel letter, five times over, and that is purple. Onomatopoeia and Oxymoron look like they might qualify because of all their O's. Onomatopoeia mixes in other vowels, and Oxymoron has only three O's, so both stay with the literary devices. Hullabaloo fails the vowel test too, which leaves it with the other words for a fuss.

## Why the traps worked

**Onomatopoeia and Oxymoron.** Onomatopoeia has four O's, yet it also uses A, E and I. Oxymoron does use only O, but three times, not five. Both are literary devices.

**Hullabaloo.** Its double O ending looks like a vowel pattern, but it mixes U, A and O. It is a word for an uproar.

**Abracadabra.** It sounds like a magic word, not a vowel puzzle. It counts in purple because its only vowel is A, five times. Beekeeper does the same with E, Invisibility with I and Protozoology with O; the Y in Invisibility and Protozoology is not counted as a vowel here.
