**The 2026 Ursid meteor shower peaks the night of Monday, Dec. 21 to Tuesday, Dec. 22, according to the American Meteor Society (AMS).** The AMS puts the Moon at 89% lit that night and expects "significant lunar interference." This story adds the Moon's setting time for 10 U.S. cities from the U.S. Naval Observatory (USNO), and works out how long the sky stays Moon-free before sunrise.

## Ursid peak and active dates for 2026

The AMS lists the Ursids as "Active through December 17–26, 2026," with the peak night on Dec. 21-22. The peak night starts on the December solstice, which the USNO puts at Dec. 21, 2026, 20:50 Universal Time (UT), or 3:50 p.m. Eastern Standard Time (EST). The solstice is the longest night of the year in the Northern Hemisphere. Our [winter solstice 2026 story](/news/winter-solstice-2026-december-21-shortest-day-sunset-times/) has sunset times.

| What | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Ursids active | Dec. 17-26, 2026 | AMS |
| Peak night | Dec. 21-22, 2026 | AMS |
| Moon on the peak night | 89% illuminated, waxing gibbous | AMS |

The AMS says observers normally see 5-10 Ursids per hour during the late-morning hours. The AMS page also notes that the radiant, the point the meteors appear to come from, "fails to clear the horizon" from the southern tropics. It lists no other rate for 2026, so this story gives none.

## How many minutes of Moon-free sky before sunrise?

The Moon decides how dark the sky is, and on this peak night it is almost full. The AMS gives 89% illuminated. The USNO lists 97% lit on Dec. 22 for most of the cities below and 98% for Los Angeles and Seattle (92% and 93% on the Dec. 21 query). The AMS and USNO figures are for different moments, so we cite each by source and do not merge them.

We pulled each city's times from the USNO rise and set data service for Dec. 21 and Dec. 22, 2026, using the city's center coordinates and local standard time. The last column is Fresh Calendars' arithmetic: the minutes from moonset to sunrise.

| City | Sunset, Mon., Dec. 21 | Moonset, Tue., Dec. 22 | Sunrise, Tue., Dec. 22 | Minutes from moonset to sunrise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New York | 4:32 p.m. | 5:53 a.m. | 7:17 a.m. | 84 |
| Boston | 4:15 p.m. | 5:47 a.m. | 7:11 a.m. | 84 |
| Washington, D.C. | 4:50 p.m. | 5:58 a.m. | 7:24 a.m. | 86 |
| Atlanta | 5:33 p.m. | 6:11 a.m. | 7:39 a.m. | 88 |
| Chicago | 4:23 p.m. | 5:55 a.m. | 7:15 a.m. | 80 |
| Dallas | 5:25 p.m. | 6:01 a.m. | 7:26 a.m. | 85 |
| Denver | 4:39 p.m. | 5:59 a.m. | 7:18 a.m. | 79 |
| Phoenix | 5:25 p.m. | 6:07 a.m. | 7:29 a.m. | 82 |
| Los Angeles | 4:48 p.m. | 5:35 a.m. | 6:55 a.m. | 80 |
| Seattle | 4:20 p.m. | 6:49 a.m. | 7:55 a.m. | 66 |

In all 10 cities the Moon sets between 66 and 88 minutes before sunrise on Dec. 22. That is the longest the Moon-free window can be, because it ends no later than sunrise. Times for other places differ; the USNO's rise and set service takes any U.S. location.

## Dates to mark around the Ursid peak

The USNO lists its phase times in UT. The EST conversions below are Fresh Calendars' arithmetic, five hours behind UT.

| Date | What happens | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Tue., Dec. 8 | New Moon at 7:52 p.m. EST (00:52 UT on Dec. 9) | USNO |
| Thu., Dec. 17 | First Quarter Moon at 12:42 a.m. EST (05:42 UT); Ursids become active | USNO, AMS |
| Mon., Dec. 21 | December solstice at 3:50 p.m. EST (20:50 UT) | USNO |
| Mon., Dec. 21 to Tue., Dec. 22 | Ursid peak night | AMS |
| Wed., Dec. 23 | Full Moon at 8:28 p.m. EST (01:28 UT on Dec. 24) | USNO |
| Sat., Dec. 26 | Last day of the active period | AMS |
