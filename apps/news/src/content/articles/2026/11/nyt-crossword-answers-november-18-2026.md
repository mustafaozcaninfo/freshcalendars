**The New York Times (NYT) Crossword for Wednesday, Nov. 18, 2026, was constructed by Doug Peterson and edited by Will Shortz: 76 clues, 37 Across and 39 Down, in a 15-by-15 grid.** Not one clue ends in a question mark, yet three of the long entries are linked by a single idea. Each clue has a hint behind a tap, and the answers close the page.

## Wordplay without question marks

A question mark usually flags a pun. Today none of the 76 clues has one, so the tricks have to be found in the wording. Four are worth knowing before you start.

- **A surname posing as a job.** The clue at 18-Down, "Driver in many casts", reads like a trade. The capital D is the giveaway: it is a person's last name, and the answer is that person's first name.
- **A tower that is not a building.** "Drop tower, e.g." at 56-Down points at a ride, not at architecture. The "e.g." says the answer is one example of a larger group.
- **A toy talking about itself.** At 4-Down the clue says "Lego person, to a (human) Lego person". The parenthesis separates the real person from the plastic one, and the answer is the toy's nickname.
- **One idea, three clues.** The clue at 60-Across tells you to reread 17-, 27- and 45-Across. Their clues describe a list status, a compliment and a French resort, and one four-letter word fits all three; the 10-letter answer at 60-Across is what a solver might say once it clicks.

## How the Nov. 18 grid is built

The grid is 15 columns by 15 rows, 225 squares in all: 36 black and 189 holding a letter, a black share of 16.0 percent. Turning it half a turn leaves the black pattern unchanged. Across has 37 entries and Down has 39, and there are no rebus squares.

Four-letter entries are the most common length at 30, with five-letter entries next at 15. Six entries run 10 letters or more. The two longest are Across: 27-Across fills row 6 from the left edge to column 14, and 45-Across fills row 10 from column 2 to the right edge. The other 10-letter Across entries are 17-Across, in row 3 from the left edge to column 10, and 60-Across, in row 13 from column 6 to the right edge.

Both 10-letter Down entries connect the two 14-letter rows. 29-Down starts at the third letter of 27-Across, in column 3, and runs to the bottom row, and 11-Down runs from the top of column 13 to row 10, so each one crosses 27-Across and 45-Across. Rows 5 and 11 hold seven black squares each, while rows 6 and 10, with the long entries, hold one apiece.

Against Tuesday's grid, which had 42 black squares, 35 Across and 41 Down and a longest entry of 11 letters, today's has six fewer black squares, two more Across entries and entries three letters longer at the top end.
