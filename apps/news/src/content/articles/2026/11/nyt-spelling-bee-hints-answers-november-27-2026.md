**Friday, Nov. 27, 2026, gives Spelling Bee players from The New York Times (NYT) a short board: U in the middle, with A, E, F, L, P and T around it.** There are 37 words worth 141 points, Genius sits at 99, and only one pangram is hiding in the list.

Three vowel letters are on the board and the center letter is one of them, so U turns up in every answer. After Thursday's 72-word list, this one is about half the size; the scoring rules are on our [Spelling Bee page](/news/topic/nyt-spelling-bee/#how-it-works).

## How are Friday's 141 points split?

Four-letter words score 1 point, longer words 1 point per letter, and a pangram adds 7. The 8-letter pangram is therefore 8 + 7 = 15 points. The running total adds the rows from the top.

- **Where Genius falls:** the total is 98 after the 6-letter words, 1 short of 99, so any 7-letter word or the pangram crosses the bar.
- **Five letters carry the most:** 9 words hold 45 points, 32% of the total.
- **Pangram bonus:** the 7 bonus points are 5% of the 141.

## The hardest words on Friday's list

- **Longest answers:** one of 8 letters and four of 7; nothing is longer.
- **Rare pairs:** EP, LT, EL, UA, LP, AE, FE and FT each appear in a single answer.
- **Doubled letters:** 12 answers repeat a letter back to back, 5 with LL, 4 with FF, and 1 each with PP, TT and EE.
- **Double U:** 4 answers hold two U's and none holds three.
- **Endings:** 10 answers end in E, 8 in L, 8 in T, 4 in U, 3 in F, 2 in A and 2 in P.

## The gap from four-letter words to Genius

The 17 four-letter answers give 17 points, about 17% of the 99 needed. That leaves 82 points to find among the 20 answers of five letters or more, which hold 124 in all. Drop the pangram and the other 36 words are worth 126, still 27 points over the bar.
