**The New York Times (NYT) Crossword for Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026, was constructed by James Mattina and edited by Will Shortz: 74 clues, 36 Across and 38 Down, in a 15-by-15 grid.** Four rows of the grid are free of black squares, and nine clues end in a question mark. Each clue has a hint behind a tap, and the answers close the page.

## Question-mark clues in the Nov. 19 Crossword

Nine of the 74 clues carry a question mark, the usual sign of a pun or a stretched meaning. None of them is answered by its plain reading, and the five short ones are quick to untangle once you see the turn.

- **A takeout spot with no menu.** At 1-Across, "takeout" is about taking money out, not food. The answer is three letters.
- **Burned, but not by fire.** 61-Across reads "Burned after reading?", and "burned" stands for an insult. The answer is a casual past-tense verb.
- **A literal heart fix.** 8-Down, "Remedies for the brokenhearted?", is a medical answer: something a surgeon places, not a sentimental cure.
- **Pampering by the initials.** 34-Down asks for "A little pampering?", and the answer is three letters that stand for gentle care.
- **A garden with a famous resident.** 54-Down, "Garden figure?", points to the oldest garden in the Bible rather than to a statue.
- **A bang-up job.** 57-Down, "Do a bang-up job on?", turns the praise around: the answer is a verb for damaging a surface.
- **The three long ones.** 17-, 27- and 43-Across ask about chapels, charities and megaphones. Each answer says what the thing exists for, as an exasperated exclamation, and three of the four 15-letter entries start with the same three letters. The fourth, at 55-Across, ends with them, and its clue says it is also the question those three answer.

Two clues use initials themselves: 51-Down mentions the Global Positioning System (GPS) and 45-Down the cyan, magenta, yellow and key black (CMYK) printing colors. Other clue signals, counted from the puzzle data:

The counts overlap: 55-Across opens with a quotation mark, has an ellipsis and points to three other entries at once. Only one clue is a fill-in-the-blank, so the day leans on wordplay and names (a skier, a rapper, a comedian) rather than on phrases to complete.

## How the Nov. 19 grid is built

The grid is 15 columns by 15 rows, 225 squares in all: 33 black and 192 holding a letter, a black share of 14.7 percent. A half-turn leaves the black pattern unchanged. No square holds more than one letter, so there is no rebus. The data lists no circled or shaded squares.

| Length | Across | Down | Total |

| 15 | 4 | 0 | 4 |

Four-letter entries are the most common length at 26, and there are no five-letter entries in Down at all. The four 15-letter entries fill rows 3, 6, 10 and 13 from edge to edge, the only rows without a black square. Row 8, the middle row, has the most black squares: seven.

The 12 seven-letter Down entries are the longest in that direction, and each crosses two of the long rows. The six that start in row 1 (1-, 2-, 3-, 7-, 11- and 12-Down) run to row 7 and cross rows 3 and 6. The six that start in row 9 (37- through 42-Down) run to the bottom edge and cross rows 10 and 13. The long answers therefore lock the grid together in two halves, split by the black squares of row 8.

Wednesday's grid, on our [Nov. 18 Crossword page](/news/nyt-crossword-answers-november-18-2026/), had 36 black squares, 76 clues and no question marks. Today's has three fewer black squares, two fewer clues and nine question marks.
