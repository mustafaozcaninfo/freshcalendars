**Tu BiShvat 5787 begins at sundown on Friday, Jan. 22, 2027, and ends at nightfall on Saturday, Jan. 23, according to Hebcal.** Tu BiShvat is 15 Sh'vat, and in 2027 it falls on Shabbat, so the evening it begins is Friday night. Below are Hebcal's candle-lighting times for four cities, the dates through 2029 and what the traditional seder uses. A free seder checklist is at the end.

## When is Tu BiShvat 2027, 2028 and 2029?

Hebcal gives each year from sundown to nightfall. We added the weekdays from those dates.

| Year | Begins at sundown | Ends at nightfall | Hebrew date |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2027 | Fri., Jan. 22 | Sat., Jan. 23 | 15 Sh'vat 5787 |
| 2028 | Fri., Feb. 11 | Sat., Feb. 12 | 15 Sh'vat 5788 |
| 2029 | Tue., Jan. 30 | Wed., Jan. 31 | 15 Sh'vat 5789 |

The Hebrew date stays the same and the Gregorian date moves, which is why the 2028 start is almost three weeks after the 2027 one. ## What happens at a Tu BiShvat seder?

Chabad says the Kabbalistic celebration that began in Safed "involves eating particular fruits in a specific order." Chabad says it was first recorded in Pri Etz Hadar, arranged by a student of Rabbi Yitzchak Luria, the Ari (1534-1572). The Union for Reform Judaism says Jewish mystics in the 16th and 17th centuries created a ritual "modeled after the Passover seder," with readings collected in Pri Eitz Hadar, published in 1753.

The seder uses four cups. Chabad describes them this way: "The first is all white, the second mostly white, the third half-and-half, and the fourth mostly red." The Reform movement describes the cups as going "from white for winter, to white with a little red for early spring, to red with a little white for late spring, to dark red for the fullness of summer." It names four worlds, Assiya, Yetzira, Beriah and Atzilut, while Chabad names Beriah, Yetzirah and Asiyah.

## Which foods does Deuteronomy 8:8 name?

The verse, in the Jewish Publication Society (JPS) translation on Sefaria, reads: "a land of wheat and barley, of vines, figs, and pomegranates, a land of olive trees and honey;". That is seven foods: wheat, barley, grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives and honey. The verse says honey. Chabad's explanation reads it as "(date-)honey," so dates are Chabad's reading and not a word in the verse.
