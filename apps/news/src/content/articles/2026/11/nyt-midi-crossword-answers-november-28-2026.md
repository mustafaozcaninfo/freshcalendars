## How does "Baby Talk" work in the grid?

The title is carried by a single column. 6-Down has 10 letters and sits in the eighth column from the left, running from the top row to the bottom row with no black square in its way. Because every square in that column is a letter, the entry crosses all 10 Across entries, one letter in each: 4-Across, 11-Across, 12-Across, 15-Across, 16-Across, 22-Across, 25-Across, 27-Across, 30-Across and 32-Across.

Its clue reads *Developmental milestone ... and a hint to this puzzle's circled letters*. The first half defines the entry, and the second half says the puzzle has circled squares. The saved data for the day holds letters, numbers and clue text, with no circle marks, so this page cannot say which squares are circled. Check the grid in the NYT app for those.

Two other entries also have 10 letters: 12-Across fills row 3 and 27-Across fills row 8, edge to edge, and the column crosses both of them. That makes three 10-letter entries, which is the same count as Friday's three 9-letter entries.

## Which clue is the least literal today?

23-Down, *In need of a hug, maybe*. The word "maybe" signals that the clue is a loose description of a state, not a definition, and a hug is only one example of what the solver is asked to picture. The entry is two short words run together, and it says how someone feels by denying that things are fine. Read it as a mood, then count five squares: it runs down column 9 from row 6 to row 10.
