## Where the "On the Fritz" theme sits in the grid

"On the fritz" is an old idiom for a machine that has stopped working. The grid takes it literally. The clue for 70-Across reads *Sign on a malfunctioning elevator … or a literal description for the answers to the italicized clues*. The entry has 21 squares and fills row 11 from column 1 to column 21, so it is also the only one in the grid that touches both edges. The saved data carries no italics, but the set is easy to spot: the only other long entries are six Across answers of 11 squares, and the letters of each one check out against the trick described below.

The six short entries mirror one another through the center. Turn the page half a revolution and 27-Across lands on 114-Across, 39-Across on 98-Across and 53-Across on 85-Across, with 70-Across staying in place. Their columns step across the grid in a zigzag, starting at the left edge, moving to the middle, then to the right edge, and back. The theme squares total 87: six times 11, plus 21.

Here is the trick without the answers. Read the first word of the middle entry and count its letters: 11. Each of the six themed answers uses exactly those 11 letters, shuffled into a new two-word phrase that fits its clue. So the first word is literally out of order six times, and once you have the middle row, every themed entry becomes an anagram puzzle with the letters already known.

## Four question-mark clues and the trick in each

Four clues end in a question mark. None of the explanations below names its answer.

- **77-Across, "Common core?"** Three letters. The "core" is literal: look at the middle of the word "common" in the clue itself, then write out the name of what you find, in the plural.
- **126-Across, "Marine leader?"** Five letters. Nobody is in command here. The word "leader" means the piece that comes first before "marine" in a compound, and the same piece also means extreme.
- **29-Down, "Source of a movie's stars?"** Five letters. The stars are not actors but ratings. The answer is whoever hands them out.
- **45-Down, "Providers of cold comfort?"** Nine letters. "Cold comfort" is normally an idiom for a poor consolation. Read it as a place that offers a bed at a low temperature, two words run together.

Two more clues end in a question mark inside quoted speech and are plain questions: 102-Across, *"Cómo ___ usted?"*, wants a Spanish verb, and 98-Down, *"r u srs?"*, wants the texting short form of its reply. Other clues play with form. 6-Down, *It rings a bell!*, has an exclamation mark and two readings of "rings". 72-Down is a bracketed word, *[Horrors!]*, and 5-Down is a clock time, *:50*. 74-Down, *Afonso I ou João IV*, is in Portuguese.

The grid has 441 squares: 68 are black and 373 hold a letter. It has 140 entries, 69 Across and 71 Down. The black squares are symmetric under a half turn, and no cell holds more than one letter, so there is no rebus square to plan for.

The long entries all run Across, so every Down answer is 9 squares or shorter, and only two reach 9: 45-Down in column 5 and 47-Down in column 17, both starting in row 7 and ending in row 15, one the mirror image of the other. Down answers cluster at 6 letters (14 of the 16) while Across answers lean to 4 and 5. Two clues print capitals as written: 64-Across names the World Wide Web (WWW) by its initials, and 37-Down spells the Italian name for Rome, Roma (ROMA), in capital letters. Nine clues carry a blank (___), and two clues are abbreviations by their own wording: 75-Across says "in brief" and 78-Across says "for short".
