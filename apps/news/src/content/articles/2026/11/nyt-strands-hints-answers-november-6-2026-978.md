**Today's Strands from The New York Times (NYT) is puzzle #978, for Friday, Nov. 6, 2026, and its theme clue is "Autumn walk in the woods."** Picture what you would see and step on along a forest path, and the word that names the ground beneath it. The puzzle was constructed by Christina Iverson and edited by Tracy Bennett. Below are hints for the spangram and each theme word; the answers wait in a marked spoiler section at the end.

## How Strands works

The board is six letters wide and eight tall. Theme words are made by linking letters that touch, up, down, sideways or diagonally, and every letter is used exactly once. One special answer, the spangram, names the theme and stretches from one side of the board to the opposite side. Find three non-theme words and you earn a hint that highlights the letters of a theme word.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: It is the ground under your boots on the walk.
- Hint 2: It is a phrase of two words, and the clue points to the setting.
- Hint 3: Eleven letters, and the first letter is F.

## Theme word hints

There are seven theme words, and each one is something you could spot on a woodland walk in autumn:

- **4 letters, starts with H:** the dry outer covering of a seed or an ear of corn.
- **4 letters, starts with M:** a soft green plant that coats rocks and tree trunks.
- **4 letters, starts with T:** a thin shoot that snaps underfoot.
- **5 letters, starts with A:** the nut of an oak tree.
- **6 letters, starts with B:** a limb that grows out from a tree trunk.
- **6 letters, starts with L:** the flat parts of a tree that change color and fall in autumn.
- **8 letters, starts with P:** the woody cone of a pine tree.

## Solving tips

Start with the spangram. On today's board it starts in the top-left corner, runs diagonally down and to the right until it reaches the right edge in the sixth row, then goes down the right edge to the bottom row and turns left to end in the third column. It is eleven letters long, three more than the longest theme word. The seven theme words use 37 letters, so the spangram and the theme words together fill all 48 tiles. Husk, Moss and Twig are the shortest theme words, at four letters each. Pinecone repeats N and E, and the spangram uses O three times. If you get stuck, spell any ordinary words you can see to earn a hint.
