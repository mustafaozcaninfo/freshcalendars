**Letter Boxed from The New York Times (NYT) for Tuesday, Nov. 24, 2026 has the sides N/R/G, M/U/E, H/O/T and C/I/Y, and a par of 4, according to the puzzle data on the Letter Boxed page.** Its saved word list has 1,026 entries, and 5 pairs of them touch every letter. This box sets par at 4, so the rules wait on our [Letter Boxed page](/news/topic/nyt-letter-boxed/#how-it-works). Below you get the thinnest side link, the pairs that can never follow each other, how first and last letters are squeezed, the longest words, five hints and four warm-up words. The Times' solution comes last.

## The four sides

| Side | Letters |
|---|---|
| 1 | N, R, G |
| 2 | M, U, E |
| 3 | H, O, T |
| 4 | C, I, Y |

## How many words connect each pair of sides?

A word has to hop between sides at every step. The table counts the dictionary words that make at least one hop between two sides, then the hops in each direction.

| Sides | Words with a hop between them | Hops, first side to second | Hops, second to first |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 and 2 | 653 | 493 | 606 |
| 1 and 3 | 580 | 505 | 450 |
| 1 and 4 | 516 | 417 | 314 |
| 2 and 3 | 538 | 258 | 479 |
| 2 and 4 | 369 | 218 | 221 |
| 3 and 4 | 508 | 314 | 482 |

The pair of sides 2 and 4 is the bottleneck at 369 words, and its two directions are almost level (218 and 221). Sides 1 and 2 are the easy road at 653. The sharpest imbalance is between sides 2 and 3: 479 hops run from side 3 to side 2, but only 258 go the other way.

## Which letter pairs can never follow each other?

Across the box, 108 ordered pairs join letters from different sides. Eleven of them appear in no word at all: G then C; M then R, G, H or T; H then M; T then M; C then N, G or M; and Y then H. Six more turn up in a single word: G then M, G then T, H then C, I then H, Y then G and Y then T.

The M is part of 7 of the 11 dead pairs, which makes it the awkward letter: it sits in 195 words and is the last letter of only 27. The C has 4 dead pairs, and it ends just 10 words even though 170 words start with it.

## How do the sides limit first and last letters?

Letters differ a lot in where they can sit. Words start with R in 171 cases and with C in 170, yet only 21 start with Y and 28 with U. At the other end, E closes 258 words, while C closes 10 and U closes 18. A route of two words must join on a shared letter, so the closing letter of the first word has to be a good opener. The Y is a rare opener and a common closer (113 words end in it), which makes it an unusual hinge, and three of the five routes use it.

## Longest words and whether one word is enough

Two words reach 13 letters, one of them Nonchurchgoer. Two more have 12 letters, and eight have 11. Long does not mean wide, though: the most any single word covers is 9 different letters, so the box cannot be solved with one word. One of the 13-letter words closes a two-word route; the other does not.

The five routes use the following numbers of letters in all:

| Route | Letters in the two words |
|---|---|
| 1 | 13 |
| 2 | 14 |
| 3 | 14 |
| 4 | 17 |
| 5 | 18 |

## Five hints, light to strong

1. The Times' route is two words, two fewer than the par of 4.
2. The first word has 7 letters and the second has 6, chosen from 134 words of the first length and 187 of the second.
3. The first word opens and closes on the C/I/Y side. The second opens on that side and closes on the H/O/T side.
4. The H is in the first word and the G in the second.
5. The first word starts with C and ends in Y. The second starts with Y and ends in T.
