## Clues for Quordle words 1 to 4

Words are numbered in the order the answer sources print them, so the numbers do not point to a spot on the grid.

- Meaning: a tree with thin, papery white bark that peels in strips.

**Word 2**

- Letters: begins with the 2nd letter and finishes with the 4th; one vowel.
- Meaning: unable to see, or the slatted shade that hangs over a window.

- Letters: begins with the 21st letter and finishes with the 25th; two vowels plus the final letter doing vowel work.

**Word 4**

- Letters: begins with the 19th letter and finishes with the 16th; one vowel.
- Meaning: the skin that covers the top of the head under the hair.

## Which letters do the four words share?

Twenty letter slots, 14 different letters. That gap comes from six repeats spread over the four boards, and no word repeats a letter inside itself.

| Words containing the letter | Letters | What it means for play |
|---|---|---|
| 3 of 4 | 1 | One vowel, and a yellow on it is likely to pay off on more than one board |
| 2 of 4 | 4 | Four consonants each turn up on two boards |
| 1 of 4 | 9 | Nine letters belong to a single word |

Three of the four words hold exactly one vowel, and the fourth holds two vowels and a final Y. A guess heavy on vowels would find the vowel in three words and then have nothing left for the consonants.

## How five common opening words score on today's four boards

Each cell gives green tiles (G, right letter, right spot) and yellow tiles (Y, right letter, wrong spot) the opener would earn against that word.

| Opener | Word 1 | Word 2 | Word 3 | Word 4 | Colored tiles in all |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crane | 0G 2Y | 1G 0Y | 0G 1Y | 1G 1Y | 6 |
| Slate | 0G 0Y | 1G 0Y | 1G 0Y | 2G 1Y | 5 |
| Adieu | 0G 1Y | 1G 1Y | 1G 1Y | 0G 1Y | 6 |
| Pilot | 1G 0Y | 0G 2Y | 0G 2Y | 0G 2Y | 7 |
| Round | 0G 1Y | 2G 0Y | 0G 2Y | 0G 0Y | 5 |

Pilot lights the most tiles, seven, yet only one is green, and it sits on Word 1. Slate has the reverse profile: five colored tiles, four of them green, which is the most greens in the table. Word 4 is where Slate does its work, with three colored tiles from one guess.

Word 1 is the quietest board. Add its column and the five openers earn five colored tiles together, against eight each on Words 2, 3 and 4. Slate leaves Word 1 completely dark, and Round does the same to Word 4, so a first guess that blanks a board means the second guess goes there.
