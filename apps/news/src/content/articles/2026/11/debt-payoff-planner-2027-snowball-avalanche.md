**The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) says there are "two basic strategies that can help you reduce debt: the highest interest rate method and the snowball method," and our worked example for 2027 shows what each costs on the same three sample debts.** The CFPB post, "How to reduce your debt," was published July 16, 2019 and is marked as archived content. Our free [debt payoff tracker for 2027](/news/downloads/debt-payoff-2027/debt-payoff-tracker-2027-snowball-avalanche.pdf) has room for six debts and a 12-month payment log.

This is a plain explanation, not financial advice. The debts below are made up, and your own balances, rates and minimums will give different results.

## What are the snowball and avalanche methods?

The CFPB's archived post gives two strategies. Of the snowball method it says "This approach focuses on your smallest debt." It closes with "Choose the strategy that's best for your situation and put it into action."

The CFPB does not use the word "avalanche." That is the common name for its highest interest rate method, which targets the debt with the highest APR first. In both methods, you pay at least the minimum on every debt and send the rest of your monthly budget to one target debt. When a debt is paid off, its minimum moves to the next target.

## How do the two methods compare on three sample debts?

We made up three debts, set a fixed budget of $450 a month and ran each method month by month from January 2027. The first payment lands in January 2027. Each month, interest of one-twelfth of the APR is added to the balance, every debt gets its minimum, and everything left goes to the target debt. Freed-up minimums roll over to the next target.

| Sample debt | Balance | APR | Minimum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Card A | $1,200 | 24.99% | $35 |
| Card B | $4,800 | 21.99% | $120 |
| Personal loan | $3,000 | 11.99% | $95 |

The three minimums add up to $250, so $200 a month goes to the target debt at first. For context, the Federal Reserve's G.19 Consumer Credit release of Oct. 7, 2026 reports that commercial banks charged an interest rate of 21.19% on all credit card accounts and 22.36% on accounts assessed interest in August 2026 (preliminary). Our sample rates are not forecasts.

## Which method pays off the sample debts sooner?

Card A is the smallest debt and also has the highest rate, so both methods start with it. They split afterward. The snowball goes to the personal loan ($3,000) next, and the avalanche goes to Card B (21.99%).

| Result (our calculation, hypothetical example) | Snowball (smallest balance first) | Avalanche (highest APR first) |
|---|---|---|
| Card A paid off | June 2027 | June 2027 |
| Personal loan paid off | February 2028 | December 2028 |
| Card B paid off | January 2029 | September 2028 |
| Months to debt-free | 25 | 24 |
| Debt-free month | January 2029 | December 2028 |
| Total interest | $1,994.19 | $1,770.39 |

The avalanche order finishes one month sooner and costs $223.80 less interest in this example. The snowball clears the second debt 10 months before the avalanche does, and the avalanche clears Card B 4 months before the snowball does. The gap is small because the three rates sit within 13 points of each other and the budget is fixed. We rounded interest to the cent each month, and the last payment on each debt is smaller than a full month's budget.

## How do you use the tracker with your own debts?

1. Write each debt in a row with its balance, APR and minimum payment from your latest statement.
2. Number the rows in the order you choose: smallest balance first for the snowball, highest APR first for the avalanche.
3. Set one total monthly amount. Pay the minimums, and put the rest on the debt marked 1.
4. Log each month in the 2027 section, and when a debt hits zero, move its minimum to the next debt.

## What this debt payoff planner leaves out

Our example has no fees, no promotional rates, no new purchases and no change in rates over time. The CFPB post does not say which method is better; it tells readers to choose the one that fits their situation.
