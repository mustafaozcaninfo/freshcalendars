## Pinpoint and Crossclimb nudges for Sunday, Nov. 8

- **Pinpoint #922, first nudge:** the five clues are not synonyms of each other; each one only works once it is attached to the same hidden word.
- **Pinpoint #922, second nudge:** the hidden word is a single syllable, and one of its senses is soil or earth.
- **Pinpoint #922, third nudge:** it is also the level of a building you enter from the street, and a verb for keeping a plane or a teen from leaving.
- **Crossclimb #922, the locked ends:** the top word is the opposite of right, and the bottom word is where ships dock; both are four letters.
- **Crossclimb #922, rungs 1 and 2:** one means skilled and nimble with the hands, the next means silly or foolish.
- **Crossclimb #922, rung 3:** to drift gently on a breeze, as a smell or a sound might.
- **Crossclimb #922, rungs 4 and 5:** a small hard growth on the skin, then a piece of a whole.

## How the Crossclimb #922 ladder moves, step by step

The ladder has seven words and six moves, and each move changes exactly one letter. Positions count from the left, 1 to 4. No word is shown in this table.

| Move | Letter position that changes | Kind of letter swapped in |
|---|---|---|
| Top row to rung 1 | 1 | consonant |
| Rung 1 to rung 2 | 2 | vowel |
| Rung 2 to rung 3 | 1 | consonant |
| Rung 3 to rung 4 | 3 | consonant |
| Rung 4 to rung 5 | 1 | consonant |
| Rung 5 to bottom row | 2 | vowel |

Position 1 changes three times, position 2 twice and position 3 once; position 4 stays put all the way down. Only the two vowel moves touch the second letter, so a stuck rung with a fixed last letter is a good place to try a different first letter.

## Starting points for Queens #922, Tango #762 and Zip #601

**Queens #922.** Look for a row or column where every open cell falls inside one colored region; that region's crown must sit there, which clears the region's other cells.

**Tango #762.** Begin with pairs of identical symbols side by side and fill the cells that would otherwise make three in a row. Then check each line's count of suns against moons.

**Zip #601.** Mark the cells on the board edge or in a corner, since they have the fewest neighbors to connect to. Work out from the numbered cells and join the stretches that line up.

## Sunday's LinkedIn answers, Nov. 8, 2026 (spoilers)

Stop here if you are still solving.

- **Pinpoint #922:** Words that come after “ground”.
- **Crossclimb #922:** Left, Deft, Daft, Waft, Wart, Part, Port. Deft, Daft, Waft, Wart and Part fill the middle in that order; Left and Port are the locked ends.

The ladder with the letter that changes at each step:

| Step | Word | Letter swapped in |
|---|---|---|
| Top row | Left | none |
| Rung 1 | Deft | D for L |
| Rung 2 | Daft | A for E |
| Rung 3 | Waft | W for D |
| Rung 4 | Wart | R for F |
| Rung 5 | Part | P for W |
| Bottom row | Port | O for A |

Left means the opposite of right, deft means skilled, daft means silly, waft means to drift, a wart is a small skin growth, part is a piece and port is a harbor.

Since the game locks Left and Port, the ladder can be built inward from both ends. From the top, Left, Deft and Daft reach Waft in three moves. From the bottom, Port, Part and Wart reach Waft in three moves too, so Waft sits exactly in the middle. Working this way, the top half uses a consonant, a vowel and a consonant, and the bottom half runs a vowel, a consonant and a consonant, read upward from Port.
