**The New York Times (NYT) Crossword for Tuesday, Nov. 24, 2026, was constructed by Karen Lurie and Peter Gwinn and edited by Will Shortz: 78 clues, 39 Across and 39 Down, in a 15-by-15 grid.** Ten clues carry a question mark, and four long entries share one idea. Each clue has a hint behind a tap, and the answers close the page.

## Question-mark clues in the Nov. 24 Crossword, and the trick in each

The puzzle data lists ten clues ending in "?", the usual flag for wordplay. Each trick is explained below without its answer, with the length in letters.

- **20-Across, "Adopt a humorless approach to vacuuming?" (12 letters).** The clue is two jokes glued together: a verb for what a vacuum does, then an adverb for the humorless manner. The answer runs both words together.
- **31-Across, "Try to lose weight quickly, but only when you feel like it?" (11 letters).** The same recipe with a twist: the first word has two meanings, one for dieting and one for speed, and the second is an adverb for doing something with no strict rules.
- **44-Across, "Departed after filling out the proper paperwork?" (11 letters).** Read "departed" as a past-tense verb and "proper" as an adverb. Two words, glued, with the same ending as the other long question-mark answers.
- **52-Across, "Underpay, but with a nice smile?" (12 letters).** A verb for shorting someone on a wage is joined to an adverb describing a pleasant manner.
- **40-Across, "Gut reaction?" (5 letters).** Take "gut" literally: the answer is a feeling in the stomach, or worry, named by a word that borrows from Italian-American speech.
- **48-Across, "Verb for you?" (3 letters).** The question mark asks for the verb form that pairs with the pronoun "you".
- **2-Down, "Base-less?" (4 letters).** The hyphen splits the word. A person without a base is gone from the military post without leave, and the answer is an initialism said as a word.
- **27-Down, "Way around town?" (6 letters).** "Way" means road and "around" is literal: the answer is a road that goes around the middle of town.
- **55-Down, "Head scratchers?" (4 letters).** Skip the fingers and look for whatever causes the itch.
- **57-Down, "Inner ear?" (3 letters).** The ear belongs to a cornstalk, and the answer is its core.

## How the Nov. 24 grid is built

The grid is 15 columns by 15 rows, 225 squares in all: 36 black and 189 holding a letter, a black share of 16.0 percent. The black pattern is unchanged by a half-turn. Monday's grid, on our [Nov. 23 Crossword page](/news/nyt-crossword-answers-november-23-2026/), had 38 black squares and 74 clues, so Tuesday has two black squares fewer and four more clues.

Four-letter entries lead at 30, and 16 of them run Across. Monday was the other way round, with three-letter entries on top at 21. The four longest entries all run Across and all carry a question mark. The two 12-letter entries are 20-Across, in row 4 from column 1 to 12, and 52-Across, in row 12 from column 4 to 15. The two 11-letter entries are 31-Across, in row 6 from column 5 to 15, and 44-Across, in row 10 from column 1 to 11. Rows 6 and 10 each hold a single black square, while rows 5 and 11 hold five apiece, the most of any row. The longest Down entries are four 8-letter answers: 5-Down and 8-Down start in row 1, and 39-Down and 40-Down start in row 8.

The theme shows in those four long clues. Each pairs a pun on two ideas with one entry made of two words run together, and the last two letters match in all four, so once one is filled the other three are easier to spot. Their crossings are short (three to eight letters), which makes the first one a good anchor.
