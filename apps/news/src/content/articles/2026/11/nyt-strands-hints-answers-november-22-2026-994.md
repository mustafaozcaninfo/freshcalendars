**Strands #994 from The New York Times (NYT) is the Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026 puzzle, and its clue is "Anything you can do ..."** Tracy Bennett is credited as both constructor and editor in the puzzle data. The board holds a 7-letter spangram and five theme words, and the rules are on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). Hints run first, then start cells and a map of the grid, and the answers wait behind a spoiler warning.

The clue is the first half of a boast, and the words it leads to are all ways of coming out ahead.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: Two sides pushing against each other for the same prize.
- Hint 2: It has 7 letters, begins with R and ends with Y, and R shows up twice.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 6, column 1, and it starts by stepping right.

## Theme word hints

Five words fit the clue, listed from shortest to longest:

- **7 letters, starts with S, three S's in all:** to go beyond a mark someone else set.
- **7 letters, starts with E, ends with E:** to put another in the shade, as the moon does to the sun.
- **8 letters, starts with O, ends with E:** to be more brilliant than the rest of the room.
- **9 letters, starts with T, ends with D:** to rise above a limit or a category.
- **10 letters, starts with O, ends with W:** to make others seem small by standing next to them.

The two 7-letter words differ in their letters: one has three S's, the other two E's.

## Start-cell hints

Rows count from the top and columns from the left; the first step is the direction from a word's first letter to its second. No word is named here.

- **7 letters, start cell row 5, column 1:** the first step goes straight up.
- **7 letters, start cell row 7, column 5:** the first step goes diagonally down and to the left.
- **8 letters, start cell row 3, column 4:** the first step goes straight down.
- **9 letters, start cell row 6, column 3:** the first step goes straight right.
- **10 letters, start cell row 2, column 2:** the first step goes straight up.

## How the Nov. 22 board divides into regions

The spangram has 6 steps and none is diagonal: from row 6, column 1 it moves right one tile, climbs to row 5, column 2, then runs right along row 5 to column 6. It never touches rows 1 to 4 or rows 7 and 8. Row 5 holds 5 spangram tiles, row 6 holds 2, and the only column with more than one spangram tile is column 2.

That low position cuts the grid into a top and a bottom. Rows 1 to 4 have 24 tiles and no spangram: the 8-letter word takes 8, the 10-letter word takes 10 and the 7-letter word that starts in row 5 reaches up with its other 6. The bottom three rows have 18 tiles: 2 spangram, 9 for the 9-letter word and 7 for the 7-letter word that starts in row 7. Rows 7 and 8 are all theme letters from those last two words.

Split the board into four blocks of 12 tiles (rows 1 to 4 or 5 to 8, columns 1 to 3 or 4 to 6) and the two top blocks are full of theme letters. The top-left block is the most crowded, since three words meet there with 6, 3 and 3 tiles. The top-right block has only two words, with 7 and 5. At the bottom, the spangram takes 4 tiles of the left block and 3 of the right.

Across all six words there are 42 steps, and 41 of them are straight. The single diagonal is the first step of the 7-letter word that starts in row 7, which is why that start cell is the one to treat with care.

## Solving tips

Begin in the top four rows, where no spangram tile can interfere: three words fill them completely. Row 5, column 1 is the one tile on that row that is not part of the spangram, and it is the start of a word that heads upward.
