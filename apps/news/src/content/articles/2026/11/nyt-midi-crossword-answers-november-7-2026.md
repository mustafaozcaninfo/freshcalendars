**The New York Times (NYT) Midi Crossword for Saturday, Nov. 7, 2026, is a nine-by-nine grid by John Kugelman with 29 clues, and five of them are two clues in one.** The title is "Leave Me Out of It." Each clue below has a hint that stays shut until you open it, and the answers wait in a marked spoiler section at the end.

## How "Leave Me Out of It" works in this Midi

The puzzle data titles the grid "Leave Me Out of It," and the title is an instruction. Five clues show two definitions joined by a slash. The first definition fits the entry that goes in the grid. The second fits the same entry once the letters M and E, the two letters of "me," have been removed. Four of the entries lose a single letter, either an M or an E. One loses both letters twice over, which cuts it by four.

That gives you a way to check a doubtful guess: take out the letters and see whether the shorter word fits the second clue. If it does, the long one belongs in the grid.

Notice the layout: 14-Across, 16-Down and 17-Down all begin on row 5, the middle row of the grid, so that one row seeds three of the five themed entries. The two nine-letter Down and Across entries, 5-Down and 14-Across, are the longest in the theme.

The least literal clue in the grid is 11-Across, "Fights to beat the rap, in brief?" Here "rap" is the music genre and not a criminal charge, and "beat" means to win a contest. The question mark and "in brief" warn that the answer starts with a shortened two-letter title for someone holding a microphone. What follows is a plural verb. The answer has nine letters and spans the whole third row from edge to edge.
