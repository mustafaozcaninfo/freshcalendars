**Connections: Sports Edition #791 is the Monday, Nov. 23, 2026 puzzle from The Athletic and New York Times (NYT) Games.** Two hints sit behind a tap for each color, and the answers wait under the spoiler heading.

Names do the heavy lifting on this board, and several of them have a second life as plain words. The rules are on our [Connections page](/news/topic/nyt-connections/#how-it-works); on Monday the skill is asking what else a surname could mean. Sunday's board is on [Sports Edition #790](/news/nyt-connections-sports-edition-hints-answers-november-22-2026-790/), and the main game has its own page, [Connections for Nov. 23](/news/nyt-connections-hints-answers-november-23-2026-1261/).

## Monday's hints for #791

Read each tile as a name first and as a word second.

- **Hint 1 (yellow):** All four tiles can finish the same sentence about a team nickname.
- **Hint 2 (yellow):** Two of the four are also names of US states.
- **Hint 1 (green):** Each tile goes in front of the name of a trophy or tournament.
- **Hint 2 (green):** Two of the four are also last names you could meet anywhere.
- **Hint 1 (blue):** Each tile is a surname, and the four people play for one national team.
- **Hint 2 (blue):** One of the four also means a large pile.
- **Hint 1 (purple):** A single word is missing from the same spot on every tile.
- **Hint 2 (purple):** One tile is three words long, and the rest are one word each.

## Which Monday tiles could fit somewhere else

Stanley and Grey are the first snag, because both double as surnames and a solver can chase the person instead of the object. Heaps is another: it reads as a quantity, not a name, so it tends to get shelved as a stray word. Keep it in play.

Injury is the odd one out. It sounds like a word from a sports desk, yet it will not sort with anything that has a mascot or a trophy. If a tile looks like it belongs to every group and none, test whether a word is missing around it. A [printable weekly calendar](/printable-weekly-calendar.html) can hold your streak.

## Monday's answers, #791, Nov. 23, 2026 (spoilers)

Stop here if you still want to solve it. Nerd's Chalk and Beebom print the same four groups and sixteen entries. The group names below are as those sites print them, and the blue title's team is the United States Women's National Team (USWNT).

- **Yellow, Wildcats:** Northwestern, Villanova, Kentucky, Arizona
- **Green, Sports "Cups":** Stanley, FA, Ryder, Grey
- **Blue, Active USWNT Players:** Swanson, Heaps, Rodman, Davidson
- **Purple, _____ Report:** Bleacher, Injury, Scouting, Last Two Minute

## Who each entry is

- **Northwestern, Villanova, Kentucky, Arizona:** each fits the group title "Wildcats".
- **Stanley, FA, Ryder, Grey:** each fits the group title Sports "Cups", as a word placed before Cup.
- **Swanson, Heaps, Rodman, Davidson:** surnames of active USWNT players, per the published group title. We name no first names or clubs, because no league or team page we checked confirms them.
- **Bleacher, Injury, Scouting, Last Two Minute:** each completes the phrase with the word Report after it.

## How the fill-in-the-blank and the Cups label work

The purple title has a gap at the front, so the tiles are the first halves of four phrases. Add Report to each and the group assembles. No sport has to be named. Last Two Minute is the tile that looks least like its neighbors, since it runs three words while Bleacher, Injury and Scouting are one word each, yet the blank in the title still sits right in front of Report for all four of them. The green label is a clue in the same spirit: the quotation marks around Cups show that each tile is what comes before the word Cup.

| Tile | Reads as | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Injury | a sports-news word | the Report phrases |
| Heaps | a large amount | the USWNT players' surnames |
| Stanley | a surname | the Cups |
| Grey | a color or a surname | the Cups |
