The Beaver Moon is full on Tuesday, Nov. 24, 2026, at 9:53 a.m. EST, according to the U.S. Naval Observatory (USNO), and by that hour it has already set across the eastern United States. That is why the nights to look are the two evenings around the full moment: sunset on Monday, Nov. 23 and on Tuesday, Nov. 24. NASA calls Nov. 24 a "Full supermoon; first in a series of three consecutive supermoons" and advises, "Look east around sunset to watch it rise." The full moon falls two days before Thanksgiving on Thursday, Nov. 26.

## When the Beaver Moon is full in each U.S. time zone

The Moon reaches its full phase at one instant worldwide, 14:53 Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). On the clock that lands at different morning hours across the country, per the U.S. Naval Observatory.

## Why the Beaver Moon has already set in the East by 9:53 a.m.

A full moon rises near sunset and sets near sunrise, so by mid-morning it is below the horizon in the East. At the 9:53 a.m. EST full moment the Moon has set in New York (moonset 7:05 a.m.), Chicago (7:07 a.m. CST) and Denver (7:13 a.m. MST). In Los Angeles it sets at 6:49 a.m. PST, four minutes before the 6:53 a.m. full moment. Farther west the Moon is still up: Seattle's moonset is 8:00 a.m. PST, Honolulu's 7:02 a.m. HST and Anchorage's 11:04 a.m. AKST.

Because the full instant comes in daylight for most of the country, the Moon looks just as full the evening before and the evening after. NASA's skywatching guide notes that a full moon "will appear full for about three days around this time."

## Why the Moon looks bigger near the horizon

A full moon low in the sky can look large, as in the photo at the top of this story, but NASA says the size is a trick of perception. "The Moon's seeming bigness is an actual illusion, rather than an effect of our atmosphere or some other physics," NASA writes. Photograph the Moon at the horizon and again high overhead with the same zoom and, NASA notes, "the Moon is the same width, side to side, in both photos." That photo shows the Dec. 3, 2017 supermoon rising over Washington.

## Is the Beaver Moon a supermoon?

NASA lists Nov. 24 as a "Full supermoon" and the first of three consecutive supermoons. A supermoon, NASA says, "occurs when a full Moon coincides with the Moon's closest approach to Earth in its elliptical orbit, a point known as perigee," and it can look "up to 14 percent bigger and 30 percent brighter than the faintest Moon of the year." Space.com reports that the Moon will be 224,170 miles (360,768 kilometers) from Earth on Nov. 24, and closer still, 221,667 miles (356,740 kilometers), at the Dec. 24 full moon in Universal Time. The calendar site timeanddate marks both the Nov. 24 and Dec. 24 (UTC) full moons as a "Super Full Moon."

## Where the Beaver Moon name comes from

The Old Farmer's Almanac ties November's full moon to the season: "This is the time of year when beavers begin to take shelter in their lodges." The almanac also lists other November names drawn from Native American traditions, including the Digging or Scratching Moon (Tlingit), the Deer Rutting Moon (Dakota and Lakota), the Whitefish Moon (Algonquin), the Frost Moon (Cree and Assiniboine) and the Freezing Moon (Anishinaabe).

The next full moon is the Cold Moon on Wednesday, Dec. 23, 2026, at 8:28 p.m. EST, per the U.S. Naval Observatory.
