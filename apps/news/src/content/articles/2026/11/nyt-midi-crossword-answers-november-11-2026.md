**The New York Times (NYT) Midi Crossword for Wednesday, Nov. 11, 2026, is titled "Informed Consumer" and has 28 clues on a nine-by-nine grid, built by Nate Cardin.** Ian Livengood is the editor. A hint for each clue stays closed until you tap it, and the answers sit in a marked section at the end.

## How the "Informed Consumer" title and the 21-Across revealer fit

A consumer takes things in, and the puzzle plays on two senses of that. The 21-Across clue reads "One's sources of information and entertainment, collectively ... or a hint to 2- and 7-Down?" The answer has 9 letters and fills row 7, the only row of the grid with no black square. It is a two-word phrase of 5 and 4 letters.

Each half of that phrase labels one of the Down entries. 2-Down, "Online stream that may be doomscrolled," is a source of information, so it matches the first word. 7-Down, "Meal enjoyed in a den, say," is something you eat, so it matches the second word, and the title's "consumer" covers both. Each of those Down entries has 8 letters, sits in its own side column (the second and the eighth) and ends in row 8, so both run through row 7 and cross the revealer one square from its edges, in its 2nd and 8th squares.

That crossing is the practical use of the hint. Solve either Down entry and you have a letter of the revealer in hand; solve the revealer from its first half and you can test your guess for 2-Down against it.

## The anagram clue, explained without its answer

The least literal clue is 20-Across, with 4 letters, which prints a first name in capitals (NATE) and calls it an anagram. The full clue is "Sicilian volcano that anagrams to the name NATE." It asks for a place name, but the way in is the wordplay: the clue hands you N, A, T and E and tells you to rearrange them. The name in the clue is also the constructor's first name. It sits in row 6 on the right, where 20-Down, 6-Down, 7-Down and 17-Down each cross one of its squares, so a couple of those settle the order quickly.
