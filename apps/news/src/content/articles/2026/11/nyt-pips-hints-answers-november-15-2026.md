**Sunday's Pips from The New York Times (NYT), Nov. 15, 2026, ends the week with a Hard board whose two bottom corners must each fill with a single value.** Ian Livengood made Easy; Rodolfo Kurchan made Medium and Hard. Those corners are equal regions, explained on [our Pips page](/news/topic/nyt-pips/#how-it-works) along with the sum and comparison signs. Placements in the answer section follow the Times' data.

## Where each Pips board starts on Nov. 15

R6C6 is row 6, column 6, the bottom-right cell of Hard.

**Easy.** R1C2 and R3C1 must both be 5s, and each touches a single cell: R1C3 and R4C1. The 5-4 and the 5-0 are the two dominoes with a 5. R4C1 belongs to a pair that totals less than 3, so it can't take the 4. The 5-0 goes to R3C1 and R4C1, and the 5-4 to R1C2 and R1C3.

**Medium.** R2C3 and R2C4 must match, and each has just two neighbors in the middle row. If they don't share a domino, R2C4 pairs with R2C5, which must be 0 and so brings the 0-5, putting a 5 in R2C4. R2C3 would then need a 5 from the 5-1, with the 1 in R2C2, which must be more than 2. So R2C3 and R2C4 share a domino inside an equal region: the 2-2, the only double.

**Hard.** R6C1 touches only R5C1 and R6C2, and R6C6 only R5C6 and R6C5, all inside the two equal squares. Each corner domino therefore sits inside its square and must be a double. The set has two, 6-6 and 0-0, so one square is all 6s and the other all 0s.

## Sunday's Pips hints, Nov. 15, 2026

**Easy (five dominoes: 0-3, 5-4, 5-0, 4-3, 0-6)**

- Hint 1: R2C4 and R3C4 add up to 9. The only 6 on the board is on the 0-6.
- Hint 2: R3C3 and R4C3 add up to 3. With no 1 or 2 in the set, that is a 0 and a 3.

**Medium (seven dominoes: 3-6, 5-1, 4-1, 1-2, 2-3, 0-5, 2-2)**

- Hint 1: R2C5 must be 0, and the only 0 is on the 0-5.
- Hint 2: R1C1, R2C1 and R3C1 add up to 10 down the left edge. The only 6 is on the 3-6.

**Hard (fifteen dominoes on a six-row board)**

- Hint 1: R1C2, R2C2 and R3C2 add up to 13, and so do R1C6, R2C5 and R2C6.
- Hint 2: R3C6 and R6C4 must each be more than 4. Seven halves show a 6 and three show a 5.

## Reading the boards before you check

Hard has nine single-cell sums, and the left column is almost all fixed: R1C1, R2C1, R3C1 and R4C1 must be 1, 5, 4 and 4. Once you know which square holds the 0s, count what remains for R1C5, which also needs a 0. On Medium, R3C5 and R3C6 must add up to 5.
