**The New York Times (NYT) Crossword for Saturday, Nov. 14, 2026, was constructed by Byron Walden and edited by Will Shortz: 66 clues, 33 Across and 33 Down, in a 15-by-15 grid.** Saturday grids carry no theme, and this one asks for ten wordplay readings. A hint for every clue sits behind a tap, and the answers close the page.

## Ten question-mark clues and the trick in each

Each of these clues is a normal phrase pushed toward a second meaning. No note below names an answer.

- **1-Across, "Leaves with a pungent taste in one's mouth?"** (4 letters). The leaves are a crop, and the taste comes from keeping them in the cheek. The answer is a slang noun for a wad.
- **26-Across, "Light refreshments?"** (8 letters). "Light" describes a color, not a calorie count. The refreshments are drinks, in the plural.
- **36-Across, "Break dancing?"** (5 letters). This is not street dance. A "break" is an interruption, and the dancing is what gets interrupted.
- **53-Across, "Sow what?"** (4 letters). Read "sow" as the verb and "what" as the object. The answer is the thing a gardener scatters.
- **7-Down, "Bad tipper?"** (4 letters). Forget restaurants. The tip is a piece of inside information, and the bad tipper gives you away.
- **9-Down, "Bunch of dubious characters?"** (14 letters). The characters are written symbols, not people, and "dubious" means hard to read.
- **11-Down, "Creature features?"** (10 letters). "Features" are billed items on a show, and the creatures are the performers. The answer is two words run together.
- **13-Down, "Finishes a shift?"** (4 letters). The shift is a dress, and finishing it means sewing its lower edge. The verb is in the third person.
- **15-Down, "Country house?"** (14 letters). The house is an office building, and the "country" is a whole group of them in one world body. The answer begins with a two-letter abbreviation.
- **45-Down, "Rump of Parliament?"** (4 letters). The rump is a body part, and "Parliament" signals British usage for the word.

Two more entries need no question mark to be tricky. 28-Across and 30-Across are a pair, and each clue points at the other.

## How Saturday's grid compares with Friday's

Friday's Crossword, by Mike Graczyk, had 70 entries and 33 black squares. Saturday's has 66 entries and 26, so black squares fall from 14.7 percent of the grid to 11.6 percent, and letter squares rise from 192 to 199. Entry count drops by four, with Across down by one (34 to 33) and Down down by three (36 to 33).

Friday's longest entries were all Across and stacked in threes. Saturday's longest run top to bottom instead, and each of the two crosses most of the grid. The black pattern is unchanged by a half turn on both days.
