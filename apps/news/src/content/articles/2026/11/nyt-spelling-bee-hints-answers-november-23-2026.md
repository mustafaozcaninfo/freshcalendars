**Monday, Nov. 23, 2026, brings a Spelling Bee from The New York Times (NYT) with R in the middle and A, J, L, M, O and Y around it.** The list has 35 words worth 162 points, and one of the seven letters is far scarcer than the rest. Genius sits at 113 points.

Our [Spelling Bee page](/news/topic/nyt-spelling-bee/#how-it-works) covers the scoring, so what follows stays with Monday's board: where the points are and which letters the list leaves out.

## The pangram clues

- **The pangram** (7 letters): it starts with M and is worth 14 points.
- **The ending:** it finishes with Y, a letter that closes 14 of the 35 answers.
- **The company:** three other 7-letter answers also start with M.

## Which letters cannot appear on Monday?

Nineteen letters are off the board: B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, K, N, P, Q, S, T, U, V, W, X and Z. With no E, I or U available, the vowel work falls to A and O, with Y finishing many words.

R is in every answer. The outer letters do far less evenly:

| Outer letter | Answers using it | Start with it | End with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 30 | 10 | 2 |
| J | 5 | 0 | 0 |
| L | 15 | 1 | 6 |

- **A and O carry the list:** A is in 30 answers and O in 26, against 5 for J.
- **Y only closes words:** it never opens one and ends 14 of the 18 that hold it.
- **R itself:** 9 answers begin with R and 7 end with it.

## Why is there only one pangram?

A pangram needs J, and J is the problem letter. It is in 5 answers, none of which starts or ends with it, and four of those five have neither L nor Y.

Counting distinct letters per answer shows how thin the top is: 1 answer holds all seven, 3 hold six, 11 hold five, 12 hold four and 8 hold three. The three answers with six distinct letters each lack only J, so they are the nearest misses without being pangrams.

## Which words hold the points?

Four-letter words score 1 point, longer words 1 point per letter, and the pangram adds 7. The running total adds the rows from the top.

- **Where Genius falls:** the running total is 104 after the six-letter words, so 113 needs 9 more points from the seven-letter block.
- **Five-letter words dominate:** the 14 of them hold 70 points, 43.2% of the 162.
- **Six letters are scarce:** only 4 answers have six letters, fewer than any other length.

## The hardest words on Monday's list

- **Longest answers:** two of 8 letters and five of 7 (one of those is the pangram).
- **Rare starts:** AJ, AL and LO begin one answer each, while MA begins 8 and RO begins 7.
- **Doubled letters:** 14 answers repeat a letter back to back, with LL in 6, RR in 4, OO in 3 and MM in 1.
- **Letter J:** only 5 answers use it, and 4 of those are four, five or eight letters long.
- **Endings:** 14 answers end in Y, 7 in R, 6 in L, 5 in M, 2 in A and 1 in O.
- **Starts:** 13 answers begin with M, 10 with A, 9 with R, 2 with O and 1 with L.

## The gap from four-letter words to Genius

The 10 four-letter answers give 10 points, under a tenth of the 113 needed. That leaves 103 points to find among the 25 answers of five letters or more, which hold 152 in all, 14 of them from the pangram. Without the pangram the list is worth 148 points, 35 above the bar.
