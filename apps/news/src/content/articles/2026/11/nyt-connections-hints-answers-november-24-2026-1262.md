**Connections #1262 from The New York Times (NYT) is the Tuesday, Nov. 24, 2026 puzzle, edited by Wyna Liu.** Sixteen tiles range from four letters to sixteen, and seven of them are phrases. With that many multiword tiles, a look at the rules on our [Connections page](/news/topic/nyt-connections/#how-it-works) helps. Two hints per color come first, then the answers behind a spoiler line.

## Today's 16 words

Playground, Paper Fan, Big Box Store, Crater, Accordion, Mountain, Trombone, Link, Tank, Cupcake Liner, Amino Acid, Microscope, Fireplace Bellows, Nosedive, PowerPoint Deck, Bottom Out

## Hints for each group

**Yellow**

- Hint 1: Picture a stock chart in a very bad week; each tile describes how that line moves.
- Hint 2: The entries have four, six, eight and nine letters.

**Green**

- Hint 1: Look at the material, not the use: the surface of each tile is creased back and forth in even ridges.
- Hint 2: The entries have eight, nine, twelve and sixteen letters.

**Blue**

- Hint 1: Each tile has a part, page or piece that goes by one shared name, and the name is also something you do on a slippery floor.
- Hint 2: The entries have eight, ten, ten and fourteen letters.

**Purple**

- Hint 1: Each tile can be one piece of a long connected series, and the series carries the same name for all four.
- Hint 2: The entries have four, eight, nine and eleven letters.

## Solving tips

Two of these tiles are instruments, and that is the bait. Treat them as separate cases and ask what each one physically has, not what family it belongs to. A tile of three words is a good place to start, because its last word often names the part you are looking for.

## Decoys in puzzle #1262: which tiles could join another group

Each overlap below can be tested by asking what the tile has or does.

| Tiles | Why they tempt | Test that sorts them | Where they sit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accordion, Trombone | Both are instruments played by moving a part | The accordion's folded bellows match a paper fan, while the trombone's slide matches a microscope | Green, Blue |
| Crater, Mountain | Both are landforms, one a hollow and one a peak | Crater is used as a verb meaning to collapse, and a mountain forms a chain of peaks | Yellow, Purple |
| Playground, Big Box Store, Mountain, Crater | All four are places you can visit | Visiting is no rule: the playground has a slide, the store belongs to a retail chain, the mountain to a range and the crater to a fall | Blue, Purple, Purple, Yellow |
| Amino Acid, Microscope | Both belong in a science lab | The microscope holds glass slides, and amino acids link into a chain of molecules | Purple, Blue |

Link is the tile with the plainest fit: a link is a part of a chain, and nothing else on the board shares that sense.

## What kind of rule is each group?

The four groups use four different kinds of rule, so one test does not sort the whole board.

| Color | Rule type | How to test a tile |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow | Meaning: tiles that describe the same action | Swap each into a sentence about a sudden fall |
| Green | Physical feature: one shared look | Picture the object and check for creased ridges |
| Blue | Hidden part: a word for something each tile contains | Ask what each one has that is also called a slide |
| Purple | Shared result: tiles that join into one series | Ask what each tile forms when you repeat it |
