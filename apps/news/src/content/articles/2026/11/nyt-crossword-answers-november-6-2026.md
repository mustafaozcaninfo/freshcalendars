## The seven question-mark clues in Friday's grid

Seven clues in the data end in a question mark. None of the explanations below names an answer.

- **1-Across, "Dating around?"** Think of "dating" as fixing a year to an old object, and read "around" as "approximately." The answer is a Latin word used before a year.
- **16-Across, "Stock exchange?"** "Stock" here is not a share of a company. It is what a farmer keeps in a field, and the "exchange" is the noise it makes. Four letters.
- **25-Across, "Theater directors?"** Nobody here calls "action." The directors point audience members along an aisle, and the answer is a plural job title.
- **53-Across, "Well-funded group?"** Treat "well" as a hole in the ground rather than a way of being healthy. The group is a bloc of countries, and the answer is a four-letter acronym.
- **11-Down, "Help line?"** The line is a sentence you say aloud, not a phone number. It is how someone asks for a favor, and the answer runs four words together to fill 10 squares.
- **30-Down, "Greenish black rock?"** Each adjective carries a double meaning: the first is about the environment, the second about a mineral that burns. The answer is two words run together.
- **47-Down, "Speak to a higher-up?"** The boss in question is not at the office. Ask yourself whom people address in a quiet moment, and the answer is a four-letter verb.
