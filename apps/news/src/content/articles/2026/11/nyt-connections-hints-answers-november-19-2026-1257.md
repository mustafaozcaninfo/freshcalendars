## Why each red herring nearly fits

Underground is the strongest lure. It describes where a hole sits, so it pulls toward Tunnel, Burrow and Dig, yet it is a word that starts and ends with the same three letters, with the same three letters opening and closing it.

Plug is the second trap. You plug a hole, so it can sit beside Bore and Dig, and a plug can be a stopper like Cork. Here it belongs with Cord, Housing and Switch on an appliance.

Cork and Cord share their first three letters, so a quick eye pairs them. One is a tree material and the other is an appliance part.

Housing and Burrow can both be read as a place to live, which is why Housing can drift toward the hole group. Switch is also a thin branch, which lets it lean toward the tree materials, and Wood could be mistaken for an appliance case.

| Look-alike entries | Why they tempt | Where they really sit |
|---|---|---|
| Underground, Tunnel, Burrow | All go below the surface | Same-letters, Hole, Hole |
| Plug, Bore, Dig | A plug fills a hole | Appliance, Hole, Hole |
| Plug, Cork | Both are stoppers | Appliance, Trees |
| Cork, Cord | Same first three letters | Trees, Appliance |
| Housing, Burrow | Both are a kind of shelter | Appliance, Hole |
| Switch, Wood, Sap | Switch is also a twig | Appliance, Trees, Trees |
