## Pinpoint and Crossclimb hints for Thursday, Nov. 26

- **Pinpoint #940, first hint:** the category describes a condition that things can be in, not a type of thing.
- **Pinpoint #940, second hint:** the clues run from household items to a personal moment, so the link is about a state, not a room or a shop.
- **Pinpoint #940, third hint:** the last clue is a dish, and the note in brackets tells you how it is made.
- **Crossclimb #940, the ladder:** seven words of four letters, joined by six one-letter swaps.
- **Crossclimb #940, the locked ends:** the top and bottom rows share one clue, which asks for a two-word phrase and warns that the first word may be at the bottom.
- **Crossclimb #940, the middle clues:** one rung is a pastime before bed, one is a bundle of printer paper, one is a pitfall, one is a streetcar and one completes a saying about teamwork.

## What to try with the Pinpoint #940 clues

Three of the five clues are ordinary objects or animals-as-toys, and the fourth is about you, not an object. That mix rules out any category built on materials, rooms or sizes. Think about the verb you would use for what is done to each item, or the adjective that results. The fifth clue is the tiebreaker: it names something you eat, and the bracket describes what is inside it. If your guess cannot fit that dish, it is not the category. This is our reading of the clues, not a LinkedIn rule.

## Which Crossclimb #940 letters move on each rung

Six swaps, positions counted from the left, 1 to 4. No word of the ladder appears here.

| Position | Times it changes |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 1 |
| 4 | 2 |

The swaps run through positions 2, 4, 1, 2, 4, 3. Position 1 changes only once, partway down, so the first four rungs share a letter that never leaves the top half of the ladder. Positions 2 and 4 carry two moves each, which is where a clue guess is most likely to land on a neighbor that differs by one of those two letters. The shared top-and-bottom clue says the first word may sit at the bottom, so when you fill the ends, test both orders against the first and last swaps.

## Starting points for Queens #940, Tango #780, Zip #619 and Mini Sudoku #472

**Queens #940.** Find the smallest color region and list its cells; any queen in it blocks the squares that touch it.

**Tango #780.** Three in a row is not allowed, so scan each line for two matching symbols side by side and fill both neighbors with the opposite one.

**Zip #619.** Mark the dead-end squares first; the path must start or end in one of them.

**Mini Sudoku #472.** Count how often each digit already appears and begin with the one that is most common, since it has the fewest places left.
