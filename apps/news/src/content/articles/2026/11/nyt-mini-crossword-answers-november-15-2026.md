**Sam Ezersky made the New York Times (NYT) Mini Crossword for Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026: a 5-by-5 grid with 10 clues and just one black square.** Each hint below opens on a tap, and the filled grid sits in the marked spoiler section at the end.

## Hints for every clue

**Across**

- **1-Across** (4 letters), *Fat-sounding freshwater fish*: a river and lake fish whose name sounds like a word for plump; it starts with C, ends with B, and its four letters begin the first four Down answers.
- **5-Across** (5 letters), *"What a headache!"*: a Yiddish cry of dismay, written as two words run together; it starts with O, ends with the letter that begins 6-Down, and has the letter Y twice.
- **7-Across** (5 letters), *Jules who wrote "Around the World in 80 Days"*: the French novelist's surname; it starts with V and ends with E.
- **8-Across** (5 letters), *Put into effect, as a law*: a verb, the way a legislature makes a bill official; it starts with E and ends with T.
- **9-Across** (5 letters), *Give a friendly greeting*: a short phrase of two words run together; it starts with S and ends with I.

**Down**

- **1-Down** (5 letters), *Sheltered inlets*: small bays where boats wait out weather, in the plural; it starts with C, the letter that opens 1-Across, and ends with S.
- **2-Down** (5 letters), *African carnivore known for its laugh*: a spotted scavenger and hunter of the savanna; it starts with H, the second letter of 1-Across, and ends with A.
- **3-Down** (5 letters), *Bit of light that sunscreen protects against, for short*: a two-part term entered as one word; it starts with U, the third letter of 1-Across, and ends with Y.
- **4-Down** (5 letters), *Park seating*: a long seat for several people; it starts with B, the last letter of 1-Across, and ends with H.
- **6-Down** (4 letters), *Snowy mountain version of Bigfoot*: a creature of Himalayan legend; it starts with Y, the last letter of 5-Across, and ends with I.

## How Sunday's grid plays

The least literal clue is 3-Down. Nothing in it is a pun, but "for short" changes what you are looking for: the answer does not spell out a whole word for the light. Its first piece is an abbreviation for a kind of light, followed by a second word for what a beam of light is called, and the two are pressed together in the grid. Read "bit of light" as the second word and "that sunscreen protects against" as the first.

The grid itself is built tightly. Counted from the saved data:

| Count | Sun., Nov. 15 |
|---|---|
| Grid size | 5 by 5 |
| Black squares | 1 (top right corner) |
| White squares | 24 |
| Clues | 10 (5 Across, 5 Down) |
| Four-letter entries | 2 (1-Across, 6-Down) |
| Five-letter entries | 8 |
| Entries crossing five others | 8 |
| Entries crossing four others | 2 |

The single black square cuts one letter from the top row and from the right-hand column, which is why 1-Across and 6-Down run four squares while the other eight entries run five. Because no other square is blocked, all 24 white squares belong to one Across and one Down entry, and no entry can be solved alone: 1-Across touches 1-Down through 4-Down, and 6-Down touches the rows from 5-Across to 9-Across.

Where to begin: eight entries tie at five crossings, the four lower rows and the first four Down answers. The clue you can answer fastest among those is the best opener, since each letter you place lands in five other entries.
