**Strands #990 from The New York Times (NYT) is the puzzle for Wednesday, Nov. 18, 2026, and its theme is musical.** The puzzle data credits Christina Iverson as constructor and Tracy Bennett as editor. The board hides a 9-letter spangram and six theme words. Today's spangram runs through the middle rows only, and what a spangram has to touch is explained on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). Hints come first; answers wait in a marked section at the end.

Today's clue reads "You're gonna blow it!" Every theme word answers it in the same way.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: Think of a musical group you would hear at a parade or a football game, with players holding shiny horns.
- Hint 2: It has 9 letters, starting with B and ending with D.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is in row 4, column 2, one step in from the left edge.

## Theme word hints

The clue plays on what players do with a mouthpiece. Six instruments make up the theme, listed here from shortest to longest:

- **4 letters, starts with T:** a big, coiled horn with a very deep voice.
- **5 letters, starts with B:** a small valveless horn tied to military calls and wake-up music.
- **6 letters, starts with C:** a compact valved horn with a mellow sound.
- **7 letters, starts with T:** the high, bright horn that leads a fanfare.
- **8 letters, starts with T:** the horn with a slide in place of valves.
- **9 letters, starts with E:** a mid-voiced valved horn, the longest theme word and the only one that begins with E.

## Where the spangram crosses Wednesday's board

Rows count from the top and columns from the left. The spangram starts in row 4, column 2, steps up and left to row 3, column 1 (the left edge), then zigzags east, switching between row 3 and row 4 at almost every tile. It ends in row 4, column 6, on the right edge. Of its 9 tiles, 5 sit in row 4 and 4 in row 3. It never touches the top or the bottom edge.

That path cuts the board in two. Rows 1 and 2 are 12 tiles shared by two theme words, 6 and 8 letters long, which also dip into row 3. Rows 5 and 6 are another 12 tiles shared by two words, of 4 and 9 letters, and rows 7 and 8 are a third block of 12 shared by words of 5 and 7 letters. The densest corner is the lower left, columns 1 to 3 of rows 5 to 8: its 12 tiles belong to four different theme words, of 4, 5, 7 and 9 letters. The lower right has only two words in the same area. The six theme words use 39 letters, and with the 9-letter spangram they fill all 48 tiles.

## Solving tips

Start in the lower left, where the shortest word hugs the left edge, and use the spangram's row 3 and row 4 zigzag as a divider: nothing above row 3 or below row 4 belongs to it. A hint, earned by finding three ordinary words, lights up one theme word.
