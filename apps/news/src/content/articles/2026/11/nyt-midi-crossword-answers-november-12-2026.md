**The New York Times (NYT) Midi Crossword for Thursday, Nov. 12, 2026, is called "Put on a Happy Face" and has 34 clues on an 11-by-11 grid, built by Zhou Zhang and Kevin Curry.** Ian Livengood edited it. Each clue has a hint behind a tap, and the answers wait in a marked section at the end.

## How the "Put on a Happy Face" theme works

The name reads like an instruction, and the grid follows it. Look at the pattern of black squares rather than the letters. Row 4 has two black squares with white on every side, at columns 4 and 8. Row 7 has black squares at the same two columns, and row 8 has three in a row between them, so the lower pair and the row beneath it curve like a mouth. The corners are cut in steps: three black squares at each end of the top row, two in the next, one in the third, and the same in reverse at the bottom.

Four clues talk about the puzzle itself. 14-Across asks for an icon "depicted in this puzzle," 24-Across is "an apt instruction for this puzzle," 13-Down asks what "Put on a Happy Face" is, for example, and 22-Down asks what the two isolated black squares represent. None of the four needs outside knowledge once you see the shape; the grid is the clue.

## The 22-Down clue, explained without its answer

The least literal clue is 22-Down, with 4 letters: "What the two isolated black squares in this puzzle represent." It defines nothing in the usual way. It points at the diagram and asks you to read a picture, and the answer is a plural noun for something a face has two of. It runs down column 4 from row 8 and crosses 21-, 24-, 28- and 29-Across, so a couple of those Across entries give you most of it. Solving it also confirms the face reading of the grid, which helps with the other three self-referring clues.

## Where to start in a grid of 34 entries

Rows 5 and 6 have no black squares, so the two 11-letter Across entries, 14-Across and 17-Across, run edge to edge. Each one crosses 11 different Down entries, one for every square. The two Down entries of two letters, 15-Down and 16-Down, sit entirely inside that pair of rows, which means they fill in the moment both long entries are done. Next in crossings are 8-Across, 24-Across, 6-Down and 7-Down, each crossing 9 entries.
