**Connections: Sports Edition #790 is the Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026 puzzle from The Athletic and New York Times (NYT) Games.** Two hints wait behind a tap for each color, and the answers sit under the spoiler heading.

Several tiles on this board wear a second meaning. The rules live on our [Connections page](/news/topic/nyt-connections/#how-it-works); today the skill is reading each word twice. Saturday's board is on [Sports Edition #789](/news/nyt-connections-sports-edition-hints-answers-november-21-2026-789/), and the main game has its own page, [Connections for Nov. 22](/news/nyt-connections-hints-answers-november-22-2026-1260/).

## Sunday's hints for #790

Sort by what a tile does in a sentence before you sort by sport.

- **Hint 1 (yellow):** Every tile describes something that goes the wrong way once it leaves a player's hand or foot.
- **Hint 2 (yellow):** Two of the four are also words a football broadcast uses for something else.
- **Hint 1 (green):** Walk the edge of an NFL field and you will see each of these tiles.
- **Hint 2 (green):** One tile is a person, one is a shelter, and the other two are a seat and a screen.
- **Hint 1 (blue):** Each tile pairs with the same number to make a familiar phrase.
- **Hint 2 (blue):** One phrase borrows the name of an ocean, and one is a top mark in a judged sport.
- **Hint 1 (purple):** Each tile is a last name, and the four people share one job on special teams.
- **Hint 2 (purple):** Two tiles look like plain English, so read them as names, not as words.

## Which Sunday tiles pull the wrong way

Wild and Wide are the sneakiest pair. Each can pass for a football term, one from the playoff picture and one from the receiving corps, and that draws a solver toward a football group before the fourth tile arrives.

Way and Anger work differently. They look like everyday words with no sport attached, so most players leave them for last and then discover nothing else wants them. If two tiles seem to have no home, read them another way.

Tablet is a third snag. Set beside Medical Tent, it sounds like something from a pharmacy. Settle the other three meanings on the board before you decide which one it carries. Big is a fourth: it begs to join a sports word, but it needs a number after it. A [printable weekly calendar](/printable-weekly-calendar.html) can hold your streak.

## Sunday's answers, #790, Nov. 22, 2026 (spoilers)

Stop here if you still want to solve it. Nerd's Chalk, Word Tips and Connections Solver print the same four groups and sixteen entries, and Beebom and Nerd's Chalk agree on the colors. The group names below are as those sites print them.

- **Yellow, Off the Mark:** Errant, Wild, Inaccurate, Wide
- **Green, Found on an NFL Sideline:** Tablet, Bench, Medical Tent, Head Coach
- **Blue, ___ 10:** Big, Perfect, Atlantic, Hang
- **Purple, NFL Punters:** Stout, Way, Anger, Mann

## Who each entry is

- **Errant, Wild, Inaccurate, Wide:** each describes a throw or shot that misses.
- **Tablet, Bench, Medical Tent, Head Coach:** each is something found on an NFL sideline.
- **Big 10 and Atlantic 10:** college athletic conference names.
- **Perfect 10:** a top score in gymnastics.
- **Hang 10:** a surfing term.
- **Stout, Way, Anger, Mann:** surnames of NFL punters, per the published solutions. We name no player or team, because we found no league page confirming them.

## Why the purple names fool solvers

| Tile | Reads as | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Way | a path or manner | NFL punters' surnames |
| Anger | an emotion | NFL punters' surnames |
| Wild | a card or an unruly shot | Off the Mark |
| Wide | a receiver or a miss | Off the Mark |
| Tablet | medicine or a device | Found on an NFL sideline |

Two of the tiles look like English words until the group title rewrites them as names. The football-sounding pair sit in the group about misses.
