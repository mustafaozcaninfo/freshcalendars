**Tuesday, Nov. 24, 2026, puts A in the middle of a Spelling Bee from The New York Times (NYT), with C, I, L, T, U and Y on the outside.** The list runs to 55 words worth 280 points, and one letter shows up far less than the others. Genius starts at 196 points.

A has to appear in every word, so Tuesday's puzzle turns on the outer six. The [Spelling Bee page](/news/topic/nyt-spelling-bee/#how-it-works) explains the scoring, and the sections below keep to this board: the 13-letter word, the weak letter and the distance to Genius.

## The pangram clues

- **The pangram** (9 letters): it starts with A and is worth 16 points.
- **The ending:** it finishes with Y, the last letter of 18 answers.
- **The company:** two other 9-letter answers exist, and neither is a pangram.

## Which letters cannot appear on Tuesday?

Nineteen letters are off the board: B, D, E, F, G, H, J, K, M, N, O, P, Q, R, S, V, W, X and Z. Without E or O, the vowel work is split between A, I and U, with Y closing many words.

A is in all 55 answers. The six outer letters are far from equal:

- **Most and fewest:** L turns up in the most answers (42) and U in the fewest (12), a gap of 30.
- **C is close behind L:** 41 answers hold it, one fewer than L.
- **Y is a closer:** 18 of the 23 words with Y end in it, and only 2 begin with it.
- **U never starts a word:** it ends one answer and sits inside the rest.

## What makes the pangram hard to find?

A pangram has to hold U, and U is in 12 answers. Counting different letters per answer shows how thin the top is: 1 answer holds all seven, 8 hold six, 12 hold five, 23 hold four and 11 hold three.

Of the 8 answers with six different letters, 6 lack only U, one lacks only L and one lacks only I. Those six are the nearest misses a solver is likely to type in first.

## The hardest words on Tuesday's list

- **Longest answers:** one of 13 letters, two of 10, three of 9 (one is the pangram) and three of 8.
- **Rare starts:** CI, CL, IL, IT, LI and LU begin one answer each, while TA begins 17 and CA begins 11.
- **Doubled letters:** 18 answers repeat a letter back to back, with LL in 12, TT in 5 and CC in 1.
- **Letter U:** it is in 12 answers, and 4 of them are only four letters long.
- **Endings:** 18 answers end in Y, 11 in L, 10 in C, 6 in T, 5 in A, 4 in I and 1 in U.
- **Starts:** 17 answers begin with T, 15 with C, 12 with A, 7 with L, 2 with I and 2 with Y.

## The gap from four-letter words to Genius

The 16 four-letter answers give 16 points, about 8% of the 196 needed. That leaves 180 points to find among the 39 answers of five letters or more, which hold 264 in all, 16 of them from the pangram. Without the pangram the list is worth 264 points, 68 above the bar.
