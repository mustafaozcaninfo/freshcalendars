**Strands #991 from The New York Times (NYT) is the puzzle for Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026, and its clue is "Quite bright."** The puzzle data credits Tracy Bennett as both constructor and editor. The board hides a 9-letter spangram and six theme words. Today's spangram crosses the whole height of the grid, and the rules are on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). Hints come first, then a list of start cells; answers wait in a marked section at the end.

The clue is a plain description, so every theme word is a different way of saying it.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: An idiom for someone who is alert and quick to grasp things, written as three short words run together.
- Hint 2: It has 9 letters, starting with O and ending with L.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is the top-left corner of the board, row 1, column 1.

## Theme word hints

Six words fit the clue, listed here from shortest to longest:

- **5 letters, starts with S, no repeated letter:** what a phone is called when it does more than make calls.
- **5 letters, starts with S, has a double letter:** practical know-how, as in street or money.
- **6 letters, starts with A:** sharp at noticing small details; the only theme word that begins with A.
- **6 letters, starts with C:** quick-witted and good at finding a way through; the only one that begins with C.
- **6 letters, starts with S, ends with D:** good at sizing up a deal.
- **11 letters, starts with I:** having a high capacity to learn, with a double L in the middle. It is the longest theme word.

## Start-cell hints

Rows count from the top and columns from the left, and the first step is the direction from a word's first letter to its second. No word is named here.

- **5 letters, start cell row 2, column 2:** the first step goes down and to the right.
- **5 letters, start cell row 6, column 4:** the first step goes down and to the right.
- **6 letters, start cell row 4, column 4:** the first step goes down and to the right.
- **6 letters, start cell row 5, column 3:** the first step goes straight left.
- **6 letters, start cell row 6, column 1:** the first step goes straight down.
- **11 letters, start cell row 1, column 5:** the first step goes straight right.

## How the spangram crosses Thursday's board

The spangram begins in the top-left corner, at row 1, column 1, and steps right to row 1, column 2. From there it drops one row at a time: row 2 in column 3, row 3 in column 4, then a zigzag between columns 3 and 4 until it ends in row 8, column 3. It touches the left edge once (the corner tile), the top edge twice and the bottom edge once, and it never reaches the right edge. Four of its tiles sit in column 3 and three in column 4.

That line divides the other 39 tiles into two groups with no crossing. The left side holds 17 tiles, filled by three theme words of 5, 6 and 6 letters. The right side holds 22 tiles, filled by words of 5, 6 and 11 letters. The right side is wider at the top, with 4 free tiles in row 1, and narrows to 2 or 3 tiles per row below.

The densest corner is the upper left, rows 1 to 4 and columns 1 to 3. Its 12 tiles belong to four words: the spangram takes 4, the 5-letter word that starts in row 2 takes 5, a 6-letter word takes 2 and the 11-letter word takes 1. The other three corners of the same size each hold only three words.

## Solving tips

Begin with the upper left, where the 5-letter word fits almost entirely inside the corner, and then let the spangram's column 3 and column 4 zigzag mark the divide: a word on one side never uses a tile on the other. Hints, earned by finding three ordinary words, light up one theme word.
