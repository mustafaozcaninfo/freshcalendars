**The New York Times (NYT) Crossword for Monday, Nov. 16, 2026, was constructed by Josef Sipkins and edited by Will Shortz: 76 clues, 39 Across and 37 Down, in a 15-by-15 grid.** One clue carries a question mark, and four long entries share a hidden link. Each clue has a hint behind a tap, and the answers close the page.

## The one question-mark clue and Monday's other clue signals

Only 47-Down ends in a question mark. The clue is "Ran fast … like Usain?". The question mark warns that "ran fast" is a pun on the runner's surname, and the answer is a past-tense verb built on that surname. The three dots lead into the joke, the way a pause does in speech.

Today's clues use small signals in their wording. Counted from the puzzle data, 17 of the 76 carry one:

| Signal in the clue | Count | What it tells you | Clue numbers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blank written as "___" | 7 | The answer completes a phrase or name | 14-Across, 30-Across, 62-Across, 4-Down, 28-Down, 39-Down, 55-Down |
| Abbreviation marker ("Abbr.", "Org.", "grp.", "inits.", "V.I.P.s") | 5 | The answer is short or an acronym | 22-Across, 48-Across, 64-Across, 65-Across, 56-Down |
| Ellipsis ("…") | 3 | The clue trails off or sets up a joke | 36-Across, 12-Down, 47-Down |
| Quotation marks around speech | 3 | The answer is something a person says | 32-Across, 12-Down, 52-Down |
| Question mark | 1 | Wordplay, not the plain meaning | 47-Down |

Two clues print a word in capitals: 15-Across names a Graphics Interchange Format (GIF) image, and 7-Down prints a medical word the way a Wheel of Fortune board shows it, in capitals (MAMMOGRAM). Neither capital marks wordplay. The other 59 clues have no marker and read as plain definitions or trivia. The counts overlap: 12-Down is both spoken and an ellipsis clue, and 47-Down is both an ellipsis and a question-mark clue, so the rows add to 19.

The theme sits in 36-Across, whose clue says a word fits between the two halves of the four long Across entries at 17, 26, 48 and 57. Test any candidate against all four before you write it in.

The grid is 15 columns by 15 rows, 225 squares in all: 36 black and 189 holding a letter, a black share of 16.0 percent. Turning it half a turn leaves the black pattern unchanged. Across has 39 entries and Down has 37.

Across leans on four-letter entries (18 of 39) and Down on three-letter ones (14 of 37). The three 11-letter entries form a long bar and two tall columns. The revealer runs through row 8, columns 3 to 13. One 11-letter Down starts in the top row of column 3 and crosses the revealer at its first square. The other ends in the bottom row of column 13 and crosses it at its last. The four theme entries sit in a staggered pattern: 17-Across at the left of row 3, 26-Across at the right of row 5, 48-Across at the left of row 11 and 57-Across at the right of row 13. Their lengths are 9, 8, 8 and 9. Rows 4 and 12 hold five black squares each, the most of any row; rows 3, 5, 11 and 13 hold one each, and row 8 holds four. The two Down entries of 9 letters sit in column 9, rows 1 to 9, and column 7, rows 7 to 15.
