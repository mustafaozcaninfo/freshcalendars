**The New York Times (NYT) Crossword for Saturday, Nov. 28, 2026, is by John Guzzetta and edited by Will Shortz: 68 clues, 35 Across and 33 Down, in a 15-by-15 grid.** Saturday's grid has fewer, longer entries than Friday's, and two answers run from the top edge to the bottom. A hint for every clue sits behind a tap; the answers close the page.

## Five question marks, and what each one signals

Five of the 68 clues end in "?", the flag for a pun or a twisted reading. None of the tricks below is given away.

- **1-Across, "One who's always missing busses?" (9 letters).** The clue is about people, not traffic: "busses" is an old word for a friendly gesture between two people. Read "missing" as failing at it.
- **15-Across, "Listing in a vampire's dating profile?" (9 letters).** A dating profile lists traits about the person. Ask what a vampire cares about most and what category it falls into.
- **29-Across, "Old flame?" (5 letters).** The question mark shifts "flame" from a former partner to the real thing, and "old" from age to what is left once it burns down.
- **35-Across, "Way to get on board SpaceX, in brief?" (3 letters).** "Board" is not a ship or a plane here. Think of how an outside investor gets in on a company, then take the initials.
- **45-Down, "Not go swimmingly?" (4 letters).** "Swimmingly" usually means smoothly. Here it is taken literally: the answer is a way to be in water without swimming.

Four more clues soften themselves without a question mark: 22-Across and 24-Across end in "maybe", 10-Down in "say" and 12-Down in "e.g.". Each one asks for a category or a loose reading, so the answer is an example and not a literal match.
