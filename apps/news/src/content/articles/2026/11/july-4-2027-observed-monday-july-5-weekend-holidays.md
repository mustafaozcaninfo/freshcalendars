**The Office of Personnel Management (OPM) lists Independence Day 2027 as a federal holiday on Monday, July 5, because July 4, 2027 is a Sunday.** The Federal Reserve and the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) use the same Monday. The date of the holiday itself is on [When is Independence Day?](/when-is/independence-day/); the tables here cover who is closed and which other holidays move off a weekend.

## Who is closed on Monday, July 5, 2027?

| Who | Monday, July 5, 2027 | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Federal offices | Holiday for most federal employees; OPM lists "Monday, July 05 **" for Independence Day | OPM federal holidays |
| Federal Reserve | All Federal Reserve offices closed: "the Federal Reserve Banks and the Board of Governors are closed on July 5, 2027" | Federal Reserve K.8 schedule |
| NYSE | Closed: "Monday, July 5 (Independence Day observed)" | NYSE hours and calendars |
| Banks | Most banks typically close when the Federal Reserve closes, but each bank sets its own hours | Federal Reserve K.8 schedule |
| U.S. Postal Service (USPS) | Not confirmed: the USPS has not posted its 2027 holiday list | USPS events and holidays |
| Private employers | No federal requirement to give the day off or pay for it | U.S. Department of Labor |

The USPS newsroom page lists a "Monday / July 5* / Independence Day" row with no year, so a July 5, 2027 post office closure is not confirmed here.

## Why is the holiday observed on Monday, July 5?

July 4, 2027 falls on a Sunday. OPM says that for a Sunday holiday the following Monday is treated as the holiday, citing Section 3(a) of Executive Order 11582. For a Saturday holiday, OPM's note reads: "If a holiday falls on a Saturday, for most Federal employees, the preceding Friday will be treated as a holiday for pay and leave purposes. (See 5 U.S.C. 6103(b).)"

The statute itself, 5 U.S.C. 6103(b)(1), says: "Instead of a holiday that occurs on a Saturday, the Friday immediately before is a legal public holiday for" employees whose basic workweek is Monday through Friday. The Sunday-to-Monday rule comes from the executive order, not from that section.

For workers who get the observed day, Saturday, July 3 to Monday, July 5 is a three-day weekend. See the [2027 long weekends and paid time off planner](/news/long-weekends-2027-pto-planner-bridge-days/) for how to stretch it.

## Which 2027 holidays move off a weekend?

Four holidays in 2027 and early 2028 land on a Saturday or Sunday. Federal Reserve Banks and the Board of Governors do not always follow the same day, and the NYSE has one exception.

| Holiday | Actual date | OPM observed date | Federal Reserve | NYSE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Juneteenth National Independence Day | Saturday, June 19, 2027 | Friday, June 18 | Banks open, Board of Governors closed | Closed Friday, June 18 |
| Independence Day | Sunday, July 4, 2027 | Monday, July 5 | All offices closed Monday, July 5 | Closed Monday, July 5 |
| Christmas Day | Saturday, Dec. 25, 2027 | Friday, Dec. 24 | Banks open, Board of Governors closed | Closed Friday, Dec. 24 |
| New Year's Day 2028 | Saturday, Jan. 1, 2028 | Friday, Dec. 31, 2027 (OPM's Saturday rule, computed) | Banks open, Board of Governors closed | No holiday: trades Friday, Dec. 31, 2027 |

The Federal Reserve rule reads: "For holidays falling on Saturday, Federal Reserve Banks and Branches will be open the preceding Friday; however, the Board of Governors will be closed." For Sunday holidays, "all Federal Reserve offices will be closed the following Monday." The K.8 schedule lists June 18, 2027, Dec. 24, 2027 and Dec. 31, 2027 as Fridays when the Banks are open and the Board is closed.

The NYSE states for 2028: "Because the holiday falls on Saturday, January 1, 2028, no New Year's Day holiday is observed." OPM's 2027 list on its page ends with Christmas Day, so the Dec. 31, 2027 date for federal workers is our computation from the Saturday rule, not a printed row.

## Do private employers have to give July 5, 2027 off?

No federal law requires it. The U.S. Department of Labor says the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) "does not require payment for time not worked, such as vacations or holidays," and that such benefits are "generally a matter of agreement between an employer and an employee." Check your employer's holiday policy, because the federal observed day does not bind it. State government closures are not covered here.
