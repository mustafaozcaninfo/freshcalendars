Each clue has a hint behind a tap, and the answers sit at the bottom. Yesterday's grid is in our [Crossword answers for Nov. 14](/news/nyt-crossword-answers-november-14-2026/).

## Six song clues behind the title "Side Tracks"

Six clues in the data are song titles followed by a year, and the entry beside each one is longer than anything else in its row: 23-Across, 34-Across, 43-Across, 93-Across, 103-Across and 116-Across. In the record's title, the main words come first and a second set of words follows in round brackets. The clue prints the first set and the grid wants the second. That is the "side" of the title: each song is a track, and the bracketed words are its side.

A seventh entry, 69-Across, has 13 squares and a clue that says what the six answers are in relation to the song titles in their clues. Fill it early from its crossings and the other six stop looking odd.

The placement mirrors itself through the middle. Turn the grid half a revolution and 23-Across lands on 116-Across, 34-Across on 103-Across and 43-Across on 93-Across, while 69-Across stays put in the center row. The six song answers add up to 80 squares (17, 11, 12, 12, 11 and 17) and the seventh brings the theme to 93 of the 367 lettered squares.

## Three question-mark clues and the trick in each

Only three clues end in a question mark. None of the explanations below names its answer.

- **123-Across, "Unconsciously make noise?"** Five letters. "Make noise" has no intent behind it here; the verb belongs to someone who is not awake, and the question mark moves it from a deliberate sound to an involuntary one.
- **53-Down, "Giving thought to somebody?"** Nine letters. "Giving" means sending, not considering. The thought travels from one head to another with nothing said, and the answer is the name for that process.
- **97-Down, "Result of someone needling you?"** Six letters. Drop the teasing sense of "needling": a needle is literally at work, and what it leaves behind stays on the skin.

Three clues without a question mark also read sideways. 64-Across is a stage direction in square brackets, *[Wags finger]*, and the answer is a sound that means no. 116-Down, *Third of three X's, maybe*, wants a word from a game played with crosses and circles. 44-Down, *"Are you out?"*, is a question whose answer is another question, four words long.

## How Sunday's grid is built

The grid is 21 by 21, which is 441 squares: 74 are black and 367 hold a letter. It has 140 entries, 69 Across and 71 Down, and the black squares are symmetric under a half turn.

By length, the 140 entries are 33 of 3 letters, 28 of 4, 38 of 5, 16 of 6, eight of 7, two of 8, eight of 9, two of 11, two of 12, one of 13 and two of 17. The seven theme entries are the only ones above 9 letters, so every Down entry is 9 squares or shorter, and the longest non-theme Across entries have 7 letters. Eight Down entries have exactly 9: 36-Down, 37-Down and 38-Down sit side by side in columns 1 to 3, starting in row 6; 52-Down, 53-Down and 54-Down mirror them in columns 19 to 21, starting in row 8; 44-Down in column 6 and 47-Down in column 16 stand apart.

One clue, 59-Across, names a pope by Roman numeral, Leo the Fourteenth (XIV). Other clue types, counted from the list: 14 clues are fill-in-the-blank with a blank line, and 16 open with a quotation mark. Five flag a shortened answer: 3-Down, 114-Down and 120-Down say "Abbr.", while 56-Across and 65-Down say "in brief". Thirty-three entries are three letters long, which is the most common length in the grid.

The grid carries no rebus squares; every cell holds one letter. With a title that points at records, the rules for the daily grid are on our [NYT Crossword page](/news/topic/nyt-crossword/#how-it-works).
