**Waffle (wafflegame.net) puzzle #1749 is the grid for Thursday, Nov. 5, 2026, and it starts with six green tiles out of 21.** Three five-letter words run across and three run down. The clues below go one word at a time, and a route that solves the board in the minimum number of swaps waits in the marked spoiler section.

Today's board starts with six green tiles; the rules and tile colors are on our [Waffle page](/news/topic/waffle/#how-it-works).

## Clues for each word

**Across**

- **Row 1:** starts with T and ends with H, and has the letter O in both the second and third spots. A noun for one of the hard white pieces set in the jaw.
- **Row 3:** starts with O and ends with R, and has the letter D in the middle. An adjective that describes someone who has lived more years than another person.
- **Row 5:** starts with N and ends with E, and has the letter R in the third spot. A verb meaning to look after a person who is ill or hurt.

**Down**

- **Column 1:** starts with T and ends with N, and shares its first letter with the row 1 word and its last letter with the row 5 word. A noun for a sharp point that grows on a rose stem.
- **Column 3:** starts with O and ends with R, and has the letter D in the middle, where it crosses the row 3 word. A noun for a neat arrangement, or a command that someone gives.
- **Column 5:** starts with H and ends with E, and shares its first letter with the last letter of the row 1 word and its last letter with the last letter of the row 5 word. A noun for a large, noisy crowd.

## What today's letter pile tells you

The 21 tiles hold four copies of one letter, four of another, three of a third, two each of three more, and four letters that appear once. Two of the six green tiles are corner tiles at the left edge, so the left column is partly settled before your first move. The center tile is green too, and it sits where a down word and an across word cross, so leave it alone. Nine tiles sit where an across word meets a down word (the four corners, the center and the middle of each edge), so a swap on one of those nine changes two words at once; the other 12 tiles each belong to a single word.
