**The New York Times (NYT) Mini Crossword for Thursday, Nov. 5, 2026, fills a five-by-five grid with 10 clues written by Tracy Bennett.** One clue appears twice, once for an Across entry and once for a Down entry, and the two answers are different words. Every hint below stays closed until you tap it, and the answers wait at the bottom in a marked spoiler section.

## Hints for every clue

**Across**

- **1-Across** (4 letters), *Experimental and provocative*: a word for art or comedy that pushes at the limits of taste; it starts with E and ends with Y.
- **5-Across** (5 letters), *"Top Chef" alum ___ Hall*: the chef's first name, which the clue leaves blank before her surname; it starts with C and ends with A.
- **6-Across** (5 letters), *Once more*: what an audience shouts when it wants a song played a second time; it starts with A and ends with N.
- **7-Across** (5 letters), *Throw ... or a protection for an injured arm*: a verb for hurling a stone, and a cloth support hung from the neck; it starts with S.
- **8-Across** (4 letters), *Something a camper sets up*: a canvas shelter held up by poles and stakes; it starts with T and ends with T.

**Down**

- **1-Down** (5 letters), *Keen-eyed bird of prey*: a large raptor known for its sharp sight; it starts and ends with E.
- **2-Down** (5 letters), *"You Can Never Go Down the ___" (Mister Rogers song for kids afraid of bath time)*: the opening at the bottom of a tub where the water runs out; it starts with D.
- **3-Down** (5 letters), *Flash of light*: a brief sparkle, like sun catching a metal edge; it starts with G and ends with T.
- **4-Down** (4 letters), *Yin's counterpart*: the bright, active half of the Chinese pair the clue names; it starts with Y.
- **5-Down** (4 letters), *Throw ... or a protection for an injured arm*: what an angler does with a fishing line, and the hard shell set around a broken bone; it starts with C.

## The clue that shows up twice

Thursday's grid prints "Throw ... or a protection for an injured arm" at 7-Across and a second time at 5-Down. Each answer has to be a verb for throwing and also a thing a doctor puts on a hurt arm. The grid asks for one word of five letters and one of four, so the letter count is the first way to tell them apart.

The entries also share a square. 5-Down starts in the first square of row 2 and runs to the bottom, and 7-Across fills row 4 from the left edge. That puts the first letter of 7-Across at the third square of 5-Down. Solve one of the pair and the other is half started, since that shared letter plus its length narrows the choice to one word.

## How the grid is built

The grid is 5 by 5, 25 squares in all. Only 2 are black: the top-left square and the bottom-right square, so the first and last rows each lose one square. The Across entries run 4, 5, 5, 5, 4 letters from top to bottom and the Down entries run 5, 5, 5, 4, 4 from left to right, which adds up to 23 letters in each direction.
