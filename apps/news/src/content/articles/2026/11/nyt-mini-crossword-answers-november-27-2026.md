## Hints for every clue

**Across**

- **1-Across** (3 letters), *When doubled, the sort of reprimand you might give an Egyptian king?*: a clicking sound of mild disapproval; the king in the clue is the boy pharaoh known by a three-letter nickname.
- **4-Across** (5 letters), *When doubled, "Sleep well!"*: the part of the day when people go to bed; said twice, it works as a bedtime sign-off.
- **6-Across** (5 letters), *Not this or that, with "the"*: a word for whatever remains after two choices are named; it fits "the ___ side."
- **7-Across** (5 letters), *Hero's everlasting acclaim*: lasting fame and honor; it ends in the same square as 5-Down.
- **8-Across** (3 letters), *Barely ___ out*: a verb for scraping together just enough, as in a living; it begins and ends with the same letter.

**Down**

- **1-Down** (5 letters), *It might change with a promotion*: the name of a position at work, and also what a book carries on its cover; its first square is shared with 1-Across.
- **2-Down** (5 letters), *"Fine, if you must"*: a reluctant yes, grumbled; the grid runs two short words together, and its first square is the middle square of 1-Across.
- **3-Down** (5 letters), *When doubled, consoling words*: what a friend says while patting your shoulder; its last square is the last square of 8-Across.
- **4-Down** (3 letters), *Christmas drink*: the second half of a creamy holiday drink's name; its first square is shared with 4-Across.
- **5-Down** (3 letters), *Word that's doubled before "again," in a phrase*: a verb for making an attempt; its first square is the last square of 4-Across.

## Four clues that say a word twice

Four of the 10 clues, 1-Across, 4-Across, 3-Down and 5-Down, carry the same instruction in different dress. Three open with "When doubled" and one says the word "is doubled before 'again.'" That is 40% of the puzzle asking you to repeat a word aloud and judge whether the result is a real phrase.

Treat them as a set. A repeat that sounds right in one clue tells you how to read the remaining three, and the grid holds each word only once, so a five-letter answer in 4-Across or 3-Down that sounds silly when said twice is probably wrong.

The 1-Across clue is the least literal of the ten. It asks about an Egyptian king, and the king is only the setup for a pun; the answer is a short sound you make at someone who has misbehaved, and the doubling is what turns that sound into a scolding. Solving the crossing Down entries first settles which reading is right.

Fagliano's black squares sit at all four corners. That trims the top and bottom rows and the left and right columns to three squares each, so 1-Across, 8-Across, 4-Down and 5-Down are the four 3-letter entries, one on each edge. The six 5-letter entries are the three middle rows across and the three middle columns down, which makes the middle of the grid the part that depends on every crossing answer.
