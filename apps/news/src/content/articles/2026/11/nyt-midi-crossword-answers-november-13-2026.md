**The New York Times (NYT) Midi Crossword for Friday, Nov. 13, 2026, is titled "Dinner and a Show" and has 41 clues on an 11-by-11 grid, credited to Ian Livengood.** Every clue gets a hint behind a tap, and the answers sit in a marked section at the bottom.

## Where "Dinner and a Show" turns up in the grid

The title is the whole theme: a meal on one side, a stage production on the other. Three clues carry both. Each ends with a question mark, mentions a Broadway musical and gives its opening year, which is the tell. The years are 2009, 2018 and 1996, and each clue also names a food.

The three entries sit in rows 3, 6 and 10 of the grid. Row 6 is the one full-width row, so its answer has 11 letters and runs from edge to edge; row 3 holds 10 letters and row 10 holds 9. Together they use 30 of the 94 letters. Each answer joins the title of a show to a food word: the show is the "show" in the title and the food is the "dinner".

There is a decoy. 29-Across, in row 9, also has 10 letters and sits directly above the 9-letter theme answer. Its clue is about stereo gear and has no question mark. Treat it as ordinary fill.

## The least literal clue today

That is 17-Across: *Italian pie at a 2018 Broadway musical?* The question mark is the signal that the clue is a pun and the words cannot be taken at face value. Read "Italian pie" as the food half. "At a 2018 Broadway musical" says the other half of the answer is a show that opened that year. The result has two words, 6 and 5 letters, and an 11-letter answer is only possible in row 6. The pie is more of a dinner than a dessert, which makes the title a literal description.

## Which entries to fill first

Count the crossings, meaning how many other entries each answer shares a square with, and the theme answers come out on top. Entries of 3 letters are the most common length in this grid, with 12 of 41 clues, and 16 of the entries have 4 letters. In order of crossings:

- 17-Across: 11 letters, 11 crossings.
- 10-Across and 29-Across: 10 letters and 10 crossings each.
- 32-Across: 9 letters, 9 crossings.
- 2-Down: 7 letters, 7 crossings.
- 4-Down: 6 letters, 6 crossings.

Solving 17-Across opens a Down entry at every square of row 6, so solve it before the others. Five clues are fill-in-the-blank: 1-Across, 15-Across, 34-Across, 5-Down and 16-Down. They are the shortest way into the corners. 29-Across is a good second target because its clue needs no wordplay at all, and it feeds the theme entry right below it.
