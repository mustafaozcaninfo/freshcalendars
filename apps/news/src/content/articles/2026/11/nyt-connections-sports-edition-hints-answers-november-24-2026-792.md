**Connections: Sports Edition #792 is the Tuesday, Nov. 24, 2026 puzzle from The Athletic and New York Times (NYT) Games.** Each color has two hints behind a tap, and the answers wait under the spoiler heading.

Today's board mixes proper names with everyday words, and one group asks you to look at how an entry ends, not what it says. The rules sit on our [Connections page](/news/topic/nyt-connections/#how-it-works). Monday's board is on [Sports Edition #791](/news/nyt-connections-sports-edition-hints-answers-november-23-2026-791/), and the main game has its own page, [Connections for Nov. 24](/news/nyt-connections-hints-answers-november-24-2026-1262/).

## Tuesday's hints for #792

Start with the tiles that read as plain words, then look at what is left.

- **Hint 1 (yellow):** Every tile is a club from the same capital city.
- **Hint 2 (yellow):** Exactly two of the four tiles run to two words.
- **Hint 1 (green):** Each tile is a word you would hear on a boat.
- **Hint 2 (green):** Three of the four have four letters; the fourth has six.
- **Hint 1 (blue):** The four tiles are surnames that follow one shared first name.
- **Hint 2 (blue):** The longest tile in this group has nine letters.
- **Hint 1 (purple):** Look at the final word of each tile, not the first.
- **Hint 2 (purple):** One tile is three words long, and one is a first name.

## The tiles that look like they belong elsewhere

Several tiles name animals, and a solver's eye drifts toward a creature group that is not on the board. Count them before you commit: there are more animal-looking tiles than one group can hold, so at least one belongs somewhere else.

One tile reads as a woman's first name and seems to have no partner on the board. Say it aloud and listen to its last few letters. A boat word can also pass as a place on a map, and two of the names double as ordinary English words, so check each spare tile against every group before you lock one in. ## Tuesday's answers, #792, Nov. 24, 2026 (spoilers)

Stop here if you still want to solve it. Beebom and Nerd's Chalk print the same four groups and sixteen entries. The group names below are as those sites print them; the purple title names the Southeastern Conference (SEC).

- **Yellow, London Soccer Teams:** Arsenal, Chelsea, Crystal Palace, West Ham
- **Green, Sailing Terms:** Hull, Keel, Mast, Rudder
- **Blue, Alex ___:** English, Morgan, Ovechkin, Rodriguez
- **Purple, Ends in an SEC Team, in Singular Form:** Alligator, Eye of the Tiger, French Bulldog, Maggie

## Who each entry is

- **Arsenal, Chelsea, Crystal Palace, West Ham:** clubs based in London, per the published group title. We add no stadiums or league facts, because no club page we checked confirms them.
- **Hull, Keel, Mast, Rudder:** each is a sailing term, per the published group title.
- **English, Morgan, Ovechkin, Rodriguez:** each surname follows the first name Alex, per the published group title. We name no sports or clubs, because no league page we checked confirms them.
- **Alligator, Eye of the Tiger, French Bulldog, Maggie:** the last word of each ends in a nickname used in the Southeastern Conference, per the published group title. We name no schools.

## Why the purple group needs the singular form

The title says the entry ends in a team nickname, and the key is that the nickname is cut down to one member of the team. Read the last word of each tile and the endings line up: Gator, Tiger, Bulldog and Aggie. Maggie is the sneaky one, because Aggie is tucked into the back half of a name given to people, so nothing about the tile hints at a sports word until you split it.

| Tile | Reads as | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Hull | a boat part, also a place name | Sailing terms |
| Morgan | a first name or a surname | Alex surnames |
| English | a language, also a surname | Alex surnames |
| Maggie | a first name | Singular nicknames |
