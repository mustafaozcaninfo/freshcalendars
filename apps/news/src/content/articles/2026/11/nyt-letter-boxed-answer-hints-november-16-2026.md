**Letter Boxed from The New York Times (NYT) for Monday, Nov. 16, 2026 has the sides A/I/E, Q/P/C, O/U/T and L/Y/X, and a par of 6, according to the puzzle data on the Letter Boxed page.** Today's par is 6, and the rules are on our [Letter Boxed page](/news/topic/nyt-letter-boxed/#how-it-works). The saved list has 731 words. The sections below cover the sides, the few routes that exist, the reason this box resists most openings, five hints and four warm-up words, with the Times' solution at the end.

## The four sides

| Side | Letters |
|---|---|
| 1 | A, I, E |
| 2 | Q, P, C |
| 3 | O, U, T |
| 4 | L, Y, X |

Each letter has two side-mates, which bars 24 ordered pairs before any word is tried.

## How far under par 6 can a solver get?

Par 6 allows six words. The saved list sets a floor of two, because the widest single word touches 10 of the 12 letters.

| Words in the finish | Against par 6 | Routes in the saved list |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 under | 0 |
| 2 | 4 under | 4 |
| 3 to 6 | 3 under to level | Not counted |

The four two-word routes share one join letter, the L, and together they run from 14 to 19 letters. A first word that ends on the L is the only way into a two-word finish.

## Why is this box hard to open?

Same-side letters cannot touch, so the list rules out more than the 24 barred pairs. Across the 108 pairs that sit on different sides, 15 never occur in the list. Nine involve the Q: Q then E, O, T, L, Y or X, plus T, Y and X then Q. Five involve the X: Q, P, C and T then X, plus X then Q. The last three are I then Y, T then P and T then C.

| Letter | Words containing it | Words starting with it | Words ending with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q | 57 | 22 | 2 |
| X | 86 | 2 | 23 |
| U | 177 | 3 | 21 |
| I | 311 | 10 | 34 |
| P | 393 | 234 | 43 |

Side 2 holds the Q, the P and the C, so none of them can follow another. After a Q, the list shows a U 52 times, an I twice and an A once, which is why the Q and the U travel together. The A, the I and the E share side 1, so vowels cannot run in pairs from that side; a word needs a consonant or the O, U or Y between them.

| Side | Letters | Words that start on it | Words that end on it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A, I, E | 144 | 304 |
| 2 | Q, P, C | 372 | 112 |
| 3 | O, U, T | 126 | 148 |
| 4 | L, Y, X | 89 | 167 |

Side 2 starts 372 words and ends 112, while side 1 ends 304. The longest words run 12 letters, and three of them tie for that length.

## Five hints, light to strong

1. The Times' own solution uses two words, the lowest count this list allows, and sits well under par.
2. The first word has 5 letters, one of 146 that long. The second has 9 letters, one of 26.
3. The first word begins on the Q/P/C side and ends on the L/Y/X side. The second word begins and ends on that last side.
4. The X and the E sit in the first word. The Q, the U and the T sit in the second.
5. The first word starts with P and ends in L. The second starts with L and ends in Y.
