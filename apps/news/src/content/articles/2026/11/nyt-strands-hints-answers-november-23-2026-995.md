**Strands #995 from The New York Times (NYT) is the Monday, Nov. 23, 2026 puzzle, and its clue is "Don't poke the bear."** Tracy Bennett is listed as both constructor and editor. Today's grid has a 7-letter spangram and six theme words, and the rules for finding them are on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). Hints come first, then a map of where each word sits, and the answers follow a spoiler warning.

The clue describes a person in a sour temper, and the six theme words are different ways of saying so.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: A sour frame of mind, the sort that makes people tread carefully around you.
- Hint 2: It has 7 letters, begins with B and ends with D, and one letter appears twice in a row in the middle.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 5, column 6, and the first step goes diagonally up and to the left.

## Theme word hints

Six words fit the clue, listed from shortest to longest:

- **5 letters, starts with T, ends with Y:** quick to snap at you over something small.
- **5 letters, starts with C, ends with S:** snappish and annoyed, with its last letter doubled.
- **6 letters, starts with C, ends with Y:** grouchy, and a shelled sea animal is hidden in the first four letters.
- **8 letters, starts with C, ends with H:** rude and surly, like someone who never says thank you.
- **8 letters, starts with P, ends with T:** sulky and childish in temper.
- **9 letters, starts with I, ends with E:** easily bothered by trifles, with the first letter repeated at the fourth.

## Start-cell hints

Rows count from the top and columns from the left; the first step is the direction from a word's first letter to its second. No word is named here.

- **5 letters, start cell row 4, column 6:** the first step goes straight up.
- **5 letters, start cell row 5, column 5:** the first step goes straight down.
- **6 letters, start cell row 4, column 2:** the first step goes straight up.
- **8 letters, start cell row 7, column 2:** the first step goes straight down.
- **8 letters, start cell row 8, column 6:** the first step goes straight left.
- **9 letters, start cell row 4, column 4:** the first step goes diagonally up and to the right.

## How the Nov. 23 spangram splits the board

The spangram starts in row 5, column 6 and ends in row 3, column 1. Between them it zigzags: row 5, column 6, then row 4, column 5, then row 5, column 4, row 4, column 3, row 5, column 2 and row 4, column 1. The seventh tile is row 3, column 1, straight above the sixth. That gives 3 tiles in row 4, 3 in row 5 and 1 in row 3, and no column is used twice except column 1.

The zigzag works as a fence. Above it, in rows 1 to 4, sit 20 theme tiles from three words: the 6-letter, the 9-letter and the 5-letter word that begins in row 4, column 6. All three begin in row 4 and head upward. Below it, in rows 5 to 8, sit 21 theme tiles from the other three, and none of them climbs past row 5.

Splitting the grid into four blocks of 12 tiles (rows 1 to 4 or 5 to 8, columns 1 to 3 or 4 to 6) shows the crowding:

| Block | Spangram tiles | Theme words in it |
|---|---|---|
| Top left | 3 | 6 tiles from the 6-letter word, 3 from the 9-letter word |
| Top right | 1 | 6 tiles from the 9-letter word, 5 from the upper 5-letter word |
| Bottom left | 1 | all 8 tiles of the 8-letter word that starts in row 7, plus 3 from the other 8-letter word |
| Bottom right | 2 | 5 tiles of the lower 5-letter word, 5 from the 8-letter word that starts in row 8 |

Rows 1 and 2 hold no spangram tile at all, so those 12 tiles are theme letters only. Across the six theme words there are 35 steps and only 4 are diagonal, so the spangram's five diagonal steps are the board's main hazard for anyone scanning in straight lines.

## Solving tips

Start with the long fence in the middle: once the spangram's diagonal pattern is clear, the top and bottom halves become two separate searches. The upper half has only three words and the lower half has three, so each half rewards a quick count of what is left.
