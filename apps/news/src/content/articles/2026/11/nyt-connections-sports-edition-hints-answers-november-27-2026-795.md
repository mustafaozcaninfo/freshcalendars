**Connections: Sports Edition #795 is the Friday, Nov. 27, 2026 puzzle from The Athletic and New York Times (NYT) Games, the day after Thanksgiving.** Two hints wait behind a tap for each color, and the answers sit under the spoiler heading.

Today's board pulls from several sports at once, so a tile that looks like it belongs to one game may not. The rules are on our [Connections page](/news/topic/nyt-connections/#how-it-works). Thursday's board is on [Sports Edition #794](/news/nyt-connections-sports-edition-hints-answers-november-26-2026-794/), and the main game has its own page, [Connections for Nov. 27](/news/nyt-connections-hints-answers-november-27-2026-1265/).

## Friday's hints for #795

Read each tile to its last letter, then look at who shares a name.

- **Hint 1 (yellow):** Each tile is a tool a player picks from a bag between shots.
- **Hint 2 (yellow):** Two tiles have six letters, and the shortest has four.
- **Hint 1 (green):** Each tile is a first name or a nickname, and all four people share one surname.
- **Hint 2 (green):** One tile is two initials with periods, and another has seven letters.
- **Hint 1 (blue):** Each tile is a surname of a player who took snaps at the same position for one National Football League (NFL) club.
- **Hint 2 (blue):** Two tiles start with the same letter, and the shortest has four letters.
- **Hint 1 (purple):** Each tile ends with something you would find in a kitchen drawer or cupboard.
- **Hint 2 (purple):** Two tiles are two words long, and the other two are single words.

## Traps on the day-after-Thanksgiving board

Chipper sounds like a short shot from a golf course, and the board does have golf vocabulary, so slow down before you lock it in. One tile is a football-sounding surname, and the board already holds four other football surnames, which makes a tempting pile. Read every tile to its end before you decide, because the surname that seems to fit the pile of football names is placed for a different reason entirely. Go slowly.

Two other tiles end in a word that is also a sport term, so test the last word of each. Endings matter here. A [printable weekly calendar](/printable-weekly-calendar.html) keeps the week's puzzles tidy.

## Friday's answers, #795, Nov. 27, 2026 (spoilers)

Stop here if you still want to solve it. Beebom and Nerdschalk print the same four groups and sixteen entries, and the group names below are as those sites print them.

- **Yellow, Golf clubs:** Driver, Iron, Putter, Wedge
- **Green, _____ Jones:** Chipper, K.C., LoLo, Cobi
- **Blue, Patriots QBs:** Bledsoe, Brady, Maye, Newton
- **Purple, Ends in a kitchen item:** Egg Bowl, Home Plate, Weatherspoon, Wilfork

## Who or what each entry is

- **Driver, Iron, Putter, Wedge:** each is a club in a golfer's bag, per the group name.
- **Chipper, K.C., LoLo, Cobi:** each is a first name or nickname that goes before Jones. The National Baseball Hall of Fame lists Chipper Jones as a Class of 2018 third baseman who played for the Atlanta Braves. The Naismith Basketball Hall of Fame says K.C. Jones was enshrined in 1989, played on eight consecutive NBA championship teams with the Boston Celtics and won gold on the 1956 U.S. Olympic team. We confirmed no pages for LoLo and Cobi, so we say nothing more about them.
- **Bledsoe, Brady, Maye, Newton:** each is a surname of a quarterback (QB) who played for the New England Patriots, per the group name. The Patriots' own site lists Drake Maye as a QB. We confirmed no team pages for the other three.
- **Egg Bowl, Home Plate, Weatherspoon, Wilfork:** each ends in a kitchen item: bowl, plate, spoon and fork. The Naismith Basketball Hall of Fame says Teresa Weatherspoon was enshrined in 2019, played for the New York Liberty and was on the U.S. Olympic team that won gold in Seoul. We confirmed no pages for the other three, so we describe them only by the group name.
