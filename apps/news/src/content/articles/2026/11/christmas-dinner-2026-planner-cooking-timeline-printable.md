**Christmas Day 2026 is Friday, Dec. 25, according to the U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM), and a 20 lb frozen turkey needs to go into the refrigerator by Sunday, Dec. 20.** That date is computed from University of Wyoming Extension's rule of 24 hours of thawing per 4 pounds. The count-back below covers turkeys from 8 to 24 lb, the safe temperature for each Christmas main and a printable with a shopping list. For the date itself, see [when is Christmas Day](/when-is/christmas-day/).

Extension services publish the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) food-safety figures; the federal pages themselves blocked automated reading when this story was checked on Nov. 15, 2026. Each number below names the extension service it comes from.

## When to start thawing a Christmas turkey

University of Wyoming Extension, in "Let's talk turkey" (MP-135, October 2018), says to allow 24 hours per 4 pounds of a whole turkey in the refrigerator. Our [Thanksgiving thaw guide](/news/when-to-thaw-thanksgiving-turkey-2026/) works from the USDA range of 4 to 5 pounds per day; the 4 lb figure used here gives the earlier, safer start. The hours and dates are computed from Wyoming's rule.

| Turkey weight | Fridge thaw time | Start thawing by | Cold-water thaw time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 lb | 48 hours (2 days) | Wed., Dec. 23 | 4 hours |
| 12 lb | 72 hours (3 days) | Tue., Dec. 22 | 6 hours |
| 16 lb | 96 hours (4 days) | Mon., Dec. 21 | 8 hours |
| 20 lb | 120 hours (5 days) | Sun., Dec. 20 | 10 hours |
| 24 lb | 144 hours (6 days) | Sat., Dec. 19 | 12 hours |

The cold-water column uses Wyoming's rule of 30 minutes per pound. The same page says to change the water every 30 minutes to keep it cold. The refrigerator must hold 40°F or below, the extension says.

## How long to roast the turkey

University of Wyoming Extension gives 2¾ to 3 hours for an unstuffed 8 to 12 lb turkey at 325°F, and 3 to 3½ hours for a stuffed one. University of Illinois Extension agrees: its unstuffed page says to preheat the oven to 325°F and roast for 2¾ to 3 hours, and its stuffed page gives 3 to 3½ hours and says the stuffing must reach 165°F or higher.

Roasting times for heavier birds are not printed here. Check the chart on the turkey's package or the University of Illinois Extension pages. A clock time for the oven depends on your dinner hour, so the printable leaves that line blank.

## Safe temperatures for every Christmas main

University of Maine Cooperative Extension, in "In the Kitchen: Safe Minimum Internal Temperatures" (March 1, 2024), lists these minimums.

| Dish | Minimum internal temperature | Rest time |
|---|---|---|
| Turkey, whole or ground, and stuffing | 165°F (74°C) | none listed |
| Beef, pork, lamb steaks, chops and roasts | 145°F (63°C) | 3 minutes |
| Fresh (raw) ham | 145°F (63°C) | 3 minutes |
| Fully cooked ham, reheated | 165°F (74°C) | none listed |
| Ground meats | 160°F (71°C) | none listed |
| Egg dishes | 160°F (71°C) | none listed |
| Casseroles with meat or poultry | 165°F (74°C) | none listed |
| Leftovers | 165°F (74°C) | none listed |

The same page notes that cooked hams packaged in USDA-inspected plants reheat to 140°F (60°C). Rest-time notes in the table are only the ones the page lists. Times for prime rib or standing rib roast are not in the sources checked.

## What to do with Christmas leftovers

University of Wyoming Extension says to put leftovers in the refrigerator or freezer within 2 hours. Refrigerated leftovers are safe for 3 to 4 days, the extension says, in a fridge at 40°F or below. Food cooked on Friday, Dec. 25 therefore stays within that window until Monday, Dec. 28 or Tuesday, Dec. 29 (computed). Reheat leftovers to 165°F, per University of Maine Cooperative Extension.
