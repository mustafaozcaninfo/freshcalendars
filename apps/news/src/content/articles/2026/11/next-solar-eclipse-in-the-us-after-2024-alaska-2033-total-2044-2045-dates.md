**The next total solar eclipse in the United States is on Wednesday, Mar. 30, 2033, over Alaska, and the next one in the lower 48 states is on the evening of Monday, Aug. 22, 2044, according to the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA).** NASA's tables date the 2044 eclipse Aug. 23 in Universal Time (UT), which is still Aug. 22 on mainland U.S. clocks. NASA's Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) page says totality in 2044 is visible only from North Dakota and Montana, and that the next total eclipse across the lower 48 from coast to coast is in 2045.

## What is the Mar. 30, 2033 total solar eclipse in Alaska?

NASA's 2031 to 2040 decade table lists the eclipse as total, with totality in "e Russia, Alaska". The path page puts greatest eclipse at 18:01:16.4 UT, with a central duration of 2 minutes 37.0 seconds and a path 781.0 kilometers wide at that instant. It falls on a Wednesday.

The NASA pages we used list the path by latitude and longitude at two-minute steps, not by Alaska town, and they give no local times. This story gives none.

## Where is the Aug. 22, 2044 total solar eclipse visible?

NASA's FAQ page, last updated Apr. 1, 2024, says: "After 2024, the next total solar eclipse visible from any point in the contiguous United States will occur in 2044. Totality will only be visible from North Dakota and Montana." The decade table adds Greenland and northern Canada to the path, and lists a central duration of 2 minutes 4 seconds.

Some news reports add South Dakota as a third state. NASA's own wording names two, and we follow it.

The date needs care. NASA's table says Aug. 23, and its path page gives the instant of greatest eclipse as 01:15:33.6 UT. By our conversion that is 9:15 p.m. Eastern Daylight Time (EDT) on Monday, Aug. 22. Either date refers to the same eclipse.

NASA's longest figure for it, 2 minutes 3.8 seconds, is at 64°34'N, 120°22'W, well north of the U.S. border. NASA's tables do not give a duration for North Dakota or Montana.

## Is the Aug. 12, 2045 total solar eclipse coast to coast?

NASA's FAQ page says the next total eclipse to travel across the lower 48 states from coast to coast is in 2045. The decade table dates it Saturday, Aug. 12 and lists totality in "s US, Haiti, Dominican Rep., Venezuela, Guyana, Fr. Guyana, Suriname, Brazil". The path page gives a longest duration of 6 minutes 6.2 seconds, nearly three times the 2044 figure.

NASA's path pages list coordinates, not state names, so we print no state list for 2045. The interactive map linked from NASA's decade table shows the track.

## When are the next annular solar eclipses in the U.S.?

NASA's table for 2031 to 2040 includes Alaska in the annular path on Tuesday, June 21, 2039, with a central duration of 4 minutes 5 seconds, alongside northern Canada, Norway, Sweden, Finland, Estonia and Russia.

The 2041 to 2050 table lists Monday, Feb. 5, 2046, with an annular path over Papua New Guinea, Hawaii, California, Oregon and Idaho. Its 9 minutes 42 seconds at greatest eclipse and the path page's 9 minutes 45.6 seconds are worldwide maxima, found in the Pacific. The longer one is at 1°56'N, 174°39'W, near the equator and far south of Hawaii. NASA's tables give no annular duration for the U.S. stretch.

NASA's table for Feb. 16, 2045 names Hawaii in the region but lists only New Zealand and the Pacific for the annular path. In NASA's decade tables from 2021 to 2050, Hawaii is named only for that eclipse and for Feb. 5, 2046. None of the total eclipses names Hawaii.

## Can you see a solar eclipse from the U.S. before 2033?

Only partial ones. NASA's Future Eclipses page, updated Sep. 28, 2026, lists the Aug. 2, 2027 total eclipse for southern Spain, North Africa, Saudi Arabia and Yemen, with a partial eclipse in eastern Canada and northern Maine. For the other 2027 eclipse dates, see our [2027 eclipse overview](/news/eclipses-2027-dates-times-total-solar-eclipse-august-2/), and for the Spain and Egypt totality times, the [Aug. 2, 2027 times by city](/news/total-solar-eclipse-august-2-2027-path-times-by-city-travel/). For a full-moon schedule that fits around these dates, see our [2027 moon phases calendar](/news/moon-phases-2027-full-moon-calendar/).

The eclipse of Aug. 12, 2026 is already past. NASA's Aug. 21, 2026 article says only a partial eclipse was visible in northern Maine, where a NASA photographer captured the International Space Station crossing the Sun near Hodgdon.

After 2027, NASA's regions are "e N. America" on Jan. 26, 2028, "N. America" on Jan. 14, 2029, Alaska on June 12, 2029 and June 1, 2030, and "s US" on Nov. 14, 2031. All are partial for the U.S., and NASA's tables give no coverage figures for U.S. cities.
