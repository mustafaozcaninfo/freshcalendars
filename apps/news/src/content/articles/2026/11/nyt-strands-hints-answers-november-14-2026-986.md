**Strands #986 is the Saturday puzzle for Nov. 14, 2026, and The New York Times (NYT) gives the clue "Preschool fun."** This one is a playground game: a line of children copies whoever is out front. Christina Iverson constructed it, edited by Tracy Bennett. The spangram is unusually long today; what it still has to connect is explained on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). Hints come first, and every answer waits at the foot of the page.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: a lineup game where every child copies the first one's moves.
- Hint 2: three words; the last names the child at the front everyone imitates.
- Hint 3: fifteen letters across three words, the longest answer today, starting with F.

## Theme word hints

Each answer is a way to move, listed shortest to longest (two answers tie at four letters, starting with S):

- **4 letters, starts with J:** to push off the ground with both feet and land again.
- **4 letters, starts with S:** to move with light hopping steps on alternating feet.
- **4 letters, starts with S:** to turn round and round in place.
- **4 letters, starts with W:** to go on foot at an ordinary pace.
- **5 letters, starts with M:** to step in strict rhythm, as a band does in a parade.
- **6 letters, starts with G:** to run in bounds, the way a horse does at speed.
- **6 letters, starts with T:** to move quietly on the balls of your feet.

## A long, weaving spangram over seven short words

At fifteen letters the spangram is the longest answer of the week, and it earns that length by weaving. It opens in the fifth row on the left edge and bends eleven times, stepping up into the third row, drifting right in short two-tile hops, then dropping into the bottom-right corner. It touches the left, bottom and right edges and holds no straight line longer than three tiles.

Around that weave sit seven short action words, four of them only four letters. The six-letter G word fills the entire top-right quarter, and the six-letter T word claims the top-left. The two four-letter S words sit at opposite ends, one up top and one in the lower-left corner. The five-letter M word and the four-letter W word share the lower-middle, with the four-letter J word near the bottom. The lower-left quarter is the thickest patch, ten of its twelve tiles themed.

## Solving tips

Four of the seven answers are just four letters, so the board is thick with short words; clear those and the spangram's weave shows through. Trace the long answer last, since its many bends are easiest to follow once the short words are gone.
