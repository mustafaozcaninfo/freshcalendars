**Waffle (wafflegame.net) puzzle #1768, for Tuesday, Nov. 24, 2026, deals 6 green tiles, 7 yellow and 8 gray.** A clue for each of the six words comes first, then the color counts, and the answers wait in the marked last section.

Today's grid leans hard on repeats: 16 of its 21 tiles carry a letter that shows up in more than one square of the finished board, so a yellow tile has several possible homes. The rules and the color meanings are on our [Waffle page](/news/topic/waffle/#how-it-works).

**Across**

- **Row 3:** starts with S and ends with D. A sharp, broken piece of pottery or glass.
- **Row 5:** starts with R and ends with R. The comparative of a word meaning uncommon or scarce.

**Down**

- **Column 1:** starts with R and ends with R. A person who gets out of bed early; also the upright part of a stair step.
- **Column 3:** starts with F and ends with R. A natural knack or stylish talent.
- **Column 5:** starts with R and ends with R. An instrument that finds aircraft and storms by bouncing radio waves off them.

## Why the repeated tiles blur the yellows

The solved grid holds four letters more than once. One fills six squares, two fill four squares each and one fills two. That makes 16 tiles in all, against 5 letters that appear once.

Every green tile belongs to the repeated group. The five single letters all start off position: four gray and one yellow. So a tile you have only one of is never a free pass; it always needs a move.

Gray is also unreliable here. Four of the gray tiles carry repeated letters, so gray does not mean the letter has no job; it can mean that the nearby squares already have their copy. The six-square letter is the sharpest case: four of its tiles are home, one of the other two is gray and the other is yellow, yet two squares still wait for it. The squares are named in the last section.

### Why 10 is the minimum

Fifteen tiles start out of place. A loop of n tiles costs n minus 1 swaps, so swaps equal misplaced tiles minus loops. Here the 15 tiles form 5 loops (one of four tiles, three of three and one of two), which leaves 10 swaps, and no route can use fewer.
