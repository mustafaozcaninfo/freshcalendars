**Yalda Night 2026 is the night of Monday, Dec. 21 into Tuesday, Dec. 22, the last night of autumn, and the winter solstice that marks it falls at 20:50 Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) on Dec. 21, according to the U.S. Naval Observatory (USNO).** That is 3:50 p.m. ET on Monday in the United States and 12:20 a.m. on Tuesday, Dec. 22 in Tehran. Zone-by-zone times and Tehran's 14-hour night follow.

## What date is Yalda Night in 2026?

Yalda is the last night of autumn, the eve of 1 Dey in the Iranian solar (Solar Hijri) calendar. By our arithmetic, 30 Azar 1405 is Monday, Dec. 21, 2026, and 1 Dey 1405 is Tuesday, Dec. 22. Yalda Night 2026 is therefore the night of Dec. 21 into Dec. 22. This date is computed by Fresh Calendars; Iran's Calendar Council website could not be reached, so no official entry is quoted. The result fits the solstice: in Tehran it falls at 12:20 a.m. on Dec. 22, so the night of Dec. 21 is the last of autumn.

The United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO) inscribed Yaldā/Chella on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2022, at the 17th session of its intergovernmental committee in Rabat. The file, No. 01877, was submitted by the Islamic Republic of Iran and Afghanistan. UNESCO describes it as "a traditional celebration of the sun and the warmth of life."

## Why is the solstice on Monday afternoon in the U.S. and after midnight in Tehran?

The solstice is one instant, 20:50 UTC. Adding 3 hours 30 minutes moves it past midnight in Tehran, which turns Monday into Tuesday. Subtracting five hours gives Eastern Time (EST) its Monday afternoon. ## What happens on Yalda Night?

UNESCO writes that families gather at the houses of elders on the last night of autumn. Its file lists red fruits "such as pomegranates, watermelons, beetroots, jujube and grapes" among the foods, and the evening includes "reciting poetry and storytelling." No official source on public-holiday status for Yalda was found, so none is given.
