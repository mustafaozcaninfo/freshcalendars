**The next Powerball drawing is Monday, Nov. 2, 2026, at 10:59 p.m. Eastern Time (ET), with an estimated jackpot of $793 million (cash value $339.9 million), according to powerball.com as of Nov. 1.** That is 9:59 p.m. Central Time (CT), 8:59 p.m. Mountain Time (MT) and 7:59 p.m. Pacific Time (PT). ## What time is the Powerball drawing?

Powerball says drawings are held every Monday, Wednesday and Saturday at 10:59 p.m. ET at the Florida Lottery draw studio in Tallahassee. U.S. clocks went back an hour on Nov. 1, so all four mainland zones are on standard time for the Nov. 2 drawing.

## What is the Powerball jackpot for Nov. 2?

powerball.com lists an estimated jackpot of $793 million with a cash value of $339.9 million. It describes the annuity as paid in 30 graduated payments over 29 years. Georgia Lottery lists the jackpot odds as 1 in 292,201,338. Florida Lottery lists overall odds of 1 in 24.87 for any prize.

A play costs $2. For an extra $1 per play, Power Play multiplies non-jackpot prizes by 2X, 3X, 4X, 5X or 10X. Double Play, also $1 extra, gives another chance in a separate drawing, and only some states offer it. powerball.com says rules differ in Idaho and Montana.

## When do you have to buy a Powerball ticket by?

The drawing is at 10:59 p.m. ET, but sales close earlier. powerball.com says sales cut-off times vary by one to two hours before the drawing, depending on where you buy. Two examples:

- **Florida:** tickets may be purchased until 10:00 p.m. ET on the night of the drawing, per the Florida Lottery.
- **Georgia:** the Georgia Lottery stops selling Powerball tickets at 10 p.m. on draw nights.

For other states, ask the retailer or check your state lottery.

## How long do you have to claim a lottery prize?

In Florida, Georgia and Texas the limit is 180 days: the Florida Lottery says Powerball prizes must be claimed within 180 days after the winning drawing date, Georgia gives 180 days, and the Texas Lottery says tickets must be claimed no later than 180 days after the draw date. Other states set their own claim periods, so read your own lottery's rules and sign the back of your ticket as it directs.

Lottery age limits are set by each state. If gambling is a problem for you or someone you know, the National Council on Problem Gambling helpline is 1-800-Gambler, and its site is ncpgambling.org.
