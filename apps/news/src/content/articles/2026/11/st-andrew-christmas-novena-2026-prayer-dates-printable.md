The St. Andrew Christmas Novena runs from the feast of St. Andrew on Nov. 30 to Christmas Eve, Dec. 24, with one short prayer repeated 15 times a day, according to the Catholic Review, the publication of the Archdiocese of Baltimore. The Archdiocese of Brisbane gives the same start: the novena "begins each year on the Feast of St Andrew, November 30." The United States Conference of Catholic Bishops (USCCB) lists the Feast of Saint Andrew, Apostle, on November 30.

In 2026, Nov. 30 is a Monday and Dec. 24 is a Thursday. Both dates are inclusive, so there are 25 prayer days and 25 x 15 = 375 recitations (our count). Christmas is Friday, Dec. 25, and is not a prayer day.

## What is the St. Andrew Christmas Novena prayer?

The Catholic Review prints the prayer as: "Hail and blessed be the hour and moment in which the Son of God was born / Of the most pure Virgin Mary, at midnight, in Bethlehem, in the piercing cold. / In that hour vouchsafe, I beseech thee, O my God, to hear my prayer and grant my desires Through the merits of Our Savior Jesus Christ, and of his Blessed Mother. Amen."

## How do the two published wordings differ?

The Catholic Review and the Archdiocese of Brisbane print the same prayer with small differences:

| Phrase | Catholic Review | Archdiocese of Brisbane |
|---|---|---|
| The Virgin | "the most pure Virgin Mary" | "the most pure Virgin" |
| The Mother | "and of his Blessed Mother" | "of His blessed Mother" |
| Address to God | "I beseech thee" | "I beseech Thee" |

Parish booklets may print other versions; the prayer's origin is unknown, so no wording here is the original.

## How do you pray it 15 times a day?

The Catholic Review says the 15 repetitions can be said "either all at once or throughout the day." Our suggestion, not a rule from either source: 5 in the morning, 5 at noon and 5 in the evening, with a mark for each one on the printable.

## Where does the novena come from?

The Catholic Review says the novena "has an obscure history." Historian Rachel Fulton Brown, quoted by the Catholic Review, said the 15 repetitions "likely stem from" a medieval monastic tradition of reciting 15 psalms associated with Mary's climb up the temple steps, and "there is evidence that the novena sprung from a movement to revive church tradition in the 19th century." The Catholic Review also says the novena "has a reputation for answered prayers." Unlike a traditional novena of nine days, it lasts from Nov. 30 to Christmas Eve.
