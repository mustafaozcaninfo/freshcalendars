**Dry January 2027 covers 31 days, from Friday, Jan. 1 to Sunday, Jan. 31**, and our free one-page tracker has a box for every one of them. The challenge is run by Alcohol Change UK, a United Kingdom (UK) charity that, as of Nov. 4, 2026, had not announced its 2027 campaign; its pages still refer to the 2026 challenge.

## Which days make up Dry January 2027?

January 1, 2027 is a Friday and January 31 is a Sunday. By our count, that gives 31 days, with five Fridays, five Saturdays and five Sundays, and four each of the other weekdays. The table matches the rows on the tracker.

| Week | Dates | Days | Weekdays |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jan. 1-3 | 3 | Friday to Sunday |
| 2 | Jan. 4-10 | 7 | Monday to Sunday |
| 3 | Jan. 11-17 | 7 | Monday to Sunday |
| 4 | Jan. 18-24 | 7 | Monday to Sunday |
| 5 | Jan. 25-31 | 7 | Monday to Sunday |

The five weekends are Jan. 2-3, 9-10, 16-17, 23-24 and 30-31.

## Who runs Dry January, and is it free?

Alcohol Change UK states that "The Dry January® challenge is brought to you by Alcohol Change UK." It describes itself as the operating name of Alcohol Research UK, a registered charity. The charity's page says you can "Get the Try Dry® app for free and track your journey" and "Sign up for free daily motivational emails." The app lets you set goals and gives personalised progress monitoring, tips, inspiration and support throughout January, and it can track money saved.

We found no US version or US partner on the charity's pages, and no 2027 page yet. Check the [charity's Dry January page](https://alcoholchange.org.uk/help-and-support/managing-your-drinking/dry-january) for the 2027 details once they are posted.

## How many people have signed up for Dry January?

The charity's own story page says the challenge started in 2013 with 4,000 people. These are the sign-up figures it prints for later years; they are the charity's numbers, and we did not check them independently. The charity published no 2026 total that we found.

| Year | Sign-ups, per Alcohol Change UK |
|---|---|
| 2013 | 4,000 |
| 2019 | Almost 80,000 |
| 2020 | Over 100,000 |
| 2021 | Almost 130,000 |
| 2022 | Over 130,000 |
| 2023 | 175,000 |
| 2025 | 200,000, globally |

In a December 2025 blog post, the charity cited a Censuswide poll that found 17.5 million people in Britain, almost one in three (32%), planned to take a month off alcohol in January 2026. That is a plan reported in a UK poll, not a count of people who took part. The same post says Try Dry users "can double their chances of achieving a fully dry month," which is the charity's claim.

## What does the research say about Dry January?

Alcohol Change UK's story page quotes University of Sussex research from 2013 by Dr Richard de Visser: "six months after the campaign has finished, seven out of ten people have continued to drink less riskily than before." We report it as the charity's page quotes it and make no health claims of our own.

## How big is one drink, and who should talk to a doctor first?

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) says that in the United States a standard drink has 0.6 ounces (14 grams) of pure alcohol (About Standard Drink Sizes, Dec. 12, 2024).

A safety note: the National Institute on Alcohol Abuse and Alcoholism (NIAAA) says that for people with alcohol dependence "the body can go into a painful or even potentially life-threatening process of withdrawal," and advises: "Seek medical help to plan a safe recovery." Anyone who drinks heavily every day should talk to a doctor before stopping. The Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA) runs a free National Helpline at 1-800-662-HELP (4357).
