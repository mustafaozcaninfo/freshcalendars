## How to use the 365-day grid

1. Print one sheet for each habit and write the habit on the Habit line.
2. Pick a mark (a tick, a dot or a fill) and use it the same way all year.
3. Mark the box under each day you do the habit, in the row of that month.
4. On each Monday, read the ISO week number to review a full week at a time.
5. At the end of a month, count the marks in the row and write the total in the margin.
6. Keep the sheet next to a [printable weekly planner](/printable-weekly-calendar.html) if you want to plan the week as well.

## How many days, weekdays and weekends are in 2027?

2027 is a common year of 365 days, because 2027 is not divisible by 4. By our count, it has 261 weekdays and 104 weekend days. The table shows each month and the ISO weeks it touches; the sheet puts the same numbers on the page.

## Where do ISO week numbers start in 2027?

ISO 8601, published by the International Organization for Standardization, starts weeks on Monday, and week 1 is the week that contains the year's first Thursday. The GOV.UK Technology blog puts it the same way: "The first week of the year is the week containing the first Thursday in January." In 2027 the first Thursday is Jan. 7, so week 1 runs from Monday, Jan. 4 to Sunday, Jan. 10.

That leaves Friday, Jan. 1 to Sunday, Jan. 3 in week 53 of 2026, which is why the first Monday on the sheet carries a 1 and the first three boxes carry none. The last ISO week of 2027, week 52, starts on Monday, Dec. 27 and ends on Sunday, Jan. 2, 2028. Microsoft's ISOWEEKNUM function returns the same ISO numbers in Excel; our [2027 week numbers guide](/news/week-numbers-2027-calendar-printable/) compares them with the U.S. Sunday-start count.

## Which federal holidays are marked on the 2027 tracker?

The OPM lists 11 federal holidays for 2027. Three fall on a weekend, so the sheet marks the observed weekday that OPM prints instead: Juneteenth National Independence Day on Friday, June 18 (June 19 is a Saturday), Independence Day on Monday, July 5 (July 4 is a Sunday) and Christmas Day on Friday, Dec. 24 (Dec. 25 is a Saturday). The ISO week column is our computation.
