## What does "Gateway to the West" say about the grid?

The title names a place, not a word, so nothing in it fits into a square. The clue for 17-Across is where it pays off: it asks for the home of a monument that today's circled squares suggest when read clockwise from the lower left. The saved data holds letters and clues only, so this page cannot show which squares carry circles. What it can show is the entry the clue wants. It is 7 letters long, it fills row 6 from column 2 to column 8, and it begins and ends with S. That row is the one to watch.

## Which clue is the least literal today?

12-Across, *Like a barista named Bean or a fashionista named Jean*. The clue never defines its answer. It hands you two names that echo a job, coffee for one and denim for the other, and leaves you to supply the three-letter adjective that describes a name which suits its owner. Think of the word you would use for a fitting name, then check it against the Down entries through the row: it begins with A and ends with T.

## Where should you start on a grid with this shape?

Rows 1 to 3 hold two solid blocks of white squares. The left one is 3 rows by 5 columns and the right one is 3 by 3, so 1-Across, 9-Across and 11-Across all cross the same five Down entries, 1-Down to 5-Down. Filling one of those three gives a letter to the other two, and the short ones in the top-right block work the same way for 6-Down, 7-Down and 8-Down. The bottom-right corner is a third 3-by-3 block.

The three long Down entries, 3-Down, 4-Down and 6-Down, all pass through row 6, which is why 17-Across is crossed by seven answers. Once two or three of its crossings are in, the 7-letter entry comes together faster, and it hands a letter back to each long Down answer.
