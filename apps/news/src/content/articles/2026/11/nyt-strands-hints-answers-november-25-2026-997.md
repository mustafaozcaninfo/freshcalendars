**Strands #997 from The New York Times (NYT) is the Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026 puzzle, and its clue is "Handmade gift for a newborn."** Tracy Bennett is listed as constructor and editor. The 12-letter spangram is longer than any of the seven theme words, and the rules are on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). Graded hints come first, then a map of the board, then the answers behind a spoiler warning.

The clue points to one keepsake, and the theme words are things you could attach to it or feel when giving it.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: A keepsake made to hang near a sleeper. Six of the seven theme words are pieces you could find on it.
- Hint 2: It has 12 letters, begins with D and ends with R, and it is two words joined. Four different letters each appear twice.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 1, column 4, on the top edge, and the first step goes straight down.

## Theme word hints

Seven words fit the clue, listed by length, shortest first:

- **4 letters, starts with B, ends with D:** a small round piece with a hole through it, made for threading.
- **4 letters, starts with H, ends with P:** a ring shape, like the one a basketball goal has.
- **4 letters, starts with L, ends with E:** the feeling behind a handmade present. Its second and last letters are both vowels.
- **5 letters, starts with C, ends with M:** a small trinket that dangles from a bracelet.
- **6 letters, starts with R, ends with N:** a strip of fabric for tying a bow on a gift, with a double letter in the middle.
- **6 letters, starts with S, ends with G:** a thin cord for tying or hanging things. Its last three letters are a common verb ending.
- **7 letters, starts with F, ends with R:** what covers a bird's wing, light and soft. One letter appears twice.

## Start-cell hints

Rows count from the top and columns from the left. The first step is the direction from a word's first tile to its second. No word is named here.

- **4 letters, start cell row 1, column 2:** the first step goes straight down.
- **4 letters, start cell row 2, column 6:** the first step goes straight up.
- **4 letters, start cell row 6, column 3:** the first step goes straight right.
- **5 letters, start cell row 5, column 6:** the first step goes straight up.
- **6 letters, start cell row 8, column 6:** the first step goes straight left.
- **6 letters, start cell row 1, column 3:** the first step goes straight down.
- **7 letters, start cell row 5, column 2:** the first step goes diagonally up and to the left.

## Where the Nov. 25 spangram runs, by row and column

The spangram opens on the top edge at row 1, column 4 and drops straight down to row 2, column 4. Then it zigzags: row 3, column 3, back to row 4, column 4, then row 5, column 3 and row 6, column 2. One more step down reaches row 7, column 2, a diagonal lands at row 8, column 3, and it turns right along the bottom edge to row 8, column 4. The last three steps climb: diagonally up to row 7, column 5, straight up to row 6, column 5, and diagonally up and left to the final tile at row 5, column 4. Seven of the 11 steps are diagonal and four are straight.

It meets the top edge once and the bottom edge twice, and it never gets to columns 1 or 6. Column 4 holds five of its tiles, column 3 holds three, and columns 2 and 5 hold two each. Rows 1 to 4 have one spangram tile apiece, while rows 5 to 8 have two apiece, so the lower half carries 8 of the 12.

That lower loop closes around a pocket. The tiles in rows 6 and 7, columns 3 and 4 form a 2-by-2 square, and the spangram sits directly above, left, below and right of it. The other two 4-letter words are also 2-by-2 squares, one in the top-left corner and one in the top-right corner, so all three are blocks, not lines.

Cutting the board into four quarters of 12 tiles (rows 1 to 4 or 5 to 8, columns 1 to 3 or 4 to 6) shows where the theme words gather:

| Quarter | Spangram tiles | Theme-word tiles | Which theme words reach it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top left | 1 | 11 | the top-left 4-letter word (4 tiles), the 6-letter word from row 1 (6), the 7-letter word (1) |
| Top right | 3 | 9 | the top-right 4-letter word (4), the 5-letter word (4), the 6-letter word from row 8 (1) |
| Bottom left | 4 | 8 | the 7-letter word (6), the walled-in 4-letter word (2) |
| Bottom right | 4 | 8 | the 6-letter word from row 8 (5), the 5-letter word (1), the walled-in 4-letter word (2) |

The top-left quarter is the most crowded with theme words, and the spangram barely enters it. The bottom half is where the spangram does its work, so the theme words there have the least room.

## Solving tips

Look for squares first. Three of the seven words are 4 letters, and each is a 2-by-2 block, so two corner squares are easy targets. The 7-letter word sits down the left side and the 6-letter word from row 8 climbs the right side, which leaves the middle for the spangram.
