## How many pips each Pips board has to spend

Add every half of every domino and compare it with what the sum regions demand. Whatever is left over must go into the equal, less-than and more-than regions.

| Level | Pips on the dominoes | Sum regions | Pips they require | Left for other regions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Easy | 34 | 1 | 7 | 27 |
| Medium | 38 | 5 | 17 | 21 |
| Hard | 96 | 19 | 72 | 24 |

Hard is the tight one. Its only non-sum regions are two four-cell equal regions, so the 24 spare pips split as four copies of one value plus four copies of another, and those two values add up to 6. On Medium the 21 spare pips sit in three equal pairs, which each add an even number, plus the single cell that must be more than 4. An even total plus that cell makes 21, so the cell is odd, and a value above 4 that is odd leaves exactly one candidate.

## Where each Pips board starts, Nov. 24

Cells are named by row, then column, from the top left. A search of every legal placement against the saved data finds one arrangement on Easy, four on Medium and two on Hard.

**Easy.** R1C1 touches only R2C1, and R2C1 belongs to a row of three equal cells. Only one value shows on three halves of the set, so the row has to use it. The double covers two cells of the row and one more half fills the third. If the double stood across R1C1 and R2C1, the other two row cells would need two more halves of that value, and only one remains. So a different domino crosses from R1C1 into R2C1, and it carries the lone half of that value.

**Medium.** Three single cells narrow the tiles fast. R3C3 must add up to 3, and exactly one half in the set shows a 3, on the 3-1. R4C1 and R4C4 must each add up to 2, and exactly two halves show a 2. The 1-1 reads the same turned around, so it adds no extra arrangement.

**Hard.** R1C1 and R2C1 add up to 12, and no half is above 6, so both hold a 6. R1C2 must add up to 0 and sits between R1C1, R1C3 (adds up to 3) and R2C2 (adds up to 4). Three dominoes carry a 0: the 5-0, the 2-0 and the 6-0. Only the 6-0 has a partner half that one of those neighbors can take. The other 28 of Hard's 30 cells are identical in both arrangements.

**Easy (five dominoes: 6-4, 1-2, 5-6, 3-3, 2-2)**

- Hint 1: R1C1 takes any value but has a single neighbor, R2C1.
- Hint 2: R2C1, R2C2 and R2C3 are equal, and just one value appears on three halves in the set.

**Medium (seven dominoes: 5-6, 1-1, 1-2, 4-1, 0-6, 2-5, 3-1)**

- Hint 1: R4C1 and R4C4 each add up to 2, and the set has exactly two halves showing a 2.
- Hint 2: R3C3 adds up to 3, and only one half in the set shows a 3.

**Hard (15 dominoes on a board 10 rows tall)**

- Hint 1: R1C1 and R2C1 add up to 12, and the biggest half in the set is a 6.
- Hint 2: R1C2, R5C1 and R6C2 each add up to 0, and the set has exactly three halves showing a 0.

## Why Hard's equal regions decide Tuesday's second half

Hard's two equal regions are the only places on the board where a value can repeat across four cells: R3C1, R3C2, R4C1 and R4C2 in the upper half, and R9C1, R9C2, R9C3 and R10C1 at the bottom. Six halves show a 1 and five show a 5, and the two regions together need eight halves of two values that add up to 6. Only the values 1 and 5 have enough halves to fill a region of four and add up to 6. Easy has the other kind of squeeze: its single free cell is the only one with no rule, and everything beside it is bound by the equal row.
