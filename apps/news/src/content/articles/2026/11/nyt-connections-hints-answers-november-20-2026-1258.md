## Solving tips

When a tile shares no meaning with any other tile, stop reading it for sense and start reading its ending. That is how a group built on a hidden word shows itself: the four entries have nothing in common until you look at the last letters. Compare their lengths with the hints above, too.

## Why each red herring nearly fits

Beast and Spot are the animal-flavored lures. Beast is a creature word, so it drifts toward the small-animal group, and Spot is a classic name for a pet dog. Neither describes a small animal; one names a skilled person and the other is a way to notice something.

Cowslip carries the word Cow at its front, which invites a farm-animal reading. The group it belongs to cares about the other end of the word.

Toy and Ace are both three letters, the shortest entries, and a quick sorter may pair them for that reason alone. Toy sits with the small-animal descriptors; Ace sits with the people who excel.

Pocket and Clock are both objects, and Pocket can also be a verb, so each can hover near the verbs for noticing. Pocket describes a size here, and Clock is one of the noticing verbs. Register is the same kind of double agent, since a register is also a till.
