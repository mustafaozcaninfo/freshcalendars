**The New York Times (NYT) Crossword for Thursday, Nov. 26, 2026, is by Sam Koperwas and Jeff Chen and edited by Will Shortz: 76 clues, 34 Across and 42 Down, in a 15-by-15 grid.** It runs on Thanksgiving Day. Two clues end in a question mark, four long entries cross the middle rows, and every clue has a hint behind a tap; the answers close the page.

## Two question marks and two quieter tricks

Only 2 of the 76 clues end in "?", the usual flag for wordplay. Thursday grids sometimes hide a multi-letter square, but this one does not: every cell holds one letter. Each trick below is explained without its answer.

- **29-Across, "Litter pickup sites?" (5 letters).** "Litter" is a brood of newborn animals here, not trash. "Pickup" is the way a parent carries one of them, so the "sites" are places on the body. The answer is plural.
- **41-Across, "Give out one's address?" (5 letters).** "Address" means a speech, not a street or a web location. "Give out" turns into "deliver", and the answer is a verb.
- **42-Down, "Yellow" (6 letters).** No question mark, but the clue is a one-word pun. The color is a figure of speech for a feeling, and the answer is a word for how someone feels when scared.
- **14-Across, "You could probably skip it" (5 letters).** The verb "skip" is literal: something you throw so that it bounces over water.

## Reading the 15-by-15 grid: lengths, black squares and Wednesday compared

The grid is 15 columns by 15 rows, 225 squares in all: 43 black and 182 holding a letter, a black share of 19.1 percent. The black pattern is unchanged by a half-turn. Wednesday's [Nov. 25 Crossword page](/news/nyt-crossword-answers-november-25-2026/) described a 16-by-15 grid with 34 black squares (14.2 percent) and 79 clues, so Thursday has 9 more black squares, 24 fewer letter squares and 3 fewer clues.
