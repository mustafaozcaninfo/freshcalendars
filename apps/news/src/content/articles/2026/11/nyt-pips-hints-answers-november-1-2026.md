## How Pips works

You place every domino so that each colored region meets its condition. A number means the pips in the region add up to it; = means every half shows the same value; ≠ means all are different; > and < set a total above or below a number; a region with no condition takes anything. Rows are counted from the top and columns from the left, so R2C5 is the second row, fifth column.

## Sunday's Pips hints, Nov. 1, 2026

**Easy (five dominoes: 4-3, 6-5, 5-4, 1-5, 5-5)**

- Hint 1: R1C5 and R4C1 must each add up to 4, so each holds a 4. The 5-4 and 4-3 dominoes supply them.
- Hint 2: R5C3 must be more than 4, so it holds a 5 or a 6. The 6-5 domino fills R5C2 and R5C3.

**Medium (seven dominoes: 1-3, 4-5, 6-6, 0-3, 3-3, 4-6, 2-6)**

- Hint 1: R2C4 must add up to 0, so it is a blank. The 0-3 domino is the only one with a 0.
- Hint 2: R2C3, R3C2, R3C3 and R3C4 are equal and all hold 6s. They come from the 6-6, 4-6 and 2-6 dominoes.

**Hard (sixteen dominoes on a six-row board)**

- Hint 1: R2C1, R3C4, R3C6, R4C1 and R4C4 must each add up to 0. The five 0 ends belong to the 0-1, 0-2, 0-3, 0-4 and 0-5 dominoes.
- Hint 2: R2C2 and R2C3 add up to 8 and both hold 4s. They come from the 1-4 and 2-4 dominoes.

## Solving tips

Look for single cells with a sum target: a cell that must add up to 0, 3 or 5 can only hold that value. An "equal" region shrinks your options fast once one value is known; with four cells, as in Sunday's Medium board, you need four matching halves, and a double can supply two of them. On the Hard board, count the blanks: there are five 0 halves among the 16 dominoes, and five single cells that need a 0.
