**Christmas decorations traditionally come down by Twelfth Night, which falls on Tuesday, Jan. 5, 2027, the eve of Epiphany; the National Trust says Christmas would then last 12 days, "ending with feasting and revels on the 5th of January."** English Heritage gives Epiphany as 6 January, a Wednesday in 2027, and U.S. Catholic dioceses keep it on Sunday, Jan. 3, according to the 2027 liturgical calendar of the U.S. Conference of Catholic Bishops (USCCB). ## Twelfth Night or Epiphany: Jan. 5 or Jan. 6?

The National Trust describes Twelfth Night as "the eve of Epiphany in the Christian calendar," so the two days sit side by side: the revels on Tuesday, Jan. 5 and Epiphany itself on Wednesday, Jan. 6. English Heritage writes that the tradition "continued until the Epiphany on 6 January." Neither source is wrong; they name the evening and the day.

For U.S. Catholics the day moves. The USCCB calendar places the Epiphany of the Lord, a solemnity, on the Sunday between Jan. 2 and Jan. 8, which in 2027 is Sunday, Jan. 3. It lists Wednesday, Jan. 6 as a Christmas weekday. The Baptism of the Lord, a feast, follows on Sunday, Jan. 10. Orthodox Christmas has its own dates, covered in [our Orthodox Christmas 2027 story](/news/orthodox-christmas-2027-january-7-three-kings-day-january-6/).

## Is it bad luck to leave decorations up after Twelfth Night?

English Heritage says no. In its press release of Jan. 5, 2021, it wrote: "The tradition that it is bad luck to keep decorations up after Twelfth Night and the Epiphany is a modern invention." Its Dr Michael Carter says: "Contrary to popular belief, the Christmas season actually continues right through to Candlemas on 2 February."

## Candlemas, Feb. 2: the medieval end of Christmas

English Heritage says Candlemas "was observed as the official end of Christmas in medieval England," and suggests readers "follow medieval tradition and keep your Christmas decorations up until 2 February." In 2027 that is a Tuesday. It also quotes Robert Herrick (1591-1674), whose poem "Ceremony Upon Candlemas Eve" has the line "So many goblins you shall see."

## What to do with a real Christmas tree

The New York City Department of Sanitation (DSNY) said on Dec. 24, 2025 that "city residents no longer must follow a limited schedule to get rid of their real Christmas trees," and the New York City curbside answer is the regular collection day. Last season's Mulchfest ran from Dec. 26, 2025 through Jan. 11, 2026; the 2026-27 Mulchfest dates are not yet announced. For other cities, no 2026-27 tree dates appear in the sources used here, so check your city's sanitation page.
