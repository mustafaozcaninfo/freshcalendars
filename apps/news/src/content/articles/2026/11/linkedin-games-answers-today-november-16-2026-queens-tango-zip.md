## Pinpoint and Crossclimb nudges for Monday, Nov. 16

- **Pinpoint #930, first nudge:** all five clues are things you apply, and none of them is worn like clothing.
- **Pinpoint #930, second nudge:** they go on the face. One is a base layer, one hides blemishes, one adds color to the cheeks, and the last two are for the eyes.
- **Pinpoint #930, third nudge:** the category opens with "Types of" and closes with a single six-letter word for products of this kind.
- **Crossclimb #930, the ladder:** every word has four letters, which is one fewer than Sunday's ladder.
- **Crossclimb #930, the locked ends:** one is a word you might shout when you need assistance, and the other is a row of people waiting or a stroke of a pen.
- **Crossclimb #930, the middle:** five words sit between the ends, and the very first move changes only the last letter.

## Reading the Crossclimb #930 ladder without the words

Seven words of four letters, six moves, one letter changed per move, positions counted from the left, 1 to 4. No word appears in this section.

| Move | Letter position that changes |
|---|---|
| Top row to rung 1 | 4 |
| Rung 1 to rung 2 | 1 |
| Rung 2 to rung 3 | 2 |
| Rung 3 to rung 4 | 3 |
| Rung 4 to rung 5 | 4 |
| Rung 5 to bottom row | 1 |

Positions 1 and 4 each change twice, positions 2 and 3 once each. Unlike Sunday, no position stays untouched. Moves 1 and 5 work on the last letter and moves 2 and 6 on the first, and the middle two letters are swapped one after the other in moves 3 and 4.

## How far apart the Crossclimb #930 locked ends sit

The two locked words share two letters. Not one of those letters occupies the same place in both words, so all four positions differ between the ends. Today's ladder has five middle rungs, so it takes six moves to cover four differing positions. The two extra moves land on the first and last letters, which each change twice.

## Starting points for Queens #930, Tango #770, Zip #609 and Mini Sudoku #462

**Queens #930.** Find two regions that between them fit in only two rows; those rows hold no crown outside those regions.

**Tango #770.** A line that already has its full count of one symbol must be filled with the other symbol everywhere else.

**Zip #609.** Trace the path backward from the highest number, since the end of the route often has fewer options than the start.

**Mini Sudoku #462.** Scan one row or column at a time and look for the cell that has only a single digit left.
