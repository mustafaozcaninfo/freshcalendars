## The first domino each Tuesday board forces

Cells are named by row, then column, counted from the top left, so R1C1 is the top-left cell.

**Easy.** R2C5 must be less than 6, and the only cell touching it is R2C4. So those two cells form one domino. R2C4 sits in a region with R1C4 that must total more than 10, which means R2C4 is at least 5. A 6 would only come from 6-6, putting a 6 in R2C5, so the domino is 5-2 with the 5 in R2C4. That makes R1C4 a 6.

**Medium.** The three cells R2C6, R3C6 and R3C5 must be equal, so one value has to appear on three halves. Only 3 (four halves) and 4 (three halves) do. Trying both against the neighboring regions leaves the 4, so 4-4 lies across R3C5 and R3C6 and the 2-4 domino puts its 4 in R2C6.

**Hard.** R10C5, R10C6 and R10C7 must add up to 0, so all three are zeros. R10C7 has just one neighbor, R10C6, and the pair therefore takes the 0-0 domino. R10C5 then pairs upward with R9C5.

## Pips hints for Nov. 17, 2026

**Easy (five dominoes: 3-3, 5-2, 1-1, 6-6, 2-4)**

- Hint 1: R2C1, R3C1 and R4C1 add up to 4. No domino today has a 0, so none of those cells can hold more than a 2.
- Hint 2: R1C4 and R2C4 total more than 10, so at least one of them is a 6.

**Medium (seven dominoes: 0-3, 3-5, 2-4, 4-4, 1-0, 2-3, 3-1)**

- Hint 1: R1C5 and R1C6 add up to 2, so neither one can hold a value above 2.
- Hint 2: R2C1 is more than 0 and R3C2 is more than 3, so R3C2 holds a 4 or a 5.

**Hard (sixteen dominoes on a ten-row board)**

- Hint 1: The 0 appears on seven halves. Three of them are already taken by the bottom-row region that adds up to 0.
- Hint 2: R2C4, R3C4, R3C5 and R4C4 must show one value. Four equal cells rule out 2 and 6, which appear on fewer than four halves.
