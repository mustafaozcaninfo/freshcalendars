## The forced first move on each board

Cells are named by row, then column, from the top left. A search of every legal placement against the saved data finds a single arrangement for Easy, eight for Medium and 16 for Hard. The cells below hold the same number in all of them.

**Easy.** R4C1 and R5C1 add up to 3, and the set has one 3, on the 6-3 domino. A 3 plus a 0 is the only way to reach 3 with this set. The 3 cannot sit at R5C1: its partner would then land on R5C2, which must be less than 5, and the 6 breaks that. So the 6-3 domino starts at R4C1 and reaches up into R3C1, and the cell left under it takes a 0.

**Medium.** R4C3 and R1C5 each add up to 2 alone, and the set holds exactly two 2s, so 4-2 and 5-2 are both spoken for. That leaves 4-5 for R3C1, which adds up to 4 by itself. Its 5 could land on R3C2 or R4C1, and only R4C1 survives the search, which also fixes R4C2 through the equal pair.

**Hard.** R3C3 adds up to 6 on its own, and the set carries one 6, on the 6-2 domino. That domino opens the board at R3C3 and the search puts its 2 at R4C3, below it. Two more one-cell sums, R5C6 and R7C1, add up to 1, and the set has two 1s, so both of them are used there.

## Pips hints for Nov. 29, 2026

**Easy (five dominoes: 0-2, 4-4, 6-3, 5-2, 0-5)**

- Hint 1: The set has a single 3, and R4C1 with R5C1 must add up to 3.
- Hint 2: R1C3, R1C4 and R2C4 add up to 13, and 4-4 is the only double in the set.

**Medium (seven dominoes: 5-1, 4-2, 6-5, 5-2, 3-6, 6-6, 4-5)**

- Hint 1: R4C3 and R1C5 each add up to 2, and the set has exactly two 2s.
- Hint 2: R3C1 adds up to 4 by itself, and a 4 appears only in 4-2 and 4-5.

**Hard (16 dominoes on a frame 10 rows by 8 columns)**

- Hint 1: R3C3 adds up to 6, and 6-2 holds the only 6 in the set.
- Hint 2: R5C6 and R7C1 each add up to 1, and the set has two 1s, in 1-0 and 2-1.

## Which Pips dominoes can be turned around on Nov. 29

On Easy, no domino swaps ends without breaking a rule, and the board has one arrangement in all. Medium has none that turn alone either, but it still has eight arrangements: three pairs of cells trade their values, R2C2 with R2C3, R2C4 with R2C5 and R3C4 with R3C5. Hard is the loosest board. Four of its dominoes can be turned end for end with every region still satisfied, which is why it has 16 arrangements. The placements below print the Times' own data for each domino.
