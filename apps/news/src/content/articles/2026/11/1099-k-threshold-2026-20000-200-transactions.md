**The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) says a payment app or online marketplace must report your 2026 payments on Form 1099-K only when they total more than $20,000 and come in more than 200 transactions.** The test is in the "Instructions for Form 1099-K (12/2026)," and both conditions must be met. Below is a table we computed for example sellers, who sends the form, and the 2027 dates with their weekdays, including the Monday, Feb. 1, 2027 date for copies sent to recipients.

## What is the Form 1099-K threshold for 2026?

We read the IRS pages in this story on Nov. 6, 2026. The instructions say a third party settlement organization (TPSO), the company that settles payments between buyers and sellers on an app or marketplace, reports a payee only if for the calendar year "the gross amount of total reportable payment transactions exceeds $20,000" and "the total number of such transactions exceeds 200." IRS Publication 1099 (2026) puts it the same way: over $20,000 in total payments and over 200 in total transactions.

The IRS consumer page "Understanding your Form 1099-K," reviewed Oct. 28, 2026, says the same for payment apps and online marketplaces and adds that they "may send you a Form 1099-K with lower amounts and/or transactions."

The instructions count the gross amount "without regard to any adjustments for credits, cash equivalents, discount amounts, fees, refunded amounts, shipping amounts, or any other amounts." A refunded sale still counts toward the $20,000 and 200 tests.

### Why some sources still say $600

The IRS newsroom release IR-2025-107, dated Oct. 23, 2025, says the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), Public Law 119-21, "retroactively reinstated the reporting threshold in effect prior to the passage of the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021" (ARPA). Section 70432 of the law is titled "Repeal of revision to de minimis rules for third party network transactions." The 2025 General Instructions for Certain Information Returns still say $600 for 2026. That text predates the law; the 2026 Form 1099-K instructions and Publication 1099 (2026) carry $20,000 and 200 transactions.

## Will an app send you a 1099-K for 2026? Examples

We applied the IRS rule to sample sellers on a payment app. The rule is "exceeds" for both numbers, so landing exactly on a limit does not trigger the form. The app may still send one, as the IRS notes above.

| Payments received in 2026 | Transactions | Required to send a form? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| $25,000 | 150 | No | Over $20,000, but 150 is not over 200 |
| $18,000 | 400 | No | Over 200, but $18,000 is not over $20,000 |
| $21,000 | 201 | Yes | Over $20,000 and over 200 |
| $20,000 | 300 | No | $20,000 does not exceed $20,000 |
| $30,000 | 200 | No | 200 does not exceed 200 |

A seller whose sales run through a card processor is a different case. The instructions write the $20,000 and 200 exception for third party network transactions, and they say every payment settlement entity that submits instructions to transfer funds to a payee's account "must file an information return (Form 1099-K) with respect to each participating payee." We did not find a minimum for payment card transactions in the instructions. Ask the processor which form it plans to send.

## Who sends Form 1099-K, and what is the test?

| Who pays you | Form 1099-K test, 2026 | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Payment app or online marketplace (a TPSO) | Over $20,000 and over 200 transactions | Instructions for Form 1099-K (12/2026) |
| Card processor (payment card transactions) | The de minimis exception is written for third party network transactions only; no card minimum found in the instructions | Instructions for Form 1099-K (12/2026) |
| Friends and family sending a gift or repaying a personal expense | Not reportable on Form 1099-K, and not taxable | IRS, "Understanding your Form 1099-K" |

Publication 1099 applies the same test to backup withholding for calendar years beginning after 2024: a TPSO withholds only if payments are over $20,000 in over 200 transactions, or if payments to the payee in the preceding calendar year were reportable.

## When will the 2026 Form 1099-K arrive?

Publication 1099 gives "January 31 for most returns" as the statement date and says a due date that falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves to the next business day. Jan. 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so copies go to recipients by Monday, Feb. 1, 2027. Our [W-2 and 1099 deadline story](/news/w2-1099-deadline-2027-february-1-irs-due-dates/) lists the other information-return dates and the 2027 penalties.

| Step | Date | Why that day |
|---|---|---|
| Copy to the recipient | Mon., Feb. 1, 2027 | Jan. 31, 2027 is a Sunday |
| Paper filing with the IRS | Mon., March 1, 2027 | Feb. 28, 2027 is a Sunday |
| Electronic filing with the IRS | Wed., March 31, 2027 | Publication 1099: "The due date is March 31 if filed electronically." |

## What to do with a 1099-K you did not expect

The IRS consumer page says to note payments from friends and family as non-business in the app when you can; it gives "sharing the cost of a car ride or meal, receiving money for birthday or holiday gifts" as examples. If a form arrives in error or with wrong figures, fact sheet FS-2023-07, which the IRS published in 2023, says to contact the issuer and keep the correspondence. It also says a personal item sold at a loss is reported on Schedule 1 with offsetting entries, and gains go on Form 8949 and Schedule D. We did not check line numbers on the current Schedule 1.

Whether or not you receive a form, the IRS says you "must still report any income on your tax return."
