## How Pips works

You place every domino so that each colored region meets its condition. A number means the pips in the region add up to it; = means every half shows the same value; ≠ means all are different; > and < set a total above or below a number; a region with no condition takes anything. Rows are counted from the top and columns from the left, so R2C5 is the second row, fifth column.

## Solving tips

Look for single cells with a limit: a cell that must be more than 4, as R2C4 is on Wednesday's Medium board, can only hold a 5 or a 6 there. An "equal" region shrinks your options fast once one value is known; a double such as the 4-4 on the Easy board can supply two matching halves at once. On the Hard board, count the single cells with an exact sum: four need a 0, three need a 1, five need a 2, six need a 3, two need a 4, three need a 5 and one needs a 6, so 24 of the 32 cells are fixed from the start. The only 6 on that board is on the 3-6 domino, and R5C4 must add up to 6, so the 3-6 sits on R5C4. Its 3 lands on R4C4, which also adds up to 3.
