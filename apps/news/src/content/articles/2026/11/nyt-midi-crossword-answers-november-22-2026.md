**The New York Times (NYT) Midi Crossword for Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026, is titled "Metal Detector," a 10-by-9 grid with 34 clues by Adrianne Baik, edited by Ian Livengood.** Each clue has a hint behind a tap, and the answers wait in the marked section at the bottom.

## How does the "Metal Detector" title work?

The title is a search instruction. Three clues name a metal, iron, gold and tin, and each ends in a question mark. The grid answer is a two-letter chemical symbol for that metal followed by a 4-letter ordinary word, and the two parts together make one 6-letter entry that also answers the clue. The metal sits at the very start of the entry, so it is the first thing a solver finds.

All three are Across entries, and the same length as three other 6-letter entries that have nothing to do with the theme.

The theme entries are 12-Across (*Iron man?*, row 3, column 5), 20-Across (*Gold rush?*, row 6, column 1) and 28-Across (*Tin god?*, row 8, column 5). Each is 6 letters long.

The three non-theme 6-letter Across entries are 10-Across, 13-Across and 25-Across, so a 6-letter Across answer is a theme answer only half the time.

## Which clue is the least literal today?

28-Across, *Tin god?* Nothing here asks for a deity in a tin can. The first two squares are tin's chemical symbol, and the remaining four letters spell the name of a Greek god, so the word that results is a plain everyday plural. The question mark tells you the clue is a pun that cuts the answer in two.

A smaller oddity is 4-Down, *[Pay attention!]*. The brackets mark a sound or an action in place of a definition, and the answer is the 4-letter noise someone makes in a throat to get noticed.

## What does the grid shape show?

The grid has 90 squares: 15 black and 75 with a letter. It holds 34 entries, 17 Across and 17 Down.

| Entry length | Entries | Across | Down |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 letters | 15 | 7 | 8 |
| 4 letters | 4 | 2 | 2 |
| 5 letters | 7 | 2 | 5 |
| 6 letters | 6 | 6 | 0 |
| 9 letters | 2 | 0 | 2 |

Every 6-letter entry runs Across and every 9-letter one runs Down. Column 4 holds 6 of the 15 black squares, in rows 1 to 3 and rows 7 to 9, so the grid looks like two halves joined by a gap of three rows in the middle. 13-Across and 20-Across run through that gap, and 15-Across stops at it. Row 4 has black squares at both edges, 2 on each side.

## Where should you start?

The two 9-letter Down entries, 3-Down in column 3 and 6-Down in column 8, run from the top row to the bottom row. Fill a few of the 3-letter Across entries first, because each one crosses a long Down answer and hands you a letter of it.
