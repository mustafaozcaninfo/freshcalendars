**The New York Times (NYT) Midi Crossword for Tuesday, Nov. 17, 2026, is titled "Finish First" and has 33 clues on a 9-by-10 grid, credited to Avery Gee Katz and Aaron Gee.** Every clue has a hint behind a tap, and the answers sit in the marked section at the end.

## What the title "Finish First" points to

Take the title literally: "finish" is the paint-shop word for a surface sheen, and "first" says where it goes. Three of the longest entries each open with a different finish, and the rest of the word carries on as an ordinary word or phrase. The three are 10-Across, 22-Across and 4-Down, with 8, 8 and 10 letters. Each one is also a theme entry with a plain clue, so none of the three clues mentions paint.

Spotting one of them gives you the other two. Once the first three letters of any of the three are in, you know what kind of word starts the next one.

## The least literal clue today

22-Across: *Where terms get spelled out?* It is the only clue in the grid with a question mark. "Spelled out" here means explained, not written letter by letter. Think of the pages at the back of a nonfiction book where unfamiliar words are defined. The answer has 8 letters and is one of the three theme entries.

## How today's grid is built

The grid has 90 squares: 18 black and 72 with a letter. Of the 33 entries, 15 have 3 letters, 8 have 4, 1 has 5, 6 have 6, 2 have 8 and 1 has 10. There are 16 Across and 17 Down.

| Entry length | Entries | Across | Down |

| 6 letters | 6 | 2 | 4 |
| 8 letters | 2 | 2 | 0 |

Down entries carry the short end of the grid: 10 of the 17 have 3 letters, while both 8-letter entries run Across. The single 10-letter entry, 4-Down, runs the full height of column 4 and crosses 10 Across entries, one in every row.

Open with the two fill-in-the-blank clues. 18-Across crosses 4-Down, the 10-letter entry, so its last letter feeds the longest answer in the grid.
