## How the shaded-letters clue at 35-Across works

The one clue in the data that points at the grid itself is 35-Across, *Honestly … or a description of the shaded letters?*. It has 13 squares, in row 8 from column 2 to column 14, and it is the only entry longer than eight letters. The first half of the clue is a plain definition of an everyday phrase. The part after "or" turns the phrase into a second reading: the same words are meant to describe how the shaded squares sit in the board.

The saved puzzle data holds letters and clue numbers but no shading, so we cannot list the shaded squares, and no hint here claims to. What the data does show is that the long entry is fully checked: 13 Down entries cross it, one at each letter, 33-Down to 38-Down among them, plus 6-Down, 11-Down, 21-Down, 25-Down, 27-Down, 31-Down and 32-Down. Solve the Down entries first and the middle row fills itself in.

No other clue refers to the grid, so the rest of the puzzle is solved clue by clue.

## Two clues that play tricks

Only two clues end in a question mark. Neither explanation names an answer.

- **13-Down, "What might make molehills out of a mountain?"** The clue reverses the saying about blowing small things out of proportion. Here a mountain is reduced to small mounds by force. The answer is three letters, the initials of a blasting agent.
- **35-Across, "Honestly … or a description of the shaded letters?"** The question mark covers the second half only: the first half is an ordinary definition, and the second half hands the clue its pun. See the section above.

The only 13-letter entry is 35-Across, and nothing has 9 to 12 letters.
