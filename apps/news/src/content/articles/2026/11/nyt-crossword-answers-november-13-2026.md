**The New York Times (NYT) Crossword for Friday, Nov. 13, 2026, was constructed by Mike Graczyk and edited by Will Shortz: 70 clues, 34 Across and 36 Down, in a 15-by-15 grid.** Four clues carry a question mark, and the grid opens with a stack of three 11-letter entries. Every clue has a hint behind a tap, and the answers close the page.

## Four question-mark clues and the trick in each

All four wordplay clues are plain-looking phrases with a second meaning. None of the notes names an answer.

- **30-Across, "Formal guidance?"** (9 letters). The guidance is about clothing, not about manners or speeches. The answer is a set of rules about attire, written as two words run together.
- **35-Across, "What a deck may be stacked with?"** (7 letters). Put the playing cards away. The deck is the floor of a ship, and the stack is people, in the plural.
- **8-Down, "Lays waste to?"** (7 letters). The question mark softens "waste" from ruin to rubbish. The verb is what a careless picnicker does to a park.
- **13-Down, "Relief pitcher?"** (7 letters). Baseball is the decoy. The answer is a brand name, and the relief it sells has nothing to do with the bullpen.

36-Across has no question mark but plays the same game: the clue describes a trick done to a Greek letter, and the answer is the letter you start from.

Ten of the 11 spoken-phrase clues have answers made of several words run together; 58-Across, a single word, is the exception. Reading those clues as something a person would say out loud is the shortcut.

## How the Nov. 13 grid is built

The grid is 15 columns by 15 rows, 225 squares in all: 33 black and 192 holding a letter, a black share of 14.7 percent. The black pattern is unchanged when the grid is rotated half a turn. The longest entries are 11 letters, and there are six of them, all running Across. The longest Down entries are 8 letters, and four share that length.

| Length | Across | Down | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 10 | 4 | 14 |
| 4 | 8 | 10 | 18 |
| 5 | 4 | 4 | 8 |
| 6 | 2 | 10 | 12 |
| 7 | 2 | 4 | 6 |
| 8 | 0 | 4 | 4 |
| 9 | 2 | 0 | 2 |
| 11 | 6 | 0 | 6 |

Rows 1 to 3 each have a single black square, in column 12, so 1-Across, 15-Across and 17-Across run 11 squares side by side. Rows 13 to 15 mirror them with a single black square in column 4, which sets 59-Across, 63-Across and 65-Across in columns 5 to 15. Rows 7, 8 and 9 hold five black squares apiece and are the most closed part of the grid.
