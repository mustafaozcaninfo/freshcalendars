**The New York Times (NYT) Pips for Tuesday, Dec. 1, 2026, runs from a five-domino Easy by Ian Livengood to a 15-domino Hard by Rodolfo Kurchan on a board nine rows tall.** Placements in the last section come from the Times' published puzzle data and satisfy every region. ## Rule census for the Easy, Medium and Hard boards on Dec. 1

Counts come from the Dec. 1, 2026 puzzle data. The frame is the span from the first to the last row and column holding a cell.

| Level | Cells | Frame (rows by columns) | Regions | One-cell regions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Easy | 10 | 3 by 4 | 6 | 2 |
| Medium | 16 | 4 by 7 | 11 | 6 |
| Hard | 30 | 9 by 7 | 20 | 14 |

Next, how many regions of each rule type every level carries.

| Level | Sum | Equal | All different | Greater than | Less than | Free value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Easy | 3 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 1 |
| Medium | 2 | 2 | 0 | 4 | 3 | 0 |
| Hard | 12 | 1 | 0 | 3 | 1 | 3 |

Easy leans on sums and has no greater-than or equal region. Medium flips that: only 2 of its 11 regions are sums, and its four greater-than regions are the most of any level. Hard is the sum board, 12 of 20, and it is the only one with more than one free-value region. No level uses the all-different rule.

| Medium | 8 | 2 (3-3, 1-1) | 3-3, 3-0, 6-0, 1-6, 3-5, 5-1, 1-1, 6-4 |

That is 28 dominoes and five doubles in all. The 6-6 on Hard matters most, because its region of three cells adds up to 18.

## The forced first move on each board

Cells are named by row, then column, from the top left. A search of every legal placement against the saved data finds one arrangement for Easy, four for Medium and eight for Hard.

**Easy.** R1C1 and R2C1 add up to 9, and the biggest tile in the set totals 7, so those two cells cannot be one domino. R2C1 has no neighbor on its right, which leaves R3C1 below it. The first move is a vertical tile on R2C1 and R3C1, and R1C1 turns sideways. The search puts 4-3 on that pair.

**Medium.** R4C3 must be more than 4 and R4C4 more than 3. Only 6-4 has halves that clear both bars side by side: 6 for R4C3, 4 for R4C4. All four arrangements keep it there, and the 1-1 double on R1C4 and R1C5 is the other tile that never moves.

**Hard.** R2C6, R3C5 and R3C6 add up to 18, which takes three 6s. The set has six 6s in all, and the 6-6 double fits R3C5 and R3C6. Every one of the eight arrangements puts it there, and 24 of the 30 cells hold the same value in all eight.

## Pips hints for Dec. 1, 2026

**Easy (five dominoes: 0-4, 4-3, 1-1, 1-6, 5-1)**

- Hint 1: R3C1 is less than 4, and the tile that starts at R2C1 comes down into it.
- Hint 2: R3C2 and R3C3 add up to 2, and the 1-1 double covers that pair.

**Medium (eight dominoes: 3-3, 3-0, 6-0, 1-6, 3-5, 5-1, 1-1, 6-4)**

- Hint 1: R2C3, R4C3 and R3C5 are each more than 4, so each needs a 5 or a 6.
- Hint 2: R1C7 is less than 2, so it takes a 0 or a 1.

**Hard (15 dominoes on a frame 9 rows by 7 columns)**

- Hint 1: R2C6, R3C5 and R3C6 add up to 18, so all three hold a 6.
- Hint 2: R9C6 adds up to 4 and R9C7 takes any value, so the 4-0 tile can cover that pair.

## Which Pips dominoes can be turned around on Dec. 1

Easy has one arrangement, so nothing can move. Medium has four arrangements, but no tile turns end for end alone: its variation comes from tiles trading places. Hard has three tiles that turn without breaking a region: 5-3, 5-4 and 3-0. The placements below print the Times' own data for each domino.
