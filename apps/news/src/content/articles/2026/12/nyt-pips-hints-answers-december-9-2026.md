**The New York Times (NYT) Pips for Wednesday, Dec. 9, 2026, deals 29 tiles over three boards, and Hard stretches 8 rows by 7 columns.** Ian Livengood, the editor, built Easy; Rodolfo Kurchan built Medium and Hard. Of Hard's 19 regions, 12 are sums. The rules are on our [Pips page](/news/topic/nyt-pips/#how-it-works), and Tuesday's boards are on the [Dec. 8 page](/news/nyt-pips-hints-answers-december-8-2026/).

## The forced first move on each Dec. 9 Pips board

Cells are named by row, then column, from the top left.

**Easy.** R5C1 adds up to 3 and touches only R4C1, so the two cells form one tile. A 3 on R5C1 means a tile that carries a 3, and 0-3 is the only one in the hand. It goes down first with the 3 on R5C1 and the 0 above it on R4C1.

**Medium.** R5C3 touches only R5C2, and the pair must add up to less than 3. Tile totals in the hand are 10, 7, 6, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 5, so 1-1 is the only tile small enough. It lies flat on R5C2 and R5C3.

**Hard.** No single tile is forced at once; two small choices come first. R1C5 touches only R2C5, which adds up to 4, so the tile holds a 4 next to a pip below 4: 4-2 or 1-4 (4-4 would put a 4 on R1C5). Separately, R2C4 adds up to 0 and can only share with R3C4, since no 4-0 tile exists for R2C5. R3C4 and R3C5 add up to 11, a 5 and a 6, so R2C4's tile is 5-0 or 6-0. Checked against every valid arrangement, the Times' board uses 4-2 at the top (2 on R1C5) and 5-0 on R2C4 and R3C4; 1-4 and 6-0 belong elsewhere.

## The deciding region on each Dec. 9 board

**Easy: R2C2 and R3C2 add up to 3.** With 0-3 on the lower left, no 0 is left for R2C3, which must be less than 2 and touches only R2C2. It takes a 1. R2C2 and R3C2 then split the 3 as 1 and 2 or as 2 and 1, and both splits can be built from the tiles 1-1 and 2-1, so this region never settles the order of the two. Elsewhere, R4C3 must be more than 3 and has a single neighbor, which forces the 6 in 6-2 onto it and the 2 onto R4C2.

**Medium: R1C3, R1C4 and R2C3 add up to 11.** R1C3 and R1C4 can be filled by the heaviest tile, 4-6, a total of 10. That leaves a 1 for R2C3, and once 1-1 is placed on the bottom row, 1-5 is the only tile left with a 1. Its 5 goes on R2C2, which takes any value.

**Hard: R2C6 and R2C7 add up to 12.** Both cells hold a 6. The one tile that fits whole is the double 6-6, and in every valid arrangement the script found it sits on exactly this pair. A double cannot be turned. Directly below, R3C6 and R3C7 add up to 2, and the board fills that pair with 1-1.

## Pips hints for Dec. 9, 2026

**Easy (five dominoes: 6-2, 2-2, 2-1, 1-1, 0-3)**

- Hint 1: R5C1 adds up to 3 alone, and one tile in the list has a 3.
- Hint 2: R4C3 must be more than 3, and only one tile in the list has a pip above 3.

**Medium (eight dominoes: 4-6, 4-3, 2-4, 1-1, 3-0, 2-2, 1-5, 3-2)**

- Hint 1: R5C3 has one neighbor, R5C2, and the pair adds up to less than 3.
- Hint 2: R4C5 touches only R3C5, and R2C5, R3C5 and R4C5 add up to 10.

**Hard (sixteen dominoes: 6-3, 4-2, 5-1, 1-2, 5-0, 0-3, 1-4, 1-1, 2-3, 2-6, 6-6, 4-4, 2-5, 2-2, 6-0, 3-1)**

- Hint 1: R1C5 touches only R2C5, which adds up to 4.
- Hint 2: R2C4 adds up to 0, and the list has three tiles with a 0.

## What the Dec. 9 boards leave open

Easy has two valid boards: the 2-1 and 1-1 tiles can swap places, and no single tile can be turned. Medium has three tiles that can be turned, 4-6 on R1C3 and R1C4, 4-3 on R3C5 and R4C5, and 2-4 on R4C1 and R5C1, for eight boards in all. Hard has two, 6-3 on R4C1 and R5C1 and 0-3 on R6C4 and R7C4, for four. Doubles never turn.
