**The Tuesday, Dec. 1, 2026 puzzle, Strands #1003 from The New York Times (NYT), carries the clue "Let this be a lesson to you."** Tracy Bennett is listed as both constructor and editor. Counting from #1 on March 4, 2024 lands on 1003. Seven theme words share this board with an 8-letter spangram, and the rules sit on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works).

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: It is a compliment for a person or thing with admirable style and polish, not a word about school.
- Hint 2: It has 8 letters, begins with C and ends with T. Three different letters each appear twice.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 1, column 3. Every step is diagonal, and the path never leaves columns 3 and 4.

## Theme word hints

Seven words fit the clue, listed by length, shortest first and alphabetical within a length:

- **5 letters, starts with C, ends with H:** the person who runs a team's practices from the sideline. The first and fourth letters match.
- **5 letters, starts with G, ends with E:** to lead a visitor along a route. All five letters differ.
- **5 letters, starts with T, ends with H:** to pass a subject or skill on to a learner. All five letters differ.
- **5 letters, starts with T, ends with N:** to build skill or fitness through repeated practice. As a noun it also runs on rails. All five letters differ.
- **5 letters, starts with T, ends with R:** a helper who works with one student at a time, often on homework. The first and third letters match.
- **7 letters, starts with E, ends with E:** to provide schooling and knowledge. The first and last letters match.
- **8 letters, starts with I, ends with T:** to give someone directions to follow, or to give lessons. The fourth and eighth letters match.

## Row and column locator hints

Rows count from the top and columns from the left. Each line gives counts and none names a word.

- **Rows 1 and 2:** five theme tiles each, spread over three words. The spangram tile is at column 3 in row 1 and column 4 in row 2.
- **Rows 3 and 4:** five theme tiles each. Row 3 touches three words and row 4 only two.
- **Rows 5 and 6:** five theme tiles each. Row 5 touches two words and row 6 three, one of them with a single tile in column 1.
- **Rows 7 and 8:** five theme tiles each, from only two words per row.
- **Columns 1 and 2:** eight theme tiles each, no spangram tile, and the same three words appear in both.
- **Columns 3 and 4:** four theme tiles and four spangram tiles each.
- **Columns 5 and 6:** eight theme tiles each, no spangram tile.

## The Dec. 1 zigzag and where the theme tiles crowd

The spangram starts in row 1 at column 3 and drops one row at a time, swinging to column 4, back to column 3 and on, with eight tiles and seven diagonal steps. It ends in row 8 at column 4. Odd rows have it at column 3 and even rows at column 4, so it never touches columns 1, 2, 5 or 6.

That path cuts the board into a left part and a right part, each with 20 theme tiles. The rows are even and the columns are not:

| Columns | Theme tiles | Spangram tiles |
|---|---|---|
| 1 and 2 | 8 each | 0 |
| 3 | 4 | 4 |
| 4 | 4 | 4 |
| 5 and 6 | 8 each | 0 |

Rows 1 to 8 each hold five theme tiles and one spangram tile. The pressure is in the corners. Take a 3 by 3 block in each corner: top right and bottom left each carry 8 theme tiles of 9 and two words, top left and bottom right carry 7 of 9.

## Solving tips

Because every row has the same five theme tiles, a word you find cannot be the last one in a row until you have counted what is left. Begin with the long word in the two left columns, since it only has a few places to go. The two outer columns on each side contain no spangram tile, so any word traced only there is a theme word.
