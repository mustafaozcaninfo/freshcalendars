**Tuesday's Strands, puzzle #1010 from The New York Times (NYT) for Dec. 8, 2026, gives the clue "Working out the kinks".** Tracy Bennett built and edited it. Six theme words and a spangram fill the 8-by-6 board; the rules for the game are on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). Every hint below points at a start cell or a letter count, never the word itself.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: A phrase from the workplace for targets set higher than you expect to reach.
- Hint 2: It has 12 letters in two words of 7 and 5. The phrase starts and ends with the same letter, and T appears twice.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 6, column 1, on the left edge. Its last tile is row 6, column 6, on the right edge.

## Theme word hints

Six words answer the clue, listed shortest first and alphabetical within a length. Rows count from the top and columns from the left.

- **5 letters, starts with B, ends with Y:** describes a drinking straw's elbow or a road full of curves. It starts at row 8, column 1, and E is its only vowel.
- **5 letters, starts with F, ends with D:** what water is, and a word for smooth, flowing motion. It starts at row 8, column 4, and its vowels are U and I.
- **6 letters, starts with L, ends with R:** what a dancer does to loosen up before a routine. It starts at row 1, column 5, and its vowels are I and E.
- **6 letters, starts with P, ends with T:** easy to mold, like soft clay, or easy to persuade. It starts at row 5, column 1, and its vowels are I and A.
- **6 letters, starts with S, ends with E:** soft and smooth, like well-worn leather. It starts at row 2, column 5, and P appears twice.
- **8 letters, starts with F, ends with E:** ready to adapt when plans change. It starts at row 4, column 4, and E appears twice.

## How the spangram arches across the board

The 12 spangram tiles run left edge to right edge in the shape of an arch, and they stay inside rows 3 to 6. From row 6, column 1 the path takes one step right, climbs three rows to row 3, runs three columns across the top of the arch, drops three rows and steps right once more onto the right edge.

| Column | Spangram tiles | Theme tiles |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 7 |
| 2 | 4 | 4 |
| 3 | 1 | 7 |
| 4 | 1 | 7 |
| 5 | 4 | 4 |
| 6 | 1 | 7 |

- Columns 2 and 5 hold four tiles each, the two legs of the arch. Columns 1, 3, 4 and 6 hold one each.
- Row 3 and row 6 each hold four spangram tiles, rows 4 and 5 hold two, and rows 1, 2, 7 and 8 hold none. Top two rows and bottom two rows are 12 theme tiles each, three words apiece.
- The arch cups a block of 8 tiles, four rows tall and two columns wide, rows 4 to 7 in columns 3 and 4. A single 8-letter theme word fills it exactly.
- The two side strips between the legs and the edges, column 1 and column 6 in rows 3 to 5, each belong to one theme word that climbs to the top row.
- The theme words crowd the bottom corners: the bottom two rows hold three words and the bottom-left 2-by-2 block is entirely one 5-letter word.
- Every region has a mirror twin across the line between columns 3 and 4, so the board is symmetrical in shape. Only the letters tell the two halves apart.

## Dec. 8 against Dec. 7

Yesterday's board held seven theme words and a 13-letter spangram that took diagonal steps and ran from row 8 to row 3. Today's tally is six words and 12 letters. The spangram never goes diagonal, and none of the theme words do either, so every tile touches its neighbour along an edge. The longest theme word has 8 letters, against 6 yesterday.

| | Dec. 7 | Dec. 8 |
|---|---|---|
| Theme words | 7 | 6 |
| Spangram letters | 13 | 12 |
| Spangram rows | 1 to 8 | 3 to 6 |
| Spangram columns | 2 to 6 | 1 to 6 |
| Longest theme word | 6 | 8 |

## Solving tips

The arch is the quickest way in. If you can place the spangram's two ends on the left and right edges of row 6, the middle block and the corner words fall out of what remains.
