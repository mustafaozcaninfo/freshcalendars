**Strands puzzle #1012 from The New York Times (NYT), for Thursday, Dec. 10, 2026, asks "But I digress ...".** Tracy Bennett is credited as constructor and editor. The grid has 8 rows and 6 columns; the rules are on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). Hints below stay with letter counts and board positions and leave the words out.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: Think of what a reader scribbles around the printed text of a page.
- Hint 2: It is a single word of 10 letters, and its five vowels alternate between two kinds.
- Hint 3: It starts at row 6, column 1 on the left edge and ends at row 6, column 6 on the right edge. It never goes above row 6.

## Theme word hints

Five words answer the clue, listed shortest first. Rows count from the top and columns from the left.

- **5 letters, starts with A, ends with E:** something said quietly, as if to one listener while others are present. It starts at row 5, column 3 and has three vowels.
- **7 letters, starts with C, ends with T:** a remark offered about what someone has said or done. It starts at row 2, column 4, and M appears twice.
- **8 letters, starts with A, ends with K:** a star-shaped mark that sends the reader to a detail elsewhere. It starts at row 3, column 6, and S appears twice.
- **8 letters, starts with F, ends with E:** explanatory text printed at the bottom of a page. It starts at row 5, column 1, and O appears three times.
- **10 letters, starts with A, ends with N:** explanatory markup a reader or editor adds to a text. It starts at row 4, column 1, and N appears three times.

## The spangram's U-shaped path on the Dec. 10 board

Counting from row 1 and column 1, the spangram begins at row 6, column 1, drops two tiles to the bottom-left corner, crosses the whole of row 8 and climbs two tiles up column 6 to finish at row 6, column 6. It touches three edges and every step is straight, with no diagonals.

| Column | Spangram tiles | Theme tiles |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | 5 |
| 2 | 1 | 7 |
| 3 | 1 | 7 |

| 5 | 1 | 7 |

- Row 8 is entirely spangram, six tiles, and row 1 is entirely one theme word, so each end of the board is a single path.
- Rows 6 and 7 each hold two spangram tiles, at columns 1 and 6, which leaves a 2-row by 4-column gap between them for two theme words.
- The 10-letter theme word is the spangram's mirror: it starts on the left edge at row 4, runs up column 1, crosses all of row 1 and drops one tile down the right edge.
- The top-left 3-by-3 block (rows 1 to 3, columns 1 to 3) is the fullest corner: nine tiles shared by just two words, 5 tiles from the 10-letter word and 4 from the 7-letter one.
- The two 8-letter theme words sit apart: one touches only the left edge, the other only the right edge.
- Diagonal steps total four on the whole board, two in the 5-letter word and two in one 8-letter word.

## Today's Strands answers (spoilers)

- **Spangram:** Marginalia
- **Theme words:** Annotation, Aside, Asterisk, Comment, Footnote

Each theme word is a way of adding to a page, and the spangram names where such additions often go. Start and end cells, counted from row 1 and column 1:

| Word | Letters | Start cell | End cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marginalia | 10 | row 6, column 1 | row 6, column 6 |
| Aside | 5 | row 5, column 3 | row 3, column 5 |
| Comment | 7 | row 2, column 4 | row 3, column 3 |
| Asterisk | 8 | row 3, column 6 | row 7, column 5 |
| Footnote | 8 | row 5, column 1 | row 6, column 3 |
| Annotation | 10 | row 4, column 1 | row 2, column 6 |

Annotation and Comment share the top-left block. Footnote sits left of the spangram's bottom run, Asterisk beside its right-hand climb, and Aside and Comment are the two words that touch no board edge.
