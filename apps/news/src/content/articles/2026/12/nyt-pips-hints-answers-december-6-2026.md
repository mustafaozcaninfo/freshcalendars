**The New York Times (NYT) Pips for Sunday, Dec. 6, 2026, hands out 29 tiles over three boards, from a five-tile Easy to a 16-tile Hard.** The Times' puzzle data lists 21 different tiles among them. Easy is a 4 by 4 frame with only one double, 5-5; the rules sit on our [Pips page](/news/topic/nyt-pips/#how-it-works). Saturday's boards are on the [Dec. 5 page](/news/nyt-pips-hints-answers-december-5-2026/).

| Level | Sum | Equal | All different | Less than | Greater than | Empty |

Easy has no sum region at all, so its eight regions are comparisons and two cells with no rule. Medium is the only level with an all-different region, over three cells. Hard leans on sums: 13 of 18, five of them single cells. Five of Easy's ten cells carry a 5.

The deal of 5, 8 and 16 adds to 29, and 8 of those are repeats, which leaves 21 different tiles. The pair 0-5 and the pair 3-5 appear on every board. Seven tiles never show: 0-0, 0-2, 0-4, 1-1, 1-6, 3-4 and 6-6. Eight of Hard's 32 cells carry a 5.

## The forced first move on each Dec. 6 board

Cells are named by row, then column, from the top left.

**Easy.** R2C2 must be less than 3, so it needs a 0, 1 or 2. Only one tile has such a pip, 0-5, so its 0 goes on R2C2. R2C1 must be less than 4, which leaves a 3, and that pip is on 5-3, whose 5 can only go on R1C1. In the Times' solution 0-5 lies flat across R2C2 and R2C3.

**Medium.** R4C1 adds up to 2 and R4C2 beside it adds up to 3. No 2-3 tile is dealt, and nothing lies below R4C1, so its 2 reaches up to R3C1. Two tiles carry a 2, and two cells need one (R2C4 is the other), so 2-6 and 4-2 each fill one of them. In the Times' solution 2-6 stands upright with its 6 on R3C1.

**Hard.** R8C4 adds up to 1 and has a single neighbor, R7C4, which is more than 4. That neighbor holds a 5 or a 6, and the list has no 1-6, so 1-5 goes down first: the 1 on R8C4 and the 5 on R7C4. Start at the bottom edge and work upward.

## Pips hints for Dec. 6, 2026

**Easy (five dominoes: 5-6, 0-5, 5-3, 6-4, 5-5)**

- Hint 1: R1C4 is more than 5, so it holds a 6, and two tiles in the list carry one.
- Hint 2: R2C2 is less than 3, and just one tile in the list has a pip below 3.

**Medium (eight dominoes: 2-6, 4-4, 5-0, 0-3, 4-2, 5-3, 1-4, 3-3)**

- Hint 1: Five one-cell regions are sums, and one of them adds up to 0; two tiles in the list carry a 0.
- Hint 2: R4C1 adds up to 2 and R4C2 beside it adds up to 3, but the list has no 2-3 tile.

**Hard (sixteen dominoes: 4-5, 5-2, 2-2, 1-3, 5-0, 1-0, 2-1, 1-5, 2-4, 6-5, 3-6, 3-2, 3-5, 5-5, 6-0, 4-1)**

- Hint 1: The lowest cell on the board, R8C4, adds up to 1 and touches only one other cell.
- Hint 2: That neighbor is more than 4; look for the tile that pairs a 1 with a 5 or a 6.

## What the Dec. 6 boards leave open

Starting from the Times' solution, neither Easy nor Medium has a tile that can be turned around alone. Hard has four. 1-0 can be turned on R3C2 and R4C2, 2-4 on R3C3 and R2C3, 3-5 on R1C4 and R2C4, and 4-1 on R2C6 and R3C6; each still satisfies its regions either way.
