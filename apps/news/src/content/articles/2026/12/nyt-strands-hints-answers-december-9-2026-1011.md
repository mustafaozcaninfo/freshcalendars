**Strands puzzle #1011 from The New York Times (NYT), for Wednesday, Dec. 9, 2026, carries the clue "If you play your cards right ...".** Tracy Bennett is credited as constructor and editor. The board is 8 rows by 6 columns, and today's spangram is the longest path on it by far; the game's rules sit on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). The hints below give a start tile or a letter count, never the word.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: An idiom for what happens to someone easy to overlook in a crowd.
- Hint 2: It has 16 letters in four words of 4, 2, 3 and 7. The last word is the one a deck goes through before a deal.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 1, column 1, the top-left corner. Its last tile is row 8, column 6, the bottom-right corner.

## Theme word hints

Five words answer the clue, listed shortest first. Rows count from the top and columns from the left.

- **4 letters, starts with S, ends with T:** a quick two-player card game. It starts at row 2, column 6, and I is its only vowel.
- **5 letters, starts with R, ends with Y:** a game of collecting sets and runs of cards. It starts at row 1, column 6, and M appears twice.
- **6 letters, starts with E, ends with E:** a trick-taking game where one suit becomes trump. It starts at row 4, column 2, and its vowels are E, U and E.
- **8 letters, starts with C, ends with E:** a game scored by moving pegs along a board. It starts at row 6, column 2, and B appears twice.
- **9 letters, starts with S, ends with E:** a card game played alone. It starts at row 4, column 4, and I appears twice.

## A spangram that crosses the board corner to corner

The 16 spangram tiles touch all eight rows and all six columns. From row 1, column 1 the path runs two tiles right along the top, turns down at column 3 and falls seven rows to row 8. Then it zigzags east: up-right, down, up-right, down, up-right, down, ending in the bottom-right corner.

| Column | Spangram tiles | Theme tiles |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 7 |
| 2 | 1 | 7 |
| 3 | 8 | 0 |
| 4 | 2 | 6 |
| 5 | 2 | 6 |
| 6 | 2 | 6 |

- Column 3 is the spine: all eight of its tiles belong to the spangram, so no theme word crosses it.
- Row 1 holds three spangram tiles, rows 2 to 6 hold one each, and rows 7 and 8 hold four each, which makes the bottom-right a 2-by-3 block of spangram.
- Left of the spine, two theme words of 6 and 8 letters fill columns 1 and 2 from row 2 to row 8, 14 tiles with none spare.
- Right of the spine, three theme words of 4, 5 and 9 letters fill columns 4 to 6 in rows 1 to 6, 18 tiles with none spare.
- The 9-letter word takes the entire 3-by-3 block at rows 4 to 6, columns 4 to 6; the 4-letter word sits in the 2-by-2 block above it, at rows 2 and 3, columns 5 and 6.
- The 4-letter word touches the 5-letter word beside it and the 9-letter word below it. On the left, the 6-letter and 8-letter words meet along rows 4 and 5.

## Dec. 9 against Dec. 8

Yesterday's board had six theme words and a 12-letter spangram that stayed in rows 3 to 6 and never took a diagonal step. Today the spangram is four letters longer, uses rows 1 to 8 and takes three diagonal steps. Counting every word, seven steps are diagonal today and none were yesterday.

| Spangram rows | 3 to 6 | 1 to 8 |

## Solving tips

Start at the corners. The spangram begins in one and ends in the opposite one, and the column between the two halves belongs to it.
