## The forced first move on each Dec. 3 board

Cells are named by row, then column, from the top left.

**Easy.** R4C2 must add up to 1 and touches only R4C1. The tile there carries a 1, which leaves 1-5 or 1-0, and R3C1 with R4C1 must total more than 9. With the largest tile number a 6, R4C1 needs at least a 4, so the 5 goes on R4C1 and the 1-5 tile lies flat along the bottom row. A search of the saved data finds only one arrangement for the whole board.

**Medium.** R1C5 adds up to 3 on its own. Exactly one tile in the set has a 3, the 6-3, so it covers R1C5, and its 6 goes to R2C5 or R1C4. Elsewhere R2C1 has the single neighbor R2C2 and must equal it, so a double sits there too.

**Hard.** R1C8 touches only R2C8, and the two cells add up to 3. One tile in the set totals 3, the 3-0, so it fills the top of the right edge before anything else is decided. R3C8 then has only R4C8 left as a partner.
