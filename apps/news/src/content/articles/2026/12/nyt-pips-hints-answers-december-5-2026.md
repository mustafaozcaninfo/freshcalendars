**Saturday's New York Times (NYT) Pips, Dec. 5, 2026, goes from a five-tile Easy to a 16-tile Hard, and Medium reuses five of its seven tiles on Hard.** The placements below come from the Times' published puzzle data and satisfy every region. Today's Easy is a 3 by 4 block whose only double is 6-6; the rules are on our [Pips page](/news/topic/nyt-pips/#how-it-works). Add up 5, 7 and 16 and you reach 28 tiles dealt, yet only 23 are different. Medium and Hard share 1-3, 3-3, 1-1, 2-0 and 4-4. The five that appear nowhere are 0-1, 0-3, 0-5, 0-6 and 1-5, so four of the five absent tiles pair a 0 with another number.

## The forced first move on each Dec. 5 board

Cells are named by row, then column, from the top left.

**Easy.** R1C2 with R2C2 adds up to 12, and so do R3C3 with R2C3. The highest pip is 6, so those four cells all hold a 6. The set carries exactly four 6s: two on 6-6, one on 6-2 and one on 6-5. In the Times' solution the double-six lies flat across R2C2 and R2C3, which touch, and the other two 6s sit above R2C2 and below R2C3.

**Medium.** R3C1 is less than 1, so it holds a 0, and 2-0 is the only tile with one. In the Times' solution that tile stands upright with the 0 on R3C1 and the 2 above it. The region that holds R2C1 adds up to 4, so the 2 leaves 2 for its other two cells.

**Hard.** Column 6 offers four stacked cells: R1C6 is more than 3, R2C6 adds up to 5, R3C6 adds up to 2 and R4C6 is more than 1. A 5 cannot share a tile with the 2 beneath it, because no 5-2 tile is dealt. So the 5 pairs upward with a cell above 3. Of the two tiles with a 5, 3-5 offers only a 3, so 5-4 goes on R1C6 and R2C6, with the 4 on top.

## Pips hints for Dec. 5, 2026

**Easy (five dominoes: 6-6, 2-5, 3-2, 6-2, 6-5)**

- Hint 1: Two pairs of cells each add up to 12, and the list holds exactly one double.
- Hint 2: R3C4 is less than 3 and has a single neighbor, which sits in a region that adds up to 12.

**Medium (seven dominoes: 1-3, 3-3, 5-5, 1-1, 2-0, 4-4, 1-6)**

- Hint 1: R3C1 is less than 1, so it holds a 0, and one tile in the list carries a 0.
- Hint 2: R3C4 adds up to 1, so it holds a 1, and three tiles in the list carry one.

**Hard (sixteen dominoes: 2-2, 2-0, 1-2, 4-3, 4-4, 4-0, 2-4, 1-3, 3-6, 5-4, 3-5, 0-0, 1-1, 3-3, 1-4, 6-4)**

- Hint 1: R2C6 adds up to 5 and R3C6 adds up to 2 just below it; look for a tile that could join them, then rule it out.
- Hint 2: R1C3 adds up to 0 and touches only R2C3, which takes any value.

## What the Dec. 5 boards leave open

Starting from the Times' solution, no Easy tile can be turned around. On Medium, 1-6 can be turned on R3C2 and R3C3, a region that adds up to 7 either way. On Hard, four tiles can be turned on their own: 1-2 on R9C5 and R10C5, 4-3 on R10C3 and R9C3, 2-4 on R6C1 and R5C1, and 3-6 on R8C3 and R7C3.
