**Monday's Strands, puzzle #1009 from The New York Times (NYT) for Dec. 7, 2026, asks "That's not yours!"** Christina Iverson constructed it and Tracy Bennett edited it. The board is 8 rows by 6 columns and holds seven theme words plus the spangram; the rules sit on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works). Each hint below points to a start cell, never to the word itself.

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: A two-word idiom for someone who cannot keep their hands off other people's belongings.
- Hint 2: The words have 6 and 7 letters. The whole phrase begins and ends with S, the first word ends in Y, and I appears twice.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 8, column 3, on the bottom edge. Its last tile is row 3, column 6, on the right edge.

## Theme word hints

Seven words answer the clue, listed shortest first and alphabetical within a length. Rows count from the top and columns from the left.

- **4 letters, starts with G, ends with B:** to seize in a hurry. It starts at row 8, column 2, and its letters are all different.
- **4 letters, starts with L, ends with T:** what a crane does to a steel beam, and slang for taking. It starts at row 1, column 6, and its letters are all different.
- **5 letters, starts with P, ends with H:** to squeeze a bit of skin hard enough to make someone yelp. It starts at row 4, column 6, and I is its only vowel.
- **5 letters, starts with S, ends with L:** the plainest verb for the clue, and also a word for a bargain. It starts at row 2, column 1, and it has two vowels, E and A.
- **5 letters, starts with S, ends with E:** to run a card through a reader, or slide a finger across a phone screen. It starts at row 5, column 3, and its vowels are I and E.
- **6 letters, starts with P, ends with R:** to take small things a little at a time. It starts at row 8, column 4, and it has two vowels, I and E.
- **6 letters, starts with T, ends with E:** to act the way a robber does. It starts at row 6, column 1, and E appears twice.

## Where each Dec. 7 word starts, and how long it runs

Seven theme words come to 35 tiles, an average of exactly 5. The spangram takes the other 13 of the board's 48.

| Word length | Words | Start cells |
|---|---|---|
| 4 letters | 2 | row 1, column 6; row 8, column 2 |
| 5 letters | 3 | row 2, column 1; row 4, column 6; row 5, column 3 |
| 6 letters | 2 | row 6, column 1; row 8, column 4 |

- Four words start in the bottom four rows (5 to 8) and three in the top four, so the starts lean low.
- Row 8 holds three starts in a row: a theme word in column 2, the spangram in column 3 and another theme word in column 4.
- Counting theme words only, columns 1 and 6 each hold two starts, columns 2, 3 and 4 hold one each, and column 5 holds none.
- Only one word begins in the top row, in the far right corner.

## The spangram's zigzag and the crowded bottom-left corner

The spangram takes 12 steps. It climbs two tiles diagonally to the right, then three diagonally to the left to reach row 3, column 2. Two more diagonal steps to the right bring it to row 1, column 4. It then drops straight down two rows, steps down and right to row 4, column 5, goes back up to row 3 and finishes one tile to the right in column 6.

| Column | Spangram tiles | Theme tiles |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 8 |
| 2 | 1 | 7 |
| 3 | 3 | 5 |
| 4 | 5 | 3 |
| 5 | 3 | 5 |
| 6 | 1 | 7 |

- The counts run 1, 3, 5, 3, 1 across columns 2 to 6, so column 4 is the spine of the path and the board is split into a left and a right strip.
- Row 3 is the busiest row for the spangram, with four tiles in columns 2, 4, 5 and 6 and two theme tiles left over. Rows 1 and 5 to 8 have a single spangram tile each.
- The densest corner is the bottom-left block of rows 6 to 8 and columns 1 to 3. Its nine tiles belong to three theme words, 4, 3 and 1 tiles each, and the spangram's first tile, so the three words overlap there.
- The top-right corner is quiet by comparison. A 2 by 2 block there belongs to a single 4-letter word.

Start where the board is crowded and the hints are firmest: the bottom row has three starting points, and one of them is the spangram.
