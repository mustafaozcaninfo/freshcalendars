**Tuesday, Dec. 1, 2026, gives Spelling Bee players from The New York Times (NYT) a board with F in the middle and A, I, L, N, O and T around it.** The list holds 54 words worth 289 points, Genius sits at 202, and five pangrams are in there.

Five pangrams in one puzzle is a lot to hide, and they change the math: the rules are on our [Spelling Bee page](/news/topic/nyt-spelling-bee/#how-it-works), and the points table below shows how much of 289 they carry.

## What do the five pangrams have in common?

- **Count:** five pangrams, four of 9 letters and one of 11.
- **The start:** three begin with F, the center letter; one begins with A and one with I.
- **The end:** all five finish with N, which closes 6 of the 54 answers.
- **The long one:** the 11-letter pangram uses three Is and is the only answer that long.

Four-letter words score 1 point, longer words 1 point per letter, and each pangram adds 7. Rows are added to the running total from the top.

- **Pangrams are 28% of the points:** 82 of 289, from 5 of the 54 words.
- **Each short pangram is worth 16:** 9 letters plus the 7-point bonus. The 11-letter one is worth 18.

## The hardest words on Tuesday's list

- **Longest answers:** nine reach 8 letters or more: four of 8, four of 9 and one of 11. Five of the nine are pangrams.
- **Rare pairs:** LF sits in a single answer. NI, IT, TO, TF and OI each sit in two.
- **Doubled letters:** 17 answers repeat a letter back to back: OO in 7, FF in 6 and LL in 6. Two of the 17 hold two different pairs.
- **Endings:** 17 answers end in L, 17 in T, 6 in F, 6 in N, 5 in A and 3 in O.
- **Few starts:** O opens one answer and T two.
- **Thin on letters:** 6 answers use only three different letters.

## How far are the four-letter words from Genius?

The 18 four-letter answers give 18 points, about 9% of the 202 needed. The other 184 points must come from the 36 answers of five letters or more, which hold 271 between them. Without the five pangrams the list is worth 207, so skipping all of them leaves a 5-point cushion over Genius: every other word must be found, apart from 5 points of misses. Compare that with Monday's puzzle, where 23 four-letter words made up 23 of 127.
