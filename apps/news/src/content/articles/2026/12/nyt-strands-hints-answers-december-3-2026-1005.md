**Strands #1005 from The New York Times (NYT) is the Thursday, Dec. 3, 2026 puzzle, and its clue is "I'm done!"** The data credits Christina Iverson as constructor and Tracy Bennett as editor. Puzzle numbering starts at #1 on March 4, 2024, which makes today #1005. Six theme words and a 15-letter spangram fill all 48 tiles; the rules sit on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works).

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: It is a saying of four words that means admitting defeat and giving up.
- Hint 2: It has 15 letters, begins with T and ends with L. One letter appears three times, four letters appear twice and four appear once.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 7, column 3. It climbs, heads left to the edge, and then winds back across the middle of the board to the right edge.

## Theme word hints

Six words answer the clue, listed by length, shortest first and alphabetical within a length:

- **4 letters, starts with E, ends with T:** the marked way out of a building. All four letters differ.
- **4 letters, starts with Q, ends with T:** to leave a job or a habit for good. All four letters differ.
- **5 letters, starts with C, ends with E:** to come to an end, the way a noise does. The second and fifth letters match.
- **6 letters, starts with D, ends with T:** a court order tells you to do this. The third and fifth letters match.
- **6 letters, starts with R, ends with N:** to give up a post, often with a letter to the boss. All six letters differ.
- **8 letters, starts with W, ends with W:** to pull back, the way money leaves a bank account. The first and last letters match.

## How the spangram coils through rows 4 to 7 on Dec. 3

Most spangrams run in a line or a slant. Today's takes 14 steps and none is diagonal: four up, five right, three left and two down. It starts in row 7 at column 3, climbs to row 6 and runs left to column 1, then goes up the left edge to row 4. From there it turns right, dips down a row, runs right again, drops once more and travels along row 6 to the right edge. It finishes by stepping up to row 5 and left one tile, ending at row 5, column 5.

The result is a coil. The path crosses the board from column 1 to column 6, and it covers rows 5 and 6 completely, so those twelve tiles hold no theme letter at all. Its highest tile is in row 4 and its lowest in row 7.

That band cuts the board into a top block and a bottom strip:

| Part of the board | Theme tiles | Theme words | Spangram tiles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rows 1 to 3 | 18 | 4 (one only partly) | 0 |
| Row 4 | 4 | 1 | 2 |
| Rows 5 and 6 | 0 | 0 | 12 |
| Rows 7 and 8 | 11 | 2 | 1 |

Four of the six theme words touch the top three rows, so that is where the theme letters crowd. The 3 by 3 block in rows 1 to 3 and columns 1 to 3 has no spangram tile, and its nine tiles come from just two words. The bottom strip is quieter: two words share rows 7 and 8, and a 2 by 2 block in the bottom-left corner belongs to one of them.

## Solving tips

Start at the top, where nothing but theme words sits. Rows 1 to 3 give 18 tiles with no spangram to confuse them, so any string of letters you find there is a theme word. Then look at the middle: with rows 5 and 6 entirely spangram, you can ignore them until the other five words are off the board, because the leftover tiles must be the long phrase.
