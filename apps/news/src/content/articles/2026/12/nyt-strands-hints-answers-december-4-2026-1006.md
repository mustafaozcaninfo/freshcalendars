**Strands #1006 from The New York Times (NYT) is the Friday, Dec. 4, 2026 puzzle, and the theme clue is "Artful storytelling".** It was constructed by Christina Iverson and edited by Tracy Bennett, and it is the 1,006th Strands since the series began on March 4, 2024. Each hint below names a start cell, so you can look at one spot on the 8-row, 6-column board without seeing a word. Today's spangram crosses from the top row to the bottom row; the rules are on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works).

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: A book-length story told through drawings and speech balloons.
- Hint 2: It is two words, of 7 and 5 letters. It begins with G and ends with L, and no letter appears twice.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 1, column 2. It goes straight down two tiles, turns right, and later reaches the bottom row in column 6.

## Theme word hints

Five words answer the clue, listed shortest first and alphabetical within a length. Rows count from the top and columns from the left.

- **5 letters, starts with F, ends with E:** the border that holds a picture in place. It starts at row 4, column 4, and all five letters differ.
- **6 letters, starts with P, ends with S:** the boxes a comic page is cut into. It starts at row 1, column 6, and runs straight down the right edge.
- **6 letters, starts with S, ends with H:** a bold image that takes up a whole page. It starts at row 1, column 4, and the first and fifth letters match.
- **7 letters, starts with C, ends with N:** the line of text under a cartoon or a photo. It starts at row 4, column 2, and ends at row 1, column 1.
- **12 letters, starts with I, ends with N:** the artwork that goes with a written text. It starts at row 6, column 3, and three letters appear twice.

## How the top-to-bottom spangram divides the board on Dec. 4

The spangram takes 11 steps and none is diagonal: seven down and four right. From row 1, column 2 it drops to row 3, turns right and runs along row 3 to column 5. It then goes down column 5 to row 7, steps right once and drops to row 8, column 6.

That path puts a wall through the middle of the board. Everything above row 3 sits in a pocket, and everything left of column 5 below row 3 sits in another:

| Row | Spangram tiles | Theme tiles | Theme words in the row |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | column 2 | 5 | 3 |
| 2 | column 2 | 5 | 3 |
| 3 | columns 2 to 5 | 2 | 2 |
| 4 | column 5 | 5 | 3 |
| 5 | column 5 | 5 | 3 |
| 6 | column 5 | 5 | 3 |
| 7 | columns 5 and 6 | 4 | 1 |
| 8 | column 6 | 5 | 1 |

Three placements are worth knowing before you start:

- The six tiles in rows 1 and 2 of columns 3 to 5 form a complete 2 by 3 block, and they belong to one word. Row 3 of the spangram closes it from below.
- Column 1 is all theme tiles: five from one word in rows 1 to 5, then three from the 12-letter word in rows 6 to 8.
- The 12-letter word fills the bottom-left corner: row 8 up to column 5, row 7 up to column 4 and three tiles of row 6. Only one other word reaches rows 7 and 8, and it is the spangram.

## Solving tips

The right column is the quickest foothold. Six tiles from row 1 to row 6 form a single word, and it never bends. After that, try the 2 by 3 block at the top. Whatever stays unused near the end is the spangram, so there is no need to hunt for it first. | Word | Letters | Start cell | End cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graphic Novel | 12 | row 1, column 2 | row 8, column 6 |
| Caption | 7 | row 4, column 2 | row 1, column 1 |
| Frame | 5 | row 4, column 4 | row 6, column 4 |
| Illustration | 12 | row 6, column 3 | row 8, column 5 |
| Panels | 6 | row 1, column 6 | row 6, column 6 |
| Splash | 6 | row 1, column 4 | row 2, column 5 |

Panels runs alone down column 6 and Splash fills the top block. Caption climbs the left edge, Frame sits between it and the spangram, and Illustration winds through the bottom-left corner.
