**The New York Times (NYT) Pips for Tuesday, Dec. 8, 2026, hands out 29 tiles across three boards, and 19 of them are different.** Rodolfo Kurchan built Medium and Hard, Ian Livengood built Easy and is the editor. Hard fills a 6 by 6 frame with four holes in it; our [Pips page](/news/topic/nyt-pips/#how-it-works) explains the rules. Monday's boards are on the [Dec. 7 page](/news/nyt-pips-hints-answers-december-7-2026/).

## The forced first move on each Dec. 8 board

Cells are named by row, then column, from the top left.

**Easy.** R3C5 has a single neighbor, R3C4, so those two cells are one tile. R2C4 must be more than 3 and touches only R1C4 and R3C4. R3C4 is spoken for, so R2C4 and R1C4 share a tile. R1C4 adds up to 2, and only 2-5 has a 2 beside a pip above 3. That tile goes down first, 2 on R1C4 and 5 below it.

**Medium.** R1C3 must be more than 2 and touches only R2C3, which adds up to 1 by itself. Of the three tiles with a 1 (4-1, 0-1, 1-2), only 4-1 has a partner above 2. So 4-1 opens the board with the 4 on R1C3.

**Hard.** R4C6 adds up to 0 and R5C6 can reach only R4C6 or R6C6. R5C6 and R6C6 must total 11, which means a 5 and a 6, and no tile pairs those two. So R5C6 shares with R4C6, a 0 next to a 5 or 6. Only 6-0 qualifies: 0 on R4C6, 6 on R5C6, leaving the 5 for R6C6.

## The deciding region on each Dec. 8 board

**Easy: R3C3 and R3C4 add up to 3.** The sum could be 0 and 3 or 1 and 2. Three cells (R2C1, R1C4, R3C5) each need a 2 on their own, and 2-5, 3-2 and 1-2 hold exactly three 2s between them, with no double. Nothing is left to put a 2 on R3C3 or R3C4, so the pair is 0 and 3. Because R3C4 shares a tile with the 2 on R3C5, that tile is 3-2, which puts the 3 on R3C4 and the 0 on R3C3.

**Medium: R3C5, R3C6 and R4C6 add up to 8.** R4C6 touches only R3C6, so those two cells are one tile, and R3C5 must then link to R3C4, which has to be less than 4. In the Times' solution the pair holds 1-2, a total of 3, so R3C5 needs a 5. Only one tile in the list has a 5, 5-3, and its 3 on R3C4 meets the less-than-4 rule.

**Hard: R5C6 and R6C6 add up to 11.** This rule starts a chain. Once 6-0 is down, R6C6 holds a 5 and its only free neighbor is R6C5, which must be less than 4. Of the two tiles with a 5, 5-4 would put a 4 there, so 1-5 is the one: 1 on R6C5, 5 on R6C6.

## Pips hints for Dec. 8, 2026

**Easy (five dominoes: 2-5, 4-5, 6-0, 3-2, 1-2)**

- Hint 1: R2C1, R1C4 and R3C5 each add up to 2 alone, and the list has three tiles with a 2.
- Hint 2: R3C5 has a single neighbor, so it shares a tile with R3C4.

**Medium (eight dominoes: 5-3, 4-1, 4-0, 0-1, 1-2, 3-3, 2-3, 0-6)**

- Hint 1: R1C3 must be more than 2, and R2C3, its only neighbor, adds up to 1.
- Hint 2: R5C2 touches only R4C2, and that pair must add up to 6.

**Hard (sixteen dominoes: 0-3, 3-1, 2-2, 2-6, 5-4, 4-3, 2-4, 1-5, 2-0, 6-0, 4-6, 3-3, 3-2, 4-1, 4-0, 1-0)**

- Hint 1: R4C6 adds up to 0, and R5C6 with R6C6 adds up to 11.
- Hint 2: R5C6 touches only R4C6 and R6C6, and the list has no tile that holds both a 5 and a 6.

## What the Dec. 8 boards leave open

Easy has no tile that can be turned around; its answer is fixed. Medium has three, 2-3 on R4C4 and R5C4, 0-6 on R4C2 and R5C2, and 1-2 on R3C6 and R4C6, since each region only cares about the total. Hard also has three: 2-4 on R4C4 and R4C5, 4-6 on R1C2 and R1C3, and 5-4 on R1C6 and R2C6.
