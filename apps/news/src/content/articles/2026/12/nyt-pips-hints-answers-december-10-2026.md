**The New York Times (NYT) Pips for Thursday, Dec. 10, 2026, deals 28 tiles over three boards, and only Hard uses all six rule types.** Ian Livengood, the editor, built Easy and Medium; Rodolfo Kurchan built Hard, where 14 of the 20 regions cover a single cell. The six rules, including the all-different one that shows up once today, are explained on our [Pips page](/news/topic/nyt-pips/#how-it-works).

Easy is the only board with no equal region and no less-than region, and it leans on empty cells: 3 of its 7 regions. Medium spreads its nine regions over five rules with no single rule above three. Hard is the sum board, 13 of 20.

## The forced first move on each Dec. 10 Pips board

Cells are named by row, then column, from the top left.

**Easy.** R1C2 and R2C2 must add up to more than 10. The hand has no 5, so 11 is out, and each cell must hold a 6. Only 4-6 and 6-1 carry a 6, so those two tiles are spoken for before anything else is placed: one lies along row 1 into R1C2, the other along row 2 into R2C2.

**Medium.** R1C2 and R2C2 must add up to more than 9, so 10 at least. Without a 6 the best pair is 5 and 4, a total of 9, which falls short. So a 6 lands on one of the two cells, and 6-1 is the lone tile with one. In all three valid boards that tile lies down R2C2 and R3C2, 6 on top.

**Hard.** R1C3 has a single neighbor, R2C3, and the four cells in that column must be equal, so the tile there is a double. Both valid boards use 6-6. A second anchor is R6C4: it adds up to 0, only 2-0 has a 0, and the 2 on R6C3 then sets the value of the four-cell equal region that runs through R4C4, R5C4, R5C3 and R6C3.

## Pips hints for Dec. 10, 2026

**Easy (five dominoes: 4-6, 0-1, 2-0, 6-1, 3-3)**

- Hint 1: R1C2 and R2C2 add up to more than 10, which takes two 6s, and two tiles in the list have a 6.
- Hint 2: R2C6 must be a 1 and touches only R2C5, an empty cell.

**Medium (seven dominoes: 2-3, 3-3, 6-1, 3-4, 0-5, 2-0, 2-1)**

- Hint 1: R1C2 and R2C2 add up to more than 9, and only one tile in the list has a 6.
- Hint 2: R3C4 must be less than 2, so it holds a 0 or a 1.

**Hard (sixteen dominoes: 6-6, 5-4, 1-1, 1-2, 6-1, 5-6, 3-1, 5-2, 3-6, 5-5, 4-2, 2-2, 2-0, 3-4, 5-1, 4-4)**

- Hint 1: R6C4 adds up to 0, and just one tile in the list has a 0.
- Hint 2: R8C3 must be less than 2 and touches only R7C3.

## What the Dec. 10 boards leave open

Easy has no spare room: one tile arrangement and one orientation fit. Medium accepts three boards, since the small tiles 2-3, 3-3, 0-5, 2-0 and 2-1 can shift between spots. Hard accepts two, and the difference is the 3-4 tile on R6C2 and R7C2, which can be turned because its three-cell region only asks for different values. Doubles never turn.
