**Strands #1004 from The New York Times (NYT) is the Wednesday, Dec. 2, 2026 puzzle, and its clue is "Dairy I ask?"** Christina Iverson is credited as constructor and Tracy Bennett as editor. Counting from #1 on March 4, 2024 gives 1004. Six theme words and a 12-letter spangram share the board, and today's tile counts are below the rules on our [Strands page](/news/topic/nyt-strands/#how-it-works).

## Spangram hints

- Hint 1: It names the craft of turning milk into the solid products of a deli counter.
- Hint 2: It has 12 letters, begins with C and ends with G. One letter appears three times and no other letter repeats.
- Hint 3: Its first tile is row 2, column 6. It goes straight down the right edge, then turns up and to the left.

## Theme word hints

Six words fit the clue, listed by length, shortest first and alphabetical within a length:

- **4 letters, starts with M, ends with D:** a hollow form that gives a soft food its shape. The same word is a fuzzy growth on forgotten leftovers. All four letters differ.
- **5 letters, starts with P, ends with S:** to squeeze down with force, the way a heavy tool flattens a solid. The last two letters match.
- **6 letters, starts with M, ends with N:** a loosely woven cotton cloth. All six letters differ.
- **6 letters, starts with R, ends with T:** an enzyme taken from a calf's stomach that sets milk. The second and fifth letters match, and so do the third and fourth.
- **7 letters, starts with C, ends with E:** a living starter of bacteria stirred into milk. It is also the word for a society's arts and customs. The second and fifth letters match.
- **8 letters, starts with S, ends with R:** a mesh tool that separates liquid from solids. The third and eighth letters match.

- **Row 1:** six theme tiles from a single word and no spangram tile.
- **Row 2:** five theme tiles from two words, plus the spangram's first tile at column 6.
- **Rows 3 to 5:** four theme tiles each, from three words, plus two spangram tiles each.
- **Row 6:** four theme tiles from three words and two spangram tiles.
- **Row 7:** four theme tiles from two words and two spangram tiles.
- **Row 8:** five theme tiles from one word, plus the spangram tile at column 6.
- **Columns 1 to 5:** seven theme tiles and one spangram tile each.
- **Column 6:** one theme tile, in row 1, and seven spangram tiles.

## How the spangram runs along the edges on Dec. 2

Three edges of the board matter today. The spangram starts one tile below the top-right corner, in row 2 at column 6, and drops down the right edge for seven tiles, all the way to row 8. That is the board's bottom-right corner. From there it takes five diagonal steps, each one row up and one column left, and finishes on the left edge at row 3, column 1. The path has 12 tiles and 11 steps: six straight down and five diagonal.

Every diagonal tile sits where the row number minus the column number equals 2. That line separates the board into two sides. Above and to the right of it are 21 theme tiles from four words, and below and to the left are 15 theme tiles from two words. The spangram therefore does more than cross the board: it sorts the theme words into a group of four and a group of two.

| Part of the board | Theme tiles | Theme words | Spangram tiles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Row 1 | 6 | 1 | 0 |
| Column 6 | 1 | 1 | 7 |
| Columns 1 to 5 | 35 | 6 | 5 |
| Above the diagonal | 21 | 4 | 0 |
| Below the diagonal | 15 | 2 | 0 |

The theme tiles crowd the bottom-left corner. Take the 3 by 3 block in rows 6 to 8 and columns 1 to 3: all nine tiles are theme tiles, from two words, with no spangram tile. The other corners are looser. The top-left block has 8 theme tiles from three words, the top-right has 7 from three words, and the bottom-right has only 4 theme tiles because the spangram takes 5 of its 9. In the middle, rows 3 and 4 at columns 3 and 4 form a 2 by 2 block that belongs to a single word.

## Solving tips

The top row is the easiest place to start, because a word has to fill all six tiles of it and nothing else touches row 1. Next, trace the right edge: column 6 holds seven spangram tiles, so anything you find there below row 1 is part of the long word. Every other column has just one spangram tile, which means the diagonal is where the spangram can hide among theme words.
