**The New York Times (NYT) Pips for Monday, Dec. 7, 2026, puts 28 tiles on three boards, and 22 of them are different.** Ian Livengood built Easy and Medium, Rodolfo Kurchan built Hard. Hard carries the 0-0 and 1-1 doubles and Easy has none; the rules sit on our [Pips page](/news/topic/nyt-pips/#how-it-works). Sunday's boards are on the [Dec. 6 page](/news/nyt-pips-hints-answers-december-6-2026/).

On Medium, three of its seven regions only ask for matching cells, and a single three-cell region carries the only sum. Hard has twelve sums on single cells and three on pairs. Easy and Medium have 6 and 7 regions for 10 and 14 cells, while Hard has 21 for 32.

The deal of 5, 7 and 16 adds to 28 tiles. Six of them are repeats, which leaves 22 different ones. 2-5 turns up on all three boards, while 6-4, 1-3, 0-6 and 3-4 appear on both Easy and Hard. The double-six set never shows 0-2, 0-5, 1-6, 4-4, 4-5 or 5-5.

## The forced first move on each Dec. 7 board

Cells are named by row, then column, from the top left.

**Easy.** R2C3 must be less than 1, so it can only hold a 0. Of the five tiles, only 0-6 has one, so that tile goes down first with its 0 on R2C3. Its 6 then lands on R1C3 above or R3C3 below, the only two cells that touch R2C3. In the Times' solution the 6 sits on R1C3.

**Medium.** R3C5 and R4C5 must add up to more than 11, and no pip is above 6, so both hold a 6. R3C5 has nothing to its left or right; only R2C5 above and R4C5 below touch it. R2C5 must be less than 3, and the tiles with a 6 (6-6, 6-3, 6-5) have no other half that low. So R3C5 shares a tile with R4C5, and the only tile that gives both a 6 is the double 6-6.

**Hard.** R1C1 has a single neighbor, R1C2, so those two cells form one tile. R1C1, R1C2 and R1C3 must add up to more than 15, which is at least 16. A pip is never above 6, so R1C1 and R1C2 must reach 10. Only 6-4 does; the next largest totals in the list are 6-2 and 3-5 at 8. That fixes 6-4 on R1C1 and R1C2 and a 6 on R1C3.

## Pips hints for Dec. 7, 2026

**Easy (five dominoes: 2-5, 6-4, 1-3, 0-6, 3-4)**

- Hint 1: R1C2 with R1C3 and R3C2 with R3C3 must each add up to more than 6, and just two tiles in the list carry a 6.
- Hint 2: R2C3 is less than 1, and one tile in the list has a 0 to put there.

**Medium (seven dominoes: 2-2, 6-6, 5-2, 6-3, 6-5, 3-3, 1-0)**

- Hint 1: R3C5 and R4C5 must add up to more than 11, and no pip is higher than 6.
- Hint 2: Three tiles in the list carry a 6, and one of them is a double.

**Hard (sixteen dominoes: 0-3, 1-1, 1-4, 2-4, 4-3, 6-4, 6-2, 3-2, 1-3, 0-6, 0-0, 4-0, 2-1, 1-5, 5-2, 3-5)**

- Hint 1: R1C1, R1C2 and R1C3 must add up to more than 15.
- Hint 2: R1C1 has only one neighbor on the board, and one tile in the list has pips that add up to 10.

## What the Dec. 7 boards leave open

Starting from the Times' solution, Medium has no tile that can be turned around alone. Easy has one: 2-5 on R3C2 and R3C3 still satisfies its region either way. Hard has three. 2-4 can be turned on R6C5 and R6C6, 6-4 on R1C1 and R1C2, and 2-1 on R3C6 and R4C6.
