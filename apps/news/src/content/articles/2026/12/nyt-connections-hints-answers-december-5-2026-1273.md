## Why five tiles nearly fit two groups on Dec. 5

Reading only the official group names, these five entries pull toward a second home. Nothing else on the board does.

| Tile | Near-fit | Where it lands | What decides it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pad | Spaceship Features, as in a launch pad | Add Protective Material To | As a verb, to pad is to add padding, the same job as Cushion |
| Pod | Add Protective Material To, since a pod wraps what is inside | Spaceship Features | The group asks for features of a ship, and a pod is one |
| Line | Spaceship Features or the "Mobile" group, as a fuel line or a phone line | Add Protective Material To | To line something is to add a layer inside it |
| Jack | The "Mobile" group, since a jack is a phone connector | Ways to Brusquely Address a Stranger | Jack works as a gruff "hey, jack" in the same way as Buster |
| Mac | The "Mobile" group, as a laptop is portable | Ways to Brusquely Address a Stranger | Mac is a form of address, like Bub |

Pad and Pod are one letter apart and each could be the other's home, so settle them together. Two groups are the loose ones: Spaceship Features and What "Mobile" Might Refer To can both soak up Line, Jack or Mac, which is why the two wordier entries are the safest place to begin.

A safe route:

1. **What "Mobile" Might Refer To.** Alabama City and Calder Sculpture each name something only "Mobile" itself can mean; Phone and Portable complete it.
2. **Ways to Brusquely Address a Stranger.** Bub, Buster, Jack and Mac are all what you might bark at someone you do not know.
3. **Spaceship Features.** Airlock, Cockpit, Pod and Thruster are parts of a craft.
4. **Add Protective Material To.** Cushion, Insulate, Line and Pad are what remains.
